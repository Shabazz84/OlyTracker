# Weightlifting Analysis: Snatch Technical & Strength Development

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Snatch Balance**: 4-6 sets of 3 reps, 70% → working up weekly while maintaining volume; once/week frequency; 115–120% of best snatch from floor (athlete: ~92–96 kg)
- **Pause-at-Knee Snatches**: 60–70% starting load, work to ~80% of best snatch; builds barbell path control
- **Snatch Grip Deadlifts**: 5 sets of 3–5 reps, work up to best snatch from floor (~80 kg); posterior chain emphasis
- **Upper Back Direct Work** (rows, lat pulldowns): high volume/weight tolerance

## 2. **Technique Cues: Snatch**
- **Start Position** (KEY for this athlete):
  - Shoulders *in front* of barbell (not on top/behind)
  - Hamstrings at/slightly below parallel (adjust for long limbs/short torso)
  - Back tight, legs loaded → balanced leg/back pull
  - **Isometric drill**: 30-second holds, 3–5 sets before every snatch session
  
- **Knee Path**: Move knees *back* during pull (not aggressive); center weight on foot to avoid pendulum effect
- **Barbell Path**: Curved arc toward hips (center of mass), not looping around knees

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- **Overhead Holds**: 5 sets of 45 seconds (empty bar), daily if mobility limited
- **Trap Mobility**: Barbell soft tissue rolling + tennis ball pinning (15–20 sec holds) to enable trap lengthening overhead
- **Snatch Balance**: 115–120% loads
- **Upper Back**: Dumbbell seal rows, barbell/cable rows, lat pulldowns

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- Overhead mobility is neurological; load safe positions (empty bar holds) for outsized returns
- Trap tightness → internal shoulder rotation → overhead instability; address via soft tissue + loaded mobility
- Daily trap/overhead work if mobility severely limited

## 5. **Athlete-Specific Relevance**

**CRITICAL ISSUES FOR THIS ATHLETE:**
- **OHS 50 kg (major snatch limiter)** ← Overhead stability work directly addresses this; 45-sec holds + trap mobility essential
- **Chronic back pain** ← Snatch grip deadlifts can be done "hamstring/glute dominant" (hips back, heels weighted) to reduce lower back load; prioritize this variation
- **Push jerk only (no split jerk)** ← Snatch balance/OHS work translates to overhead stability; doesn't directly fix jerk deficit
- **Start position cue** (shoulders in front) ← Critical for balanced leg/back pull; likely a technical weak point limiting 80 kg clean
- **Night shift (Wed–Sun)** ← No specific advice given; consider: snatch mobility work is low-intensity, ideal for fatigue management

**Recommendation**: Lead hypertrophy block with overhead holds (daily), trap mobility, and snatch-specific technical work. Snatch grip deadlifts (hinge-dominant) mitigate back pain while building posterior chain.
