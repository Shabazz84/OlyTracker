# How To Deep Squat As A Tall Lifter
**Channel:** Sika Strength | **Date:** 2025-12-05 | **Language:** en

## Key Points
- "Being tall / long legs" is not an excuse for a shallow squat. Leg length won't change, but technique and physique can be optimized for a deep, heavy, upright squat.
- Rejects the three common "lazy coaching" fixes for tall lifters: (1) stand wider + push hips back, (2) settle for just-below-parallel, (3) accept you'll never squat as well as a short-levered lifter. All three cap progress.
- The deep squat for a tall lifter is built on a standardized framework (feet → knees → hips → midline → bar/upper back → head) **plus** three non-negotiable keys: ankle/hip mobility, bigger quads, and the discipline to keep the new technique under heavy load.

## Technique Cues (back squat, ground-up framework)
- **Stance:** ~shoulder-width. Too wide → glute/posterior-dominant, hips drive back, quads under-developed long-term. Avoid pigeon-toed too.
- **Toe angle:** ~15° out, NOT 45°. Excessive toe-out feels like it tears the groin at depth.
- **Knee tracking:** knees track straight forward over center of toes. Push knees forward through the **entire** squat and keep them forward as long as possible — don't shove knees forward at the start then run out of room and kick hips back. "Control the knees → control the quads."
- **Hips/lower back:** STOP thrusting hips forward at the top. Set hips in natural lumbar extension and hold it the whole set — no flat back, no hyperextension. (Directly relevant to a back-pain athlete: maintain neutral, no end-range thrust.)
- **Midline/bracing:** get tight like bracing for a punch to the stomach; neutral ribs, don't flare or crunch the rib cage down.
- **Bar position:** Sika's **mid-bar / hybrid** placement — bar on the acromion process (bony point on the back of the shoulder), between high-bar and low-bar. Supports an upright torso.
- **Hands/grip:** hands narrow enough to lock upper-back tightness, but not so narrow you lose wrist position. Too wide → upper back loosens → forward tilt out of the hole. Strong grip + neutral wrists = confidence under heavy bar. Avoid fingertip/talon grips.
- **Head:** ear-hole stacked over shoulders (neutral). Tight upper backs drift the head forward, ruining weight distribution and leg drive.

## Mobility (Key 1 — daily, ~1×/day for the first month)
- **Calves:** roll with a barbell (more effective than foam roller), ~1 min/side × 3–4 rounds. Then **heavy weighted** static calf stretch (push-up position, heel down, 25→50 kg / up to 50% BW), driving knee forward, breathe and relax. PNF optional.
- **Pause squats** on empty bar (5–10 reps) to drill the deep position as motor skill, not just stretch.
- **Hips/glutes:** roll TFL (just above femur head) + glute max/med with a hard ball, ~1 min. Runner's lunge (elbow inside the knee), 1 min/side. Banded couch stretch (band distraction pulling hip forward) for hip flexor/quad.
- Principle: stretching is mostly the nervous system **relearning what's safe** — go slow and consistent, not aggressive; maintenance work keeps the gains.

## Programming / Exercise Recommendations (Key 2 — bigger quads)
- Tall lifters lack quad mass; run a focused quad-hypertrophy block.
- **Belt squat** (or improvised: dip belt / old lifting belt + chain off boxes) — heavy, quad-dominant, spares the back. ← strong fit for a back-pain athlete who needs leg size without spinal load.
- **Heels-raised narrow-stance goblet squat** — big knee ROM, forward knee travel, loads quads without loading the whole body. Use a timber block (not a plate) to avoid toe splay.
- **Split squats** with forward knee drive (not a vertical hip drop), heel may rise; 15–20% BW each hand, 8–12 reps/side, high volume.
- **Knees-forward band drill** (empty bar / bodyweight) for kinesthetic feedback on quad drive standing out of the hole.
- Key 3 = discipline: most lifters do the work, then revert to shallow heavy squats chasing PBs. Keep the depth and technique once load goes up.

## Athlete Relevance (102.5 kg, BS 118 kg, OHS 50 kg limiter, back pain, tall)
- **Direct hit on this athlete's profile.** He is the exact case: tall, long-femur, squat/OHS depth is the snatch ceiling. This is a self-coaching blueprint for that limiter.
- **Back-pain safe:** the "hold natural lumbar extension, never thrust the hips at the top, neutral ribs" cues align with Principle 12 (no flexion under load, upright posture). Belt squat lets him build quads with near-zero spinal load.
- **OHS carryover:** more ankle dorsiflexion + deeper, more upright bottom position directly addresses the 50 kg OHS limiter — same depth/torso demand as the snatch catch.
- **Mid-bar position** is consistent with Day 1/Day 3 squat work and the upright-torso requirement for OLY.
- **Actionable additions:** add belt squat + heels-raised goblet + forward-knee split squat as named quad accessories on leg days; add the calf-barbell-roll → heavy calf stretch → empty-bar pause squat sequence to the Mobility tab as a daily ankle protocol; adopt "knees forward the whole rep, control the knees → control the quads" and "ear over shoulder, no hip thrust at top" as squat cues.
