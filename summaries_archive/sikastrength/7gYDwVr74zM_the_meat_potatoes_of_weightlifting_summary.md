# Weightlifting Analysis: Coaching Vlog Transcript

## 1. Programming Principles
- **Prioritization over perfection**: Addressing deficits (back strength, leg drive) takes precedence over perfect technique in heavy singles
- **Session sequencing**: Heavy squat + pulls before heavy snatch/clean-jerk session acceptable if deficits are priority
- **Volume emphasis**: High repetition pulls (below-knee) prioritized to rebuild weakened back
- **Intensity progression**: Working up to heavy singles (120kg snatch, 130kg clean) within same block
- **Rep ranges**: 3–5 reps at 70–80% for movement prep (push press example: 70kg for sets of 5)

## 2. Technique Cues
**Snatch**: Active arm pull in third pull; aggressive pull-under; focus on lockout speed
**Clean**: Push harder from ground initially (over-relying on back pull); minimize bar height drop; strong turnover timing; aggressive pull-under
**Push Press**: Prioritize *lockout speed* over absolute load; avoid heavy grinds; solid rack position; dip-drive extension quality

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
- **Push presses**: Light (70kg), higher reps, speed-focused
- **Below-knee/low-hang pulls**: Heavy emphasis for back position and tension maintenance
- **Snatch/clean pulls & deadlifts**: Acknowledged as neglected; needs increased volume

## 4. Recovery/Mobility
- **Lat mobility critical for overhead**: Grab-and-stretch into corners, armpit push-out emphasis
- **External rotation hangs**: Addressing tight lats from judo background
- **Bilateral attention**: One side typically tighter; monitor asymmetries

## 5. Athlete-Specific Relevance
⚠️ **High relevance to YOUR profile**:
- **Back pain context**: Coach explicitly addressing weakened back; high-volume pull work is core strategy
- **Snatch limiter (OHS 50kg)**: Mobility work (lats) + aggressive arm pull under + pull-under mechanics directly applicable
- **Push jerk only**: Coach mentions "rack position used for split jerk"—foreshadowing split jerk introduction; push press speed work bridges gap
- **Intermediate transitioning**: Heavy single approach + accepting technical imperfection near-term while building capacity matches progression model

**Note**: Coach is analyzing *his own* training; some cues highly individualized (120kg snatch suggests elite level). Adapt intensity percentages to your 80kg clean baseline.
