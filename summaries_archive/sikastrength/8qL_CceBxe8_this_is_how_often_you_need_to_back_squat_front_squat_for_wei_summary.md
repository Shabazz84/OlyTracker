# Weightlifting Analysis: Squat Frequency for Olympic Weightlifting

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Best general frequency for intermediate lifters**: 1 Front Squat + 2 Back Squats/week
  - Back squat drives front squat progress more efficiently than front squatting alone
  - Higher tonnage on back squat = better leg & upper body strength carryover
  - Sustainable long-term; prioritize consistency over extreme intensity/frequency
- **Peaking phase option**: 2 Front Squats + 1 Back Squat (6-8 weeks max)
  - Useful when front squat strength directly impacts clean & jerk performance
  - Focus on technique practice, not max-effort volume
- **Advanced option**: 2 Front Squats + 2 Back Squats (intermediate-Elite lifters with high training volume)
  - Only if training 5+ days/week and recovery capacity exists

## 2. **Technique/Sport-Specific Cues**
- Maintain upright torso and deep squat on both movements
- Front squat = sport-specific skill for cleans; back squat = strength driver
- Front squat quality supported by mobility + assistance work, not frequency alone

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- **For this athlete**: Start with **1 Front Squat + 2 Back Squats/week**
  - Back squat (102.5 kg BW, 118 kg BS) has room to grow; drives front squat up
  - Addresses chronic back pain risk by distributing squat stress strategically
  - Compatible with night shift (Wed–Sun) if programmed on non-consecutive days

## 4. **Recovery Notes**
- Squat every day = overuse injury risk; not recommended unless Elite with excellent recovery
- Consistency beats extreme frequency

## 5. **Relevant to This Athlete**
- **OHS/snatch limiter (50 kg)**: Prioritize front squat technique quality & thoracic mobility
- **Chronic back pain**: Avoid excessive back squat frequency; 2/week is safer than 3+
- **Night shift**: Squat 2x/week fits better than daily squatting for recovery compliance
