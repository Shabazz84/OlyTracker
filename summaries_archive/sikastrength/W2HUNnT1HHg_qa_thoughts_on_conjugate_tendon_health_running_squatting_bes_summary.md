# MERGED WEIGHTLIFTING SUMMARY

## Key Points

**Hypertrophy Block (6 weeks)**
- Use 8–15 rep range with lower intensities to preserve CNS recovery for sport-specific work
- Start light in weeks 1–3 (feels "easy") to build technical runway and avoid mid-block fatigue wall
- Prioritize caloric surplus (+25%, ~2,500 kcal/day for 102.5 kg) and sleep over program design
- Progression structure: Complex movements (snatch pull + snatch + hang snatch) → full lifts as block advances

**Strength Phase (Post-Hypertrophy)**
- Back squat priority: Add 20–40 kg within 6–12 months post-block to enable snatch consistency at 90%+ intensity
- Strength gains require hypertrophy foundation first (muscle mass = capacity), then neurological adaptation
- RTA1 (8-week) periodization preferred over RTA2 (12-week) for weightlifters—avoids interfering with snatch/clean-jerk frequency

**Deload Structure**
- Plan 3–4 full days off after ~24 weeks of consistent training; avoid 2-week breaks (maintains conditioning)

## Technique Cues

**Corrective Exercise Timing**
- Use very light correctives (40 kg reference) in specific warm-up *before* standard cascade (50–60–70–80–90%)
- Pause variations + full lift complexes early; remove derivatives by heavy warm-up loads to prevent dependency

**Snatch/OHS-Specific (50 kg Limiter)**
- Mobility benchmarks: Hit all positions (OHS, front rack, snatch catch) unweighted with PVC/empty bar early in warm-up
- Static stretching timing: Perform 2+ hours *before* training (morning, before night shift) to avoid neurological inhibition near maximal efforts
- Isometric quad extensions for tendon strengthening: 5×30 sec/leg → build to 1.5–2 min holds → add light plyometrics (box jumps, landing control)

**Core Work Philosophy**
- Pallof press: Coordinates torso stiffness and upper/lower body connection; treat as *skill/coordination* (anti-rotational, static bracing) before strength
- Core work essential for maintaining torso angles in squat and protecting chronic back

**Split Jerk Development (65 kg Push Jerk)**
- Likely technical/positional gap—use light-weight pause + full jerks early in warm-ups
- Prioritize consistent split jerk work during hypertrophy block (lower CNS demand, builds positional strength)
- Front rack mobility + shoulder stability prerequisites; address before heavy split jerk loads

---

## Programming Principles

- **Avoid conjugate method**: Random exercise rotation kills specificity; stick to progressive, consistent movements
- **Complex → specific progression**: Don't reverse (accessories toward full lifts, not vice versa)
- **Exercise consistency**: Full-session volume bias during hypertrophy; minimize heavy loads to preserve recovery capacity for Olympic lift practice
- **Tendon conditioning**: Heavy Olympic lifts already tax tendons via rapid deceleration; add isometrics if mobility work has been slow/pause-heavy

## Athlete Relevance

| Factor | Application |
|--------|------------|
| **OHS 50 kg (primary snatch limiter)** | Aggressive structured mobility work non-negotiable; isometric holds + plyometrics during hypertrophy block; early-warm-up correctives |
| **Chronic back pain** | Pallof press + postural stability work critical; full-depth squatting for hypertrophy; mobility sessions essential; avoid aggressive volume spikes |
| **Night shift (Wed–Sun)** | Mobility sessions in morning (before shift) to offset postural fatigue and protect evening training power; caloric surplus + sleep management crucial |
| **Push jerk only (no split jerk)** | Front rack mobility prerequisite; consistent light split jerk work during hypertrophy block; use 65 kg as baseline, build technique early |
| **Back squat 118 kg** | Primary lever for snatch consistency; target +20–40 kg over 6–12 months; emphasize in strength phase post-hypertrophy |
| **6-week hypertrophy block** | RTA1 structure recommended; start weeks 1–3 light; maintain 8–15 rep range; ensure +25% caloric surplus; plan 3–4 day deload after ~24 weeks total work |

**Critical action**: Commit to 30–60 min mobility sessions 4–5×/week *separate* from training, with emphasis on static stretching in morning hours.
