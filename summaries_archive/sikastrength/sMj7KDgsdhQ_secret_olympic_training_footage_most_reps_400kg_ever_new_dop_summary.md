# Merged Weightlifting Summary

## Key Points
- Block lifting (snatch/clean from blocks) enables overload training; heavy singles/doubles/triples are standard post-Olympic programming.
- Vertical snatch extension with minimal lean-back is optimal; deeper hip extension in hang snatch translates better to full snatch.
- Aggressive foot placement and speed under the bar are critical for clean efficiency; split jerk preferred for heavy loads over push jerk.
- Extra mobility (10–15% beyond ideal catch position) acts as a safety margin for bar position errors during heavy lifts.
- **Source limitation**: Focused on elite maximal strength/competition footage; lacks hypertrophy programming, chronic pain management, and shift-work periodization.

## Technique Cues
**Snatch**: Maintain vertical extension at top with no lean-back; achieve consistent sweep off floor. Deeper hip extension (not just dip-drive) in hang snatch improves full snatch positioning. Build 10–15% extra ankle ROM as recovery margin.

**Clean**: Drive feet actively off platform for faster positioning. Prioritize speed under bar with aggressive pull; catch high and adjust depth as needed. Maintain strong hook grip and postural tension throughout pull for jerk setup readiness.

**Jerk**: Time dip/drive to match bar bend precisely. Generate speed off chest—critical for heavy loads. Split jerk superior to push jerk for maximal weights.

**Back Squat**: Maintain consistent upper back angle; keep elbows behind shoulder blade level throughout to avoid excessive rise.

## Programming Principles
- Block work for overload (heavy singles/doubles on snatch and clean from various heights).
- Maximal strength emphasis (post-Olympic training model); power variations historically used on lighter/off days but less common now.
- Volume concentrated at heavy relative intensities; no structured hypertrophy block present in source.
