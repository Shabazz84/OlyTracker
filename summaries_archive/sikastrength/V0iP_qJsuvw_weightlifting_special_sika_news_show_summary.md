# MERGED SUMMARY

## Key Points

- **Assistance work volume matters for weightlifters**: Heavy Olympic lifts (snatch, clean, jerk, squat) create significant neurotransmitter depletion. Moderate-volume, sub-maximal intensity assistance work is safer and more effective than bodybuilding-style high-volume training to failure.
- **Posterior chain + midline stability are critical**, especially for athletes with chronic back pain or limited overhead strength. Butt wink during squat/jerk dip collapses upper back and pushes barbell forward—increases injury risk.
- **Tricep/shoulder assistance transfers directly to overhead pressing**: Heavy, high-volume tricep work (2x/week) compounds into measurable jerk/OHS gains over training blocks.
- **Strength-to-technique mismatch is common in intermediate lifters**: Technical refinement must follow hypertrophy/strength blocks to consolidate gains.

## Technique Cues

**Clean:**
- Pull under the bar aggressively; don't rely on pulling height alone.

**Snatch:**
- Patience in start position → explosive hip extension and acceleration through lockout.

**Squat/OHS (quad-dominant positioning):**
- Hip-back position with knees slightly out recruits posterior chain throughout descent and ascent.
- Prevent butt wink by maintaining posterior chain engagement; avoid collapsing at bottom without loaded back.

**Jerk dip (push jerk focus):**
- Slight knee valgus (knees tracking out) during dip stabilizes lower back position and prevents pelvic tuck.
- Maintains upper back rigidity and keeps barbell centered.

## Programming Principles

- **Assistance work volume cap**: ~20 sets/week per body part maximum when combining heavy Olympic lifts; exceeding this causes excessive fatigue.
- **Machines preferred over free weights** when fatigued (safer for tired nervous system).
- **Priority assistance movements**: Rows, tricep extensions (heavy, 2x/week), shoulder work—all build overhead strength with lower spinal/joint stress than free-weight alternatives.
- **Sequencing recommendation**: Hypertrophy block (6 weeks) → technique/strength refinement block (6+ weeks) aligns with intermediate-to-Olympic WL transition.

## Athlete Relevance

| Constraint | Application |
|-----------|------------|
| **Chronic back pain + OHS limiter (50kg)** | Prioritize posterior chain assistance work during hypertrophy block. Strengthen triceps, shoulders, upper back via machines to build overhead capacity. Focus squat technique on posterior chain engagement; avoid butt wink. |
| **Push jerk only (no split jerk)** | Knee valgus in dip supports lower back stability; use this cue to protect spine during high-volume pressing work. |
| **BS 118kg vs. Clean 80kg (strength-to-technique gap)** | 6-week hypertrophy block should emphasize technical refinement in clean pulls + assistance work; don't chase squat strength at expense of Olympic lift mechanics. |
| **Night shift worker (Wed–Sun)** | Not addressed in source; prioritize sleep/recovery optimization outside this guidance. |
