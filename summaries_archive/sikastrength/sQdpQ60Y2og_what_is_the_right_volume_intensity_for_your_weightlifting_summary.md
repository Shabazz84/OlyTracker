# What Is The Right Volume & Intensity For Your Weightlifting?

# Weightlifting Coaching Analysis

## 1. Programming Principles
- **Volume**: Remains consistent across career stages; starts higher (5×3, 6×3 snatch; 5×2+2, 3+1 clean/jerk) and progresses through mid-range to singles
- **Intensity**: Typically 60–65% starting intensity (higher than general strength work due to lower starting volumes); progress intensity weekly via load increases OR exercise difficulty (e.g., snatch → snatch balance)
- **Frequency**: 4–5 sessions/week sustainable for years; natural lifters can reach 120–150 kg snatch, ~300 kg total
- **Progression structure**: Higher volume → mid-range volume → heavy singles/maxes
- **Block length**: 8–12 weeks typical; avoid long off-seasons between blocks (wastes 25–33% of training time)
- **Snatch vs. Clean/Jerk volume**: Snatch uses 10–20% more volume; snatch emphasizes precision/technique refinement; clean/jerk more strength/attribute-dependent

## 2. Technique Cues
- **Snatch**: "Fine-tuning masterpiece" — each rep refines technique; even at 60–70% can yield quality sessions with poor fatigue tolerance
- **Clean/Jerk**: More strength-dependent; requires being "fresh and peaked" — sloppy/fatigued reps at 60% less useful than snatch equivalent

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
- Use complexes/variations to modulate intensity without changing load (e.g., snatch + OHS → snatch + snatch balance keeps volume, increases difficulty)
- Support work critical for clean/jerk: prioritize pulling and squatting strength, recovery
- No "light technique blocks" — start blocks with appropriate volume/intensity from week 1

## 4. Recovery/Mobility
- Clean/jerk demands good conditioning; fatigue impacts performance more than snatch
- Support adequate pulling/squatting capacity before heavy clean/jerk sessions

## 5. Relevance to Athlete
- **Back pain concern**: Not addressed in text; OHS limiter (50 kg) suggests mobility/stability issue — prioritize OHS work in hypertrophy block
- **Push jerk only**: Clean/jerk is strength-heavy; needs strong squat (118 kg BS is solid baseline). Jerk 65 kg suggests weak dip strength or technique — focus split jerk development
- **Night shift**: Not addressed; recovery timing important given fatigue
- **6-week hypertrophy block**: Text emphasizes NOT doing "light technique blocks" — start with appropriate intensity/volume immediately

---
**Key Takeaway**: Don't delay with light work; begin hypertrophy block at 60–65% intensity with proper volume structure. Address OHS mobility and split jerk mechanics.
