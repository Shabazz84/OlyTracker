# Squat Tips For Tall Lifters!
**Channel:** Sika Strength | **Date:** 2025-01-23 | **Language:** en

## Key Points
- A good squat needs only two things: bar over base of support, hips below parallel. Tall lifters can reach respectable weights and good positions — but must manage specific flaws.
- **The signature tall-lifter flaw:** knees shoot back early → hips go back → big forward torso inclination (squat becomes a good morning). At ~90% loads a good squatter counteracts this by keeping knees forward and hips forward standing out of the hole.
- **Four-pronged fix:** (1) stronger legs/quads, (2) train the knees-forward motor pattern, (3) mobility (ankle, hip, shoulder), (4) midline control. Going straight to the "ideal" pattern without these drops you to ~60–70% of your old 1RM — not sustainable; run a prioritized leg/quad block first, then surpass old numbers.

## Programming Principles
- **Work capacity, not just strength:** tall lifters' quads lack both absolute strength AND work capacity. Positions hold around 65–70% 1RM / 3–5 reps but break down above that. → Start the strength cycle with **higher-volume working sets** and keep high-volume leg-accessory work in for a long time.
- **Posterior-bias correction:** tall lifters start posterior-dominant (hamstrings/glutes/back > quads); strength training deepens the bias (hips sit back, shins vertical sooner). A deliberate, prescribed quad-priority block is required.
- **Mobility is slow:** ankle mobility took the coach ~6 months of weekly PT + daily stretching. Build the strength block around the current mobility; raise heels / shorten depth while ankles catch up.

## Technique Cues & Motor-Pattern Tools
- Keep knees forward and hips forward standing out of the bottom; keep quads engaged the whole rep. A little forward torso lean is natural even for elite morphology — counteract it, don't amplify it.
- **Heels-raised, narrower (toes near-straight-forward) stance squats** for max forward knee travel. Start as goblet squat / belt squat (weight in front → easier upright torso); progress to barbell when kettlebells/goblet load runs out.
- **Temporary just-above-parallel squat** (narrow, toes-forward): keeps hips forward (never reaches the deepest hip-back point), lets you keep training depth while ankle mobility lags, and maintains constant quad tension (avoids the bottom bounce that makes knees/hips shoot back). Explicitly a short-term tool, not long-term.
- **Bar position:** mid-bar / hybrid on the acromion shelf (between high- and low-bar); stack hands narrower for a stronger upright upper back.

## Mobility (three areas — join into one daily routine)
- **Ankle (most important):** load it heavily — bent-knee stretch with 15–25 kg on the knee driving it over the toe, or bodyweight on a step pushing down. Hold **long: 90 s–2 min**, 3–5 sets/side, **daily**. Long tibias → poorer ankle mobility.
- **Hip:** mostly external rotation + general flexion/extension. Runner's lunge (push-up position, foot up by hand, drop elbow to floor); couch stretch / banded couch stretch for hip flexors (esp. desk workers).
- **Shoulder/upper back:** needed to hold the narrow mid-bar rack. Tight shoulders → wider hands → weaker upper back → more forward lean out of the hole. Stack hands closer.

## Midline Control (anterior + posterior + anti-rotation)
- 2–3×/week, ~one per session. Anterior: plank (push-up or elbow). Posterior: prone back-extension hold (glute bridge hold if back is already strong). Anti-rotation: side plank (Pallof press later).
- **Isometric progression ladder:** 3–5 (or 4–6) sets; start 15–30 s holds, add 5–10 s/week, build to **90 s**, then switch to a harder variation (plank → Superman plank; back-extension hold → heels-anchored raised version) and rebuild to 90 s.

## Athlete Relevance (102.5 kg, BS 118 kg, OHS 50 kg limiter, back pain, tall)
- **Third independent tall-lifter squat source, same conclusions — now very high confidence.** Reinforces Sika #1 and Telander/Aita: prescribed quad-priority block + back-sparing accessories + heavy daily ankle work.
- **New, directly actionable pieces:**
  - **Volume rule:** open Block 1 squat work with higher-volume sets (~65–70%, 3–5 reps) where positions stay perfect; keep leg-accessory volume high for a long stretch. Fits the existing hypertrophy-first block.
  - **Just-above-parallel heels-raised squat** as a temporary bridge drill while ankle mobility improves — useful given the 50 kg OHS limiter is partly ankle-gated.
  - **Concrete ankle dosing** (15–25 kg loaded, 90 s–2 min, 3–5 sets, daily) → drop straight into the Mobility tab; expect months, not weeks.
  - **Midline isometric ladder** → directly serves the back-pain athlete (anti-good-morning trunk control); the 90 s → harder-variation progression is a clean app-trackable scheme.
- **Back-pain fit:** the whole premise — stop the hips shooting back / torso folding — is the same mechanism that protects the lower back under load (Principle 12). Belt/goblet squats and isometrics build the legs and trunk without spinal loading.
