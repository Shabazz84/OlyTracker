# Follow This Warm Up For Your Snatch

# Analysis: Snatch-Specific Warm-Up Protocol

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **General → Specific progression**: Mobility → cardio → general barbell → snatch-specific work
- **Consistency emphasized**: Same warm-up recipe every session
- **Volume**: 6 min or less for full general warm-up (3 rounds); 5 sets of empty bar snatches (~1 min rest between)
- **Frequency of mobility**: Between barbell sets, target 1 tight area for ~1 min (static stretches, 30 sec each side or 8-10 breaths)

## 2. **Technique Cues (Snatch-Focused)**
- **RDL**: Look straight ahead, upper back tight, shoulders back
- **Bent-over rows**: Pull bar into hip crease (not to chest)
- **Overhead press**: Small pause overhead, smooth
- **Snatch balance**: Actively press elbows/upper body up into bar; drive bar over head; move feet fast to catch position
- **Snatch triple**: Start mid-shin, stand bar to top, hold 1 sec, lower controlled (don't drop early)

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- General barbell warm-up complex: 5 RDLs → 5 bent-over rows → 5 strict overhead press → 5 back squats (repeat 3×)
- Snatch-specific: 3 strict presses behind neck → 3 overhead squats → snatch balances (dip/drive, catching high then low)
- Empty bar snatches: 5 sets × 3 reps before loading

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- **Shoulder/lat/pec tightness**: Elbow position, pull down (not lean in), push hips back; stay square; 8–10 deep breaths per side
- **General principle**: Identify one tight area between sets and address efficiently

## 5. **Relevance to This Athlete**
- **OHS limiter (50 kg)**: Overhead mobility work (strict press behind neck, OHS emphasis) directly addresses weak point
- **Chronic back pain**: Consistent RDL form (neutral spine, upper back tight, no spinal flexion) and bent-over row positioning reduce loading risk
- **Night shift (Wed–Sun)**: Efficient 6-min warm-up fits tight schedule
- **No split jerk**: OHS/overhead stability work transfers well; no split-jerk-specific warm-up needed

---

**Key takeaway**: Structured warm-up prioritizes overhead mobility and snatch technique before intensity, supporting hypertrophy block and addressing OHS/back limitations.
