# Don't Keep Making These Snatch Mistakes!

# Weightlifting Coaching Transcript Analysis

## 1. Programming Principles
- **No foot snatches**: 5-6 sets of 3 reps (warm-up or dedicated session); emphasize speed/extension since foot movement won't aid velocity
- **Pause at knee snatches**: Reinforce hip positioning without foot interference
- **High pull + snatch complex**: 5-6 sets of 2 high pulls + 1 snatch; progress by dropping one high pull and increasing weight
- **Snatch grip deadlift + snatch**: Focus on knee trajectory, followed by full snatch
- **Slow pull snatches**: Warm-up or full session to correct timing and arm bend
- **Low hang snatches**: Practice chest position and hip delay

---

## 2. Technique Cues (Snatch Focus)

| Issue | Cue |
|-------|-----|
| **Hip early/forward jump** | Keep hips back through knee; second pull lasts as long as possible; bar travels back over center of balance for power expression |
| **Knees out** | Knees primarily move *back* (not out) during pull; narrower pull position = more leg drive; widen feet for catch |
| **Early arm bend** | Arms stay relatively straight at hip; traps extend first, then pull with elbows after; keeps bar close and speed high |
| **Two-beat rhythm** | Fast off floor BUT faster above knee—avoid aggressive initial pull |
| **Chest drop** | Maintain upright torso; dropping chest pushes balance forward, causing early hip engagement |
| **Jump back** | Caused by early hips + subtle form breaks; delays hip engagement by milliseconds = critical |

---

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
- No foot snatches
- Pause at knee snatches
- High pull + snatch complex
- Snatch grip deadlift + snatch
- Slow pull snatches
- Low hang snatches
- Muscle snatches (warm-up)
- No contact snatches/muscle snatches

---

## 4. Recovery/Mobility Advice
- Weightlifting shoes improve stability even with excellent mobility
- Good ankle/hip/thoracic mobility demonstrated but still benefits from shoes

---

## 5. Relevance to This Athlete

**Highly relevant.**

- **OHS limiter (50 kg)**: Early arm bend and hip positioning issues directly impact catch depth and stability—no foot/pause snatches would help
- **Push jerk only**: Chest drop and early arm bend will also plague jerk pull—slow pull work transfers
- **Chronic back pain**: Early hip engagement + knees-out pattern increase lumbar demand; emphasize: (1) delayed hips, (2) knees back, (3) upright chest to reduce shear
- **Intermediate level**: All five errors shown are common at this stage; systematic fix via variations is appropriate
- **Night shift fatigue**: Slow pulls and no-foot snatches require less CNS demand than heavy max attempts; suitable recovery window option

**Priority fix: Hip delay + chest position → reduces back stress + improves OHS catch.**
