# Top 5 Partner Stretches for Weightlifting

# Weightlifting Analysis: Partner Stretches for Weightlifting

## Relevant to This Athlete's Profile

**Recovery & Mobility Advice (Primary Focus):**

1. **Hip Flexor Tightness** — Critical for this athlete
   - Night shift worker (Wed–Sun) + chronic back pain = prolonged sitting/shortened hip flexor positions
   - Weighted quad stretch with partner (knee off floor) to target psoas insertion
   - Kettlebell hip flexor stretch variation
   - Key cue: Keep lower back/pelvis flat on floor; prevent hip scooting
   - Tight hip flexors can cause downstream knee pain (relevant given chronic back issues)

2. **Overhead/Snatch Mobility** — Directly addresses OHS limitation (50 kg)
   - Snatch position stretch with partner, starting wide grip → narrowing over time
   - Med ball under back to increase shoulder extension range
   - Allows moderate rib flare; don't force "ribs down" during mobility work
   - Partner holds stick while athlete relaxes subscaps/posterior delts (passive stretch ~90 sec)
   - Reverse hanging stretch for athletes with existing good shoulder ROM

3. **External Rotation** — Weak overhead position component
   - Prisoner stretch + doorway stretch
   - Athlete will be internally rotated from phone/desk work (night shift recovery environment)
   - Hardest to self-stretch; partner-assisted recommended

4. **Front Rack Mobility** — Supports clean development (80 kg)
   - Reciprocal inhibition drill: partner applies opposing force to stretch while athlete contracts antagonist
   - Enables greater range of motion in rack position

5. **Soft Tissue Work**
   - Address adhesion points/trigger points via lacrosse ball or manual therapy
   - Not aggressive; use varying stimulus intensity
   - Quads noted as particularly tight (pain carryover from hip flexors)

**Programming Principle:**
Mobility work should be frequent + consistent to embed new ROM. Passive stretching (2–3 min holds) more effective than forced ranges.
