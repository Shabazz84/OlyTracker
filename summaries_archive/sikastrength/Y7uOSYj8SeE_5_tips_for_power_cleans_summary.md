# 5 Tips for Power Cleans

# Weightlifting Analysis: Power Clean Coaching

## Programming Principles
- **Warm-up structure**: General barbell warm-up (5 RDLs, 5 bent-over rows, 5 push presses, 5 front squats) → specific weakness work (2-3 sets high/mid/low hang) → working sets
- **Intentional training**: Mental focus on movement specifics throughout warm-up

## Technique Cues – Power Clean

**1. Start Position (Static vs. Dynamic)**
- *Beginner recommendation*: Static start (hips set low initially) → easier coordination
- Avoid "grip and rip" mentality; position matters more than speed off floor

**2. Bar Contact (Quad Smash)**
- Spread contact over 3–4 inches on quads, not one explosive collision
- Cue: avoid pushing hips forward into bar
- Smooth bar path > feeling of power

**3. Foot Movement**
- Move from narrow pulling stance → wider catching stance
- Benefits: mechanical advantage, efficient squat position, strong catch
- **Key**: Don't exceed your natural squat width; moderate catch stance prevents long-term issues

## Relevant to This Athlete

| Issue | Application |
|-------|-------------|
| **Chronic back pain** | Static start reduces cumulative spinal stress vs. dynamic. Emphasis on "back set" during RDLs. |
| **OHS 50 kg (weak)** | Front rack mobility work critical; warm-up includes front squats to prime catch position. |
| **Push jerk only** | Power clean footwork/catch position translates to split jerk progression when ready. |
| **Night shift (Wed–Sun)** | Consistent barbell warm-up routine aids neurological priming despite schedule fatigue. |

## Exercise Recommendations
- Weightlifting shoes (non-negotiable platform)
- Box jumps in warm-up (dynamic priming)
- High→mid→low hang variations (specific pull weakness diagnosis)
