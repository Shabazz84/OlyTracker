## 1. Programming Principles
- **Frequency**: Strict press 2–3x/week (low fatigue, high frequency sustainable)
- **Intensity**: Moderate-to-heavy loads; don't need to max out
- **Volume approach**: Moderate-to-high rep volume on strict press yields most benefits
- **Assistance work**: 2–3x/week upper body bodybuilding/assistance work (non-maximal, doesn't require training to failure)
- **Periodization note**: Upper body stability work × 3 months = measurable improvement

---

## 2. Technique Cues for Jerk (Split Jerk)
- **Dip & drive tempo**: Fast but controlled entry into dip; minimal pause at bottom (spring-like compression/release)
  - Too slow = poor power output, hard to change direction, barbell separation from chest
  - Avoid pause jerks for tempo training; they lag behind actual split jerk capability
- **Barbell connection**: Maintain continuous chest-to-barbell contact throughout dip; separation = timing/power loss
- **Dip depth**: Excellent/full dip depth needed for smooth tempo
- **Stability in split position**: Requires technique + strength + mobility; enables reliable attempt prediction and confidence

**Primary support:**
- **Front squat** (most important): Should be ~1.4–1.6× clean & jerk
  - This athlete: Clean 80 kg → target FS 112–128 kg (currently unknown; back pain may limit)
- **Strict press** (top-2 for split jerk stability): 2–3x/week, moderate-heavy volume
- **Upper body assistance** (all useful): L-raises, weighted push-ups, leg extensions, general bodybuilding work

**NOT recommended:**
- Static hold dips (useless)
- Jerk dips (marginally useful only 170+ kg range; less efficient than front squat)
