# Merged Summary: Weightlifting Coaching Analysis

## Key Points

- **Technique foundation is critical before hypertrophy**: Early arm bend in snatch and forward shrug in pull can each cost 40+ kg lifetime. At 50 kg OHS (primary limiter), technical/mobility fixes must precede volume work.
- **General physical capacity** (not just programming) underpins injury resilience. Chronic back pain + volume intolerance suggests unaddressed mobility or work tolerance issue, not a need to push harder.
- **Night-shift worker timing**: Cardio and recovery protocols require scheduling flexibility (not morning-of or pre-session); acclimatization to steady-state work takes weeks.
- **Trap bar/RDL consideration**: Given chronic back pain and night-shift fatigue, these preserve quality over conventional deadlift.

## Technique Cues

- **Snatch pull**: Eliminate early arm bend and forward shrug; both limit snatch potential significantly for intermediate lifters (50 kg+ differences common between poor and ideal technique).
- **OHS position**: 50 kg ceiling indicates thoracic, ankle, or hip mobility limitation—prioritize mobility assessment over intensity increases.
- Intermediate athletes cannot "get away with" technique flaws like elites; ideal technical framework is non-negotiable before hypertrophy block.

## Programming Principles

- **Cardio protocol**: 2×/week, 10–15 min starting, progressing ~5 min/month to max 45 min. Machine-based (stationary bike preferred for heavier athletes).
- Schedule flexibility critical for Wed–Sun night-shift worker; build in recovery windows before high-volume sessions.
- **Periodization structure**: 6-week hypertrophy block followed by technique/strength work is sound; ensure mobility/technique foundation is solid first.
- **Recovery sequencing**: Drop jumps/plyometric work viable ~6 weeks before fatigue saturation; allow 4–8 week recovery post-strength block before peaking.

## Athlete Relevance

**Immediate action**: Assess OHS mobility (thoracic/ankle/hip) and snatch pull technique before hypertrophy block—50 kg OHS suggests positioning issues limiting snatch potential.

**Program design**: Use trap bar or RDL instead of conventional deadlift; conservative cardio progression; prioritize quality over volume given chronic back pain.

**Work tolerance**: Establish general capacity baseline given night-shift fatigue and pain history before escalating volume.
