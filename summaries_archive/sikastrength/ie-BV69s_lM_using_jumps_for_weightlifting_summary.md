# Weightlifting Analysis: Plyometric Priming & Periodization

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Plyometric frequency**: 3x/week as session primer (aligns with snatch/clean/jerk training days: Mon/Wed/Fri)
- **Plyometric block structure**: 4-6 week rotations in early blocks; intensity increases as competition approaches
- **Depth jump periodization**: Light variation now → intensified 3 weeks before competition; aware of 2-week performance dip post-intensity
- **Load management**: Uses RPE/feel instead of fixed percentages; establishes rep range (e.g., 135–145 kg snatches) and adjusts based on jump quality
- **Competition peaking**: ~6 weeks out begins strategic depth jump accumulation (inspired by Russian "shock method" — up to 200 reps/block for 10–20 kg total increases)

## 2. **Technique/Performance Cues**
- Plyometric quality = readiness indicator: "snapping off ground" and explosive jumps = higher end of daily range; heavy/sluggish jumps = lower end
- **Depth jump transfer to jerk**: Strongest correlation observed; improved depth jump height directly predicts jerk improvements
- "Spring off the box" feel is measurable proxy without force plates

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
**Plyometric template** (4–6 week rotation):
- Counter-movement jump (preload focus)
- Single-leg jump variation
- Static/bilateral jump (seated variation mentioned)
- Depth jump (introduced strategically 3 weeks pre-meet)

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- Aware of 2-week fatigue/performance dip post-high-intensity plyometric blocks; plans accordingly
- Light depth jump variation used as bridge before peaking

## 5. **Athlete-Specific Relevance**
⚠️ **Chronic back pain**: No explicit mention of load tolerance. High-volume depth jumps (200 reps) may need **careful progression** given back history. Consider monitoring impact stress.

⚠️ **Night shift (Wed–Sun)**: Mon/Wed/Fri programming feasible but recovery optimization critical around depth jump blocks.

⚠️ **Push jerk only (no split)**: Depth jump emphasis on jerk is positive, but may limit force absorption in lower body if split mechanics develop later.

**Overall fit**: Plyometric priming aligns well with intermediate athlete transitioning OLY; daily RPE-based load management suits shift work variability.
