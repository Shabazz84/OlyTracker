# Exercise Selection & Order - How To Write Olympic Weightlifting Training Programs | Part 6

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Periodization:** Early cycle = high volume, high exercise variety (variations + basic strength). Late cycle = lower volume, fewer exercises, focus on competition lifts.
*   **Frequency/Structure:** 5-day model recommended for intermediate athletes:
    *   Mon: Snatch variation + Pulls + Back Squat.
    *   Tue: Jerk variation + Snatch technique + Overhead strength (Push Press).
    *   Wed: Clean (+Jerk) + Pulls + Front Squat.
    *   Fri: Heavy Total (Snatch/C&J variations or competition lifts) + Back Squat.
    *   Sat: Technique focus + Overhead strength + Accessories.
*   **Volume Distribution:** Generally less volume on Clean & Jerk than Snatch due to higher systemic fatigue and lower skill demand for the clean compared to the snatch.
*   **Exercise Ordering:** Standard rule: High technical/speed demand first (e.g., Snatch -> Pulls -> Squat). Exceptions: Squat first if leg strength is a priority; group related exercises (Pulls after Snatch) to minimize transition time and maintain warm-up state.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Staying Over the Bar:** Use halting deadlifts, segment lifts with pauses (floor/knee), or slow pull snatches to prevent drifting forward.
*   **Jerk Balance/Drive:** Address "bobbling elbows" by fixing the dip/drive mechanics first; ensure bar is not too far forward before lockout.
*   **Overhead Stability:** Hold receiving position for 3 seconds; perform overhead carries after sets; use snatch push press + OHS complexes to reinforce lockout positions.
*   **Squat Control:** Slow eccentric on the first rep; pause for 2–3 reps (early cycle) or all reps (Front Squat day) to build control and postural strength.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Snatch Focus:** Halting deadlifts, snatch pulls (progressing from floor/knee), pull-to-hip high hang snatches, slow pull snatches, snatch push press + OHS complexes.
*   **Clean/Jerk Focus:** Clean with pauses, clean + jerk dip squat (paused), power cleans for turnover, power jerks for vertical drive, jerk behind the neck (with holds).
*   **Strength/Accessory:** Back Squat (high rep/low intensity early cycle; pause/slow eccentrics), Front Squat (paused for mobility/control), Push Press/Push Jerk variations, Overhead Squats.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Squat Variations:** Use paused squats to improve mobility and postural strength without excessive fatigue.
*   **Session Flow:** Group related exercises to minimize transition times, ensuring the body remains adequately warm for subsequent movements (e.g., doing pulls immediately after snatches).

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text emphasizes "paused" squats and "slow eccentrics" early in cycles to strengthen positions and control without maxing out spinal compression, which is safer for a painful back than heavy, fast loading.
*   **Push Jerk Only (No Split):** The text explicitly recommends "jerk behind the neck" with holds and "overhead carries" as specific drills to reinforce overhead stability and lockout, directly applicable to refining a push jerk without split mechanics.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** While not explicitly mentioned in the text, the recommended 5-day structure allows for flexibility (dropping Saturday or consolidating days) if energy levels fluctuate due to shift work; the "lighter" Saturday session offers a low-stress option for recovery days.
*   **OHS Limiter:** The text suggests specific complexes like "snatch push press + OHS" and "pause in bottom of OHS for 3 seconds" to build strength and stability in the overhead position, directly targeting the identified weakness.
