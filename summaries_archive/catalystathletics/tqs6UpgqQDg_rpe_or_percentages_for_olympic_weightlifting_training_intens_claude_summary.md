# Weightlifting Coaching Analysis: Intensity Prescription Methods

## Programming Principles
- **Intensity prescription options**: Absolute (kg), relative (%), or subjective (RPE/RIR)
- **Percentage-based prescribing** is most objective and reliable; accounts for individual effort tendencies
- **Adjustments needed for**:
  - Newer/intermediate lifters: use higher percentages (neurological efficiency lower; technical limitations on competition lifts)
  - Pull variations: prescribe independently of competition lift %, based on assessed ability (technique-limited, not strength-limited)
- **RPE vs. RIR for Olympic lifting**: RPE (1–10 scale) preferred over RIR for low-rep work; RIR lacks precision at low reps
- **Risk with subjective methods**: Athletes misestimate effort based on training tendencies; coach must monitor and educate

## Relevant to This Athlete
1. **Intermediate status + low competition lifts** (Clean 80, Jerk 65, OHS 50):
   - Pull prescriptions should be % of actual pull capacity, NOT % of clean (technique-limited competition lift)
   - Likely benefits from **percentage-based prescribing** vs. RPE
   
2. **Night shift work** → fatigue management critical:
   - Monitor RPE/effort accuracy closely; may systematically underestimate effort when fatigued
   
3. **Chronic back pain** + hypertrophy block:
   - Percentages allow precise load management without relying on athlete judgment (safer for pain management)

4. **OHS limiter (50 kg)**:
   - Overhead work % should reflect OHS capacity, not competition jerk %, if technique-limited
