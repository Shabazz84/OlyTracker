# Weightlifting Analysis: Jerk Dip Technique

## 1. **Technique Cues – Jerk (Push Jerk)**
- **Primary principle**: Vertical dip only—knees bend, trunk angle stays constant (no hip hinge)
- **Common error**: Hip hinging forward → bar drifts forward, poor force transfer, early arm push, upper back collapse
- **Cue**: Watch trunk angle, not glute prominence (visual illusion from muscular development)
- **Spine position**: Maintain neutral lumbar curve; avoid pelvic tuck or hip shift forward
- **"Double bend" misconception**: Slight forward trunk inclination at setup is acceptable *if* dip remains vertical; hips don't move back during descent
- **Analogy**: Dip is a piston; staying over the bar = driving bar forward (same error as snatch/clean)

## 2. **Programming/Recovery Notes**
- Acknowledges athlete backgrounds create hip-extension bias (need to override ingrained patterns)

## 3. **Relevance to This Athlete**
- **CRITICAL for this athlete**: Push jerk only (no split jerk yet) + chronic back pain → vertical dip is essential to protect lumbar spine and reduce anterior shear
- Forward bar drive → increased back strain (problematic for night-shift fatigue)
