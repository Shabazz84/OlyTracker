# Laying Out The Training Week Schedule for Olympic Weightlifting

# Analysis for This Athlete

## **Programming Principles**

**Directly applicable to athlete's constraints:**
- **Night shift worker (Wed–Sun):** Text recommends starting with conventional structure then adjusting. For this athlete, consider flipping the schedule—train Mon–Tue as big days, then adjust Wed–Sun around shift work. Could use 4-day/week (Mon, Tue, Thu, Sat) as more manageable with erratic availability.
- **Frequency recommendation:** 4–5 days/week optimal; athlete's schedule may limit options. Text emphasizes: "start with conventional micro cycle, then make necessary adjustments."
- **Microcycle structure:** Alternate big/small days. Strategically place rest days before heavy sessions.
  - *Big days:* Snatch/clean + heavy squat
  - *Small days:* Jerk/overhead focus (less systemically fatiguing)

**Volume/intensity distribution:**
- Tuesday equivalent = lowest workload (between two big days)
- Thursday = rest or very light (recovery day before big session)
- Overhead/jerk emphasis on small days allows harder intensity with less fatigue recovery cost

## **Exercise Recommendations**

**Highly relevant to athlete's profile:**
- **OHS limiter (50 kg):** Prioritize overhead work on small days; use light technique-focused volume here without systemically taxing recovery
- **Back pain consideration:** Distribute squat/heavy comp lift work strategically; text emphasizes exercise selection distribution across week to avoid clustering fatiguing movements
- **Push jerk only (no split):** Likely benefits from jerk/overhead emphasis on lower-workload days to build volume without compounding lower-body fatigue from squats

---

## **Recovery/Mobility Advice**

- Text suggests 6-day week option: add light Thursday session focused on "technique-oriented training and accessory work that will create very little fatigue and even encourage recovery and mobility with quality movement rather than athlete being sedentary."
- **Applicable:** Low-volume mobility + jerk/overhead technique work on Thursday could manage back pain better than full rest day.

## **Schedule Recommendation for This Athlete**

Given night shift Wed–Sun: **Mon/Tue big, Thu light/tech, Sat big = 3–4 day week**
- Avoids mid-week shift fatigue
- Honors periodization principles
