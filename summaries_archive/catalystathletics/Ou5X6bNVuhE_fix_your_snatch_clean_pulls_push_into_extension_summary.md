# Fix Your Snatch & Clean Pulls - PUSH into Extension

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** 3–5 sets of 2–3 pulls (Power Position) progressing to 2–3 pulls from mid-thigh, then lower hang positions. Total pull volume: 3–5 sets per session.
*   **Intensity:** High focus on perfect mechanics; use light-to-moderate loads for drills, heavier for final pull variations once technique is solid.
*   **Frequency:** Perform these pull drills as a warm-up before any snatch or clean training sessions.
*   **Progression:** Start at Power Position → Master vertical extension → Progressively lower the hang position (mid-thigh → floor) only when the previous stage is consistent.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Trunk/Posture:** Maintain a "nice vertical trunk"; do not lean back or throw the head back.
*   **Bar Path:** Keep arms relaxed; let the bar slide lightly against the body (hips for snatch, upper thigh for clean). The bar must never move away from the body.
*   **Drive:** Push straight down into the floor through the whole foot with balanced weight distribution. Extend the whole body vertically.
*   **Hip Movement:** Do *not* push hips through the bar; let knee forward motion happen naturally as a result of proper leg drive and balance.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Drill:** Power Position Pulls (Pause at bottom, vertical extension).
*   **Progression Drills:** Mid-thigh Hang Pulls → Low Hang Pulls → Floor Pulls (only after mastering the hang positions).
*   **Structure:** Warm-up with Power Position pulls followed by the lowest achievable hang position for that day.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The cue "Don't lean back" and "vertical trunk" directly addresses common compensatory patterns that exacerbate lumbar stress.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The emphasis on not mimicking the extension but understanding the *mechanics* (pushing down vs. pulling up) is critical for an athlete likely relying on strength rather than technique to move weight.
*   **Jerk Limitation (Push Jerk only):** While this text focuses on Snatch/Clean pulls, the principle of "driving straight down" and "vertical extension" applies universally to generating force without leaning back, which is vital for a push jerk where hip drive mechanics differ from a split jerk.
*   **OHS Limiter:** Improving pull efficiency helps reduce the energy cost of the lift, potentially aiding performance in overhead movements by ensuring better kinetic chain transfer.
