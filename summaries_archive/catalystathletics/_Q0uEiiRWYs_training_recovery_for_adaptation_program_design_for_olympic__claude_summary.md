# Training & Recovery for Adaptation - Program Design for Olympic Weightlifting

# Analysis: Progressive Overload & Periodization Framework

## 1. Programming Principles
- **Progressive overload** is foundational—continually increase demand over time to force adaptation
- **Stimulus-Response-Adaptation curve**: Introduce stimulus → body fatigues → recovery → adaptation to new baseline
- **Training:Recovery ratio is critical**—three scenarios:
  - Optimal: Adaptation completes before next stimulus (progressive improvement)
  - Too high ratio: Fatigue accumulates faster than adaptation occurs (regression)
  - Too low ratio: Body returns to baseline before next stimulus; skill/confidence suffer with excessive gaps
- **Frequency matters**: Can't train once every 7–10 days despite high intensity/volume—skill maintenance, confidence, and technical development require consistent sessions
- **Periodization structure**: Microcycles → Mesocycles → Macrocycles (progressive baseline elevation → specific competition focus → peak performance)

## 2. Relevance to This Athlete
**Directly applicable:**
- **Night shift (Wed–Sun)**: Recovery optimization is critical; manage training:recovery ratio carefully during fatigue-heavy work schedule
- **6-week hypertrophy block**: Microcycle dosing (intensity/reps/volume/frequency) must balance muscle development with maintaining snatch/clean/jerk skill
- **Chronic back pain**: Avoid excessive volume in single sessions; frequent, moderate-intensity sessions may be better tolerated than heavy, infrequent ones
- **OHS limiter (50 kg)**: Consistent technical work needed—longer gaps between OHS/snatch work will degrade skill gains
