## 1. PROGRAMMING PRINCIPLES

**Stimulus-Adaptation-Recovery Model:**
- Progressive overload via continual demand increases
- Body overcompensates if recovery is adequate
- Three failure scenarios: (a) training:recovery ratio too high → regression; (b) ratio too low → deconditioning; (c) optimal timing → consistent adaptation

**Microcycle Design (Weekly Structure):**
- **Frequency:** 5-day week is most common/effective for intermediate athletes
- Standard 5-day: Mon/Tue/Wed/Fri/Sat or Mon/Tue/Wed/Thu/Sat
- **Distribution:** Alternate big/small training days to allow consecutive training + recovery for heavy days
- **Rest placement:** Thursday typically off (recover from 3 consecutive days), Sunday off (weekly recovery)
- Typical structure: Mon/Wed = moderate-high workload; Tue = lowest (between two big days); Fri = heaviest; Sat = moderate-low

**For this athlete (night shift Wed–Sun):**
- Cannot follow conventional Mon/Tue/Wed/Fri/Sat schedule
- Recommend: Start with realistic schedule, adjust from there (coach's explicit advice)
- May need 4-day week (Mon/Tue/Thu/Sat) or creative distribution around work days

**Volume/Intensity Considerations:**
- Fewer training days → less total volume and exercise variety possible
- 5-day week allows multiple snatch/clean variations; 3-day week requires strategic exercise selection
- Overhead/jerk work is less systemically fatiguing than snatch/clean/squat complex
- Small days can have MORE reps/exercises if volume is light, technique-focused

**Key for hypertrophy block:** Frequency matters for skill retention alongside volume/intensity.

## 3. EXERCISE RECOMMENDATIONS

**Strategic Principles:**
- Distribute each lift (snatch, clean, jerk, squat) across the week rather than clustering
- Consider systemic fatigue: heavy snatch/clean/squat days vs. lighter overhead days
- Light technique work on small days supports skill development without excessive fatigue

**Not specific to this athlete's deficits** (OHS/snatch mobility, push jerk-only limitation) in this excerpt.

## 4. RECOVERY & MOBILITY ADVICE

**Key Points:**
- Skill and confidence suffer with excessively long breaks between sessions (argues against massive single sessions)
- 6-day week option: Add light Thursday session for "technique-oriented training and accessory work...encourage recovery and mobility with quality movement"
- Multiple daily sessions: Only justified for advanced full-time athletes; can improve focus/energy if volume is excessive for single session

**Relevant to night-shift athlete:** Schedule flexibility matters more than ideal structure; recovery timing critical given fatigue from shift work (not explicitly addressed).
