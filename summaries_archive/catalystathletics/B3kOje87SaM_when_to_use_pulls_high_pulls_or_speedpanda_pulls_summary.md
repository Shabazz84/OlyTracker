# When to Use Pulls, High-Pulls or Speed/Panda Pulls

1. **Programming principles**: None specified in this text; it focuses solely on equipment selection for pulling variations rather than sets, reps, intensity, or periodization structures.
2. **Technique cues**: Use conventional poles to correct early arm pulling and train proper extension; use high poles if arms are fine but the athlete struggles with cutting the drive short or swinging the bar away; use speed/panda poles only if extension is dialed in but explosive rhythm at the top is lacking.
3. **Exercise recommendations**: High pulls and speed poles (specifically for pulling work), contingent on the athlete's ability to execute them correctly first.
4. **Recovery or mobility advice**: None mentioned; the text warns against using high/speed poles if fundamentals are not solid, as they may create more problems than they fix.
5. **Athlete profile relevance**: 
   - **Back Pain/Intermediate Status**: The text's strict warning ("unless you can truly do them well... likely to create more problems") is highly relevant for an athlete with chronic back pain; improper high/speed pulls could exacerbate injury risks if the athlete lacks the technical maturity to manage the load.
   - **Push Jerk Only**: While not explicitly addressed, the focus on "explosive rhythm at the top" applies directly to the jerk phase of a push jerk, where timing is critical since there is no split to absorb momentum.
   - **OHS Limiter**: The advice to "sort out fundamentals first" and avoid complex pulling variations until extension is perfect supports addressing the 50kg OHS limitation through foundational strength rather than premature speed work.
