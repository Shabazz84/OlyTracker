# Live Q&A with Greg Everett

## Key Points
*   **Recovery & Fatigue Management:** Prioritize sleep (critical for night shift workers), nutrition, and stress management over high-volume training. Use "stimulate, not annihilate" principles due to chronic back pain; avoid high-volume destruction that aggravates the spine. Implement regular back-off weeks every 3–4 weeks.
*   **Programming Structure:** Start conservatively (3 days/week) with segmented learning (teaching receiving positions first). Avoid excessive volume (~250 reps/week max vs. 600); heavy singles should be performed weekly but never to failure unless on a prepared max day. Use wave loading (e.g., 95% → drop → 98% → drop → 101%) to manage mental blocks and technical limits without triggering breakdowns.
*   **Hypertrophy & Strength Focus:** The upcoming 6-week block should prioritize unilateral leg strength (single-leg squats, lunges, step-ups) and hip stability over generic bodybuilding routines. Jump squats are superior to push presses for developing second-pull explosion.
*   **Overhead Limitation:** Address the 50kg OHS limiter by avoiding passive stretching or boxes/balls; instead, use active tension at the bottom of the squat (hold for 3 seconds after settling) and focus on scapular retraction/upward rotation to create a stable base.

## Technique Cues
*   **Jerk Mechanics (Push Jerk Specific):** Since no split jerk is used, explosive vertical drive is non-negotiable. Drive the bar high enough to prevent forward lean; ensure continuous leg drive from start to finish of the pull to eliminate slack. Maintain active tension and back extension at the bottom of the receiving position without resting on a box/ball.
*   **Scapular Stability:** Focus on retraction and upward rotation of scapulae (squeezing upper inside edges together) rather than internal/external arm rotation. Lock shoulders to the rib cage to create a stable base for the overhead position, reducing lumbar shear forces.
*   **Pull Mechanics:** Maintain full circumferential pressure (abs, back, diaphragm) before and during the pull; do not flare ribs mid-lift. Aim for "continuous contact" in the second pull—legs still driving at the moment of bar contact—to ensure a fluid transition into the arm pull without horizontal momentum ("banging").
*   **Snatch Depth:** If receiving high enough for a power snatch, force yourself to squat it. Sit in the bottom for 3 seconds (counting only after settling) to build strength/stability.
*   **Knee Valgus & Stability:** Maintain continuous tension against the ground during transitions; use unilateral hip stability work to prevent knee valgus and ensure eccentric control.

## Programming Principles
*   **Volume & Intensity:** Avoid "heavy singles" failure unless prepared. Aim for the heaviest lift doable reasonably (1–2 reps in reserve if fatigued). Misses should be rare indicators of pushing limits, not a training norm.
*   **Individualization:** No universal squat program exists; individualize based on response to intensity/volume/frequency combinations. For hypertrophy, focus on leg strength and unilateral hip stability rather than generic routines.
*   **Progression Strategy:** Use big jumps in weight progression during warm-ups (avoid tiny increments) to build speed and confidence at light weights (e.g., 50kg). Minimize high-volume doubles before max attempts.
*   **Recovery Protocol:** After missed training or life events, restart with a modified "week zero" (10% volume reduction) rather than ramping up immediately. For night shift workers, ensure high-quality sleep windows are non-negotiable to avoid fatigue accumulation.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain & Scoliosis:** The "stimulate, not annihilate" approach and emphasis on core tension (locking spine/ribs) directly address chronic pain risks. Manual therapy check-ins should be utilized to monitor imbalances. Avoiding boxes/balls during squats maintains necessary core tension to protect the spine.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** The lack of a split jerk requires perfect vertical drive and scapular stability to compensate for the smaller margin for error. Explosive second-pull mechanics are critical here, as there is no leg recovery time to adjust balance.
*   **Night Shift Schedule (Wed–Sun):** The conservative frequency start (3 days/week) and wave loading strategies support recovery management for shift workers, preventing fatigue from overwhelming the system during non-standard hours.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** The specific cue to "sit in the bottom" with active tension and avoid passive stretching addresses the primary strength bottleneck without relying on mobility aids that reduce stabilization needs.
*   **Transitioning Athlete:** Segmented learning (teaching receiving positions first) is highly recommended for intermediate athletes transitioning to Olympic lifting to prevent ingraining bad habits early in their technique development.
