# Training Volume & Prilepin's Table - How to Write Olympic Weightlifting Training Programs | Part 5

# Analysis: Volume Programming for Weightlifting

## Relevance to Text
This is a **detailed coach education piece** on calculating and distributing training volume across mesocycles. Highly relevant to program design.

---

## 1. **Programming Principles**

- **Volume counting convention:** Start at 70% intensity (60% acceptable but less precise); be consistent within each athlete
- **Complexes:** Count each exercise separately, not as one rep (e.g., snatch pull + snatch + OHS = 3 reps, not 1)
- **Clean & jerk singles:** Count as 2 reps (1 clean + 1 jerk)
- **Accessory work:** Track separately or use sets/time instead of reps; prioritize timing to avoid local fatigue interference
- **Mesocycle distribution (prep phase):** Typical 4-week pattern = 30%, 27%, 23%, 20% (volume decreases; intensity increases weeks 1–3, deload week 4)
- **Competition prep mesos:** 25–35% lower total volume; steeper weekly drops (e.g., 35%, 28%, 22%, 15%)
- **Prilepin's Table:** Use as verification tool, not prescription; broader applicability to strength lifts than competition lifts
- **Start low, work up:** Prevents lingering fatigue masking true volume tolerance

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- RDLs, good mornings counted as volume despite <60% intensity (impactful despite lighter load)
- Technique work: Include in volume calculations despite lower intensity if comprising meaningful reps (>45 reps/session)
- **Accessory timing:** Place ab/trunk work same day as competition lifts it affects most (not the day before heavy clean & jerk or squat)
- Avoid "beach work" (tricep/shoulder isolation) day before heavy jerk sessions

---

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- Prioritize **accessory timing** to maximize recovery between relevant sessions
- Deload structure: 50% volume reduction in final week recommended during early prep phases

---

## 5. **Relevance to This Athlete**

**CRITICAL CONSIDERATIONS:**
- **Chronic back pain + night shift (Wed–Sun fatigue):** Volume should start conservatively; accessory work (ab, trunk work) timing crucial—place on same day as squat/C&J sessions to avoid compounding back stress
- **OHS limiter (50 kg):** Technique work volume won't meet 70% threshold; include separately to track mobility/stability work
- **Push jerk only:** Clean & jerk singles still count as 2 reps; address split jerk learning curve impact on volume distribution
- **6-week hypertrophy block:** Use higher prep-phase volume (e.g., 250–300 reps/week for intermediate), then reduce 25–35% when transitioning to strength/technique phase
- **Split jerk introduction risk:** Expect initial technical volume (low intensity, high reps) during weeks 4–6; don't dilute prep phase analysis by mixing in new movement patterns mid-block
