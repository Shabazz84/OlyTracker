# Quit Trying to Catch All Snatches in the Bottom

# Analysis: Snatch Receiving Position & Pull-Under Technique

## Relevant to This Athlete:
**Directly applicable** — OHS 50kg is listed primary snatch limiter; receiving position depth/consistency is critical.

## Key Points:

**Technique Cues (Snatch):**
- Maintain **consistent pulling motion** across all weights; don't change mechanics to force depth
- With lighter loads: complete full extension ("pole"), then pull under with normal maximal speed
- Sit fluidly into bottom squat rather than cutting the pull short and dropping prematurely
- Avoid incomplete extension on light weights → habit that prevents finishing pull on heavy weights

**Programming Principle:**
- **Graduate force with load progression** — don't reduce bar height by reducing effort; match force to weight
- Prevents compensatory habits (incomplete extension, early squat commitment) that plateau heavy lifts

**Why This Matters for This Athlete:**
- With weak OHS (50kg), receiving position consistency is essential before loading
- Chronic back pain + weak squat receive = high injury risk if rushing into depth without locked-out position
- Night shift fatigue may increase form breakdown; this cue prioritizes movement quality over volume

## Bottom Line:
Focus snatch work on **consistent pull mechanics + deliberate bottom-position control** at lighter weights before attempting heavy singles.
