# Weightlifting Analysis: Program Design — Intensity & Reps

## 1. Programming Principles

**Key Framework:**
- **Intensity-rep interdependence**: Higher intensity = fewer reps; higher reps = lower intensity possible
- **Maximally effective dose** (not minimal): Push for greatest adaptation within athlete's recovery capacity, not just "enough"
- **Purpose-driven rep ranges:**
  - Strength: 1–5 reps, 80–100% (bias 1–3)
  - Power/Speed: 1–3 reps, 60–75%
  - Technique: 1–3 reps, 50–70%
  - Hypertrophy: 5+ reps, 60–80% (high proximity to failure)
  - Recovery: 2–3 reps, <60%

**Technical lifts (snatch/clean/jerk)** use lower end of strength range (1–2 reps vs. 3–5) at high intensity to preserve technique under fatigue

**Intensity prescription methods:**
- Absolute weight (best if you have training history)
- Percentages (most common; objective; need adjustment for neurological efficiency)
- RPE/RIR (subjective; best for Olympic lifts due to low reps; requires athlete accuracy)
- Bold rep/intensity ranges (allows athlete flexibility on final set; useful for gauging individual capacity)

## 3. Exercise Recommendations

- **Accessory work**: Use rep ranges rather than fixed reps; can use simpler intensity descriptors (light, easy, moderate)
- **Pull/deadlift variations**: Prescribe somewhat independently of snatch/clean percentages if athlete's lifts are limited by technique, not strength

---

## 4. Recovery & Mobility

- Recovery work uses 2–3 reps, <60%, minimal fatigue—lifts "to keep motor skills and mobility fresh while allowing the body to recover"
- Use deload weeks strategically; coach can allow bold sets before deload to assess true capacity

---

## 5. Relevant to This Athlete

**Critical application areas:**

1. **OHS limiter (50 kg)** & **chronic back pain**: Technique work (1–3 reps, 50–70%) with strict form emphasis is essential; avoid high-rep hypertrophy on technical lifts; prioritize motor-pattern consistency over volume
2. **Night shift (Wed–Sun)**: Variable recovery window—use percentage prescriptions (objective) rather than RPE/RIR, which depend on athlete's subjective state and may be unreliable given fatigue
3. **6-week hypertrophy block**: Use 5+ reps, 60–80%, higher proximity to failure on accessory/squat work; keep snatch/clean/jerk at lower rep ranges (1–3) to protect technique despite fatigue
4. **Push jerk only** (no split): May fatigue quicker at higher intensities; consider rep caps and monitoring to avoid form breakdown
5. **Intermediate level transitioning to Olympic lifting**: Likely needs percentage-based prescriptions (not RPE) and higher percentages on pulls due to technique-limited competition lifts; track data for future adjustments
