# https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/hip-snatch-exercise-how-to-benefits-and-variations

### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Intensity:** Recommended range of **60–75%** of Snatch 1RM for technique/speed work; advanced lifters may occasionally exceed this due to the shortened pull distance.
*   **Sets/Reps (Intermediate):** **4–5 sets of 3 reps**.
*   **Frequency:** Can be used as a primary technical tool or accessory within a cycle; suitable for daily practice of mechanics if volume is managed.
*   **Periodization:** Ideal for the "sharpening" phase to refine timing and bar speed before heavy attempts, or during hypertrophy blocks to maintain technique without high systemic fatigue from floor pulls.

### 2. Technique Cues (Snatch Focus)
*   **Bar Path & Contact:** Focus on **precise hip contact** at the crease; avoid early arm bending ("arms guide, do not pull").
*   **Turnover:** Execute an aggressive "pull and drop myself under" motion rather than lifting higher.
*   **Overhead Stability:** Lock elbows tight, engage traps, and actively press up into the bar (reach shoulders to ears).
*   **Lat Engagement:** Keep lats tight throughout the pull to prevent the bar from drifting away from the body.

### 3. Exercise Recommendations
*   **Primary Drill:** **Hip Snatch** (from power position/hip crease) as the core movement for this profile.
*   **Variations:**
    *   **Hip Power Snatch:** For athletes needing to commit to the catch without deep squat mobility demands.
    *   **Hip Muscle Squat Snatch:** To teach aggressive "punching under" while maintaining overhead tension.
    *   **Hip Snatch Pull (High Pull):** To reinforce vertical extension and bar proximity without the overhead requirement.

### 4. Recovery or Mobility Advice
*   **Mobility Requirement:** The text highlights this exercise as having **lower mobility requirements** than full snatch pulls, making it ideal for athletes with limited pulling range of motion.
*   **Grip Management:** For multiple reps, using **lifting straps** is explicitly permitted to support grip fatigue without compromising technique.

### 5. Relevance to Athlete Profile
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** Highly relevant. By removing the first pull from the floor (a high-stress position for the lower back), the Hip Snatch isolates explosive extension while reducing spinal loading and shear forces associated with deadlifting heavy weights from the ground.
*   **OHS Limiter (50 kg):** The exercise specifically targets "fast turnover" and "vertical bar path," which are critical for improving overhead stability and efficiency, potentially aiding in overcoming the snatch overhead deficit.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** Provides a low-skill entry point to learn proper contact and timing without the complexity of floor mechanics, bridging the gap between strength training and Olympic technique.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The reduced systemic fatigue compared to full snatches makes it suitable for athletes with irregular sleep schedules (Wed–Sun shift), allowing technical work on days when recovery might be compromised by shift work.
