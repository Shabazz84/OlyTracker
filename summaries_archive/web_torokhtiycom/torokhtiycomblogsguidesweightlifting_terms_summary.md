## Key Points
*   **Periodization Strategy:** Adopt a **Block Periodization** model (sequential focus on hypertrophy, then technique/strength) rather than undulating models. This aligns with the 6-week hypertrophy block followed by technical refinement.
*   **Hypertrophy Mechanics:** Muscle growth requires moderate-to-heavy loads with higher repetitions and controlled **Time Under Tension (TUT)**.
*   **Progressive Overload:** The primary driver for adaptation; achieved by systematically increasing weight, volume, or reducing rest intervals over time.
*   **Recovery Protocols:** Essential for managing the risks of night shift work (irregular sleep). Implement scheduled **Deload Weeks** to prevent overtraining and utilize active recovery (light cycling/yoga) to flush metabolic waste without exacerbating fatigue.

## Technique Cues
*   **Pull Mechanics:** Maintain straight arms until the "power position" to maximize leg/hip transfer; ensure a straight or slightly curved bar path.
*   **Overhead Lockout (Critical):** Strictly avoid **"Press Out"** faults (using arms to push rather than driving with legs). For a Push Jerk-only athlete, full elbow lockout and rigid torso fixation are mandatory for a valid lift.
*   **Squat Depth:** Perform "Ass to Grass" squats to maximize muscle engagement, contingent on mobility availability.
*   **Start Position:** Maintain a flat or slightly arched back with chest up; align the center of mass over the midfoot with hips higher than knees but lower than shoulders.
*   **Bracing:** Utilize diaphragmatic breathing and core bracing (abs/obliques) to generate intra-abdominal pressure, protecting the spine during heavy loads.

## Programming Principles
*   **Volume Management:** Calculate volume as Sets × Reps × Weight; balance this against recovery capacity to avoid overtraining, particularly given the night shift schedule.
*   **Accessory Integration:** Use isolation movements and static (isometric) training to address muscle imbalances and build strength in specific positions without excessive spinal loading.
*   **Equipment Utilization:** Wear weightlifting shoes to elevate heels, improve ankle mobility for deeper squats, and ensure a firm base for power transfer.
*   **Training Density:** Balance frequency with recovery; utilize methods like EMOM or circuit training cautiously, prioritizing fatigue management over work capacity due to chronic back pain.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The emphasis on proper bracing, diaphragmatic breathing, and a neutral spine start position is critical for injury prevention. Avoid "cheating" (using momentum) which exacerbates spinal stress.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** Since the athlete lacks a split jerk, the definition of **"Press Out"** serves as a primary technical warning; failure to lock elbows in one fluid motion results in a no-lift.
*   **Night Shift Schedule:** The risk of overtraining (insomnia, irritability) is heightened by irregular sleep cycles. Sleep hygiene and deload weeks are non-negotiable for recovery.
*   **Hypertrophy Block:** The 6-week focus on muscle growth via higher reps and controlled TUT directly supports the transition from intermediate strength to Olympic lifting, addressing the OHS (Overhead Squat) limitation through increased muscular capacity.
