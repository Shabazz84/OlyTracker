**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency:** The text suggests a "Clean Cycle" program of 5 days/week, but explicitly advises allowing muscles "a day or two every week to relax and recover." For this athlete (night shift + chronic pain), a 3–4 day frequency is safer than the generic 5-day recommendation.
*   **Sets/Reps:** No specific rep ranges are provided in the text; it emphasizes "high pulls" for power and varying reps/sets to prevent boredom.
*   **Intensity:** Warns against overestimating capabilities or lifting weights that compromise form ("not so much that it messes up your form").
*   **Periodization:** Recommends rotating exercises (alternatives) to break monotony, reduce repetitive strain, and allow specific muscle groups to rest while others remain active.

**2. Technique cues for snatch, clean, or jerk**
*   **Clean/High Pull:** "Keep elbows high and out," ensure the bar stays close to the body, and avoid pausing between hip extension and pull ("without pause").
*   **Hang Snatch:** Focus on adding momentum via explosive hip/knee extension; catch in a deep squat with arms fully extended.
*   **Kettlebell/Dumbbell Clean:** Keep elbows close to the body; let the weight rest on the elbow (kettlebell) or catch in rack position.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Primary Alternatives:** Hang Snatch, Power Clean, Clean High Pull, Kettlebell Swing, Single Arm Dumbbell Clean, Kettlebell Clean.
*   **Specific Selection for Athlete:** The text explicitly recommends **Kettlebell Clean** and **Single Arm Dumbbell Clean** as "easier on your wrists and shoulders" or addressing imbalances, which suits an athlete with chronic back pain transitioning from powerlifting.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Rest Days:** Explicitly states muscles need 1–2 days of rest per week; advises to "chill and do nothing."
*   **Injury Prevention:** Emphasizes learning proper form before increasing load to prevent overuse injuries caused by repetitive strain ("mileage is quite often responsible for injuries").

**5. Specifically relevant to this athlete's profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain & Joint Issues:** The text highlights that Kettlebell and Single Arm Dumbbell cleans are "easier on your wrists and shoulders" and good for those with joint issues, offering a lower spinal loading alternative to barbell cleans.
*   **Transitioning Athlete:** The advice to use "High Pulls and Muscle Cleans" to challenge coordination without the full turnover/catch phase is ideal for an intermediate lifter (102.5 kg) focusing on technique before heavy overhead work, specifically addressing their OHS limitation by building pulling power safely.
