# https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/critical-weightlifting-mistakes

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Avoid "quantity over quality." Do not increase sets/reps at the expense of form.
*   **Progression:** Use gradual progressive overload (increase weight, reps, or sets slowly) rather than sudden jumps in intensity.
*   **Periodization:** Incorporate exercise variation and change rep ranges/intensity periodically to avoid plateaus; do not repeat the same exercises week after week.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Form Priority:** Master proper technique and movement patterns *before* adding load.
*   **Range of Motion:** Ensure full range of motion is maintained; lack thereof leads to inefficient workouts.
*   **Stabilization:** Avoid relying solely on machines; engage stabilizing muscles through free weights and functional movements.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Free Weights vs. Machines:** Prioritize free weights and bodyweight training over machines to build overall strength, stability, and prevent muscle imbalance.
*   **Cardio Integration:** Include 5–10 minutes of cardio before lifting to warm up muscles and increase blood flow.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Warm-up/Cool-down:** Never skip warming up (to activate muscles/blood flow) or cooling down/stretching (to prevent tightness/DOMS).
*   **Mobility Work:** Regularly incorporate mobility exercises, stretching, and foam rolling to maintain joint health and flexibility.
*   **Rest Days:** Ensure rest days between training sessions; listen to the body to avoid overtraining and fatigue.
*   **Sleep/Nutrition:** Prioritize sleep regime and protein intake (1–1.5g per lb of body weight) for muscle repair and recovery.

**5. Athlete-specific relevance**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly warns that "incomplete range of motion" and "lack of mobility" lead to injuries; this is critical for an athlete with chronic back pain who must prioritize form over load.
*   **Intermediate Plateau:** As an intermediate lifter, the athlete risks "overweighting" (lifting too heavy too quickly) or creating muscle imbalances by focusing only on favorite lifts; diversification and accessory work are required.
*   **Night Shift Work:** The text emphasizes that "nutrition plays a significant role in performance and recovery," which is vital for a night shift worker to manage energy levels and recovery timing effectively.
*   **Ego Lifting:** The athlete must avoid lifting heavy weights to impress others ("ego lifting"), as this worsens form and increases injury risk, especially given the existing back pain.
