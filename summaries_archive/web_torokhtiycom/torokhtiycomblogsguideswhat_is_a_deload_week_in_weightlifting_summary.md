# https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/what-is-a-deload-week-in-weightlifting

**1. Programming Principles (Deload Specifics)**
*   **Definition:** A planned week of global load reduction (40–60% intensity, 50% volume) to prevent overtraining and facilitate adaptation.
*   **Frequency:** Recommended every 4–8 weeks of intense training, or immediately upon signs of plateau, performance decline, or sleep disruption.
*   **Structure:** Reduce working weights to 40–50% of 1RM; drop sets/reps (e.g., from 5x5 to 2-3x3-5); extend rest intervals to 2–3 minutes.
*   **Goal:** Rebalance cortisol/testosterone, replenish glycogen, and resolve micro-tears without complete detraining.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   *None provided in text.* The article focuses on recovery protocols rather than biomechanical cues for lifts.

**3. Exercise Recommendations (Deload Context)**
*   **Variations:** Swap primary lifts for variations to reduce spinal load (e.g., Back Squat $\to$ Front Squat or Leg Press).
*   **Accessory Work:** DB Bench Press, Bent Over Rows, Pull-Ups (assisted), Light Cardio (jogging/rowing <65% HR max).
*   **Mobility:** Mandatory 30-minute total body mobility/stretching session.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Active Recovery:** Include light cardio and stretching to improve circulation; do not take a complete "week off."
*   **Sleep/Nutrition:** Prioritize sleep quality and appetite restoration as markers of recovery status.
*   **Post-Deload Protocol:** Do not jump back to max intensity immediately; progressively increase load over the following 1–2 weeks.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Night Shift, Back Pain)**
*   **Night Shift Alignment:** The text explicitly flags "Sleep Problems" and "Increased Heart Rate" as signs of overtraining for night shift workers, making deloads critical for circadian rhythm management.
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** Suggests swapping heavy back squats for front squats or leg presses during the deload to reduce spinal compression while maintaining stimulus.
*   **Psychological Burnout:** Highlights mental recovery as a primary benefit, crucial for an athlete transitioning to Olympic lifting who may face high cognitive load from night shifts and technical learning curves.
*   **Hormonal Health:** Notes that intense training disrupts hormones (cortisol/testosterone); a deload is necessary to restore the hormonal environment required for hypertrophy and strength gains in this specific transition phase.
