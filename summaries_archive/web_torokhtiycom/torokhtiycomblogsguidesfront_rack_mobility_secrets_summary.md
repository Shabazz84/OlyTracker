# https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/front-rack-mobility-secrets

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** Perform mobility drills daily or pre-workout; specific protocols recommended 2-3x/week for targeted improvement.
*   **Sets/Reps:** Static holds (elbow/wrist stretches) for 20 seconds, repeated 2–4 times; dynamic drills (PVC rotations) performed at slow tempo with high repetition for motor learning.
*   **Intensity:** Low intensity; focus on range of motion and joint health rather than load. Strapped front squats should be light (warm-up only).

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Bar Placement:** Bar must rest on the shoulders, never the collarbones; fingers must cover the bar in the palms.
*   **Elbow Position:** Elbows must point up and out; they must not touch the knees during a squat.
*   **Shoulder Rotation:** Shoulders must be externally rotated to prevent wrist overload.
*   **Breathing:** The bar position should not interrupt breathing mechanics.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Warm-up/Activation:** Resistance Band Elbow Stretch, PVC Elbows Rotation + Tall Muscle Clean, Shoulder External Rotation Stretch with PVC.
*   **Corrective Work:** Front Squats with Lifting Straps (light weight only), Elbow Banded Distraction (floor-based), Lat & Triceps Stretch (band-assisted).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Routine:** Pre-workout stretching/activation is mandatory; post-workout routines are equally critical for tendon flexibility.
*   **Testing:** Perform specific mobility tests before starting to establish baselines and track progress weekly.
*   **Systemic View:** Address the "system" (thoracic spine, lats, elbows, scapula) rather than isolated joints to prevent chronic inflammation.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain & OHS Limiter:** The text explicitly links poor front rack mobility to joint overload and injury risk; improving shoulder/elbow flexibility is critical for the athlete's 50kg OHS limitation and back safety during cleans/front squats.
*   **Transitioning Athlete:** The "PVC Tall Muscle Clean" is highlighted as a must-have drill for adjusting correct positions before loading with a barbell, ideal for an intermediate transitioning to Olympic lifts.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** While the text does not address sleep schedules directly, it emphasizes that consistent daily work yields significant improvement in weeks, which fits a structured 6-week block despite irregular hours.
*   **No Split Jerk:** The focus on front rack stability is vital for the athlete's push jerk technique to ensure bar control and safety without the dynamic movement of a split.
