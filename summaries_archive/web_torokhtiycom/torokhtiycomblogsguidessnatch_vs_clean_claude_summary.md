# Merged Summary: Snatch vs Clean & Jerk for Intermediate Olympic Weightlifter

## Key Points

- **Separate snatch and clean sessions** to prevent technical interference (bar path, hip contact, timing differences: snatch pull 0.45s vs clean pull 0.52s)
- **Snatch is mobility-dependent; clean allows greater strength expression** — particularly relevant given athlete's 50 kg OHS limitation
- **Progression before full lifts**: master positions (front rack → front squat → power clean → full clean) and build power variations before full movements
- **Combined sessions acceptable only during competition prep**

## Technique Cues

**Snatch:**
- First pull: "Push floor away" (slow, controlled, relaxed arms)
- Keep bar close to body; lats engaged
- Full hip/knee/ankle extension *before* moving under
- Turnover: elbows up and out; move *under* bar
- Receive: reach up into catch with active tension
- Common errors: early arm bend, bar drift, weak extension, slow turnover, incomplete overhead mobility

**Clean:**
- First pull: chest up, mid-foot balance, knees/hips rise together
- Second pull: explosive hip/knee/ankle extension ("almost like a jump")
- Fast, aggressive elbow rotation into front rack
- Common errors: lifting with back (hips too high), early arm bend, bar away from body, catching on wrists

## Programming Principles

- **Progression pathway for snatch**: Power Snatch (builds speed/confidence) → Full Snatch
- **Progression pathway for clean**: Tall/Muscle Clean → Hang Clean (above/below knee) → Power Clean → Full Clean
- **OHS-specific work first**: Overhead Squat, Snatch Balance, and mobility drills before heavy snatch attempts
- **Accessory hierarchy**: (1) Overhead strength (OHS, Snatch Balance), (2) Turnover (Tall Snatch, Drop Snatch, Muscle Snatch), (3) Contact/power (Hip Snatch, High Pulls, Snatch Deadlifift), (4) Full movement
- **Thoracic/shoulder/hip/ankle mobility essential** for snatch; **front-rack mobility** (wrists, shoulders, upper back) critical for clean

## Athlete Relevance

| Factor | Application |
|--------|-------------|
| **OHS 50 kg (primary snatch limiter)** | Prioritize Snatch Balance and OHS progressions before progressing to heavy snatches; don't rush full snatch work |
| **Chronic back pain** | Emphasize proper positioning (chest up, mid-foot balance, controlled first pull) to avoid compensation patterns; clean progression may be safer initially than snatch overhead demand |
| **No split jerk / Push jerk only** | Power Jerk better suited than split jerk (lower ankle/mobility demand) |
| **Intermediate → Olympic transition** | Use Power Snatch and hang variations to build technical foundation; separate snatch/clean sessions prevent crossover errors |
| **6-week hypertrophy block ahead** | Prioritize foundational positions and mobility work during this phase; defer heavy full snatch until strength/technique block begins |
| **Night shift (Wed–Sun)** | Not addressed in source; recovery strategy needed separately |
