# https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/4-week-olympic-weightlifting-program

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Structure:** 3-day weekly split (Snatch Focus, Accessory/Conditioning, Clean & Jerk Emphasis).
*   **Intensity/Load:** No specific % targets; prescribe "comfortable but challenging" weights where form is maintained.
*   **Progression:** Target 2–3% load increase weekly provided technique remains impeccable.
*   **Volume vs. Intensity:** Inverse relationship advised (reduce volume as intensity increases).
*   **Periodization:** Explicitly recommends transitioning to a periodized approach after the foundational phase.

**2. Technique Cues & Focus**
*   **Snatch:** Emphasis on "Initiating movement pattern," "Power position," and "Bar receiving & stability overhead."
*   **Clean & Jerk:** Focus on "Marrying power and precision" via explosive hip drive (Clean Pull) and shoulder/upper body power (Sitting Press).
*   **General:** Prioritize "form over load"; record sessions from multiple angles for feedback.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Lifts:** Snatch, Clean & Jerk, Snatch Balance + Overhead Squat, Front Squat (with straps), Back Squat.
*   **Pulling/Power:** Snatch Pull, Clean Pull, Box Jumps.
*   **Accessory/Core:** Supermans, Reverse Planks, Hyperextensions, Good Mornings, Thrusters, Split Squats, Incline Bench Press, Iron Butterfly, Sitting Press.

**4. Recovery & Mobility Advice**
*   **Warm-up:** 10 minutes of dynamic stretches (shoulders/hips for Snatch; wrists/hips/shoulders for C&J).
*   **Recovery:** Explicitly states "More training demands more recovery"; emphasizes rest days, stretching, and mobility work as intensity ramps up.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Chronic Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **⚠️ Critical Mismatch:** The text is explicitly designed for **beginners** ("foundational stone," "no specific load target"). It lacks the periodization and intensity management required for an intermediate athlete with a 102.5 kg bodyweight and chronic back pain.
*   **Back Pain Risk:** High-volume posterior chain work (Supermans, Good Mornings) is prescribed without modification for chronic pain; this could exacerbate symptoms in a night-shift worker already prone to fatigue-related form breakdown.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The text mentions sleep concerns in references but offers no specific scheduling advice for shift workers (e.g., managing training around Wed–Sun shifts).
*   **Jerk Technique:** The program includes "Clean & Jerk" generally but does not address the athlete's specific constraint of using a **Push Jerk only** or the lack of Split Jerk technique.
*   **Overhead Limitation:** No specific cues for OHS stability (the athlete's primary limiter) beyond general "stability overhead."

**Conclusion:** This text is a generic beginner guide. It is **not suitable** as-is for this intermediate, pain-prone athlete without significant modification to load management, exercise selection for back safety, and shift-work recovery strategies.
