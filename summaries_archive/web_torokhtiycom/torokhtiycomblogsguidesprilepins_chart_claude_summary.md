# Weightlifting Analysis: Prilepin's Chart Guide

## Relevant Content Identified

**1. Programming Principles (Sets/Reps/Intensity/Frequency)**

| Intensity | Reps/Set | Optimal Total | Range |
|-----------|----------|---------------|-------|
| 55–65% | 3–6 | 24 | 18–30 |
| 70–80% | 3–6 | 18 | 12–24 |
| 80–90% | 2–4 | 15 | 10–20 |
| 90%+ | 1–2 | 7 | 4–10 |

- Balances volume & intensity to avoid overtraining.
- Structured periodization prevents plateaus; rotate training phases regularly.

---

## **Specific to This Athlete**

**⚠️ Critical Limitations for Your Profile:**

1. **Back Pain + High Volume Risk**  
   - Prilepin's chart emphasizes moderate-to-high volume (18–30 reps/session). With chronic back pain, monitor cumulative load carefully—avoid compounding volume without deload phases.

2. **Night Shift Worker (Wed–Sun)**  
   - Recovery capacity is reduced. Consider:
     - Lower end of recommended rep ranges (e.g., 12–18 reps vs. 18–30).
     - Prioritize sleep quality over absolute volume.

3. **OHS Limiter (50 kg)**  
   - Your primary snatch constraint is overhead stability, not pure strength. Prilepin's chart works best for max-effort lifts (squat, deadlift, C&J). OHS mobility/positioning work may be more beneficial than strict rep-range adherence here.

4. **6-Week Hypertrophy Block (Stated Goal)**  
   - Use **55–80% intensity ranges** (18–30 total reps) for accessory work.
   - Keep snatch/clean technique work *separate*—don't mix hypertrophy volume with skill acquisition.

---

## **Key Takeaway**

Prilepin's chart is scientifically valid but requires **individual adjustment** for night-shift recovery, back pain, and technical development. Prioritize consistency over maximal volume.
