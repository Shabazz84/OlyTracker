# https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/olympic-lifting-complexes

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** Complexes are performed as continuous sequences (e.g., 3 pulls + 2 power snatches) with minimal rest between movements within the set. Rep schemes vary by goal: higher reps for endurance, lower reps with heavier loads for strength.
*   **Intensity:** Can be tailored; lighter weights used for technique focus, heavier for strength/endurance.
*   **Frequency/Placement:** Recommended for warm-ups (light complexes), accessory work, or finishers to cap off sessions.
*   **Periodization:** Designed to accumulate volume without excessive fatigue by distributing workload across muscle groups in a single set.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **General:** Focus on "controlling the weight through every phase," not just moving it. Emphasize fluidity and precision between phases.
*   **Snatch:** Use complexes (e.g., Snatch Pull + Power Snatch) to refine bar path and transition from pull to catch.
*   **Clean:** Complexes like Clean Pull + Power Clean emphasize explosive hip extension and proper posture during the pull.
*   **Jerk/Overhead:** Overhead Squats within complexes target shoulder stability, core strength, and total body mobility while holding the bar overhead.

**3. Exercise Recommendations (Complexes)**
*   **Snatch Focus:** Snatch Pull + Power Snatch; Snatch Balance + Overhead Squat (TOR Complex).
*   **Clean Focus:** Clean + Front Squat; Clean High Pull + Hang Clean.
*   **Jerk/Overhead Focus:** Front Squat + Jerk; Push Press + Jerk.
*   **Full C&J:** Clean + Front Squat + Jerk (flexible rep schemes).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Warm-up:** Use light complexes (e.g., 3 high pulls + 2 power snatches) to activate muscles and improve mobility before heavy lifting.
*   **Mobility:** Overhead squats within complexes specifically demand and develop shoulder stability and total body mobility.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The text explicitly states complexes are ideal for "intermediate" lifters or those transitioning, as they break down lifts into manageable parts to solidify movement patterns and correct errors (e.g., pull-to-catch transition).
*   **Hypertrophy/Volume:** Complexes allow high training volume without extending workout duration, suitable for the proposed 6-week block.
*   **Limiters:** The "Snatch + Overhead Squat" complex directly addresses the athlete's OHS limitation (50kg) by forcing stability under load immediately after the snatch.
*   **Caution (Back Pain/Night Shift):** While complexes build strength endurance, the text warns that they involve "continuous movement with varying loads." For an athlete with **chronic back pain**, performing high-volume complexes without adequate rest between sets could exacerbate fatigue and injury risk. The article suggests using complexes as *finishers* or in *warm-ups*, but does not explicitly address chronic pain management during heavy complex loading.
*   **Night Shift:** No specific advice regarding circadian rhythm or sleep recovery is present, though the text notes complexes are efficient for those with "limited time."
