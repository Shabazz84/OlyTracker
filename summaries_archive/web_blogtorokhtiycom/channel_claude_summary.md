## 1. Core Training Philosophy

**Principle 1: Sport-Specific Weakness Prioritization**
Address the primary limiting factor first. Your overhead squat (50 kg) is critically low relative to back squat (118 kg), indicating thoracic mobility, core stability, or positional weakness in the receiving position. This directly constrains snatch performance.

**Principle 2: Individualized Constraints Management**
Chronic back pain requires intelligent volume distribution, not avoidance. Use pain-free ranges during hypertrophy blocks; prioritize technique and loading precision over absolute tonnage. Back pain often stems from poor positional awareness in the catch, not weakness alone.

**Principle 3: Technical Maturity Before Intensity**
You lack split jerk mechanics entirely. A 6-week hypertrophy block should include dedicated split jerk teaching (even unloaded) before intensity phases. Pushing jerk efficiency gains plateau without hip/leg drive contribution.

**Principle 4: Contextual Load Management**

Heavy sessions placed during best recovery windows (early week if possible); moderate-intensity technique work fits fatigue-resistant days.

## 2. Programming Approach

### Structure: 6-Week Hypertrophy Block

**Weeks 1–3: Movement Competency + Hypertrophy**
- 3–4 training days/week (respecting shift schedule)
- Focus: Snatch → Squat → Accessory pattern (daily)
- Rep ranges: Snatch/Clean complex work 3–5 reps; squats 5–8 reps; accessory 8–12 reps
- *Why*: Builds work capacity and positional comfort without excessive CNS fatigue; addresses split jerk deficit through accessory work (split squat variations)

**Sample Day (Mon/Tue—if accessible):**
1. Snatch from boxes (3×3 @ 70–75%) + Overhead squat (5×5 @ 60%)
2. Back squat (4×5 @ 75–80%)
3. Paused front squat (3×6 @ 70%) + Good mornings (3×8)
4. Core: Suitcase carries, planks with perturbation

**Weeks 4–6: Hypertrophy + Split Jerk Introduction**
- Increase squat/accessory volume; add split jerk teaching (unloaded → light)
- Snatch: reduce frequency slightly; emphasize quality
- Clean: 2–3×3 @ manageable loads
- Jerk: introduce split mechanics in warm-ups and separate technical sessions

**Sample Day (Wed/Thu—night-shift compatible):**
1. Clean (3×2 @ 80%) + Push jerk (3×3 @ 75%) + Split jerk practice (5×1 unloaded, then 3×2 @ 40%)
2. Front squat (4×6 @ 75%)
3. Split squat variation (3×6/leg); reverse hyper (3×8)

### Periodization Post-Hypertrophy Block (Weeks 7–12)
- **Weeks 7–9**: Competition movement emphasis (Snatch/Clean&Jerk heavy singles/doubles); accessory at 70–80%)
- **Weeks 10–12**: Deload/comp prep or repeat cycle with increased loads

### Intensity Prescription
- Snatch/Clean: 75–85% during hypertrophy block (technique quality maintained)
- Back squat: 75–80% (respect back pain; prioritize depth and alignment)
- Jerk: 70–75% push jerk; split jerk unloaded → 40% once introduced

## 3. Key Technique Cues & Exercise Preferences

**Snatch (Primary Focus)**
- Cue: "Chest up, bars over mid-foot at setup; pull elbows high and wide into catch"
- Problem area: OHS weakness → prioritize receiving position mobility
- Accessory: Snatch grip RDLs (3×5), pin snatches from mid-thigh (3×3), overhead squat holds (3×30sec)

**Clean & Jerk (Split Jerk Development)**
- Push jerk is acceptable for strength base-building; introduce split mechanics in controlled environment
- Cue: "Split jerk = front foot forward, rear foot back, land shallow"
- Teach split squat variations first to build comfort (Bulgarian split squat, deficit split squat)
- Accessory: Tall jerks (unloaded), dip walks, leg press emphasis on asymmetrical loading

**Back Squat (General Strength)**
- Respect back pain: use elevated heel if needed; avoid forced depth
- Cue: "Neutral spine, brace core before descent, depth achieved without rounding"
- Reduce range if pain present; build isometric strength in pain-free range

**Anti-Rotation Work (Back Pain Prevention)**
- Paloff press (3×8/side)
- Suitcase carries (3×40m/side)
- Dead bugs with resistance (3×8/side)
