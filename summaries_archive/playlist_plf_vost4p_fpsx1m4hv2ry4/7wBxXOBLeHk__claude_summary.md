## Key Points
- Hip extension and leg-driven mechanics are primary power sources; avoid early hip rise in snatch pull and weak compensatory positions in jerk
- Maintain vertical bar path and full extension in jerk finishing position
- Setup intention and controlled descent prevent technical breakdown (bar drift, loss of position)
- Limit coaching cues to 1–2 per set to reduce cognitive overload during training
- Posterior chain tightness is a common constraint; mobility/massage work supports recovery
- Early coaching investment in technique foundation prevents compensation patterns

## Technique Cues
- **Snatch pull:** Prioritize power position (torso angle, leg engagement); delay hip rise until bar reaches knee
- **Jerk:** Emphasize vertical finishing position with full leg extension; use hips/legs as primary driver, not upper body compensation
- **General:** Control eccentric (descent) phase deliberately; maintain bar proximity through intentional setup

## Programming Principles
- Progressive loading paired with technique refinement (not maximal attempts before technical mastery)
- Reduce session density when fatigue/recovery concerns arise
- Mobility and posterior chain work integrated into programming

## Athlete Relevance
**High relevance:**
- Posterior chain tightness aligns with chronic back pain + weak OHS (50 kg); hip-driven snatch/jerk cues may address technical deficit
- Controlled descent cue supports back pain management
- Single-cue limitation suits night-shift fatigue (reduced cognitive demand)

**Caution:**
- Multiple near-maximal attempts mid-session conflict with shift-work recovery needs; periodized structure essential for this athlete
- Posterior chain mobility work should precede hypertrophy block to reduce injury risk during increased volume
