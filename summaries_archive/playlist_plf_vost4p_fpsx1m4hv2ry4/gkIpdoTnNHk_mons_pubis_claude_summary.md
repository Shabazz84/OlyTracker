# Consolidated Weightlifting Coaching Summary

## Key Points

- **Receiving position is the primary technical limiter**, not pull power. Athlete must catch HIGH and RIGID (hip parallel or above), allowing bar weight to drive downward rather than falling under it.
- **Drive-dominant mechanics**: Explosive leg drive precedes back extension (not simultaneous). Low/weak pull forces catching lower than optimal.
- **Quality reps over load**: Multiple correct repetitions build motor memory faster than heavy singles—critical for intermediate athletes and technical mastery before loading increases.
- **Training frequency appears insufficient** for competitive progression; double sessions (snatch AM, clean+jerk PM) or increased weekly frequency recommended.
- **Evening training aligns with athlete's chronotype** (night shift worker Wed–Sun); nutrition timing: 2–3 meals pre-session required for muscle readiness.

---

## Technique Cues

| Movement | Cue |
|----------|-----|
| **Snatch/Clean Catch** | Meet bar HIGH and RIGID. Catch at/above hip parallel. Bar weight pushes you down—don't fall into the hole. |
| **Pull Sequence** | Leg drive → back extension (sequential, not simultaneous). Premature back extension = weak pull = forced low catch. |
| **Foot Positioning** | Blocks at toe (not midfoot). Drive hard enough that feet spread *with intention* post-extension; if feet haven't moved, drive was insufficient. |
| **Power Position** | Pause at midthigh after first pull; feel the angle before extending. |
| **Extension Cue** | "Drive, don't jump." Drive accounts for ~70% of bar flight; jump is secondary. Transmit "stiffness/vibration" through entire body post-extension. |

---

## Programming Principles

- **Snatch paused + snatch-stance squats**: Sequential pairing reinforces power position awareness and fixes catch-position weaknesses.
- **Romanian deadlift progression** (legs-only → back-only → combined): Builds posterior chain safely for athlete with chronic back pain; emphasis on upper back tension, not lumbar stress.
- **Exercise sequencing**: Movement resets (massage breaks) optimize recovery for night-shift athlete; separate strength/technique from conditioning to reduce CNS fatigue.
- **Periodization**: 3 competitions/year (1 main + 2 test events) recommended. Current phase should prioritize technical mastery at light loads (70–75 kg) before percentage jumps.

## Athlete Relevance

| Profile Factor | Application |
|---|---|
| **OHS 50 kg limiter** | Paused snatch + snatch-stance squats directly build positional strength in catch. High-quality reps at lighter loads (70–75 kg) preferred over heavy misses. |
| **Chronic back pain** | RDL progressions with emphasis on upper back engagement (vs. lumbar stress). Upright catching position reduces spinal loading vs. deep forward collapse. Avoid heavy front squats; prioritize technical work. |
| **Night shift (Wed–Sun)** | Train evening when alertness peaks. Ensure 2–3 meals pre-session for glycogen/muscle readiness. Strategic recovery windows between sessions. |
| **Push jerk only (no split)** | Not addressed in source material; defer to separate jerk-specific coaching. |
| **Intermediate level + back squat 118 kg** | Receiving position (high, rigid catch) is immediate limiting factor, not leg strength. Focus on technical mastery before hypertrophy block (planned). |
| **Clean/Jerk totals low relative to squat** | Increase training frequency; double sessions or 4–5x/week sessions needed to build pulling/receiving capacity. |

## Summary for Next Phase

Before the 6-week hypertrophy block: prioritize **snatch/clean receiving position refinement** at lighter, technical loads (70–75 kg), supported by RDL posterior chain work, evening scheduling, and adequate pre-training nutrition. High training frequency essential.
