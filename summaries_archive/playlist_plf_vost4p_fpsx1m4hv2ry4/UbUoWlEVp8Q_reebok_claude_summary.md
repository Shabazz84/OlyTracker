# Merged Weightlifting Coaching Summary

## Key Points

- **Bar path verticality** is non-negotiable; forward drift requires mid-lift correction and positional reinforcement through drilling
- **Emotional/mental state** accounts for ~50% of performance success; aggressive mindset improves catch quality and pull speed
- **Static postural holds** (pause squats, back extensions with load) build stability without excessive dynamic stress
- **Shoulder/thoracic mobility** is essential, not optional—directly addresses OHS deficit and back health
- **High-frequency technical drilling** builds motor patterns; early technical work should not be judged by speed/intensity
- **Contact point matters**: Forehead (hard bone) redirects energy upward; soft tissue contact (belly/thighs) absorbs energy and kills trajectory
- Training frequency constrained by life/work schedules; prioritize **quality over volume**

---

## Technique Cues

**Snatch-Specific:**
- **Vertical pull**: Bar travels from floor to pubis in straight line; mental image is vertical despite natural backward lean (consequence of body extension, not intentional hip thrust)
- **Hip extension timing**: Drive bar upward to contact point; avoid excessive forward hip drive ("pushing hips forward" creates loss of control)
- **Contact point**: Deliberate forehead contact (hard bone redirects energy like a catapult); avoid soft tissue absorption
- **Catch position**: Relax shoulders entering overhead; avoid tension during reception; maintain forward knee position for stability
- **Starting position**: Maintain consistent setup; don't shift between positioning and pull initiation; hold start position under tension

**General Positioning:**
- Wide foot stance in receiving position; emphasize hip mobility
- Allow head to move back naturally as bar passes; avoid blocking with head
- Minimize unnecessary trunk motion; isolate shoulder/hip movement
- "Don't drop the bar"—aggressive catching prevents momentum loss

**Wrist/Grip:**
- At maximal weights, wrist contribution is negligible; light-weight wrist-pull habits transfer poorly to heavy loading

---

## Programming Principles

- **Rep ranges**: 3–5 reps preferred for technical/strength work; avoid high-rep ranges (5+ perceived as conditioning, not strength/technique)
- **Intensity progression**: Move from technical drilling → heavier weights to "sharpen" performance
- **Static work prioritized**: Pause squats, back extensions with bar behind head, hold-position drills establish postural stability under load
- **Repetitive drill work** builds "muscle memory" for stabilizing imperfect pulls; simplified progressions (e.g., "floor to pubis" snatch pull) reduce cognitive load
- **Recovery management**: Massage, mobility, and wrapping scheduled proactively for fatigue management
- **Emotional activation**: Consciously practice aggressive/confident mindset alongside technical reps—especially critical for night-shift fatigue

## Athlete Relevance

**Direct Application to Your Profile:**

| Factor | Application |
|--------|------------|
| **OHS limiter (50 kg)** | Vertical bar path + forehead contact + shoulder relaxation cues + aggressive catch mindset directly address snatch receiving position weakness; high-priority technical work |
| **Chronic back pain** | Static hold positions (pause squats, back extensions) prioritized over dynamic pulling; vertical pulling pattern (not hip thrust) reduces lumbar strain; postural reinforcement builds stability |
| **Push jerk only** | Upper-back static work (back extensions, pause squats) transfers directly to jerk receiving position; postural holds build confidence |
| **Night shift (Wed–Sun)** | High-frequency technical drilling suits non-linear recovery; emotional activation practice essential (fatigue impairs mental state ~50% of performance); prioritize quality sessions over volume |
| **6-week hypertrophy block** | Static/pause work fits hypertrophy (time-under-tension); bar positioning and technical drilling now builds technique carryover for strength block |
| **Intermediate → OLY transition** | Simplified drill progressions (floor to pubis) reduce cognitive load; focus on repeatable patterns before intensity escalation |

**Immediate Priority:** Shoulder/thoracic mobility work + vertical bar path drilling + static postural holds. These address your OHS deficit, back pain, and jerk receiving position simultaneously.
