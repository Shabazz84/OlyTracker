# Weightlifting Coaching Summary

## Key Points
- Snatch technique centers on vertical bar path and hip-pubic bone contact as primary alignment markers
- Three-point vertical alignment (knees → pubic bone → catch position) prevents forward bar drift and wasted energy
- Hang snatch work emphasizes explosive hip extension with controlled bar sliding contact along body
- ~4 competitive lifts/month + daily GPP (squats, pressing, shoulders) as baseline structure
- Recovery protocols: sauna, massage, sleep, nutrition prioritized alongside training
- Power development requires aggressive intent; slower lifts force compromised squat-under mechanics

## Technique Cues
- **Setup**: Toes inward, knees OUT (wider than parallel), full foot contact, bar at pubic bone in relaxed hang
- **Pull**: Dip from knees only; keep bar at/above knees; explosive hip snap to pubic bone contact point
- **Bar Path**: Glide bar along body ("sliding contact")—don't push bar forward; return hips to vertical line
- **Catch**: Pull under happens automatically if bar speed sufficient; if slow, forces compromised receiving position
- **Mental**: Visualize 3 alignment points; activate full-foot pressure before each rep

## Programming Principles
- Session-level: 5 sets prescribed for movement evaluation
- Block structure: Hang snatch emphasis (current focus)
- Frequency: 4 guaranteed weightlifting sessions/month minimum
- Adjunct work: Daily GPP (squats, pressing, shoulders) outside main lifts

## Athlete Relevance
**High relevance to OHS limiter (50 kg):** Vertical bar path, hip extension control, and pubic bone strike mechanics directly transfer to overhead position strength and stability.

**Not addressed:** Back pain accommodation, split jerk progression, 6-week hypertrophy block design, night shift periodization.
