# Technique: Front SQUAT / A.TOROKHTIY

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency:** Suggests a 5-day split (Mon/Wed/Fri main, Tue/Thu auxiliary) for the "Taktika" project, though this specific video focuses on accessory work.
*   **Intensity:** Warm-up sets should be max 10% of the lifter's best result in that exercise to avoid injury and fatigue. Main working weights must be lighter than back squats (barbell front squat loads < back squat loads).
*   **Periodization:** Used as a complementary accessory, often paired with overhead squats or cleans for "harmonic" leg loading.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Bar Placement:** Bar rests on the *shoulders/traps*, never directly on the chest/sternum.
*   **Grip:** Must use a "full grip" (thumbs wrapped) to prevent elbows from flaring out, which causes bar instability and injury risk.
*   **Torso Angle:** Maintain a constant torso angle; do not perform a "nod" (leaning forward then back). The shoulder should cover the knee at the bottom.
*   **Knee Path:** Knees drive forward but must not travel significantly past the toes/shoulder line.
*   **Chest/Elbows:** Keep chest high and elbows pointed to the sides.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Primary:** Barbell Front Squat (specifically for Olympic lifting mechanics).
*   **Accessory:** Overhead Squats (implied as a pairing partner).
*   **Warm-up Tools:** Resistance bands for shoulder mobility; wrist straps (lifting belts/straps) to secure the bar on the shoulders if grip is weak.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Mobility:** Perform thoracic spine warm-ups (arms extended, mimicking yoga movements) and shoulder mobilization with resistance bands before squatting.
*   **Footwear:** Use weightlifting shoes with a heel or place a wedge under the heels if wearing flat shoes to improve ankle dorsiflexion.

**5. Relevance to athlete's profile**
*   **Back Pain:** The text explicitly warns that front squats load the core and back more than back squats due to the forward lean; it advises caution for those with heart issues or weak backs, suggesting strict form over heavy weight to prevent injury.
*   **Technique Focus:** Emphasizes "full grip" and bar placement on shoulders, which is critical for an athlete transitioning to Olympic lifting who may lack the upper back stability of a powerlifter.
*   **Limitation:** The text notes front squats place significant cardiovascular demand; if the athlete feels heart strain ("heart starts to fail"), they should reduce load immediately.
