# The Power Position In the Snatch / Torokhtiy

# Weightlifting Coaching Analysis

## Programming Principles
- **Structure**: Progressive complexity—isolate elements (legs, arms, contact), then layer together
- **Frequency**: Multiple sets/reps of foundational drills before loaded movement
- **Intensity**: Low-moderate weight, emphasizing quality and position over load

## Technique Cues – Snatch (Primary Focus)

**Shoulder Position (Critical for this athlete's OHS limiter)**
- Shoulders must cover knees/barbell throughout deadlift and into power position
- Maintain forward shoulder inclination as long as possible (not premature back lean)
- Back lean is acceptable *only* at very heavy weights for stability/speed bonus—not with light loads

**Extension & Contact**
- Straighten legs completely before opening hips; avoid incomplete leg drive
- Contact occurs at hip; follow with aggressive arm bend (elbows up) *simultaneously* with final leg drive
- Position = "knees slightly soft, forward," shoulders covering barbell

**Drop Under (Snatch Balance Focus)**
- Fastest possible descent into full squat (not half-squat)
- Pull under barbell rather than push it
- Receive at lowest fixation height achievable

**Deadlift Position Hold**
- Keep barbell 10–15 cm forward from knees (easier to accelerate than directly at knee)
- Shoulders forward, knees slightly forward in alignment—maintain this until final extension

## Exercise Recommendations
1. **Muscle snatch → 3-rep leg activation drill** (shoulders over knees, leg-driven extension)
2. **Snatch balance from toes** (pulling under, full squat reception)
3. **Short deadlift (knee→hip) + hang snatch** combination (feel angle, transfer to dynamic snatch)
4. **Snatch balance with slow descent** (hip stop, arm direction work)
5. **Elevated barbell** (using plates) for better feedback/vibration on light loads

## Recovery/Mobility
- Shoulder position drilling emphasizes scapular relaxation: "Tighten shoulders maximally, find middle ground for static work + relaxation"
- Slow eccentric deadlifts for position learning
