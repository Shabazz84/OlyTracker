## **2. Technique Cues**

**Clean:**
- **Grip width**: Wider grip (jerk grip) shifts pull position lower—aim for lower 1/3 to mid-thigh for European arm length; Chinese athletes pull higher due to shorter arms.
- **Elbow positioning**: Elbows must point **forward at setup only**—forget this cue during pull. Forward elbows prevent hand-catching the bar during acceleration.
- **Pull quality**: Prioritize **full foot contact throughout pull** (no forward lean). Stand on full foot, maintain vertical position → add aggression once stable. Aggressive pull = +20%; pushing bar forward = -30% power loss.
- **Standing**: Full upright extension on toes (not forward/backward) = indicator of clean pull quality and bar control.

**Jerk:**
- **Energy conservation in clean matters**: Clean efficiency preserves energy for jerk.
- **Split jerk consideration**: Push jerk to squat is energy-intensive for tall athletes (longer path ~2m) vs. shorter athletes (~1m). Implies tall athletes should optimize technique efficiency.

## **3. Exercise Recommendations**
- **Mobility drills** for elbow/shoulder mobility (2 exercises shown for "elbow up" deficiency):
  - Hand-behind-back diagonal rotation with knee bend
  - Hands-behind-back shoulder press (arms crossed, pressing into shoulders)
  - Prone elbow press into floor

## **4. Recovery/Mobility**
- Address chronic elbow mobility limitations preventing proper "elbow up" position in clean.

## **5. Relevance to This Athlete**
- **OHS 50kg limiter + no split jerk**: Limited shoulder mobility/thoracic extension directly impacts jerk stability. Mobility work is **critical**.
- **Back pain + tall athlete (102.5kg)**: Tall athlete jerk = longer path = higher fatigue. Push jerk to squat very demanding; split jerk may reduce spinal stress if mobility allows.
- **6-week hypertrophy block**: Mobility work should precede strength block.
