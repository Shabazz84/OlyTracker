# WEEK #2 / TOROKHTIY_GANG training program

## Key Points
*   **Phase Focus:** The program operates in a "Base/Preparatory" phase prioritizing technique, speed-strength, and foundational hypertrophy before transitioning to high-intensity loading.
*   **Session Structure:** A 5-day weekly framework (3 main sessions Mon/Wed/Fri + 2 optional technique Tue/Thu) designed for high-frequency Olympic lift practice with specific compound movements repeated across days.
*   **Pulling Mechanics:** Snatch pulls utilize loads up to 105% of max, emphasizing speed over absolute load; Clean pulls begin at ~80% with a dip/pull mechanic before transitioning to standard pulls.
*   **Squat Loading:** Leg strength is developed through high-intensity (up to 90%) low-rep sets or hypertrophy-focused volume, utilizing both back and front squat variations.
*   **Spinal Health Priority:** Daily hyperextensions (2–3 sets) are non-negotiable for building spinal endurance, specifically prescribed as a primary intervention for chronic back pain before heavy loading is introduced.

## Technique Cues
*   **Snatch Pulls:** Maintain a constant back angle from the start through the pull; avoid "breaking" at the hips. Actively splay knees wide to prevent spinal rounding ("hunchback"). Focus on passing the bar *through* the body using torso movement rather than just pulling upward.
*   **Clean & Jerk (Stability):** In the jerk, achieve full squat depth before driving; avoid "dumping" into the knees prematurely. For footwork, maintain a wide stance for stability with the front foot slightly turned inward to ensure proper alignment.
*   **Snatch from Hang:** Control the descent slowly to engage the back and establish balance (avoiding forward/backward lean). Do not drop the bar; use the controlled lowering phase to set up an explosive pull.
*   **Squats:** Keep elbows high and chest up with knees flaring outward while pushing hips back. Maintain a "free" upper body that does not lock, allowing efficient power transfer from the lower back and hips.

## Programming Principles
*   **Periodization Strategy:** Begin with base preparation focusing on technique refinement and speed-strength development. Intensity (load) increases only after a solid foundation is established.
*   **Pull Differentiation:** Utilize straps to isolate pulling mechanics and prevent grip fatigue. Distinguish between "standard" pulls (floor to hip/chest) and "high" pulls (to chest/chin).
*   **Range of Motion Drills:** Implement Snatch/Clean from Blocks using plates to increase range of motion and encourage proper knee splay and depth.
*   **Explosive Integration:** Incorporate jump training immediately following squats (jumping off a box/platform) to develop explosive speed rather than just vertical height.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain Management:** The daily hyperextension protocol is critical for this athlete, serving as active recovery and spinal endurance building necessary to tolerate the transition to Olympic lifting loads safely.
*   **Transitioning Intermediate Profile:** The emphasis on "base preparation," slow bar descent drills, and high-repetition technique work aligns with an athlete needing to build a foundation rather than peak immediately.
*   **Push Jerk Constraint:** While the source text mentions split/jerk mechanics (scissors), the specific cues regarding foot width and stability are directly applicable to the athlete's exclusive use of the Push Jerk.
*   **Night Shift Considerations:** The high-frequency structure requires careful recovery management; the daily spinal endurance work acts as a stabilizing active recovery component suitable for an irregular sleep schedule, though the 5-day commitment must be balanced against fatigue from night shifts.
