# The Clean: Explosion / Как улучшить подрыв штанги [ENG SUB] A.TOROKHTIY

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency:** 3–4 sets of the specific "pull" drill at the start of every session; additional sets can be added later in the workout or as a standalone technique finisher.
*   **Intensity:** Start with empty bar (or light load) to master the feel, progressing to +40kg for the lock-grip variation once the movement pattern is established.
*   **Reps:** 15 reps per set for the locked-barbell drill; standard sets for the initial pull drills.
*   **Periodization:** Use this as a foundational technique block before heavy loading; the coach notes the first month may cause pain/skin damage ("calluses") which indicates correct force application.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **"Let it play":** Relax hands completely, allowing the bar to swing freely away from the body and return on its own momentum before striking.
*   **Elbow drive:** As the bar returns, pull elbows up aggressively ("elbows up").
*   **Knee position:** Knees must be spread wide (abducted) at the start of the pull.
*   **Bar path:** The bar must "cover" the shoulders; do not let it drift forward or backward.
*   **Foot angle:** Maintain a 90° angle between the shin and floor (heels down, shins vertical); avoid shifting weight too far onto toes or heels during the setup.
*   **Hip position:** Keep hips back ("taz ostanovlen nazad").

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **"Butterfly Effect" Drill:** A specific drill using a barbell with plates loaded at the very ends (maximizing the "swing/play") to enhance proprioception of energy transfer.
*   **Lock-Grip Pulls:** Performing the pull while gripping the bar in a "lock" position (fingers interlocked) or using lifting straps to protect calluses, focusing on the explosive upward elbow drive.
*   **Static Hold Drill:** Holding the top position of the pull for 2 seconds to reinforce the feeling of the bar "playing."

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Hand Care:** Use lifting straps frequently (even for Olympic lifters/CrossFitters) to allow calluses and skin time to recover; alternate training days with and without straps.
*   **Callus Management:** Expect initial pain, bruising, and blisters in the first month as the body adapts to the correct force application; this is a sign of proper intensity, not injury requiring cessation (once calluses form).

**5. Athlete-specific relevance**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The coach emphasizes strict posture: "spine like a whip" and keeping hips back. This specific cue helps prevent rounding the lower back during the explosive pull phase.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The drill is designed to correct a common intermediate error (weak, flat pulls) by forcing an exaggerated, high-velocity movement that feels foreign initially but builds necessary power.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** While not explicitly mentioned in this text, the advice to use straps and alternate grip styles supports hand durability for athletes who may have reduced recovery time due to shift work schedules.
*   **No Split Jerk:** The focus on "elbows up" and maintaining a vertical shin angle is critical for a push jerk athlete to ensure proper bar path stability without relying on leg drive from a split stance.
