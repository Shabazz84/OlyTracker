# Technique: PUSH JERK / A.TOROKHTIY (Weightlifting)

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Structure:** Break the push jerk into three isolated phases (arms, legs/drift, squat/jerk) before combining them.
*   **Drill Progression:** Start with a stick/broom for arm mechanics, progress to "toes-up" drills (lifting onto toes while pushing), then combine leg drift and press.
*   **Reps/Intensity:** High focus on technique; use sub-maximal loads initially to master the "scoop" depth and timing. No specific set/rep schemes provided, but implies high repetition of drills for automation.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Rack Position:** Do not rest the bar on the chest or neck. Rotate elbows down (hook) so the bar rests on the front deltoids; open elbows to the sides to clear the clavicle.
*   **Posture:** "Arch the lower back" (narrow waist, slight posterior pelvic tilt) and keep shoulders engaged before lifting.
*   **The Press:** Inhale fully at the start. Push up by extending arms while simultaneously shrugging and squeezing scapulae at the top. Hold the lockout for 2 seconds to ensure stability.
*   **The Dip (Squat):** Depth should be 10–15% of body height. Do not go too deep or shallow artificially; depth depends on leg strength to drive the bar. Imagine holding a "bowl of water" in your hips—keep it level during the dip to avoid spilling (leaning forward/back).
*   **Timing:** Arms and legs must move simultaneously. No sequential movement (e.g., push then squat, or squat then push).
*   **Foot Stance for Catch:** The receiving stance should be 10–15 cm wider than shoulder width initially; adjust based on comfort and ability to sit under the bar.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Stick/Broom Drills:** For isolating arm mechanics and rack position.
*   **"Toes-Up" Drill:** Stand on toes, push bar up while simultaneously spreading legs into the receiving stance (isolates the leg drive timing).
*   **Push Jerk (Shvung):** The primary movement recommended over Split Jerk for this context.
*   **Comparison Note:** Mentions Split Jerk as an alternative but suggests Push Jerk is often superior for strength transfer and stability once mastered.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Breathing:** Inhale fully before the dip; do not exhale during the movement. Exhale only after locking out.
*   **Mobility Focus:** Hip flexibility to maintain a level "bowl" (pelvis) during the squat; shoulder mobility for the rack position and scapular shrug.

**5. Specifically relevant to this athlete's profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The cue to "arch the lower back slightly" while keeping hips back ("water bowl") is critical for spinal stability, but must be balanced carefully given the chronic pain history; avoid excessive lumbar extension if it triggers pain.
*   **No Split Jerk:** The text explicitly supports a Push Jerk focus, aligning with the athlete's current limitation (push jerk only).
*   **Strength Transfer:** The text notes that mastering the push jerk can quickly match or exceed split jerk performance (citing a 190kg lifter example), which is encouraging for an intermediate athlete looking to maximize their 65kg push jerk potential.
*   **Depth Warning:** Explicitly warns against "artificially" dipping deep if leg strength isn't sufficient, suggesting the athlete should not force a deep squat if they cannot drive the bar up from there—this prevents injury and wasted energy for an intermediate lifter.
