# BEND with a barbell or "GOOD MORNING" /  A.TOROKHTIY

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** 8–10 sets total per session; start with 6–8 reps at low weight (20–40 kg), progressing to 3–4 reps as load increases.
*   **Intensity:** Do not chase maximal loads; prioritize speed and technique over heavy weight.
*   **Frequency/Progression:** Perform this movement in every training session ("start every workout with it"). Use the "bounce" (oscillation) only for mobility at low weights; remove the bounce when adding load to focus on explosive concentric contraction.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Stance:** Feet shoulder-width or slightly wider, knees pushed outward.
*   **Movement Pattern:** Hinge hips back ("push hips back"), drop torso between legs (deep squat position), maintaining a flat spine. Visualize forming a "T-shape" with the bar.
*   **Tempo:** Slow eccentric (descent) to engage muscles; explosive concentric (ascent) like a spring.
*   **Common Error Correction:** Do not let shoulders and torso rise simultaneously; drive hips up first. Keep spine flat throughout; if lumbar extension occurs, reduce range of motion or load immediately.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Primary Movement:** Stiff-legged deadlift variation / Good Morning variation with a deep squat hinge (described as "bending over" with a bounce).
*   **Specific Variation:** Use the oscillation/bounce only for warm-up/mobility; perform strict, explosive reps when loading.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Lumbar Health:** Specifically recommended for athletes with lumbar lordosis (excessive arch) to "push vertebrae back" and relieve pinching/strain after fatigue.
*   **Mobility:** The deep hinge position increases flexibility in the hips and lower back, mimicking a snatch start position.

**5. Relevance to athlete's profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain/Lordosis:** This exercise is explicitly highlighted for athletes with lumbar lordosis or chronic back pain ("pinching") to strengthen the posterior chain without heavy loading, potentially alleviating symptoms by correcting spinal alignment.
*   **Back Squat/Deadlift Weakness:** Addresses the specific issue of "back pulling back" during pulls due to weak posterior muscles.
*   **Olympic Transition:** The deep hinge position mimics the snatch start, helping develop the explosive power needed for the first pull.
*   **Caution:** While beneficial for pain relief, the athlete must strictly avoid "dead stop" positions and ensure they do not over-extend the spine during the ascent to prevent injury, given their existing back issues.
