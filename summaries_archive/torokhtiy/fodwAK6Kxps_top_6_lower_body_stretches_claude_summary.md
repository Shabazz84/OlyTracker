# TOP 6: LOWER body STRETCHES

# Analysis: Olympic Weightlifting Mobility/Stretching Guide

## Content Type
Russian-language coaching video demonstrating 6 post-training stretching exercises for weightlifters.

---

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Post-training stretching**: 10–15 seconds per stretch (recovery focus) vs. 30–60 seconds, 3–4 sets (mobility development)
- **Static stretching post-workout**: Preferred over dynamic; aids CNS recovery and pulse reduction
- **Frequency**: Post-training emphasis

---

## 2. **Technique Cues**

**Hamstring stretch** (seated):
- Neutral spine; pelvis tilted back (not forward)
- Knee extended
- Pull with hands toward shin
- Hold 30 sec × 3 per leg

**Glute/piriformis stretch** (seated figure-4):
- Hip bones level, facing forward
- Lean forward along leg line
- Increase stretch by moving heel forward
- 30 sec × 3

**Adductor stretch** (wide stance forward fold):
- Shift weight side-to-side to find angles
- Can flex/extend bent leg
- Pelvis stays neutral

**Calf stretch** (wall/support):
- Keep heel planted (key point: don't lift)
- Weight forward
- 15 sec per leg

**Hip mobility** (butterfly pose):
- Feet close to glutes
- Knees down
- Option: back against wall for support
- Deeper variation with partner assistance available

**Quad stretch** (kneeling, back-supported):
- Not recommended for knee issues
- Alternative: supine pull-toward-chest with partner
- Requires adequate lumbar mobility

---

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**

✅ **Relevant to this athlete:**
- **Glute/hip stretching priority** — critical for Olympic lifting mechanics and deep positions (squat, clean pulling)
- **Adductor mobility** — supports receiving positions
- **Calf/ankle mobility** — impacts ankle dorsiflexion in snatch/clean
- **Avoid aggressive quad stretching** — athlete has chronic back pain; kneeling quad stretch risks lumbar discomfort

---

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**

- Stretching improves joint ROM, muscle pliability, recovery speed, and metabolic rate
- "Myofascial knot" concept: addressing one area (e.g., glutes) releases tension in related areas (hamstrings, lower back)
- Choose challenging (not pain-free) positions for adaptation
- **Critical for heavy sports**: Greater joint mobility → wider range of angles → better muscle recruitment → improved progressio

---

## 5. **Athlete-Specific Relevance**

⚠️ **High priority:**
- **Chronic back pain** → avoid aggressive lumbar flexion; monitor pelvis positioning in all stretches
- **Low OHS (50 kg)** → ankle/hip/thoracic mobility work here directly supports snatch technique
- **Night shift (Wed–Sun)** → post-training stretching aids sleep quality and CNS recovery
- **Hypertrophy block ahead** → mobility prep enables better positions and muscle engagement

**Recommendation**: Hip/glute/adductor/calf stretches align with this athlete's needs. Avoid aggressive spinal flexion stretching.
