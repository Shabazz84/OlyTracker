# POWER POSITION in SNATCH / A.TOROKHTIY (weightlifting)

# Russian Olympic Weightlifting Snatch Pullunder Coaching Analysis

**Content Summary:** Detailed snatch pullunder (подрыв) coaching lesson—this is highly relevant.

---

## **1. PROGRAMMING PRINCIPLES**
- Pullunder is a **feel-based, technical element** requiring repetition and sensation development ("difficult to explain, must be felt")
- Progression: Start with **light weight** (coach uses max 80 kg even in peak form)
- Sequencing: Progress from **no heel rise → heel rise variation** after initial technical mastery

---

## **2. TECHNIQUE CUES FOR SNATCH**

| Phase | Cue |
|-------|-----|
| **Setup** | Knees out, hips back, shoulders over bar, full foot pressure |
| **Pull** | Hands engage *simultaneously* with leg extension (not sequentially) |
| **Height** | Pullunder occurs at groin/lower abdomen OR mid-thigh (varies by anthropometry) |
| **Critical error** | If groin contact causes pain → you're too "open"; must be "covered" at pullunder to contact femur, not pubic bone |
| **Arm finish** | Elbows **high and forward**, NOT back; slight inward wrap of elbows |
| **Heel rise** | Add *after* feeling the pull; increases "brightness" and bar flight |
| **Sound check** | Audible "pop/click" = correct pullunder happening |

---

## **3. EXERCISE RECOMMENDATIONS**

**Exercise 1: Snatch Pullunder Position (Trainer Bar/Empty Bar)**
- Snatch grip, load legs, push vertically, finish with arms/traps (low load, feel-based)

**Exercise 2: Pullunder Accent (from Hang)**
- Push arms away → inertia returns bar → hip strike + elbow drive upward  
- Hold top position 1–2 sec (elbows high)
- Progress: add heel rise

**Exercise 3: Snatch Pullunder from Blocks (Recommended)**
- Bar at knee height (optimal leverage)
- Use straps to avoid grip distraction
- Knees out, full foot, upright torso
- Progress: start without heel rise, add later
- Keep weights **light** (technical tool, not strength)

---

## **4. RECOVERY/MOBILITY ADVICE**
- **Bruising/soreness on thighs is normal** ("characteristic of weightlifters"); indicates correct bar contact
- Hair loss on thighs = sign of proper technique (humorous but true)

---

## **5. ATHLETE-SPECIFIC RELEVANCE**

| Issue | Application |
|-------|-------------|
| **OHS limiter (50 kg)** | Weak pullunder = poor bar extension/flight → weak positional foundation. Prioritize snatch pullunder drills—directly improves overhead position. |
| **Push jerk only (no split)** | Related: similar arm/elbow position issue; pullunder drills build high-elbow finish. |
| **Chronic back pain** | ⚠️ **CAUTION**: Pullunder with heavy loads or poor form can compress lumbar spine. Use **light weight, straps, perfect positioning**. Consider block work to reduce demand on back extension. |
| **Night shift worker** | Recovery tool: light, technical pullunder work requires less CNS demand than heavy pulls; fits fatigue state. |
| **6-week hypertrophy block ahead** | Pullunder drills should be **supplemental/accessory**, not primary—use light loads, higher rep ranges (3×5–8 reps with light weight). |

---

## **Key Takeaway**
Snatch pullunder is your **rate-limiting technical step** before the next block. Dedicate 2–3× weekly to blocks work + light pullunder drills (50–70 kg range) using straps to bypass grip fatigue given your night shift schedule and back condition.
