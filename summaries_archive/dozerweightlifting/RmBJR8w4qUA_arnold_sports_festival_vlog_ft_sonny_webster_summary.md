## Key Points
*   **Dip Mechanics:** Treat the dip as a vertical jump (load then unload), not a continuous squat. The drive must originate from the balls of the feet; heels may lift, and weight should remain neutral or slightly back to ensure vertical force transfer.
*   **Front Rack Stability:** Maintain an active rack by driving elbows up and pushing shoulders forward. Keep the bar below the knuckle line with fingers closed to prevent wrist collapse under load.
*   **Vertical Force Transfer:** For push jerks, focus on "punching" the ceiling tiles and keeping hips directly under shoulders. Avoid horizontal displacement which strains the midline.
*   **Recovery Constraints:** High caffeine intake, fatigue from night shifts, and weight cutting significantly degrade leg strength and technical execution. Heavy loading while exhausted is counterproductive; prioritize technique drills when fresh.

## Technique Cues
*   **Dip Execution:** Pause at the bottom ("dead position") to reset pressure points before driving. Visualize "dunking a basketball" to engage an athletic, explosive dip.
*   **Foot Pressure:** Maintain a 50/45 or neutral foot split during the setup; avoid loading heavily into heels prior to the dip. Drive through the balls of the feet with knees tracking over toes (outside edge) to maintain glute activation.
*   **Receiving Position:** Keep shoulders directly on top of hips with active triceps engagement. Maintain a vertical posture to prevent shoulder joint opening.
*   **Natural Movement:** Allow feet to land in their natural rotation during the clean rather than forcing external angles, preventing knee collapse and spinal shear.

## Programming Principles
*   **Fatigue Management:** Intensity must be managed around recovery status (caffeine, sleep cycles). Heavy loading is discouraged when fatigued; prioritize high-frequency practice of specific mechanics (e.g., paused dips) over maximal loads initially.
*   **Transition Strategy:** For athletes moving from powerlifting to Olympic lifting, emphasize "natural movement" patterns rather than forcing rigid technical positions that conflict with existing strength habits.
*   **Safety First:** Avoid horizontal bar displacement during the dip phase to protect the lumbar spine, particularly for those with chronic back pain.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Push Jerk Only:** The cues regarding vertical force transfer and "punching" overhead are directly applicable to a push jerk, eliminating the need for split-jerk footwork while maintaining the necessary stability for a 65 kg lift.
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The emphasis on neutral weight distribution (avoiding excessive heel loading) minimizes horizontal shear forces, reducing strain on the lower back during the dip and drive phases.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** Acknowledges that circadian disruption and caffeine reliance impair performance; suggests scheduling technique work during peak alertness rather than pushing heavy loads during fatigue.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The focus on natural foot rotation and active front rack engagement addresses common pitfalls for powerlifters transitioning to Olympic lifting, specifically regarding ankle mobility and spinal stability.
