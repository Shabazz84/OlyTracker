# Weightlifting Analysis: Snatch Technique & Pre-Taper Training

## Programming Principles
- **Pre-taper phase**: Heavy singles and maximal effort work to accumulate fatigue before deload
- **Rationale**: Athletes should feel "beat down" pre-taper; high fatigue allows supercompensation during taper
- **Frequency of heavy attempts**: Repeated heavy singles before taper familiarizes athlete with competition weights

## Technique Cues for Snatch (5 key cues provided)

1. **Starting position & first pull**: Shoulders and hips rise simultaneously at same rate
2. **At knee**: Shins vertical with torso angle rising consistently (prevents early hip rise)
3. **Tall snatches**: Build tension in back via lat engagement; focus on "staying up" and adding height as long as possible before turnover
4. **Elbows**: Keep higher than barbell until bar passes chest (not chest height); prevents early turnover and loss of connection
5. **Power position**: Keep arms long/straight, shoulders down; arms only bend after legs/hips extend (don't pull with arms prematurely)

**Bracing error**: Over-extension in start position causes premature hip rise and poor core pressurization → use neutral spine instead

## Exercise Recommendations
- Tall snatches (reinforces connection and high elbows)
- Heavy singles in final pre-taper week
- Progressive snatch volume leading to taper
