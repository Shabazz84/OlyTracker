# Merged Summary: Weightlifting Programming & Technique

## Key Points
- **Training frequency**: 5–6x/week superior to 2–3x/week; both snatch and clean should appear in most sessions (not split by day)
- **Squat blocks effective**: increase volume (10×3, 5×5 protocols) before transitioning back to Olympic lifts
- **Avoid concurrent overload**: excessive squat volume + Olympic lift training simultaneously causes fatigue
- **Power variations acceptable**: power snatch, snatch balance, power clean are valid progression tools
- **Frequency dependency**: 2–3 days off from squats = ~15kg strength loss; deadlift retention longer than squat

## Technique Cues
- **Rack position mobility** (front squat/clean): load heavy and hold (70kg+ straps, 30sec holds) before heavy clean-jerk sessions
- **Overhead squat mobility**: stability ≠ mobility; thoracic extension is often the limiting factor (not shoulder flexibility)
- **Block snatch vs. snatch from floor**: expect block snatch to be significantly stronger in earlier training phases

## Programming Principles
- Structure hypertrophy block with high squat volume, then reduce squat frequency when reintroducing heavy Olympic lift work
- Both snatch/clean in most sessions maintains technical competency
- Load mobility drills (rack holds, OHS) consistently during heavy blocks

## Athlete Relevance
- **OHS limiter (50kg)**: mirrors elite lifter's thoracic extension/overhead stability issue—prioritize thoracic mobility work during 6-week hypertrophy block before strength phase
- **Chronic back pain**: squat frequency critical for pain management; avoid 2–3 day gaps; loaded holds may help stability
- **Night shift (Wed–Sun)**: 5–6x/week frequency requires disciplined recovery; training despite fatigue is advocated but monitor cumulative fatigue
- **Push jerk only**: no split jerk development mentioned; power variations are viable alternatives during transition phase
