# EOIN MURPHY - Training w/ Dozer Ep. #12

## Key Points
*   **Core Lift Dominance:** Prioritize high frequency of Snatch, Clean & Jerk, and Back Squats over accessory work, plyometrics, or "weird" challenges; these provide the greatest return on investment for strength and technique.
*   **Bar Selection:** Avoid powerlifting bars (stiff) for Olympic lifting; utilize standard Olympic bars with sufficient whip to facilitate proper turnover and bar path mechanics.
*   **Load Management:** Do not rush heavy loads if technique is failing. Implement a "weak phase" before attempting strength peaks. Use larger warm-up jumps initially, then refine with smaller increments to find the groove.
*   **Recovery & Lifestyle:** Sleep quality (specifically REM cycles) is the primary driver of recovery and injury prevention; lifestyle factors like poor sleep hygiene or substance use significantly increase injury risk (e.g., quad tears). Avoid "copying" elite techniques without a solid foundational base.

## Technique Cues
*   **Elbow Management:** If elbows exhibit "nervy pain," avoid heavy power cleans immediately preceding snatch work to prevent overuse and sequencing errors.
*   **Bar Speed:** Move heavier weights with increased speed to utilize the stretch reflex, but exercise extreme caution as this increases injury risk if technique is compromised.
*   **Mental Calibration:** Focus on "boring guidance" (consistency in basics) rather than chasing elite trends or specific head positions that may not suit your current biomechanics.

## Programming Principles
*   **Frequency over Volume:** Structure training to maximize repetitions of the main lifts and squats rather than high-volume accessory work.
*   **Progression Logic:** Accept necessary "weak phases" where technical stability is prioritized over load; do not force heavy attempts (e.g., 110kg snatches) if form degrades.
*   **Injury Prevention:** Avoid daily maxing or counter-productive stunts that lead to overtraining. Ensure adequate nutrition and sleep duration before attempting high-intensity sessions.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The emphasis on "boring guidance" and avoiding blind imitation of routines minimizes the risk of exacerbating back pain through flawed form or excessive accessory volume.
*   **Night Shift Work (Wed–Sun):** Given the high correlation between poor sleep (6 hours) and injury, prioritize strict sleep hygiene during off-shifts over recovery gadgets (ice baths/saunas). Consistency in rest is more critical than training frequency for this schedule.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The strategy of avoiding "weird challenges" aligns with transitioning from general strength to Olympic lifting; focus on mastering the main lifts (Snatch, C&J, Squat) rather than social media stunts or maxing daily.
*   **Specific Limiters:** While the source does not address the OHS directly, the principle of "Core Lifts Only" supports focusing energy on the Clean and Jerk mechanics while managing the squat volume to accommodate the 102.5 kg bodyweight without overloading the chronic back issues.
