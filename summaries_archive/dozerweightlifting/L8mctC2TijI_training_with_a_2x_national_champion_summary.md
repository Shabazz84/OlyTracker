# Training with a 2x National Champion

## Key Points
*   **Technical Focus:** The dialogue emphasizes that the Clean must be "perfect" and non-grindy; a grindy clean inevitably causes Jerk failure due to lost rhythm.
*   **Jerk Mechanics:** A critical cue is keeping weight on the balls of the feet (not heels) during the dip/drive to maintain posterior chain tension. The "front foot out" position is identified as essential for correcting hip positioning and allowing a smooth flow into the catch.
*   **Snatch Timing:** Athletes must stay over the bar until it is fully dug into the hip; getting behind the bar prematurely disrupts the pull path. Block Snatches are recommended to enforce this timing and patience.
*   **Exercise Selection:** Strict Press and Push Press are prioritized for teaching vertical force application, preventing the "spray" of the bar forward during a Jerk.
*   **Myth Busting:** The source explicitly dismisses "fascial training" trends as irrelevant to elite lifting performance; the focus must remain on high-intensity lifting mechanics rather than ancillary mobility drills.

## Technique Cues
*   **Jerk Dip/Drive:** Maintain pressure through the balls of the feet; avoid collapsing onto heels which kills force application. Use a "front foot out" stance to optimize hip alignment and drive flow.
*   **Clean-to-Jerk Sync:** Prioritize rhythm over raw power. If the Clean is not clean (grindy), the Jerk will fail. The movement must be fluid, not segmented.
*   **Snatch Pull Path:** Maintain vertical bar path by staying directly over the bar until the hip contact point is established; never initiate the second pull before the bar is "dug in."

## Programming Principles
*   **Force Directionality:** Utilize Strict Press and Push Press variations to isolate and teach pure vertical force application, specifically to correct Jerk mechanics where the bar travels forward.
*   **Timing Drills:** Implement Block Snatches as a primary tool for athletes struggling with bar path timing or getting behind the bar too early.
*   **Complex Integration:** Consider Clean-Front Squat-Jerk complexes as a viable alternative to heavy Back Squats for those requiring spinal load management, though this is athlete-specific.
*   **Load Management Warning:** The source notes anecdotal "lifting through pain" strategies but explicitly flags them as dangerous for chronic injury; programming must prioritize structural integrity over volume when back pain is present.
