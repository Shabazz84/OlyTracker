# HOW TO BE STRONGER OVERHEAD ON SNATCH

# Weightlifting Coaching Analysis

## 1. Programming Principles
- **Periodization**: Structured block training ("dog pack" program); Monday = snatch-focused, high technique emphasis; Friday = intensity-focused
- **Snatch work**: Snatch balance + full overhead squats (1+1), then snatch pull + low hang snatch; heavy singles prioritized
- **Squat**: Heavy 3x5 described as "most challenging squat workout of this block"
- **Volume management**: Deliberately dropping volume week-to-week

## 2. Technique Cues
- **Snatch pull/connection**: Never lose connection to the barbell throughout the lift; maintain connection during the pull
- **Avoid "panda pulls"**: Don't teach yourself to lose the bar or dive under prematurely (common mistake mimicking elite lifters)
- **Overhead position**: Rear deltoid activation > passive stretching. Active muscle engagement supports the bar better than mobility alone

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
- Snatch balance + full overhead squat (addresses overhead strength)
- Snatch pull + low hang snatch (maintains pull strength and timing)
- Heavy 3x5 squats (leg/posterior chain)

## 4. Recovery/Mobility Advice
- Activate rear delts instead of relying solely on stretching for overhead mobility
- Good sleep noted as important; ensure adequate warm-up on stiff mornings

## 5. **Athlete-Specific Relevance**
- ⚠️ **OHS limiter (50 kg)**: Snatch balance + OH squat prescription directly addresses this weakness
- ⚠️ **Chronic back pain**: Heavy 3x5 squats may aggravate; night shift fatigue could compound recovery
- ⚠️ **No split jerk**: Program appears snatch-focused; jerk work not detailed here
- ⚠️ **Back pain + night shift**: Recovery advice minimal; mobility/activation work helpful but insufficient for this profile
