# How to not jump back

# Analysis for This Athlete

## 1. **Technique Cue — Snatch (Highly Relevant)**
- **Problem identified:** Jumping back in snatch
- **Root cause chain:** Hips rise too soon → shoulders shift forward over bar → weight shifts forward → body jumps back to rebalance
- **Key cue:** "Stay in the middle" — maintain centered foot position throughout pull

## 2. **Exercise Recommendation**
- **Snatch deadlift + low hang snatch deadlift + low hang snatch** ("yo-yo snatches")
- **Purpose:** Teaches leg use and maintains torso angle; prevents forward weight shift and early hip rise
- **Programming:** Run 2–3 sets before working sets, every snatch session

## 3. **Direct Relevance to Athlete**
- **OHS 50 kg is primary snatch limiter** → fixing jumping back (likely caused by poor positioning) is critical for snatch development
- **Chronic back pain** → maintaining proper torso angle and centered position reduces spinal stress vs. forward-shifted/jumping patterns
- This drill prioritizes positioning integrity over load — excellent for hypertrophy block with technical focus

## Summary
Actionable snatch-specific fix addressing a common fault pattern that likely contributes to limited OHS and potential back compensation. Implement immediately.
