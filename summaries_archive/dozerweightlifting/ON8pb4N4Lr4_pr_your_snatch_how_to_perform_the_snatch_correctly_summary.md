# PR your snatch - How to perform the snatch... CORRECTLY

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Structure:** The text advocates a "top-down" progression (starting from the catch/overhead position and working backward to the floor) rather than the traditional bottom-up approach.
*   **Tempo:** Emphasizes slow, controlled eccentric movements during warm-ups (e.g., 5-second descent in overhead squats).
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Suggests practicing specific drills (muscle snatches, high pulls) for sets of 5 reps to ingrain mechanics before moving to full lifts.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Overhead Position:** Find a "middle ground" between excessive external and internal rotation where hips can relax and sit comfortably without shaking or rounding the back.
*   **Head/Neck:** Keep eyes straight forward; do not jam the head through. The head position should naturally sort itself based on levers (longer femurs = more forward).
*   **Arm Mechanics:** Initiate by lifting elbows while keeping hands passive ("whip" motion). Do not press under early; let gravity keep the bar close. The "crack" of the wrists occurs only at the very last second before the catch.
*   **Power Position:** Shoulders directly over the bar, bar resting on the shoelaces (hip crease), flat feet, and lats tight but not shrugging.
*   **Leg Drive Cue:** Imagine a beach ball above the head; bounce it straight up rather than whipping hips or yanking shoulders back.
*   **Transition (Floor to Knee):** Keep torso angle locked, squat with legs only, keep weight balanced in feet (active quads), and let bar fall straight down to load hamstrings without shifting onto heels.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Warm-up:** RDLs (kicking hips back, straight lower back), High Pulls (elbows up, ribs locked), Muscle Snatches, Strict Presses, Overhead Squats.
*   **Drills:**
    *   Tempo Overhead Squats (5s down, pause at bottom).
    *   Static Arm Mechanics (lifting elbows, flipping hands without leg drive).
    *   Power Snatch from High Hang (visualize bar reaching only forehead height).
    *   Pause Snatches (catch at forehead, pause 1-2 seconds, then ride down to squat).
    *   Position Drills: Moving between Power Position (1), Knee Position (2), and Floor Position (3) with emphasis on "pushing up" from position 2 before extending.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Mobility:** Use tempo overhead squats to gradually increase depth; do not force the bottom if uncomfortable, but aim for a stable, relaxed squat where hamstrings touch calves.
*   **Stability:** Focus on finding an elbow rotation that allows hip relaxation without upper back shaking or lower back rounding.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, 102.5kg, Chronic Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly warns against "rounding the back" in the overhead position and emphasizes keeping the torso angle locked during the pull from the floor. It advises finding a comfortable overhead rotation to avoid shaking or instability that could strain the spine.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** While not explicitly mentioned, the emphasis on "relaxing into positions" and avoiding excessive tension ("don't be active through your hips") aligns well with an athlete needing to manage fatigue and chronic pain by reducing unnecessary muscular effort.
*   **Transition to Olympic Lifting:** The "top-down" approach is highly beneficial for a beginner/intermediate transitioning from powerlifting, as it prioritizes the catch (the most common failure point) before adding complexity like floor pulls.
*   **Limiters:** The focus on finding the correct overhead rotation directly addresses the OHS limitation mentioned in your profile.
