# HOW TO IMPROVE YOUR OVERHEAD STABILITY ON SNATCH

### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Reactivation Phase:** The text advocates for a **2-week reactivation block** at the start of any cycle (especially before major competitions like the Arnold). This involves high volume but very low intensity to minimize adaptation lag and prevent early burnout.
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Suggests performing accessory movements **3 times per week**. The main lifts should focus on "pure practice" with high volume from the floor in the subsequent phases.
*   **Intensity Strategy:** Prioritize consistency over maxing out early; the goal is to feel "on autopilot" and recovered by week 3, rather than surviving the first two weeks of a new program.

### 2. Technique Cues (Snatch Focus)
*   **Overhead Position:** Utilize **internal rotation** in the catch position. This keeps the thoracic spine extended and reduces the need for shoulder external rotation during the turnover, allowing elbows to stay up longer.
*   **Torso Angle:** For lifters with long femurs, consciously manipulate the start position to maintain a consistent torso angle off the floor; do not let the bar swing out.
*   **Stability:** Focus on a "structurally sound" overhead lockout to prevent missed lifts behind the pool (a common issue at Nationals).

### 3. Exercise Recommendations (Accessories)
The coach identifies three specific staples for carryover:
1.  **Bamboo Bar Carries (or Band-Loaded Bar):** Walk backwards with a bar/bands hanging from the sides to engage stabilizers, upper back, and serratus anterior while resisting forward momentum. *Alternative:* Max hold if walking space is unavailable.
2.  **Single-Arm Farmer's Carries:** Focus on keeping hips and shoulders level. Cue: "Pull elbow up and back" and squeeze abs to prevent drooping. Use heavy loads; light weights are ineffective for this cue.
3.  **Unilateral Lunges (Walking or Kettlebell Front Rack):** Goal is a completely vertical torso. Squeeze glutes and lengthen hip flexors on the descent. Do not lean forward.

### 4. Recovery & Mobility Advice
*   **Rest Days:** Acknowledges that "Fridays suck" for night shift workers; suggests using rest days to heal rather than forcing technique work if the body isn't ready.
*   **Travel Maintenance:** If traveling (e.g., holidays), prioritize getting squats in over complex lifts to maintain general conditioning without risking injury or inconsistency.
