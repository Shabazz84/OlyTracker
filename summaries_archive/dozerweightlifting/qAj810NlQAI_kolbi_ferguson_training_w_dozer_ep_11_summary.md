# KOLBI FERGUSON - Training w/ Dozer Ep. #11

## Key Points
*   **Training Frequency:** Optimal frequency appears to be 4 days/week (Snatch/C&J split across Mon/Tue and Wed/Thu, with one dedicated heavy day on Sat) to maintain consistency without excessive fatigue.
*   **Warm-up Protocol:** Utilizes progressive jumps for both Snatch and Clean & Jerk (starting at 50–70kg, progressing through 90kg+) rather than static warm-ups.
*   **Core Stability Strategy:** Use of a lifting belt specifically during heavy Jerk attempts to create intra-abdominal pressure ("push against the belly"), facilitating harder leg flexion and bar stability.
*   **Nutrition & Recovery:** Consistent caloric intake (3 meals/day) is critical to prevent muscle catabolism; sleep quality is identified as the primary limiter for strength adaptation, requiring strict adherence to a circadian rhythm or artificial light management for shift workers.

## Technique Cues
*   **Jerk Mechanics:** "Relax hands and shoulders" during the catch phase; excessive tension causes bar whip. Focus on a smooth dip-and-drive trajectory where the bar travels vertically without crashing into the body.
*   **Snatch Positioning:** Catch the bar in a more upright, vertical position ("back here") rather than leaning forward, indicating a need to adjust grip width or torso angle.
*   **Mobility Limiters:** Shoulder tightness (likely from auxiliary pressing) is a primary cause of technical breakdown; prioritize shoulder stretching and mobility work before heavy loading.

## Programming Principles
*   **Periodization Approach:** Relies on self-regulation based on daily feel rather than rigid linear periodization; intensity varies by day to avoid "destroying" the athlete.
*   **Hypertrophy Block Priority:** Before increasing load, prioritize addressing mobility (shoulders) and core stability to resolve technical wobbles.
*   **Load Management:** Heavy loading is concentrated into one weekly session; frequency of missed reps is accepted as part of the learning curve for intermediate lifters.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** The athlete's natural circadian rhythm (sleeping at sunset) contrasts with your Wed–Sun shift schedule. You must artificially replicate darkness during the day and light exposure before your sleep window to match their recovery standards, as sleep is paramount for strength gains.
*   **Back Pain & Belt Usage:** While a belt aids stability in the Jerk, it is not a cure-all; the root cause of pain here appears to be compensatory tightness from other movements. Address shoulder mobility and core bracing first to reduce strain on the lower back during the jerk.
*   **Transition Strategy:** As an intermediate athlete with "wobbly" technique (similar to the dialogue subject), your 6-week block should focus on **mobility** and **core stability** rather than maximal loading. This resolves the tightness causing technical breakdowns before introducing heavy volume.
*   **Bodyweight Management:** At 102.5 kg, ensure you are eating sufficient calories (3 meals/day) to support the hypertrophy block without catabolism; avoid "one meal a day" patterns which lead to shakiness and strength loss.
