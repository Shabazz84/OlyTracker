# NATE CERWINSKE - Training w/ Dozer Ep. #19

## Key Points
- Elite lifters train ~4×/week, 60–90 min sessions (snatch, clean & jerk, back squat focus)
- Heavy singles accumulate fatigue faster than triples; your athlete likely tolerates low volume better
- Low training volume + pain avoidance = injury prevention & longevity
- Bar control in elite lifters appears effortless with no excessive CNS depletion at maximal loads
- Recovery quality (sleep, massage, mobility) trumps session length, especially relevant for night shift workers

## Technique Cues
- Maintain bar control—bar should never appear to be "pulling" on the lifter
- OHS limiter (50 kg) suggests thoracic mobility/stability deficit; aligns with chronic back pain; prioritize mobility work
- Elite lifters show no visible fatigue/compensation patterns even under maximal loads—technical efficiency reduces injury risk

## Programming Principles
- Avoid pushing through pain; skip sessions if fatigued rather than accumulate CNS depletion
- Design 6-week hypertrophy block with moderate frequency (~4×/week), conservative volume progression
- Triples cause faster depletion than singles for this athlete—structure accordingly
- Cortisol management: maintain calm between sessions, prioritize sleep, integrate massage/mobility

## Athlete Relevance
- Night shift (Wed–Sun) + chronic back pain = non-negotiable recovery emphasis; optimize sleep quality over session duration
- Low-volume tolerance suggests 4×/week structure works better than high-frequency programming
- OHS deficit (50 kg vs. 80 kg clean) is primary snatch limiter—dedicate mobility/stability work to thoracic spine
- Conservative volume progression recommended given back history; monitor lift quality over load accumulation
