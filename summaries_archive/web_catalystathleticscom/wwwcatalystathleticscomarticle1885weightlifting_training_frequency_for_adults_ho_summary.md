**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** Determined strictly by life demands (job/family). For adults with outside stressors, start low (2–3 days/week) and increase only if recovery allows; or start high (4–5 days) and reduce if overtraining signs appear.
*   **Volume Distribution:** Choose one of two models:
    *   *High Density:* 2–3 sessions/week with heavy volume per session (2–3 hours).
    *   *Low Density:* 4–5 sessions/week with lighter volume per session (45 mins – 1.5 hours).
*   **Progression:** Use a "bottom-up" approach for adults: Start conservatively and add days only when the body acclimates without chronic soreness or functional decline.
*   **Intensity/Periodization:** Not explicitly detailed in this text, but emphasizes that heavier lifters generally tolerate less volume than lighter lifters.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Self-Monitoring:** The primary recovery metric is "how you feel." If soreness/tiredness prevents functioning and persists despite rest, the workload is too high (overtraining).
*   **Acclimation:** Expect to feel sore and beat up; this is normal. Adjustments are needed only when the feeling becomes debilitating or chronic.
*   **Lifestyle Integration:** Training must be designed around non-lifting life demands (marriage, children, career) rather than ignoring them.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Adult/Intermediate Context:** The text is highly relevant for an intermediate athlete transitioning to Olympic lifting as an adult. It explicitly warns against copying "Eastern Bloc" programs designed for young professionals (10–20 years younger) who have full-time coaching and no life stressors.
*   **Night Shift/Chronic Pain:** While the text does not mention night shifts or back pain specifically, its core philosophy supports this profile: prioritize lower frequency (starting at 2–3 days) to manage systemic fatigue and injury risk. The author notes that as athletes age and accumulate stress, they must reduce volume to avoid injury.
*   **Actionable Advice:** Do not attempt a high-frequency program immediately. Start with the "low frequency" model (e.g., 2–3 sessions/week) to ensure the body can handle the new sport's demands alongside night shift work and existing back issues before attempting to increase volume.
