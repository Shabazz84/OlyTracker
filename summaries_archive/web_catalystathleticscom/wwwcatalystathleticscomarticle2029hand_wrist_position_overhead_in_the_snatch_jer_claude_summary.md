# Weightlifting Analysis: Hand & Wrist Position Overhead

## Relevant Technique Cues (Snatch & Jerk)

**Hand/Wrist Position:**
- Cup hand in U-shape; thumb at ~90° to forearm
- Bar over forearm, slightly *behind* midline (not centered)
- Wrist **extended** (not neutral)—heel of palm pointing toward ceiling
- Cradle the bar like a squat rack post; don't grip excessively
- Grip only as tightly as needed to control; tight grip = elbow flexor tension = slow elbow extension (problematic)

**Hook Grip Overhead:**
- Can maintain through turnover only with proper wrist/hand position
- Most male lifters need to release hook grip during turnover (hand/finger anatomy limits it)
- Hook grip only for pulling phases

**Common Error—Neutral Wrist:**
- Creates instability, relies on muscle tension rather than bony/ligamentous support
- Places bar too far forward
- Often painful; corrects naturally as weights increase

**Mobility Link:**
- Ankle/hip/thoracic immobility cascades → poor upper body position → wrist/elbow compensation (excessive extension, bar shifted back toward fingers, increased lever arm strain)

---

## Specific to This Athlete

**Critical Issue: 50 kg OHS (primary snatch limiter) + Chronic Back Pain**
- Poor wrist/hand positioning → bar placement too far back/forward → instability → increased spinal load
- Ankle/hip mobility restrictions may be forcing compensatory wrist extension → contributing to back pain
- **Priority:** Assess and correct hand/wrist position; address lower-body mobility (ankles, hips) to reduce overhead compensation

**No Split Jerk:**
- Wrist stability overhead is even more critical without split jerk to dissipate load

---

## Actionable Takeaways

1. **Diagnostic:** Film overhead position; check if bar sits properly over forearm or shifts toward fingers
2. **Mobility work:** Focus ankles/hips/thoracic before blaming wrists
3. **Wrist strengthening:** Jerk supports (10-sec holds) + wrist curls only at weights allowing proper position
4. **Back pain management:** Proper bar positioning reduces compensatory load on spine
