**1. Programming principles**

**2. Technique cues for snatch, clean, or jerk**
*   **Depth:** Squat to "full depth" (lowest position possible without surgical alteration), not just parallel. Goal: Maximal knee closure with a correctly arched back.
*   **Knee Path:** Allow knees to travel forward over toes; do not restrict them behind the feet unless anatomically impossible.
*   **Foot Placement:** Start slightly outside hip-width at heels, toes turned out 20–35 degrees.
*   **Alignment:** From above, foot and thigh should be parallel; from front, foot directly under knee. Hips sit between (not on top of) the heels.
*   **Knee Drive:** Consciously push knees outward to prevent valgus collapse during descent/ascent.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Primary Focus:** Back Squat, Front Squat, Overhead Squat (OHS), and receiving positions for Snatch/Clean/Jerk.
*   **Specific Adjustment:** If femur length limits depth despite ankle flexibility, adjust foot width/angle or use lifted heels (weightlifting shoes) to increase dorsiflexion range.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Mobility Targets:** Ankle dorsiflexion, hamstrings, hips, glutes, and quads.
*   **Pain Management:** If lower back arch breaks below parallel, limit depth to the point of maintaining a good arch; deepen only as flexibility improves.
*   **Knee Pain Protocol:** Ensure weight is on the front edge of heels (not balls of feet); loosen up quads before/after training; consult a PT or Chiro if pain persists.

**5. Relevance to athlete's profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly warns against "butt wink" (loss of lumbar arch) and advises limiting depth if the back breaks, prioritizing spinal integrity over maximal range until mobility improves.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** Emphasizes that OHS depth is governed by ankle dorsiflexion; suggests using lifted heels to compensate for limited flexibility to achieve proper receiving position.
*   **No Split Jerk:** The text notes foot positioning must be identical for all squats *except* the split jerk, aligning with this athlete's push-jerk-only constraint (no need to train split-specific footwork).
*   **Transition Phase:** Reinforces that individual variation exists but strict principles should remain the goal; modifications are acceptable only if caused by correctable flexibility issues, not "stubborn habits."
