# Analysis: "Getting Over Fear in the Snatch" by Greg Everett

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Wave loading** for heavy snatch attempts (e.g., 48-52-55-52-55-57-55-57-59-57-59-61) to gradually approach threshold without psychological breakdown
- Emphasizes consistency across all sets (warm-up to max attempts should be technically identical)

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- Relax arms during first and second pulls ("arms like ropes") — reduces perceived weight and psychological barrier
- Eliminate technical inconsistencies that create imbalance or bar crash
- Ensure stable, comfortable receiving position

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- **Overhead Squats** — build confidence in overhead position
- **Snatch Balances** — verify dynamic catch capability
- **Snatch Presses / Snatch Push Presses** — reinforce overhead stability & position
- Address ankle & hip mobility (directly impacts trunk positioning overhead)

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- Lower body mobility assessment (ankle & hip) — poor ankle mobility forces poor trunk positioning and overhead instability
- Mobility work essential for solid receiving position

## 5. **Relevant to This Athlete**
⚠️ **Critical Match**: OHS 50 kg (snatch limiter) — Greg explicitly prescribes OHS work. Build OHS confidence & strength to unlock snatch progress.
- **Back pain concern**: Poor ankle/hip mobility → poor trunk positioning → overhead instability. Mobility work is non-negotiable.
- **Wave loading** fits well into 6-week hypertrophy block (can reduce psychological barriers while building overhead strength).
