# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1902/Jumping-Forward-in-the-Snatch-or-Clean-Error-Correction/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Diagnosis First:** Do not apply a "scorched earth" correction (obstacles) without identifying the mechanical root cause (e.g., starting position, first pull balance, or third pull mechanics).
*   **Drill Integration:** Use corrective drills (lift-offs, segment deadlifts, high-pulls) to retrain the nervous system before attempting heavy loads.
*   **Frequency:** Apply physical barriers (mat/PVC) in *every* snatch/clean session until improvement is seen; transition to mental cues once progress stabilizes.
*   **Progression:** Gradually reduce the distance of corrective obstacles (e.g., moving the mat closer to toes) as technique improves.

**2. Technique Cues & Error Correction**
*   **First Pull Balance:** Immediately shift body and bar slightly *backward* after the break in the floor; do not pull vertically or tip over the bar.
*   **Bar Path (Mid-Pull):** Keep the bar moving back toward the body as it passes the knees to maintain proximity.
*   **Second Pull:** Drive aggressively with legs while keeping the bar close; avoid letting hips move too far forward through the bar.
*   **Third Pull:** Actively pull elbows up and out *before* turnover to accelerate downward; do not "float" or stop short of receiving the bar.
*   **Receiving Position:** Aggressively establish the overhead/rack position (snatch push press, OHS) to prevent stopping short, which causes forward drift.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Scorched Earth (Immediate Feedback):**
    *   **Coach Mat:** ¼" rubber strip placed in front of toes (distance = current jump length).
    *   **Coach PVC:** Vertical pipe held or flanged to stand near the bar path.
    *   **Chalk/Tape Line:** Visual barrier ("hot lava") behind which feet must not cross.
*   **Finesse (Mechanical Correction):**
    *   **Pull Variations:** Snatch/Clean lift-offs, segment deadlifts, halting deadlifts, slow-pull snatches/cleans.
    *   **Proximity Drills:** High-pulls, pull + snatch/clean, dip snatch/clean.
    *   **Receiving Strength:** Snatch push presses, Overhead Squats (OHS), muscle snatches/cleans, tall cleans/snatches.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   *Note: The text does not contain specific recovery, mobility, or sleep advice.* It focuses exclusively on technical diagnosis and mechanical correction of the forward jump.

**5. Athlete Profile Relevance**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The article emphasizes "tipping over" (hips rising faster than shoulders) as a cause for jumping forward; correcting this requires keeping shoulders, hips, and bar moving together, which reduces shear force on the lumbar spine during the first pull.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** The text explicitly links failure to bring the bar back in the third pull to OHS weakness/stability issues. It recommends **snatch push presses** and **Overhead Squats** as primary remedies for this specific deficit.
*   **"Push Jerk Only" Constraint:** While the article discusses "Clean" generally, the mechanics of the third pull (bringing bar back) are identical for a Push Jerk setup; ensuring the bar is vertical over the center of mass before the dip is critical for stability in a push jerk.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The text warns that fixing this error can be "long and discouraging" and may not be fully resolved quickly; managing expectations is vital for an athlete transitioning from powerlifting to Olympic lifts.
