# Merged Summary: Weightlifting Programming & Technique Principles

## Key Points
- Olympic lifts (snatch, clean, jerk) require **frequent, regular technical training** throughout a training cycle—cannot be deemphasized like powerlifting competition lifts can be
- Sport-specific technical work takes priority over general strength transfer; accessory volume should support, not replace, competition lift practice
- Bar timing, feel, and mechanics are highly technical variables; training tools (bands, accommodations) that distort these should be avoided
- During hypertrophy blocks, maintain competition lift frequency to prevent technique decay, especially given the athlete's technical limitations (push jerk only, weak OHS, suboptimal clean turnover)

## Technique Cues
- **Clean turnover**: Maintain grip longer through the turnover; **meet the bar with your body** rather than jumping down indiscriminately
- **Against banded work**: Bands alter bar timing and teach incorrect mechanics; prioritize high-block variations, hang cleans, tall cleans, and heavier weights instead
- Technique refinement (especially turnover) should precede heavy accessory loading

## Programming Principles
- Frequent snatch and jerk technical work must be **embedded in all training blocks**, including hypertrophy phases
- Use high-block variations, hang work, snatch/clean pulls, and tall cleans to reinforce mechanics
- Clean/squat ratio concern (80 kg / 118 kg): technical efficiency likely a limiting factor; prioritize turnover quality before loading

## Athlete Relevance
- **6-week hypertrophy block**: Maintain snatch/clean/jerk frequency (2–3× per week minimum) to prevent technique loss and address OHS/split jerk deficits
- **Clean turnover + OHS weakness**: These technical gaps should be addressed *during* the hypertrophy block via frequent competition lift work, not deferred
- **Back pain & night shift**: Source does not address recovery constraints; plan accessory volume conservatively to avoid exacerbating chronic pain
- **Push jerk limitation**: Split jerk development requires consistent jerk practice; cannot be built from accessory work alone
