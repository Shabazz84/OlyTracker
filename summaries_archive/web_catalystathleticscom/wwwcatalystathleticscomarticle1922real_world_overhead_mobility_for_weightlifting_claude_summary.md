# Weightlifting Analysis: "Real World Overhead Mobility for Weightlifting"

## **Directly Relevant to This Athlete**

### 1. **Mobility Work (CRITICAL)**
Your OHS is 50 kg—your primary snatch limiter. This article diagnoses exactly your issue:
- **Thoracic spine hyperkyphosis** (tight upper back) + **short pectorals** destroy overhead position
- **Lower body mobility often overlooked**—ensure ankles/hips aren't forcing compensation patterns
- **Consistency matters more than intensity**: expect months of work, not weeks

**Priority mobility routine:**
- Foam roll T-spine daily (pre-training minimum)
- Leaning bar hang (bonus: decompresses lower back—valuable given chronic back pain)
- Door jamb pec stretch
- Partner T-spine mobilization (if accessible)

### 2. **Overhead Strength/Stability Exercises**
All relevant given weak OHS:
- **Overhead squat** (your primary exercise)
- **Snatch push press**
- **Press in snatch**
- **Heaving snatch balance**
- **Jerk support holds** (2–3 sec overhead holds before recovery)

**Avoid:** Behind-the-neck exercises initially; start push press behind neck only as mobility improves.

### 3. **Recovery/Back-Specific**
- Leaning bar hang provides lower back traction (beneficial for chronic pain)
- Soft-tissue work recommended (chiropractor/massage)

### 4. **Programming Note**
Case study athlete trains 5–9x/week; **you** (night shift, Wed–Sun work) need realistic frequency. Mobility should be daily/multiple daily; strength work 3–4x/week during hypertrophy block.

---

## **Bottom Line**
Your OHS bottleneck is fixable but requires 6–12 months of consistent mobility + overhead stability work. Integrate stretches into daily routine; make OHS, snatch push press, and holds your staple exercises.
