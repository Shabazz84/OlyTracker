# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2270/Knee-Valgus-in-the-Squat/

This text is a specific technical article by Greg Everett regarding **Knee Valgus in the Squat** within Olympic weightlifting. It does not contain general programming principles, hypertrophy block structures, or recovery advice for night shift workers.

Here are the specific extractions relevant to your profile:

### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Correction Timing:** Implement corrective work (unilateral movements) during early phases of a training cycle or as prep work; do not rely on it for heavy squat efforts where fatigue exacerbates valgus.
*   **Tempo/Control:** Utilize slow eccentric and pause squats to develop better control, specifically when the knees begin to cave inward at the sticking point.

### 2. Technique Cues
*   **"Knees Out":** Use this cue *only* if the lifter demonstrates knee valgus (knees caving in) during the recovery phase of the squat or jerk dip.
    *   **Purpose:** To prevent hip collapse and forward trunk lean, which causes failure. It is not primarily for injury prevention but for maintaining upright posture.
    *   **Sequence:** Pair with **"Hips In"** immediately after ("Knees out, Hips in") to regain proper alignment.
*   **Stance Alignment:** Ensure thighs are aligned with feet (knee over second toe). Do not force knees outward if the stance is already correct; this creates torque.

### 3. Exercise Recommendations
*   **Corrective/Prep Work:** Focus on strengthening **adductors and internal rotators** (the text argues weakness here, rather than lateral hip strength, causes valgus).
    *   Unilateral squat variations.
    *   Staggered RDLs.
    *   Split squats and lunge variations.
*   **Avoid:** Squatting with mini-bands around the knees (deemed ineffective for this specific issue by the author).

### 4. Recovery or Mobility Advice
*   **Mobility Myth:** The text explicitly states that knee valgus is **not** a mobility issue if the athlete can achieve the position during the eccentric phase. Do not waste time on hip mobility drills expecting to fix the collapse.
*   **Stability Focus:** Prioritize global hip stability and control over isolated lateral hip strengthening (glute medius/abductors), which the author notes rarely corrects the problem.

### 5. Relevance to Athlete Profile
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text highlights that knee valgus leads to a forward trunk lean, increasing the moment on spinal joints and likelihood of collapse. Correcting this via "Knees out/Hips in" is directly relevant to mitigating back pain caused by poor squat mechanics.
*   **Jerk Limitation (Push Jerk):** While the athlete uses a push jerk, the text explicitly warns about knee valgus in the **jerk dip and drive**. Since the athlete has no split jerk, the stability of the dip phase is critical for receiving the bar; correcting valgus here ensures better posture under load.
*   **Snatch/Clean Pulls:** The author notes that if an athlete shows valgus during the pulls of the snatch or clean, it must be actively corrected using the unilateral/adductor work mentioned above, as it negatively affects lift execution.
