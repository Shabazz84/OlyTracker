# Analysis: Greg Everett - "Train Like A Champion"

## Relevant to This Athlete's Profile

**1. Programming & Technique Principles**
- **Corrective work volume**: Don't abandon exercises after just 30 reps; longstanding habits require weeks of consistent practice to correct
- **Every rep matters**: Each repetition reinforces either good or bad technique—there's no "neutral" rep
- **Full-session integration**: Corrective work must be reinforced across ALL lifts in the session, not isolated to one block (critical for this athlete's OHS limitation and chronic back pain)

**2. Technique Cues (Direct Application)**
- **Overhead position** (highly relevant—OHS 50kg is primary snatch limiter):
  - Hold every overhead lift 1–2 seconds before dropping
  - Maintain active positioning even with light weights (20 kg)
  - This multiplies practice volume without adding reps
- **Cue limit**: Focus on max 1–2 cues per lift (before + during); too many causes failure across the board

**3. Exercise Strategy**
- Correction via **exercises > verbal cues** (practice the body directly rather than overthinking)
- Use corrective complexes targeting weaknesses; allows athletes to *feel* proper positions
- Minimize thought during actual snatches/cleans/jerks after building positions in auxiliary work

**4. Habit Replacement (Mental)**
- Replace bad habits actively (e.g., can't "stop dropping jerks"—must actively hold them longer)
- Focus on what *to do*, not what *not to do*
- Positive, engaged mindset critical for long-term progress in monotonous training

**5. Recovery Note**
- Implicit: Quality over quantity; intentional practice beats volume
- Relevant for night-shift worker—fewer, higher-quality reps better than exhaustive sessions
