# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2071/GPP-for-Weightlifting-The-Big-Secret-or-a-Waste-of-Time/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Timing:** GPP is most effective immediately following the biggest competition of the year (active rest transition) or at the very beginning of a training cycle; reduce as specificity increases toward competition.
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Start with 1 carrying/dragging exercise per week, 1–2 jumping exercises per week, and 2–3 days of bodybuilding work per week. Daily ab work is recommended.
*   **Intensity:** Use low-intensity, high-repetition (8–20 reps) for connective tissue health; avoid high-volume monostructural conditioning (running/rowing) as it offers little benefit to weightlifters and can be counterproductive in large doses.
*   **Periodization:** Dose GPP heavily relative to specific work if the athlete is new or lacks an athletic background; reduce barbell volume during off-seasons to "have fun" with novel movements.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   *Note: The text does not provide technical cues for the snatch, clean, or jerk mechanics.*
*   **General Concept:** Emphasize "kinesthetic awareness" through simple gymnastics-type movements and calisthenics to improve body control and position awareness.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Joint Stability/Control:** Farmer's carries (1 or 2 arms), sled pushes/pulls, wheelbarrow walks, sandbag carries, overhead dumbbell/kettlebell walking, barbell with chains/bands.
*   **Explosiveness:** Jumping, bounding, short-distance sprinting, throwing.
*   **Hypertrophy/Connective Tissue:** Bodybuilding-type exercises (lighter weights, 8–20 reps).
*   **Unilateral Work:** Lunges, step-ups, single-leg RDLs, single-arm pressing/pulling.
*   **Grip:** Spring grippers, hanging from a pull-up bar, pinch lifts.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Conditioning:** Use low-intensity monostructural activity (walking, light rowing) only as active recovery on rest days to prevent stiffness from sedentary behavior; do not rely on it for performance stamina.
*   **Trunk Stability:** Prioritize direct ab and back exercises alongside carrying movements.
*   **Mental Health:** Incorporate novel GPP movements periodically (e.g., once a year) as a mental break from the monotony of repetitive Olympic lifting to maintain focus and motivation.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly recommends "joint preparation & health" via lower-intensity, higher-repetition work (8–20 reps) and a variety of movements to reduce the likelihood of repetitive use injuries and chronic pain.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** As an athlete not trained from age 8 (typical US context), this profile requires "considerable catching up" on fundamental traits like trunk stability, grip strength, and connective tissue strength before high-level specificity can be safely applied.
*   **Hypertrophy Goal:** The text validates bodybuilding-type training as functional for building muscle mass necessary to handle the rigors of training, supporting the planned 6-week hypertrophy block.
*   **Split Jerk Limitation:** The author notes that weightlifting is "nearly exclusively bilateral" and recommends unilateral work (lunges, step-ups) specifically to avoid imbalances in strength, mobility, and control, which is crucial for an athlete who cannot utilize the split jerk for balance recovery.
