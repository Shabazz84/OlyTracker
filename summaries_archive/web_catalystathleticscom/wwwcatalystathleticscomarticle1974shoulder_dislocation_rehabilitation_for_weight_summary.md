# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1974/Shoulder-Dislocation-Rehabilitation-for-Weightlifting-Part-3/

**Note:** This text is a personal case study regarding shoulder dislocation rehabilitation (Part 3 of a series) by Greg Everett. It does **not** contain specific programming for an intermediate athlete with chronic back pain or night shift work schedules. The following points are extracted strictly from the text and applied hypothetically to your profile:

1.  **Programming Principles:**
    *   **Frequency:** Training limited to **3 days/week** due to recovery constraints (applicable if injury limits volume).
    *   **Structure:** Alternating strength focus (e.g., Day 1: Squat/Lunge/Pull; Day 2: Squat/Snatch Pull; Day 3: Squat/Clean Pull/Core).
    *   **Intensity/Volume:** Early stages use very low weights (empty bar, 70kg pulls) with high control and no acceleration. Progression is gradual (e.g., moving from empty bar to 30kg over weeks).
    *   **Periodization:** Linear progression based on mobility milestones rather than a fixed hypertrophy block; "beach work" (hypertrophy) added to regain lost mass.

2.  **Technique Cues:**
    *   **Snatch/Clean:** Focus on **elbows high** and keeping the bar close during turnover, even if full overhead position isn't reached yet.
    *   **Rack Position:** Use a **narrower grip** than usual; ensure shoulder blades are depressed and retracted (especially during pressing/bench).
    *   **Overhead Stability:** Actively work on **protraction and elevation**; avoid "hiking" the shoulder blade to achieve range.
    *   **Pulls:** Execute with a **controlled, constant speed** from bottom to top initially; no explosive acceleration until mobility allows.

3.  **Exercise Recommendations (from text):**
    *   **Mobility/Prep:** Hanging from pull-up bar (pronated/supinated), Sleeper stretch, Apley internal rotation, Foam roller flexion with PVC pipe.
    *   **Strength Substitutes:** Safety squat bar (initially), Back squats on normal barbell once grip allows, Front squats (narrow grip), Lunge variations, Drop to split snatches (for footwork without full jerk).
    *   **Accessory Work:** 1-arm supports from knees, push-ups (slow/controlled), bench press variations (low incline), DB rows, band internal/external rotations, standing Ys.

4.  **Recovery or Mobility Advice:**
    *   **Frequency:** Perform full mobility/stretching set **minimum once daily** (ideally twice); initially 2-3 times/day during early recovery.
    *   **Specific Techniques:** Rolling underarm area with a softball; hanging from pull-up bar to open flexion; "Sleeper" and Apley stretches for internal rotation.
    *   **Pain Management:** Avoid end-range movements if painful/weak; focus on "fluidity" over full range initially.

5.  **Relevance to Athlete Profile:**
    *   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text emphasizes **daily back and ab work** (weighted/unweighted extensions, glute-ham raises, heavy sit-ups, planks) as a foundational requirement for stability during rehab.
    *   **Night Shift/Work Schedule:** Explicitly notes that training frequency was reduced to **3 days/week** due to work schedule constraints; suggests working within available time windows rather than forcing higher volume.
    *   **Hypertrophy Goal:** Mentions intentionally increasing "beach work" (bodybuilding-style accessory lifting) to regain weight lost during immobilization, aligning with a hypertrophy block goal.
    *   **Limitation Warning:** The text highlights that **overhead mobility** is often the primary limiter for returning to snatching/cleans after shoulder injury, which parallels your OHS limitation (50kg).

*Disclaimer: This athlete has a history of shoulder surgery and chronic pain; applying these specific rehab protocols without medical clearance could be dangerous.*
