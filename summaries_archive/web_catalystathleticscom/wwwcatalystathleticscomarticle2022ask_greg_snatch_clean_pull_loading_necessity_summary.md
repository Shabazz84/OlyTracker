# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2022/Ask-Greg-Snatch-Clean-Pull-Loading-Necessity/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Pull Intensity:** Generally 80–105% of best snatch/clean, but flexible based on individual strength-speed ratios. Advanced lifters may align better at these percentages; others need lighter pulls for technique or heavier pulls for raw strength.
*   **Speed vs. Grinding:** Balance heavy, slow pulls with finishing sets at 85–90% to maintain speed and "feel." Never end a pull session grinding slowly without a speed finisher.
*   **Necessity:** Pulls are highly beneficial but not strictly mandatory; prioritize them over squats only if time is extremely limited (though the author prefers keeping both).

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Posture & Timing:** The primary goal of pulls is reinforcing proper posture and timing, not just loading weight. If the athlete cannot maintain posture/timing at a given load, it is too heavy for that session's purpose.
*   **Speed Maintenance:** Ensure the movement retains speed; if the lift becomes "obviously slow," the load has exceeded the threshold for technical reinforcement.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Snatch & Clean Pulls:** Recommended as a staple accessory exercise.
*   **Squats:** Retain squats above pulls in priority hierarchy (only eliminate pulls before squats).

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   *None explicitly mentioned in this specific text excerpt.* (The text focuses on load selection and the utility of pulls, not recovery protocols or mobility drills.)

**5. Relevance to athlete profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The author's emphasis on "proper posture" over grinding heavy loads is critical for an athlete with chronic back pain; avoiding excessive loading that compromises spinal alignment will mitigate injury risk.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning Status:** The text notes that advanced lifters have better alignment between classic lift weights and pulling strength, implying this athlete may need to experiment within the 80–105% range rather than adhering strictly to a fixed percentage.
*   **Jerk Limitation (Push Jerk only):** While the text focuses on Snatch/Clean pulls, the principle of balancing heavy grinding with speed work applies directly to their push jerk development to ensure they don't sacrifice technique for load.
