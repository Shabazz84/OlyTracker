# Analysis: Snatch & Clean Extension Technique Article

## Technique Cues (Primary Focus)

**Bar Path & Body Contact:**
- Bring bar back into body as it leaves floor; use lats to push bar INTO hips (not near)
- Bar must contact body—if it doesn't, "you're doing something horribly wrong"
- Keep bar tight to legs on way up; if banging hips, you've let it drift too far early
- Prevents premature forward balance shift and over-reaching

**Extension Timing:**
- **Critical mistake:** initiating explosion too soon
- Maintain shoulders over bar until upper thigh/hip position
- "Jump" initiates ~upper thigh (clean) or hip (snatch), though timing cues vary individually
- Complete hip extension before transitioning under bar

**Results of Correct Execution:**
- Improved bar speed and height
- Better pull-under mechanics
- Improved receiving position balance

## Relevance to This Athlete

**High Priority:**
- **OHS limiter (50 kg):** Tight bar path + complete extension = better positioning into receiving positions
- **Chronic back pain:** Proper lat engagement for bar control reduces compensatory stress; avoid anterior balance shift
- **Clean technique (80 kg):** Bar contact and timing issues directly impact clean consistency

**Less Critical:**
- Split jerk focus means extension mechanics less critical to jerk
- Back squat strength (118 kg) supports receiving position capacity
