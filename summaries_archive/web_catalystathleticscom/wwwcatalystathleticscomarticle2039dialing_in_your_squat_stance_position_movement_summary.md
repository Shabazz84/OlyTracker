# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2039/Dialing-in-Your-Squat-Stance-Position-Movement/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Volume/Intensity:** Prioritize high volume of *correctly performed* squats below the failure threshold; limit heavy/imperfect exposures relative to good reps.
*   **Tempo:** Execute slow, controlled squats (even if feeling slower) rather than fast but technically compromised lifts.
*   **Specific Modality:** Integrate **pause squats** (at the bottom) to prevent "tipping" and strengthen the weakest range of motion; utilize front squats for quad dominance.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Stance:** Heels slightly wider than hip-width, toes out 20–30°. Thighs must align parallel with feet (knees track over toes).
*   **Trunk:** Neutral or *exaggerated* lumbar arch; flat thoracic spine. "Cinch" pressure by inhaling deeply to expand the trunk in all directions (not sucking waist in) and locking breath at the throat.
*   **Movement Path:** Sit directly down, stand directly up. Avoid excessive hip retreat ("sitting back") or forward tipping. Hips should not shoot up faster than the chest/bar initially.
*   **Knee Stability:** Maintain constant tension; knees must track over toes (no valgus collapse). Push knees out if they cave in.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary:** Pause Back Squats, Front Squats.
*   **Mobility/Prehab:** Spiderman Lunge, Squatting Ankle Stretch, Superman Hold (for spinal extension control), Foam Rolling Thoracic Spine (with abs tight to isolate T-spine).
*   **Stability:** Unilateral work: Lunges, Step-ups (driving through the up leg), Single-leg RDLs.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Frequency:** Perform spiderman lunges and ankle stretches in warm-ups, between sets, and during spare moments throughout the day.
*   **Thoracic Work:** Use foam rolling to mobilize T-spine (keep abs tight/ribs flat) followed by static overhead holds over the roller.
*   **Extension Control:** Practice isolating spinal extensors (curling back up from prone) before squatting sets.

**5. Athlete-Specific Relevance**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly addresses "butt wink" (lumbar flexion at depth). It warns that if the spine transitions from extended to flexed, it is a problem requiring mobility work or reduced depth until resolved. Emphasizes *exaggerated* arching and trunk pressurization for safety.
*   **Intermediate/Transition:** The advice to "retrain" hip mechanics (sitting back vs. upright) directly addresses the common transition error where athletes pull from high hips/deadlift patterns into squats, causing tipping.
*   **Night Shift/Schedule:** While not explicitly mentioned in the text, the recommendation to do mobility work "between every set... and every other spare moment" is highly adaptable for a night-shift worker with fragmented recovery windows.
*   **Limiters (OHS/Back):** The focus on quad strength relative to posterior chain prevents tipping, which is crucial for an athlete struggling with overhead stability (OHS 50kg) likely due to poor squat depth or trunk rigidity.
