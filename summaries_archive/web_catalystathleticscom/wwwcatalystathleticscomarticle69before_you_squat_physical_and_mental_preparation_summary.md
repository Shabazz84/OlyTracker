# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/69/Before-You-Squat-Physical-and-Mental-Preparation/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Timing:** Do not perform heavy squats at or near the end of a workout if they are the primary focus; prioritize them when fresh or ensure adequate prior warm-up.
*   **Intensity Progression:** Begin with empty bar, progress to 50–70 kg for slow sets/pauses, then ramp to working weight based on daily readiness.
*   **Mental Prep:** Dedicate time immediately before the lift to visualize success and eliminate distractions (stop talking, stop watching others).

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Unracking:** Drive the bar out of the rack forcefully without hesitation; a meek unrack signals weakness and increases perceived load.
*   **Stance/Positioning:** Push femurs away from hips (not just knees) to open the hip socket; keep feet planted firmly while pushing against the band/bar.
*   **Trunk Setup:** Take a big breath, set the trunk solidly, and plant squarely under the bar before lifting.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Prep Work:** Foam rolling (T-spine, glutes, quads, adductors, calves/soleus).
*   **Mobility Drills:** "Shoe grab" stretch (elbows between thighs, hips down), ankle mobility stretches in squat position.
*   **Activation:** Mini-band squats below knees with simultaneous foot drive and knee abduction.
*   **Specific Squat Work:** Slow empty bar squats with pauses at the bottom; gentle bouncing to increase depth.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Foam Rolling Focus:** Prioritize T-spine, glutes (lateral area), rectus femoris origin, VMO/lateral quad insertion, adductor origins, and soleus (4–6 inches above Achilles).
*   **Ankle Care:** Address tight ankles/soleus to prevent lower leg pain that limits depth.
*   **Neuromuscular Activation:** Slap quads, glutes, and lower back with fists before lifting to stimulate blood flow and proprioception.

**5. Athlete-specific relevance (102.5 kg, chronic back pain, night shift)**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The article explicitly links "pain or discomfort" to limited capacity and poor positioning; the recommended foam rolling of the T-spine and lower back slapping is critical for this athlete's safety.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The emphasis on mental preparation (quitting distractions, visualizing success) is vital for a night-shift worker who may have reduced cognitive alertness or higher fatigue levels during training windows.
*   **Back Squat as Limiter:** Since the athlete has a strong squat relative to their lifts but limited OHS depth, the specific advice on "sitting in completely" and addressing ankle/soleus tightness directly targets the barrier to increasing overhead strength.
