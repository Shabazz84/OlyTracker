# Analysis: Hang Positions Article by Greg Everett

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Hang variations by purpose**: Below knee (reinforce posture at knee), knee (balance of load & technique), mid-thigh (second pull timing), high-hang (aggression/speed work), power position (leg drive/vertical trunk), dip (jerk-mimicking countermovement)
- **Load reduction strategy**: Hang variations useful during recovery/injury periods (below knee recommended)
- **Blocks vs. hang**: Blocks better for rate of force development; hang better for proprioceptive balance practice

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Mid-thigh focus**: Ideal point to initiate second pull explosion; prevents premature pull initiation
- **Power position**: Vertical trunk (like jerk dip), knees only, no forward trunk lean; bar stays in hip crease
- **Tall snatches/cleans**: Minimal bar elevation; focus on third pull timing/mechanics under bar
- **Foot pressure**: Maintain balance at mid-foot early pull → shift to balls of feet (don't actually shift weight forward) in second pull

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- **Tall snatches** → improves third pull mechanics (directly relevant to your low OHS—weak pull-under?)
- **Dip snatches/cleans** → tech primer; leg drive work (useful given split jerk deficit)
- **3-position snatch complex** (mid-thigh → knee → floor) for sequencing

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility**
- Below-knee hangs reduce leg/back demand during reduced workload phases

## 5. **Athlete-Specific Relevance**
- **Back pain**: Mid-thigh hang positions reduce spinal load vs. floor pulls; consider emphasizing these during hypertrophy block
- **Weak OHS/pull-under**: Tall variations & dip work address third pull/receiving position aggression
- **Split jerk limitation**: Dip work reinforces proper leg drive; may help transition to split mechanics
