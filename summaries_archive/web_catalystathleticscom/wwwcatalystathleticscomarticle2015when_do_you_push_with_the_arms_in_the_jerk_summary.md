**Note:** The provided text is a specific technical article about the **timing of the arm push in the Jerk** (Strength vs. Elastic styles) and hand positioning; it does not contain programming principles, recovery advice, or mobility protocols for hypertrophy blocks. Therefore, only points directly extractable from this specific text are listed below:

1.  **Programming Principles:** None mentioned in this text.
2.  **Technique Cues (Jerk):**
    *   **Timing Rule:** Begin pushing with the arms when the bar reaches its **maximal upward speed**, not necessarily after full leg extension.
    *   **Strength Jerk Style:** For lifters who are strong but less explosive (slower transition), push later in the drive, closer to complete leg extension. Requires a deeper dip and slower transition.
    *   **Elastic Jerk Style:** For quick, snappy lifters with high vertical jump potential, push sooner in the drive (before full extension) to match earlier bar speed. Requires a shallower dip and quicker transition.
    *   **Execution:** Follow through with leg extension even after the arm push begins to continue elevating the bar.
3.  **Exercise Recommendations:** None specific beyond "experimenting with power jerks" at 70-80% intensity to test timing/dip depth.
4.  **Recovery or Mobility Advice:** None mentioned in this text (comments mention wrist/lat mobility affecting grip, but no protocols provided).
5.  **Relevance to Athlete Profile:**
    *   **Jerk Style Determination:** The athlete should self-assess as a "Strength" vs. "Elastic" jerker based on general explosiveness and vertical jump ability before selecting their timing strategy.
    *   **Hand Positioning:** Grip position (metacarpals vs. phalanges/monkey grip) is dictated primarily by **anatomical proportions** (upper/lower arm length disparity), not just mobility; do not force wrist flexibility to achieve a specific grip if anatomy dictates otherwise.
