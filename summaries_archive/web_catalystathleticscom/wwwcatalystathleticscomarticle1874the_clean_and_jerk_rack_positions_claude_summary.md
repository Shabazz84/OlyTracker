**Not addressed.** Article focuses on technique positioning, not sets/reps/intensity/periodization.

## 2. Technique Cues – Directly Relevant to Athlete

### Universal (Both Lifts)
- **Bar support**: Barbell supported by trunk directly, not arms. Arms are passive.
- **Shoulder position**: Elevated + protracted (pushed forward) to create secure channel for bar between shoulders and throat.
- **Spinal alignment**: Maintain erect, straight upper back—avoid excessive thoracic rounding despite scapular protraction. Creates rigid trunk "piston."
- **Bar placement**: Rest in shoulder channel, NOT on top of collarbones (prevents bruising, carotid artery compression risk).

### Clean Rack Position
- **Grip**: Slightly outside shoulders; full grip (loose tension) if mobility allows; finger position between bar and shoulders acceptable.
- **Elbow height**: Lift as high as possible *without* dropping shoulders—prioritize shoulder position over maximal elbow height.
- **Purpose**: Support bar securely in front squat; encourage upright posture for recovery.

### Jerk Rack Position
- **Grip width**: Often wider than clean grip; adjust hands into palms as much as possible while maintaining trunk support.
- **Grip tension**: Loose (tight grip moves bar prematurely, slows elbow extension).
- **Elbow position**: Lower and spread to sides (unlike clean)—elbows slightly in front of bar, not directly under. Improves pressing mechanics & intra-abdominal pressure.

### Hand-to-Jerk Adjustment
- Use aggressive clean recovery momentum to pop bar off shoulders, create space to adjust hands/elbows.
- Avoid wasting energy with separate leg drive post-recovery.

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
**None explicitly stated.** Comments section mentions:
- Bar on back (jerk grip): Lift elbows forward/high, push shoulders forward, maintain full grip.
- Partial squat under bar in rack: Set elbows/hands in jerk position, drive legs up while maintaining arm position (mobility work).

---

## 4. Recovery/Mobility
- **Collarbone/shoulder mobility**: Protraction + elevation prevents chronic bruising and tissue buildup.
- **Shoulder/elbow mobility**: Some lifters cannot achieve full grip due to body structure; mobility work may help if limitation is mobility, not skeletal anatomy.

---

## 5. Athlete-Specific Relevance

**Highly applicable:**
- **Back pain concern**: Emphasizes trunk rigidity, erect spinal position, and direct bar support—reduces spinal shear stress. Proper protraction + elevation stabilizes loads over hips.
- **Push jerk only (no split jerk)**: Jerk rack position guidance (lower, spread elbows, pressing mechanics) directly supports push jerk drive mechanics.
- **Weak OHS (50 kg)**: While article doesn't address snatch overhead position, clean rack shoulder/scapular position carries over to front squat stability, which builds positional strength foundation.
- **Intermediate lifter**: Article emphasizes fine-tuning via experimentation—small adjustments can yield large performance gains.

**Action items:**
1. Audit clean/jerk rack position separation (hand width, elbow height/spread).
2. Prioritize shoulder protraction + elevation to protect chronically stressed back.
3. Use mobility work if grip/elbow position limits jerk rack setup.
