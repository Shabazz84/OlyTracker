# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2025/Does-it-Matter-When-You-Jump-in-the-Snatch-or-Clean/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Focus:** Technique correction for early jumping (timing and bar path).
*   **Method:** Use partials (blocks/hangs), flat-footed pulls, and complexes to rewire timing.
*   **Intensity/Volume:** Not explicitly defined in text; implies high repetition of sub-maximal technical drills (e.g., muscle squat snatches) to feel weight distribution before jumping.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **"Stay over the bar":** Keep shoulders directly over the bar until the intended explosion point (above pubis for snatch, upper thigh for clean).
*   **Full Foot Contact:** Initiate the jump only when weight is distributed across the entire foot; avoid shifting to toes/balls of feet prematurely.
*   **"No Early Shoulder Retraction":** Do not pull shoulders back until the jump is initiated; doing so shifts weight forward and causes an early jump.
*   **Landing Plane:** Land in the same vertical plane as the start (or max 0.5 ft behind); avoid forward displacement.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Muscle Squat Snatches/Cleans:** To isolate pull mechanics without the jump; reveals true timing issues.
*   **Snatch/Clean from Blocks/Hangs:** Partial pulls to feel weight on the full foot before jumping.
*   **Flat-Footed Pulls:** Explosive extension where ankles do not rise (no toe-off); only torso and hips extend upward.
*   **Complexes:** Combine a pull-to-jump position with the full lift to diagnose early jumping under load.

*   *None explicitly mentioned in this specific text.* (Note: The text focuses strictly on kinetic timing errors).

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The "Flat-Footed Pull" and "Muscle Squat Snatch" are highly relevant as they remove the explosive ankle drive that often exacerbates lumbar shear forces in early jumpers, allowing for a more vertical bar path with less spinal torque.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The emphasis on "feeling weight distribution" via slow drills (muscle snatches) is critical for an athlete transitioning to Olympic lifts who may rely on momentum rather than technique.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** While the text focuses on Snatch/Clean, the principle of "staying over the bar until extension" applies universally; early jumping in the pull often leads to a weak, forward-leaning jerk setup.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** The recommendation for slow, deliberate drills (Muscle Squat Snatches) is beneficial for fatigue management, allowing technique work without high CNS demand compared to heavy max attempts.
