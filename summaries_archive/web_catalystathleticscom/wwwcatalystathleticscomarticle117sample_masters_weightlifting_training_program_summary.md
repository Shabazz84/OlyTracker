# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/117/Sample-Masters-Weightlifting-Training-Program/

## Key Points
*   **Cycle Structure:** A 6-week block designed for hypertrophy followed by a transition to technique/strength. Weeks 1–4 utilize linear progression (70% → 95%), Week 5 peaks intensity with reduced volume, and Week 6 serves as a taper/recovery phase.
*   **Frequency & Duration:** Training occurs 3 days per week (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri) to accommodate recovery needs, with sessions lasting approximately 90 minutes including warm-up.
*   **Load Management:** Intensity is prescribed via percentages of working max but must be dynamically adjusted based on daily "feel," particularly given the athlete's night shift schedule and chronic back pain.
*   **Injury Adaptation:** The program prioritizes substitution over forced execution; movements causing pain (e.g., Back Squat) should be replaced with safer variations (Front Squats, Power Snatch/Clean) to maintain training continuity without exacerbating injury.

## Technique Cues
*   **Movement Substitution:** Utilize overhead squat alternatives or split-jerk variations if mobility limitations prevent the standard barbell position, though the athlete's specific constraint is a push-jerk only (no split jerk).
*   **Complex Execution:** When performing complexes (e.g., Deadlift + Hang Snatch), execute one rep of the first movement immediately followed by one rep of the second for the prescribed set count.
*   **Notation Logic:** Understand "3+1" structures as three consecutive cleans followed immediately by a jerk on the same bar, ensuring technical continuity between lifts.

## Programming Principles
*   **Progressive Overload:** Implement a structured ramp-up in intensity over 4 weeks, peaking at Week 5 before tapering in Week 6 to manage fatigue from night shift work and chronic pain.
*   **Accessory Focus:** Prioritize Front Squats, Back Squats (substituted if painful), and Overhead Squats (OHS) as primary strength drivers; specifically target OHS weakness given it is the identified limiter for the snatch.
*   **Speed & Power Integration:** Include dynamic movements such as Muscle Snatch, Snatch Balance, and Push Press to develop rate of force development without excessive spinal loading.
*   **Pulling Volume:** Incorporate heavy pulling variations (Halting Deadlifts, Mid-Hang pulls) at intensities exceeding snatch/clean maxes to build raw strength capacity.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Transition Strategy:** The 6-week hypertrophy block aligns with the athlete's goal of building a base before shifting focus to Olympic technique and strength.
*   **Pain Management:** The principle of substituting painful movements (e.g., swapping Back Squat for Front Squats or Power variations) directly addresses the chronic back pain, allowing the athlete to train through discomfort safely.
*   **Schedule Adaptation:** The 3-day weekly frequency is ideal for a night shift worker (Wed–Sun), providing sufficient rest days between sessions and accommodating irregular sleep patterns by reducing overall volume compared to advanced programs.
*   **Specific Limiters:** Programming explicitly targets the Overhead Squat deficit (50 kg) through accessory work, while the push-jerk-only constraint is respected by focusing on upper body pressing strength via Push Press rather than Split Jerk variations.
