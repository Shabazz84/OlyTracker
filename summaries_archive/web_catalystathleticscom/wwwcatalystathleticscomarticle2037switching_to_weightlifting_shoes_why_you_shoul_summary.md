**2. Technique cues**
*   **Squat Posture:** Aim for a "very upright trunk" in a full-depth squat; avoid excessive forward lean or extreme lumbar arching to compensate for depth.
*   **Foot Pressure:** Expect initial increased pressure on the balls of the feet and feel "forward"; this is an adjustment phase, not a failure of technique.
*   **Muscle Recruitment:** Shift reliance from posterior chain (hips/glutes) to quadriceps during squats when wearing shoes.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Primary Recommendation:** Switch training for Snatch, Clean & Jerk, and Squat to **weightlifting shoes**.
*   **Specific Models:** Nike Romaleos II (recommended for wide feet), SABO Power Perfect 2 (inexpensive/well-built).

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Adjustment Period:** Expect a temporary decrease in strength and comfort during the transition; do not discard shoes if squats feel weaker initially.
*   **Mobility vs. Stability:** Acknowledge that while some lifters have "incredible mobility," lifting shoes provide superior stability and force transfer that flat shoes cannot replicate, even for mobile athletes.

**5. Relevance to athlete's profile**
*   **Back Pain & Mobility:** The raised heel in weightlifting shoes facilitates a more upright trunk position with less lumbar flexion/extension, which is highly relevant for an athlete with chronic back pain and limited ankle mobility (indicated by the 50kg OHS limiter).
*   **Technique Transition:** As an intermediate transitioning to Olympic lifts, the shoe's stability will help correct "suboptimal posture" (excessive forward lean) common in flat-shoe squats.
*   **Jerk Specifics:** The text notes a comment that shoes improve Clean/Snatch but *not* necessarily the Jerk; however, the author argues the stability benefits apply to all lifts, which is relevant for an athlete using only Push Jerks (where foot stability remains crucial).
*   **Foot Width:** If the athlete has wide feet (common in heavier athletes), specific shoe models like the Romaleos II are recommended over narrower options.
