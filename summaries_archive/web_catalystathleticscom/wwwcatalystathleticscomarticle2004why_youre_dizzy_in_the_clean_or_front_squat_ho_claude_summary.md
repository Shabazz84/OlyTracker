# Analysis: Dizziness in Clean/Front Squat

## Relevant to This Athlete's Profile

**HIGHLY RELEVANT** — This athlete has chronic back pain and works night shift (Wed–Sun), creating sleep deficit and recovery stress. Dizziness during front squat/clean is a legitimate concern given his limited OHS (50 kg), which suggests potential rack position issues.

---

## 1. Programming Principles
- None explicitly stated; article is troubleshooting-focused.

## 2. Technique Cues (Clean/Front Squat)
**Rack Position (CRITICAL for this athlete):**
- Protraction + slight elevation of shoulder blades to create secure bar placement
- Prevents barbell pressure on carotid arteries (not trachea)
- Poor rack position OR improper clean technique (bar crashing) → carotid compression → dizziness

**Breath Control:**
- Release small controlled amounts of air (audible hiss) during hardest phase of lift to reduce intrathoracic pressure without losing stability

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
- **Fix technical errors in clean** that cause bar crash
- Drop/bail safely if dizziness occurs
- Back squat unaffected (as in article example) — suggests rack position is the culprit, not cardiac/vagal response alone

## 4. Recovery & Mobility Advice
**Critical for night shift worker:**
- Get adequate sleep (major deficit exacerbates dizziness)
- Stay hydrated + maintain electrolyte/salt intake
- Avoid dehydration, don't overtrain
- Eat enough before training (caloric deficit worsens effect)

## 5. Athlete-Specific Relevance
- **Low OHS (50 kg)** + chronic back pain suggests weak/unstable rack position — likely **carotid compression** cause
- Night shift fatigue amplifies exhaustion/dehydration risk
- Before progressing heavy front squat/cleans, **establish bulletproof rack position** with light weight
- Prioritize this in hypertrophy block before intensity increases
