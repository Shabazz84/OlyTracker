# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2006/Bodybuilding-Accessory-Work-for-Weightlifters/

### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Frequency:** 1 day per week for each body part/movement pattern (e.g., upper pressing).
*   **Sets/Reps:** 3–5 sets of 8–15 reps; total volume 50–150 reps per movement group.
*   **Intensity:** Moderate loads (higher rep range); focus on "damage" for hypertrophy, not maximal strength.
*   **Rest:** Short rest periods (60–90 seconds) to induce metabolic stress.
*   **Timing:** Best during the early preparation phase; reduce significantly 1–2 weeks pre-competition.
*   **Priority:** Strictly accessory work; never replace core weightlifting sessions.

### 2. Technique Cues for Accessory Work
*   **Tempo:** Perform concentric (lifting) phases with **maximal speed**; slow eccentrics are acceptable but not required for all lifts.
*   **Range of Motion:** Train through a **full, complete lockout** to ensure joint stability and tendon strength; avoid partial reps that limit mobility or encourage tendinitis.
*   **Function over Form:** Prioritize exercises that reinforce weightlifting mechanics (e.g., pressing) rather than those solely for aesthetics.

### 3. Exercise Recommendations
*   **Type:** "Beach work" / Flexercise (Isolation and compound accessories).
*   **Examples:** Barbell/Dumbbell Presses, Incline Bench, Skull Crushers, Overhead Triceps Press, Rows, Curls.
*   **Variation:** Frequently vary exercises to maximize muscle damage/growth stimulus; do not stick to one lift for long periods.
*   **Legs:** Avoid dedicated bodybuilding leg days (weightlifting squats provide sufficient volume).

### 4. Recovery or Mobility Advice
*   **Mobility Risk:** Avoid limited ranges of motion in accessory work, as they can encourage tendinitis and reduce tendon strengthening benefits.
*   **Scheduling:** Do not perform high-volume pressing/pulling immediately before heavy snatch/clean sessions (e.g., avoid blasting delts/triceps the day before a tough snatch workout).
*   **Rehabilitation:** Light bodybuilding work can serve as active recovery/rehab for weak areas or injuries.

### 5. Relevance to Athlete Profile
*   **Hypertrophy Block:** The text explicitly supports using this "beach work" during the early preparation phase (your upcoming 6-week block) to gain weight via increased food intake rather than fat ("chub").
*   **Back Pain/Mobility:** Emphasizes full ROM and tendon strengthening, which is critical for an athlete with chronic back pain; however, caution is needed not to overload the lower back with heavy accessory leg work.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The short rest periods (60–90s) and high volume require good recovery capacity; given the night shift schedule, ensure these sessions do not compound fatigue before key weightlifting days.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** Since the athlete uses a push jerk, accessory pressing work (Overhead Triceps Press, Bench) is highly relevant to strengthen the lockout and triceps without requiring split mechanics.
*   **Snatch Limiter (OHS):** While OHS is a primary limiter, this text advises against heavy overhead lifting in accessory work if it conflicts with technique; focus on high-rep, lower-weight pressing or tricep isolation to build volume without compromising the snatch pull.
