# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2051/Ask-Greg-Correcting-Tipping-Over-the-Bar-in-the-Snatch-Clea/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Volume Strategy:** Prioritize high volume at sub-maximal weights to reinforce correct posture; heavy squats must be minimized in frequency if they force a breakdown in form (keep "suboptimal" squats to the smallest fraction of total volume).
*   **Tempo/Isometrics:** Incorporate 3-second pauses (1 inch off floor, at knee) and slow eccentrics (3–6 seconds lowering) to build strength in specific joint angles.
*   **Position Specificity:** Strength gains are angle-specific; training the "upright pull" position is required to transfer to the snatch/clean.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Primary Cue:** Maintain an upright posture (chest up, hips in) during the first pull rather than allowing hips to rise faster than the chest.
*   **Mechanism Correction:** Fight the natural tendency of knees opening too quickly; keep knee angles more closed initially to force leg strength engagement over back leverage.
*   **Self-Checks:** Use pauses at 1 inch off the floor and at the knee to verify posture before extending.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Squats:** Front or Back Squats performed with strict upright torso positioning (chest up, hips in).
*   **Pull Variations:** Snatch/Clean pulls on a riser; Segment deadlifts; Slow-pull snatch/clean.
*   **Complexes:** Segment pull + Snatch/Clean combinations to practice position first, then apply to the full lift.

*   *Note: The text does not explicitly address recovery schedules, sleep, or mobility drills.* It focuses on injury prevention by correcting mechanics (preventing lumbar stress caused by weak legs).

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** Directly addresses the root cause of back pain in this profile: "Weak legs leads to leaning too far over the bar... putting a large amount of stress on the lumbar area." The text argues that strengthening legs in an upright position is the cure for the back acting as a lever.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** Reinforces that "hobby and recreation lifters" cannot rely on raw back strength to compensate for poor technique; leg strength must be built to support Olympic lifting mechanics.
*   **OHS Limiter:** The focus on strengthening legs in the specific pulling position addresses the underlying weakness likely limiting overhead stability and pull efficiency.
