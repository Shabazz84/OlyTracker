# Analysis: Bondarenko on Programming Mistakes

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Frequency**: 3 days/week optimal for most non-professional lifters (recovery + motor learning balance)
- **Intensity**: Work at moderate weights to build technique/strength; avoid maxes daily
- **Periodization**: Don't blindly copy elite methods; understand *why* exercises are selected
- **Avoid**: Training too seldom or too often without purpose

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- High pulls: avoid early—master fast extension, toe rise, shrug timing first
- Keep elbows straight initially (1st year); arm pulling = technical error

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
**DO:**
- Place classical lifts early in session (legs fresh for speed/technique)
- Snatch on separate day from heavy squats
- Heavy squats on snatch day (before 2-day rest) = good pairing
- Alternate snatch/C&J auxiliary work; don't mix both in one session
- Include GPP (box jumps, pull-ups), flexibility, abs work—not just max lifts

**AVOID:**
- Classical lifts after pulls/squats (fatigued legs)
- Heavy snatch day after heavy squat day
- Pull variations from same position as classical lift that day
- Heavy back squats on heavy clean day

## 4. **Recovery**
- Recovery time often *more important* than gym time
- Respect body feedback; distinguish "good days" vs. "bad days"

## 5. **Relevant to This Athlete**
- **OHS limiter (50 kg)**: Mobility/flexibility work essential—aligns with auxiliary emphasis
- **Night shift + chronic back pain**: 3x/week frequency safer; GPP/mobility critical
- **Hypertrophy block ahead**: Ensure snatch/clean aren't preceded by taxing squats
- **Push jerk only**: Will need split jerk work eventually—don't neglect pull position variations

---
**Key takeaway**: Systematic exercise pairing > volume; respect fatigue management around night shift work.
