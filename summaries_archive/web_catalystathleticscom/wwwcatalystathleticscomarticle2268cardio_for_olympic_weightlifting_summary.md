# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2268/Cardio-for-Olympic-Weightlifting/

1. **Programming Principles**: Do not dedicate time to traditional cardio (running/rowing) for weightlifting performance; it creates contradictory muscular adaptations and limits progress. If cardiovascular conditioning is required for general health, limit total training volume by reducing sport-specific work. Use high-intensity interval circuits (20–60s per activity) mixing different muscle groups to elevate heart rate without local muscular fatigue.

2. **Technique Cues**: None provided in this text.

3. **Exercise Recommendations**: For cardio: Circuit-based mixing of activities (e.g., bike, rower, treadmill) or bodybuilding accessories performed with little rest. Avoid monostructural leg-dominant endurance work.

4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**: Use nutrition, not cardio, for weight loss/cutting to preserve energy and restoration capacity. Walking is acceptable but offers minimal conditioning unless at extreme intensity (which risks interference).

5. **Athlete Profile Relevance**:
   - **Chronic Back Pain/Transitioning**: The text advises against high-volume leg-dominant cardio that could exacerbate fatigue or injury risk; suggests using accessory work in circuits to minimize specific muscle stress.
   - **Night Shift Worker**: No specific scheduling advice, but the emphasis on "finite energy resources" supports prioritizing recovery over added conditioning load.
   - **Goal (Hypertrophy/Technique)**: The text explicitly warns against using cardio for cutting or weight management; recommends achieving body composition goals through nutrition to avoid interfering with strength and technique development.
