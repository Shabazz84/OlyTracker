# Analysis: Heaving Snatch Balance Article

## 1. Programming Principles
- **Sets/reps**: 2-5 reps per set
- **Intensity**: Not heavy; prioritize smooth movement over load
- **Placement**: Auxiliary exercise near/at end of snatch workout, OR lighter at beginning as warm-up/positioning reinforcement
- **Frequency**: Suitable as regular assistance work

## 2. Technique Cues
- **Feet connection**: Keep feet flat on platform throughout—forces proper receiving position and prevents over-reliance on foot movement during turnover
- **Elbows**: Quick, active punching under the bar
- **Balance & posture**: Perfect positioning emphasized over load
- **Application for this athlete**: Addresses foot placement inconsistencies; builds confidence receiving weight overhead

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
**Heaving Snatch Balance (HSB)** — Hybrid between snatch balance + overhead squat:
- Feet in squat stance, snatch grip on back
- Smooth knee dip + heave the bar
- Punch down under into overhead squat
- Feet remain flat (no foot transition)

**Benefits for this athlete (OHS limiter at 50 kg, chronic back pain)**:
- Builds overhead strength/stability without wrist strain from rep OHS
- Active stretching of hips/shoulders without slack
- Reinforces proper receiving position (critical for weak snatch)

## 4. Recovery/Mobility
- Acts as active stretching for hip and shoulder flexibility
- Reduces wrist fatigue vs. traditional OHS volume

## 5. Athlete-Specific Relevance
**Highly applicable**: OHS is your primary snatch limiter (50 kg vs. 80 kg clean). HSB bypasses wrist limitations while building exact positions needed for snatch reception. Ideal as warm-up or finish-of-workout auxiliary during your 6-week hypertrophy block.
