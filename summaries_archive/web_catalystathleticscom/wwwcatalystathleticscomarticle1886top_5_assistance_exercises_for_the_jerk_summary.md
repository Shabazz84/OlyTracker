# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1886/Top-5-Assistance-Exercises-for-the-Jerk/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** Jerk assistance should be trained on a dedicated "second jerk training day" (lighter technical focus) or integrated into complexes.
*   **Intensity/Volume:** Use the Power Jerk/Push Jerk for heavier loads than standard jerks to build strength; use Dip Squats and Recovery exercises at controlled speeds with sub-maximal weights to emphasize position over load.
*   **Structure:** Utilize complexes (e.g., Push Press + Split Jerk or Power Jerk + Split Jerk) to train the transition from dip/drive to overhead lockout simultaneously.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Dip & Drive:** Maintain a "balanced, vertical" drive; avoid hips moving back or weight shifting forward (common technical failure).
*   **Bar Path:** The bar must move *back* behind the neck as the trunk inclines slightly forward to achieve proper overhead positioning.
*   **Heel Pressure:** Keep constant pressure on the heels during the dip squat to prevent forward shifting; maintain quad tension throughout.
*   **Recovery:** Focus on "holding" and controlling heavy weights overhead (Jerk Support) and maintaining stability in the split position before standing up (Jerk Recovery).

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Push Press:** Primary multidimensional lift for timing, postural strength, and rate of force development.
*   **Power Jerk / Push Jerk:** Essential for training vertical dip/drive mechanics and overhead flexibility; preferred over split jerk for technical precision.
*   **Jerk Dip Squat:** Controlled speed exercise to fix improper dip balance and strengthen quads in the specific dip position.
*   **Push Jerk Behind the Neck (Split):** Positional strength drill to practice deep split stability while maintaining overhead bar placement.
*   **Jerk Support / Recovery:** Maximize overhead position strength and confidence with weights exceeding best jerk attempts; strengthens split recovery mechanics.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Overhead Flexibility:** The Power Jerk demands greater flexibility than the Split Jerk; lifters must address shoulder/thoracic mobility to perform it correctly without compensation.
*   **Controlled Tempo:** Use slow, controlled speeds in Dip Squats and Recovery exercises to build proprioception and stability rather than explosive speed.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, 102.5kg, Push Jerk Only, Back Pain)**
*   **Push Jerk Focus:** The text explicitly validates the athlete's current style ("Push Jerk... feet remain connected"), noting it is ideal for lifters who struggle with cutting the drive short or misplacing feet.
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** The "Jerk Dip Squat" emphasizes keeping pressure on heels and quad tension to prevent forward shifting, which can reduce shear force on the lumbar spine compared to a poor dip.
*   **Strength vs. Technique:** Since the athlete is transitioning from intermediate strength, the text suggests using Power Jerks and Push Presses as "strength exercises" (heavier loads) while keeping technical focus on vertical drive balance.
*   **Note on Split Jerk:** The article heavily advocates for the Split Jerk; however, it notes that if an athlete struggles with foot placement or cutting the drive short, the Push Jerk is a superior alternative to fix those specific mechanical flaws without the complexity of splitting.
