# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/33/Kyphosis-Shoulder-Mobility-Practical-Correction/

**Note:** This text is a general article on shoulder mobility and kyphosis correction within the CrossFit/Olympic lifting community; it does **not** contain specific programming for an intermediate lifter with back pain or shift work constraints. However, relevant principles can be extracted as follows:

### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Frequency:** Dedicated mobility work (shoulder dislocates, OHS, glide kips) should be performed **before every training session**.
*   **Sets/Reps:** 3–5 sets of 10 repetitions for corrective exercises.
*   **Intensity:** Low intensity for mobility drills ("do not explode," "go easy"). Strength work follows a "Starting Strength" style progression (low reps, linear load increase) before metabolic conditioning.
*   **Periodization:** Corrective mobility is treated as a prerequisite; once an athlete achieves specific benchmarks (OHS 5–10 reps @ BW, L-sit pull-ups, kipped pull-ups), the dedicated corrective work can be reduced to maintenance within general S&C.

### 2. Technique Cues
*   **Shoulder Dislocates:** "Shrug traps HARD" during both forward and backward phases; maintain a firm grip on the dowel; do not explode through the movement.
*   **Overhead Squat (OHS):** Use the narrowest grip possible that allows an upright torso; keep the bar over the ear/heel; feet between hip and shoulder width; chin neutral or slightly down/forward to activate shoulders; drive the bar up actively.
*   **"Glide" Kip:** "Push away, raise the toes! Pull under the bar!" (Simultaneously drive bar away with hands while elevating toes into a hollow position).

### 3. Exercise Recommendations
*   **Corrective Circuit:** Shoulder dislocates, Overhead Squats (OHS), and Glide Kips (or "Beat-swing" pull-ups).
*   **Strength Base:** Squats, Deadlifts, Presses, Weighted Pull-ups (Starting Strength style progression).
*   **Scapular Retractor Developer:** L-Sit Pull-ups.

### 4. Recovery or Mobility Advice
*   **Discomfort is Expected:** OHS mobility drills should be "uncomfortable" and require focus/patience; this indicates the correct stretch is being applied to tight structures.
*   **Long-term Approach:** View mobility work as a "long-term relationship," not a quick fix. Consistency is key for structural change.
*   **Load Management:** For athletes with kyphosis, avoid overloading deadlifts or overhead movements until mobility improves; focus on upper back strength and active mobility simultaneously.

### 5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)
*   **Kyphosis & Back Pain:** The athlete's chronic back pain may be exacerbated by the "rounding forward" (kyphosis) described in the text. The article suggests that tight shoulders and poor thoracic extension are primary drivers of this posture.
*   **OHS Limiter:** The athlete's 50kg OHS limit is directly addressed; the text identifies shoulder mobility as a primary barrier to overhead squat depth and stability, recommending narrow-grip OHS drills to force thoracic extension.
*   **Night Shift/Posture:** The article notes that sedentary behaviors (sitting in chairs) cause "postural failure" and kyphosis. For a night shift worker with likely prolonged sitting hours, the recommendation for **pre-session mobility** is critical to counteract daily postural degradation.
*   **Caution on Heavy Loads:** One commenter explicitly advises *not* overloading deadlifts or overhead pressing for individuals with kyphosis until ROM improves, which aligns with managing this athlete's chronic back pain during the hypertrophy block.
