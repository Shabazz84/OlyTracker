# Analysis: "Squat Cycles and Other Crap That Doesn't Work" by Matt Foreman

## Relevant to This Athlete

**1. Programming Principles**
- Squat strength ≠ automatic C&J/Snatch improvement; transfer depends on **squat technique matching Olympic lift receiving positions**
- Dedicated squat cycles (Russian, Smolov) increase squat numbers but don't guarantee classic lift carryover
- Skill/technique development is the primary driver for competitive results; squat is important but "still an assistance exercise"

**2. Technique Cues**
- **Critical for this athlete:** If squat bottom position mirrors snatch/clean catch position, transfer is high. If powerlifting-style (wide stance, sit-back), transfer is low
- Improper catch mechanics (e.g., rounding back, energy waste) nullify leg strength

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
- Avoid treating squat cycles as primary problem-solver
- Pair squat strength gains with dedicated technical work on SN and C&J
- Pull strength mentioned as equally/more important than squat for Olympic transfer

**4. Recovery/Mobility**
- Not addressed in article

## Key Takeaway for This Athlete
With 118 kg back squat but only 50 kg OHS, your **snatch limiter is technique/mobility in the receiving position, not leg strength**. Foreman's point is direct: don't expect a 6-week hypertrophy squat block alone to fix your OHS. Pair it with aggressive positional/mobility work (especially overhead stability given chronic back pain).
