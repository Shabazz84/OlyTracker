# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1834/When-Do-You-Reach-Technical-Mastery-in-Weightlifting/

### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Stages of Mastery:** The text outlines four stages: Remediation (mobility/strength), Mime (light resistance/motor patterns), Proficient (80%+ intensity with sound technique), and Technical Mastery (years of high-volume work at 80%+).
*   **Intensity Threshold:** Technique is only considered "mastered" if it remains sound at loads of **80% of maximum** or heavier.
*   **Frequency/Volume:** The "Technical Mastery" stage requires thousands of lifts over several years, implying high frequency and volume for advanced athletes.

### 2. Technique Cues
*   **"Mime" the Lift:** Practice perfect motor patterns with a dowel or empty bar before adding significant resistance.
*   **Dynamic Rhythm & Timing:** Focus on developing rhythm during the transition from light to moderate loads.
*   **Arm Positioning (Snatch/Clean):** Arms should act as "ropes" (referenced in related article titles within the text).
*   **Adjustments:** Learn to make minute adjustments *during* the pull/jerk or after the bar is fixed if slightly out of position.

### 3. Exercise Recommendations
*   **Remediation Phase:** Static stretching (when warm), specific local exercises for weak areas, jumping, two-handed overhead tossing, sprinting, and ballistic push-ups.
*   **Mime Phase:** Dowel work or empty barbell drills to isolate motor patterns without heavy load.

### 4. Recovery or Mobility Advice
*   **Targeted Mobility:** Focus on shoulders, hips, and ankles as common limitations.
*   **Stretching Protocol:** Perform static stretching only when joints are warm.
*   **Strength Balance:** Strengthen disproportionately weak areas (shoulders, lower back, legs) to create a "balanced strength anatomy."

### 5. Relevance to Athlete Profile
*   **Current Stage Identification:** The athlete is likely in the transition between **Remediation** and **Mime**. With chronic back pain and limited overhead mobility (OHS 50kg), they must prioritize the Remediation stage before attempting heavy loads.
*   **Back Pain Management:** The text explicitly identifies "lower back" as a common weakness requiring specific training to attain sound technique positions; this is critical for an athlete with chronic back pain.
*   **Night Shift Work:** While not explicitly mentioned, the emphasis on "years of preparation" and the need for a coach to spot flaws suggests that consistency (often disrupted by shift work) requires strict adherence to the Remediation/Mime phases before pushing intensity.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** The text emphasizes that technique must be efficient enough to lift 80%+; if the athlete cannot jerk heavy weights due to a lack of split or poor overhead mechanics, they are not yet in the "Proficient" stage and should not increase load significantly.
