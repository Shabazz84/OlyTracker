# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2058/How-to-Fix-Powering-More-Than-You-Squat/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Eliminate Power Variations:** Stop all power snatches and power cleans immediately; perform only full squat variations (snatch/clean).
*   **Punitive Squatting:** If an athlete accidentally powers a lift, they must sit into a full squat immediately after standing up or add a front squat to that set.
*   **Isometric Holds:** Hold the bottom position of all snatches for 3–5 seconds to build stability and confidence.
*   **Supersets:** Add a front squat directly after any clean or clean & jerk set (e.g., Clean + Front Squat).
*   **Progression:** Build overhead squat strength until it exceeds the best power snatch; build snatch balance until it exceeds the best snatch.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **"Quit Powering":** The primary cue is to force the athlete to receive in a deep squat rather than standing up with the bar.
*   **Aggressive Extension & Turnover:** Use high-hang or high-block variations of full lifts to maintain aggressive extension and quick turnover without allowing the athlete to stand up for the catch.
*   **Upright Trunk Stability (Clean):** Maintain an upright trunk in the bottom position to withstand the "pounding" of the bar; fear of this position often causes powering.
*   **Overhead Stability (Snatch):** Focus on holding the bar still and getting it into the right place rather than just raw strength overhead.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Full Variations Only:** Snatch and Clean (full squat catch).
*   **High-Hang/High Block:** Full snatch/clean variations starting from a high hang or block to force receiving under the bar without standing up.
*   **Snatch Balance:** To develop overhead stability and confidence exceeding power snatch numbers.
*   **Pulls & Deadlifts (Riser):** Pulls off a riser to strengthen the pull if weakness is causing fear of pulling under.
*   **Tall Snatch/Clean:** From power position or high hang, to accumulate exposure to the "pull under" movement safely.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Daily Stretching:** "Stretch your face off every day"; prioritize consistent mobility work over shortcuts.
*   **Smart Exposure:** Increase time spent in deep squat receiving positions gradually to improve mobility without injury.
*   **Overhead Squat Depth:** Must be able to sit into a deep, stable overhead squat and hold it for 3 seconds before increasing load.

**5. Athlete-Specific Relevance (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Management:** The text explicitly warns against "searching for a mobility shortcut that doesn't exist" which could exacerbate chronic back pain; the solution is consistent daily stretching and building stability in the squat position rather than forcing depth prematurely.
*   **Night Shift Fatigue:** The recommendation to hold lifts for 3–5 seconds or add front squats increases volume/intensity; given the Wed–Sun night shift schedule, this athlete must ensure these additional sets do not compromise sleep hygiene or recovery on off-days (Mon/Tue).
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** Since the athlete only uses a push jerk, the text's emphasis on "overhead stability" and "holding overhead for 3 seconds" is critical to compensate for the lack of split mechanics which often provide more dynamic stability.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** The article directly addresses OHS as a primary limiter; the athlete must prioritize building an OHS that exceeds their current power snatch capability before attempting to increase the Clean or Snatch.
