# Analysis: Weightlifting Training Frequency & Volume

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Volume range:** Individual—some athletes thrive on 400–500 reps/week; others on ≤200 reps/week (not age-dependent)
- **Frequency:** 3–4 days on, 1 day off is standard but not mandatory; can be scaled based on adaptation
- **Intensity/Volume modulation:** Day-to-day fluctuation essential—not every session maximal
- **Periodization approach:** Block-based cumulative effect; complete recovery between sessions *not* required
- **Progression:** Gradual adaptation over years; don't jump into high frequency suddenly

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- Snatch, clean & jerk less systemically taxing than squat/deadlift (smaller % of true maximal effort)
- Olympic lifts train primarily *neurological* adaptations (not morphological), allowing higher frequency without tissue remodeling delays
- Minimal eccentric loading (comment in thread) supports higher frequency tolerance

---

## 4. **Recovery & Mobility Advice**
- Cortisol concerns: stress is multifactorial (training, work, sleep, life stress)—isolating training as the cause is difficult
- Drug-free athletes *can* adapt to high volume/frequency, but slower and to lower absolute levels than enhanced athletes
- Adaptation is learned: years of progressive buildup required (elite lifters don't start at 6 days/600 reps)
- Psychological conditioning matters—past training history shapes tolerance
