# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1827/When-in-Doubt-Simplify-Olympic-Weightlifting-Program-Design/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Structure:** Alternate high/low volume days (e.g., 80 reps Mon/Wed/Sat vs. 40–60 reps Tue/Thu) and match intensity to volume (high intensity on heavy days, lower relative intensity/accessory focus on light days).
*   **Exercise Hierarchy:** Prioritize Snatch & Clean & Jerk > Front/Back Squats > Pulls > Power Variants. Treat pulls/power lifts as optional based on needs; avoid "fancy" accessories before mastering the big three.
*   **Volume Counting:** Only count reps at 70%+ for main lifts (squats, pulls). Use judgment for accessory work (e.g., empty bar Sots presses do not count toward daily volume; heavy SLDLs do).
*   **Prescription Method:** Do not rely on fixed percentages initially. Prescribe weights based on the intended "look and feel" of the lift, then adjust upward over a 3-week block once a baseline is established.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary:** Snatch, Clean & Jerk.
*   **Secondary:** Front Squat, Back Squat.
*   **Tertiary (Optional):** Snatch Pulls, Clean Pulls.
*   **Variants:** Power Snatch, Power Clean, Power Jerk (recommended for lower intensity/volume days to reduce leg fatigue).
*   **Overhead Work:** Suggested on lighter days (e.g., jerk variations) to shift load away from legs.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The advice to "start with priority exercises and build around them" is critical for an intermediate athlete overwhelmed by conflicting online information; it prevents over-complicating the program with unnecessary accessories.
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The recommendation to use **Power Variants** on lower intensity days or focus on **overhead work (jerk variations)** on light days can help reduce spinal loading and leg fatigue, potentially aiding pain management during volume training.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** While the text does not address circadian rhythms, the principle of "alternating high/low" allows for flexible scheduling to accommodate shift changes without rigid daily requirements.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** The text supports using specific variations (like jerk variations) on lighter days to manage overall systemic fatigue, which aligns with a push-jerk focused approach if it reduces spinal stress compared to split jerks.
