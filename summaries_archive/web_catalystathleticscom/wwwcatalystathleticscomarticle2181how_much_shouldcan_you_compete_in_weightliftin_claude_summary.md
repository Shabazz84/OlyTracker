# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2181/How-Much-ShouldCan-You-Compete-in-Weightlifting/

# Analysis: Competition Frequency Article

## Relevance to Athlete Profile
**Moderately relevant.** This article addresses competition strategy, not the athlete's immediate needs (hypertrophy block, technique development, back pain management).

---

## Key Extracts

### 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Beginner/Intermediate lifters:** Can compete frequently (limited by technique/mobility, not strength); competition has "negligible physiological effect"
- **Advanced lifters:** Require 12–24 week training cycles; limit meaningful competitions to 2–3/year with 1–3 "tune-up meets" between
- **Tune-up meets:** Train *through* them (no taper/program change); treat as heavy training days

### 2. **Competition Mindset (Relevant to Back Pain/Consistency)**
- Early stage goal: **6/6 success rate** (build confidence & consistency over PRs)
- Weights should be considerably below maximum capacity
- Reduces psychological and physical stress

### 3. **For This Athlete**
- **Status:** Intermediate transitioning; lifts limited by technique (OHS 50 kg vs BS 118 kg) and mobility
- **Implication:** Could handle frequent "tune-up" meets without disrupting 6-week hypertrophy block + subsequent 12-week strength phase
- **Caution:** Back pain may contradict high meet frequency if tapering/maximal attempts occur

### 4. **Not Addressed**
- Recovery/mobility for chronic back pain
- Technique cues
- Exercise selection
