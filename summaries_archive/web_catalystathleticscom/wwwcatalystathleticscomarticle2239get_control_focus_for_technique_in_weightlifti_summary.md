# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2239/Get-Control-Focus-for-Technique-in-Weightlifting/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Focus:** Limit technical focus to a maximum of 3 distinct cues per rep: one for the start (pre-lift), one during the pull/movement, and one at the finish/overhead hold.
*   **Prioritization:** Ruthlessly prioritize the "first and worst" error; resolving early errors often fixes later ones automatically. Prioritize earlier faults over later ones.
*   **Cue Reduction:** Reduce cues to single words or streamlined phrases (e.g., "back," "whole foot," "tight") rather than descriptive instructions.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Example Set:** "Back" (arch setup), "Whole foot" (pushing through the entire foot), "Tight" (holding overhead position).
*   **Mantra Strategy:** If overwhelmed, select one critical cue and repeat it like a mantra to reduce distraction and self-doubt while maintaining technical focus.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   *None specific.* The text focuses on mental programming rather than physical exercise selection or loading schemes.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Visualization:** Use rest periods before sets for visualization (engaging sight, sound, smell, and feel of the lift) to de-stimulate the brain and improve focus. *Note: Avoid overthinking during this process.*
*   **Mental Reset:** Use mantras to block out negativity ("how terrible I am") and self-doubt.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Pain & Night Shifts:** The "Mantra" technique is highly relevant for an athlete with chronic pain and a disrupted sleep schedule (Wed–Sun) to manage anxiety, reduce mental fatigue, and prevent the mind from "spinning out of control" during lifts.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The rule of "3 cues max" prevents cognitive overload common when transitioning to Olympic lifting, where technical complexity is high.
*   **Limiters:** While not addressing the OHS or back pain directly physically, the mental discipline helps isolate and fix specific mechanical errors (like arch setup) that may be exacerbating back issues due to poor technique under load.
