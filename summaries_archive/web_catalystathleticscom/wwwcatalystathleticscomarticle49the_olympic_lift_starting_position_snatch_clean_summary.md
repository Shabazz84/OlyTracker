# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/49/The-Olympic-Lift-Starting-Position-Snatch-Clean/

## Key Points
*   **Starting Position Philosophy:** The primary goal of the start is to optimize the second pull; do not force a "vertical bar path" at the expense of optimal body positioning. Hip height is a byproduct of limb length and bar placement, not a preset target.
*   **Upright vs. High-Hip Debate:** An upright first pull (vertical torso/arms) is biomechanically superior for most lifters to minimize lumbar torque. Departing from this position at heavy loads indicates a strength deficit (specifically quad weakness), not a technical impossibility.
*   **Bar & Body Mechanics:** The bar must start over the balls of the feet. Shoulders should be slightly in front of the bar initially, moving closer as knees extend. Knees flare outward to shorten leg length and allow a more upright torso.
*   **First Pull Execution:** Initiate by shifting weight slightly backward as the bar leaves the floor to create a natural slight rearward curve without active arm pushing. Active "pushing" of the bar back toward the body occurs only after passing the knees.

## Technique Cues
*   **Foot Placement:** Barbell over the balls of the feet (farthest forward point while maintaining vertical arms).
*   **Arm Angle:** Arms must be perfectly vertical at the start; shoulders slightly in front of the bar to accommodate shoulder mass.
*   **Torso Posture:** Maintain an upright posture to preserve spinal rigidity and minimize lumbar torque during the first pull.
*   **Knee Action:** Flare knees out (light contact with inner arms) to clear the path and facilitate a vertical torso.
*   **Transition Cue:** "Let hips rise faster than shoulders" naturally; avoid sweeping the bar back aggressively immediately off the floor. Only actively push the bar back once past the knee line.

## Programming Principles
*   **Strength-Driven Technique:** If an athlete cannot maintain an upright posture under load, prescribe strength work to shore up weak spots (specifically quadriceps) rather than adopting a high-hipped start as a compensation.
*   **Drill Recommendations:**
    *   **Segment Pulls/Pause Deadlifts:** Perform pauses 1 inch off the floor, at the knee, and mid-thigh to reinforce upright posture and strength in specific positions.
    *   **Pause Back Squats:** Prioritize "nice and upright" squats with pauses to build leg strength required for maintaining pulling posture under load.
    *   **Halted Deadlifts:** Use these to address early arm bend and strengthen the ability to keep shoulders over the bar at mid-thigh levels.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The advocate for an upright starting position is highly relevant, as it minimizes hip/lumbar torque and spinal extensor fatigue compared to extreme high-hip starts.
*   **Strength Limitations (80kg Clean):** The text suggests that the athlete's current inability to pull upright at heavy loads is likely a quad strength deficit rather than a technique flaw. Building specific quad strength in an upright position is required before transitioning to more aggressive pulls.
*   **Transition Phase:** For an intermediate lifter, focusing on "shoring up" the upright start prevents falling into bad habits (high-hipped starts) prematurely as load increases.
*   **Limitations of Source:** The provided text does not address the athlete's specific 6-week hypertrophy block, night shift recovery needs, or split jerk mechanics (as the source focuses on snatch/clean first pulls).
