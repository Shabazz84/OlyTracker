# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2182/How-to-Quit-Hitting-Your-Pubic-Bone-When-You-Snatch/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Incorporate specific accessory work to reinforce the "stay over the bar" position: halting snatch deadlifts, snatch segment pulls with pauses at mid-thigh, and slow-pull snatches.
*   **Intensity:** Focus on technique refinement (staying over the bar longer) rather than maximal loading during these specific drills.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Grip Width Test:** Stand holding the bar at arms' length; lift one knee to create a hinge point. Adjust grip width until the bar contacts exactly in that hip crease (soft tissue between pubic crest and anterior superior iliac spines).
*   **Timing Cue:** "Stay over the bar longer." Do not initiate the second pull (hip extension) too early, as this causes the bar to contact the body prematurely.
*   **Shoulder Position:** Keep shoulder blades locked in, back, and tight; do not necessarily shrug unless compensating for unusual proportions (short trunk/long arms).

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   Halting snatch deadlifts.
*   Snatch segment pulls with pauses at mid-thigh.
*   Slow-pull snatches.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   *None explicitly mentioned in this text.* (The article focuses strictly on technique mechanics to prevent acute impact pain).

**5. Athlete-specific relevance**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The cue to "stay over the bar longer" and delay hip extension is critical for athletes with back issues, as it reduces shear force and premature lumbar flexion often caused by rushing the second pull.
*   **Push Jerk Only (No Split):** While this article focuses on the Snatch, the principle of delaying the second pull applies universally to the Clean & Jerk. For a push-jerk-only athlete, mastering the "stay over the bar" timing in the clean ensures better vertical drive and less forward lean before the jerk, which is vital for stability without a split stance.
*   **Night Shift Work:** The text does not address sleep or circadian rhythm management; however, the high focus on technical drills (slow pulls/pauses) requires mental freshness, which may be challenging given the Wed–Sun night shift schedule.
