# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1737/10-More-Things-New-Weightlifters-Should-Know/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Periodization:** Implement "hit it hard for 2-3 weeks, then back off" cycles to prevent burnout (Item 13).
*   **Intensity/Volume Rule:** Use the "80-80 rule": perform working sets at roughly 80% of your intensity and volume if programming independently.
*   **Daily Readiness:** Adjust daily based on feeling: full lifts if great, power versions if slightly off, pulls if "busted up" (Item 3).
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Avoid overtraining; "more isn't always better" (Item 1).

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Mental Cues:** Maintain a logbook to track specific mental cues that work for you (e.g., "Chest Up," "Pull & Punch") and review them regularly (Item 12).
*   **Power Position:** Use block work (below knee, above knee, mid-thigh) to help new lifters identify the power position (Item 8).

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Variations:** Utilize power versions of lifts if full lifts feel difficult; use pulls if feeling significantly fatigued or injured (Item 3).
*   **Block Work:** Incorporate block pulls/snatches/cleans at various heights to develop the power position (Item 8).

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Nutrition Timing:** Have food ready immediately after training; consume it before distractions delay intake (Item 9).
*   **Gear Care:** Hang knee sleeves to dry rather than using a dryer to maintain integrity and prevent odor (Item 10).

**5. Specifically relevant to this athlete's profile**
*   **Chronic Pain Management:** The text explicitly advises switching to power versions or pulls if the athlete feels "busted up" or has pain, which is critical for managing chronic back issues without stopping training entirely (Item 3).
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** While not explicit about shift work, the advice to "Plan your breaks, before they plan you" and the strict 80-80 rule are vital for a night-shift worker who may struggle with sleep consistency; structured volume prevents overtraining when recovery is compromised (Item 13).
*   **Logbook Utility:** For an intermediate athlete transitioning to Olympic lifting, keeping a log of cues and PRs is essential to track the shift from general strength to technical proficiency (Item 12).
*   **Bar Variability:** The advice to switch bars/platforms helps simulate meet conditions where equipment varies, which is crucial for a lifter with back pain who needs to ensure their technique holds up on different surfaces (Item 6).

*(Note: The text does not address the specific "push jerk only" limitation or the 102.5 kg bodyweight constraint directly beyond general weight class advice.)*
