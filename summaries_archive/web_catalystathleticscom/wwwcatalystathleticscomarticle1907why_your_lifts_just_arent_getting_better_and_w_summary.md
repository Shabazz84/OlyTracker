# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1907/Why-Your-Lifts-Just-Arent-Getting-Better-And-What-to-Do-Abou/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Periodization:** Re-evaluate current programming; return to "basics" (Snatch, Clean & Jerk, Squat, Pull) rather than seeking complex new methods.
*   **Intensity/Motivation:** Avoid complacency ("big fish little pond"); train with or against athletes stronger/better than you to force higher effort.
*   **Volume/Load:** If stuck on the clean recovery, increase Front Squat volume; if weak off the floor, incorporate Pull variations.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   *Not explicitly detailed in this text.* The article focuses on program design and mindset rather than specific technical cues (e.g., "elbows high," "knees out"). It advises finding a coach to identify bad habits.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary:** Snatch, Clean & Jerk, Back Squat, Pulls.
*   **Accessory:** Front Squats (specifically for clean recovery issues).
*   **Equipment:** Ensure proper footwear; upgrading shoes is suggested as a potential fix if other variables fail.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Sleep & Nutrition:** Critical at this stage of development; "little sleep" and poor diet now result in failed lifts, whereas they didn't for beginners.
*   **Warm-up:** Must be done properly to align the stars for a PR.
*   **Injury Management:** Address nagging injuries immediately via evaluation/treatment rather than training through pain.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, 102.5kg, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Hypertrophy:** Supports the goal of a hypertrophy block; explicitly states "Time to get bigger" is necessary for strength gains, especially for taller athletes or those needing more mass to lift heavier totals.
*   **Injury Management:** Directly addresses the athlete's chronic back pain under point #9 ("Nagging Injuries"); advises getting it evaluated rather than ignoring it, as it may be the sole barrier to progress.
*   **Mindset/Environment:** Suggests training with knowledgeable coaches or peers to correct bad habits (relevant for an intermediate lifter who may have developed compensations).
*   **Night Shift Context:** The article emphasizes sleep and recovery ("Getting enough sleep... is more important than ever"), which is a critical variable for a night shift worker where circadian rhythm disruption can hinder recovery.
