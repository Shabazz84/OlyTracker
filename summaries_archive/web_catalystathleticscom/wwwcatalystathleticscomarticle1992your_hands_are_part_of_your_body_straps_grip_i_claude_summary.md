# Analysis: "Your Hands Are Part of Your Body: Straps & Grip in Weightlifting"

## Relevant to This Athlete's Profile

**Exercise Recommendations:**
- **USE STRAPS for:** Snatch/Clean pulls, deadlift variations, RDLs/SLDLs
- **AVOID STRAPS for:** Snatches, hang snatches, snatch complexes (prioritize grip development)
- Grip security directly impacts movement quality—insecure grip will slow pulls and alter positioning

**Programming Principle:**
- Develop grip strength *in parallel* with hip/leg power and overhead stability; unbalanced development limits total capacity
- If grip is limiting, dedicate extra sets of pull variations *without straps* to address the weakness

**Technique Cue (Snatch-Specific):**
- Hook grip is NOT necessary when using straps; choose one or the other

---

## Application to This Athlete

**High relevance:** Your 50 kg OHS is your primary snatch limiter—grip strength may compound this. 
- Train snatch pulls **with straps** to maximize hip/leg drive without compromised positioning
- Do grip-building work on snatch deadlifts/RDLs **without straps** to keep pace with overhead development
- Avoid straps on actual snatch reps to reinforce grip-overhead stability balance

**Note on back pain:** Weak grip during pulls can cause postural compensation; using straps on pulls may reduce unnecessary spinal stress.
