# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2009/Sample-Bodybuilding-Work-for-Weightlifters/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** 3–4 days of "beach work" (hypertrophy) per week is sufficient; once weekly for each major movement group (Push, Pull, Legs) is the baseline recommendation.
*   **Sets/Reps:** Primarily hypertrophy ranges: 5x8–12 or 3x10–15 for main lifts; 3x10–15 for accessories. High rep calf work (15–30 reps).
*   **Intensity:** Moderate intensity focused on volume and metabolic stress rather than maximal loads.
*   **Organization:** Organize by movement patterns (Vertical Push/Pull, Horizontal Push/Pull) rather than body parts to ensure balance.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   *None provided in text.* The article focuses strictly on accessory exercise selection and programming structure, not technical execution of the snatch or clean & jerk.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Upper Body Push:** Press (5x8-12), Incline Bench (3x10-15), Upright Row (3x10-15), DB Lateral Flye, DB Front Raise.
*   **Upper Body Pull:** Pull-ups/Chin-ups (5x8-12 or 50 total reps), Dumbbell/T-Bar Rows (3x10-15), Curls/Wrist curls.
*   **Legs (Push):** Bulgarian Split Squat, Step-up, Lunge, Leg Extension.
*   **Legs (Pull):** Stiff-Legged Deadlift (SLDL), RDL, Good Morning, Glute-Ham Raise (GHR).
*   *Note:* The text suggests unilateral leg work once weekly is sufficient for weightlifters due to the volume already provided by Olympic lifts.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   *None explicitly detailed in this specific excerpt.* The author emphasizes "variation" and keeping training "fresh/engaging" as a psychological recovery tool, but offers no specific mobility protocols or sleep advice.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, 102.5kg, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain:** The text recommends posterior chain isolation exercises like **SLDLs, RDLs, Good Mornings, and GHRs**. *Caution:* For an athlete with chronic back pain, these must be introduced carefully; the text warns to "choose exercises that work best for you" (e.g., avoiding skullcrushers if they cause tendinitis).
*   **Hypertrophy Block:** The 8–12 rep range and specific movement group organization align perfectly with a dedicated hypertrophy block.
*   **Night Shift/Time Management:** The article suggests that **3 days** of accessory work is often enough, which fits well into a schedule for a night-shift worker who may have limited recovery windows or energy levels.
*   **Upper Body Focus:** The text notes weightlifters generally need *less* leg hypertrophy work than upper body work; this supports focusing the 6-week block on the athlete's specific limiter (OHS/Shoulder strength) via the recommended Push/Pull accessory movements.
