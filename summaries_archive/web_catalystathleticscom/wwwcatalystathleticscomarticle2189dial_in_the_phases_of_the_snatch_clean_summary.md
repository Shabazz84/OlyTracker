# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2189/Dial-in-the-Phases-of-the-Snatch-Clean/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Focus:** The text is instructional, not a specific programming template. It advocates for **segmented motor learning**: breaking complex lifts into phases (Starting, First Pull, Second Pull, Third Pull, Receiving) to master each component before integrating them into fluid motion.
*   **Intensity/Frequency:** Not specified in this excerpt; the focus is on technical mastery rather than load management.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Snatch:** "Punch straight up" with arms while sitting into the squat; maintain internal rotation of elbows (points out); lock shoulder blades immediately upon receiving to ensure bar security.
*   **Clean:** Drive elbows high and reach chest/shoulders up to meet the bar during the turnover; prioritize a quick, aggressive drive out of the bottom to utilize elastic energy for the jerk.
*   **General Pulls:** "Push like a squat" in the first pull (separate bar with legs); keep shoulders directly above or slightly forward of the bar; maintain back angle through the first pull; actively bring bar close to hips during extension (second pull).

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   The text does not list auxiliary exercises. It recommends drilling specific **phases** of the snatch and clean as standalone technical drills (e.g., isolating the second pull extension or the third pull turnover) before combining them.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Recovery:** Emphasizes "stabilizing" in the bottom of the squat if bar security is not 100% confident; suggests using "progressively larger bounces" to generate elastic energy for standing up from a dead stop in the clean.
*   **Mobility:** Notes that starting positions vary based on individual proportions and mobility, but the goal is to achieve specific structural alignments (e.g., shins inclined, back arched with air pressure).

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly emphasizes "back arched completely," "circumferential muscular tension" (bracing), and maintaining a consistent back angle during the first pull. This is critical for an athlete with chronic back pain to ensure spinal stability under load.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The advice to break lifts into digestible segments directly addresses the learning curve of transitioning from powerlifting (squat focus) to Olympic lifting, where timing and bar path are paramount.
*   **Jerk Limitation:** While the text mentions the clean-to-jerk transition requires a quick drive out of the squat, it does not address the specific "push jerk only" constraint or the lack of split jerk technique.
