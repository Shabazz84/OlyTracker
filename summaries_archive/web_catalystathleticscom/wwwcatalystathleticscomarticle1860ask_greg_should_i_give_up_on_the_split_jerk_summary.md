# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1860/Ask-Greg-Should-I-Give-Up-on-the-Split-Jerk/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** High frequency of specific jerk variations (push press + split jerk complexes; power jerk + split jerk complexes) to build neural pathways and timing.
*   **Intensity/Volume:** Not explicitly defined in this text, but implies a focus on technical repetition at sub-maximal loads ("Give yourself some time... don't master quickly") before attempting heavy singles.
*   **Periodization:** Suggests a "mastery" approach where the athlete must persist through the awkward learning phase rather than switching permanently to an easier variation (Power Jerk) immediately.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Dip & Drive:** Must be identical to a Power Jerk: straight down, straight up, and complete. No forward drive.
*   **Knee Position:** Do not start the dip from a "passive knee lock." Unlock knees slightly before dipping to remove slack/prevent freefall which causes forward momentum.
*   **Breathing/Bracing:** Take a big breath expanding the abdomen; lock it in tightly to turn the trunk into a rigid piston.
*   **Footwork:** Focus on lifting/reaching the *front heel* and driving hips under the bar. Do not consciously "shoot" the back foot back, as this pulls hips out from under the bar.
*   **Bar Path/Position:** Actively push the bar *back* behind the head during the split to achieve the ideal overhead position (bar over back of neck). Keep chest/head forward only as a reaction to pushing the bar back, not prematurely.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Complexes:** Push Press + Split Jerk; Power Jerk + Split Jerk.
*   **Drills:** Push jerks in a split position; Presses in a split position; Jerk balances.
*   **Variations:** Split Jerks behind the neck (to improve overhead positioning).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Mobility Focus:** The text notes the Split Jerk requires the *least* flexibility compared to Squat/Power jerks, but implies a need for hip mobility to handle the split position comfortably (addressing potential anatomical limits like retroversion).
*   **Stability:** Emphasizes full foot contact (not rocking back on heels) and quad tension to maintain vertical balance.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Transitioning to Split Jerk:** The athlete currently uses a Push Jerk only due to lack of technique/strength in the split. This text strongly advises against abandoning the Split Jerk, noting that "driving forward" is a common novice error fixable with specific cues (unlocking knees, rigid bracing).
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The cue to "turn trunk into a piston—totally rigid" via abdominal bracing and glute activation is critical for spinal stability during the jerk.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** While this text focuses on the Jerk, the emphasis on vertical drive and avoiding forward momentum directly addresses the mechanics needed to stabilize heavier weights overhead, which may indirectly help with OHS stability if the athlete transitions to a Split Jerk later.
*   **Night Shift/Back Pain:** The advice to "check balance after taking breath" and maintain full foot contact helps prevent the rocking motion that often exacerbates lower back strain in lifters who lack core rigidity.
