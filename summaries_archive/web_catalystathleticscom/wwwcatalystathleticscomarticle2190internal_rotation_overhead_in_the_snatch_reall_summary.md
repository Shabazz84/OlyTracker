**2. Technique Cues for Snatch/Jerk**
*   **Elbow Orientation:** Do not aim for maximal internal or external rotation. Aim for the "neutral" position: orient the bony point of the elbow approximately halfway between straight down and straight back (or the inside of the elbow pointing halfway between forward and up).
*   **Scapular Stability:** Ensure shoulder blades are retracted and upwardly rotated; do not confuse arm rotation with scapular protraction/retraction.
*   **Arm-Scapula Connection:** Keep the arm connected solidly to the scapula to prevent the elbow from aligning directly with the force trying to flex the joint.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Not Applicable:** The text focuses on the *position* of the bar in the overhead phase, not specific accessory exercises or drills.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Mobility Limitations:** Acknowledge that anatomical limitations may force some athletes into a position that looks like "full internal rotation" even if it is their neutral point; do not judge technique solely on end-range mobility without considering individual anatomy.
*   **Injury Prevention:** Avoid maximal internal or external rotation to prevent undue strain on shoulder structures and impingement (specifically noted by a commenter as causing inflammation).

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain & OHS Limiters:** While the text focuses on shoulders, the emphasis on "no undue strain" and proper scapular retraction is critical for an athlete with chronic back pain; poor overhead stability often forces compensatory lumbar extension (arching) which exacerbates back issues.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** The article notes that internal rotation range reduces further in a jerk position compared to a snatch due to the fixed hand on the bar, implying strict adherence to the "neutral" elbow angle is even more vital for a push-jerk athlete who lacks the split mechanics to adjust body position.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** For an athlete transitioning from powerlifting (where external rotation/"armpit showing" cues are common in some circles), this text provides the necessary correction to avoid shoulder impingement and improve overhead stability for the snatch/jerk.
