# Analysis: Correcting Jerk Weaknesses (Greg Everett)

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Jerk-specific strength work**: Push presses, power jerks, jerk dip squats, jerk drives, jump squats
- **Loading**: Jerk supports/recoveries can be loaded to 100%+ of best jerk
- **Complexes**: Combine assistance exercises with jerk for dip strength, speed, elasticity
- **Front squat emphasis**: Primary driver for jerk leg drive (quad-dominant)

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Common jerk faults**: (1) driving bar forward instead of up/back; (2) pulling hips back from under bar
- **Weight distribution**: Balance weight during dip/drive; excessive forward weight shifts bar forward
- **Split position**: Hips must remain under bar; torso changes position on front jerk but this can cause unintended hip retreat
- **Behind-the-neck drills**: Help athletes feel correct body placement and balance when bar path drives forward

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- **Overhead support**: Press, push press, jerk supports, jerk recoveries
- **Split strength**: Lunges, split-position walks (Smalcerz method—can load 100%+ of best jerk)
- **Dip/drive speed**: Jerk dip squats, jerk springs, push presses
- **Receiving position**: Press in split, push jerk in split, jerk balance, jump to split

## 4. **Relevant to This Athlete**
- **CRITICAL**: Push jerk only (no split jerk) = massive gap in technical development and split-position leg strength
- **OHS 50 kg limiter** suggests upper body overhead strength deficit—prioritize **press/push press programming**
- **Chronic back pain**: Front squat/jerk dip squat emphasis safer than heavy back squats; split-position work may aggravate if technique breaks down
- **Recommendation**: Split jerk development is urgent before 6-week hypertrophy block; single-leg/split work (lunges, split walks) addresses receiving position weakness
