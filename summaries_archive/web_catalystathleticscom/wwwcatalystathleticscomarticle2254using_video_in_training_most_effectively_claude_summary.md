# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2254/Using-Video-in-Training-Most-Effectively/

**This text is about video analysis methodology for weightlifting—not programming, technique cues, or exercises.**

**Relevant to athlete's profile:**

1. **Remote coaching setup** – As a night shift worker (Wed–Sun), this athlete likely benefits from remote coaching. Key takeaway: communicate clearly with coach about *what angles/lifts to film* rather than sending excessive footage.

2. **Focused analysis** – Don't watch videos between sets (disrupts flow). Post-session: identify 1–2 critical errors max, not every flaw. Plan one corrective method per session.

3. **OHS/Snatch work** – Since OHS is the primary snatch limiter, filming from an oblique angle to review form after dedicated OHS sessions (not mid-set) would be appropriate for self-analysis.

4. **Chronic back pain consideration** – Video can help identify positioning issues (e.g., spinal alignment in squat, front rack setup) that aggravate the back, but only if reviewed strategically post-session with a coach.

**Not addressed:** Programming, intensity/frequency for hypertrophy block, split jerk progression, mobility/recovery specifics.
