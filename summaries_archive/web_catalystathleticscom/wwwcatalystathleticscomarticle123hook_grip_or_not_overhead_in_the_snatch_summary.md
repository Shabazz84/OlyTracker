# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/123/Hook-Grip-or-Not-Overhead-in-the-Snatch/

1. **Programming principles**: Not specified; text focuses on grip mechanics rather than sets/reps/intensity/frequency/periodization.
2. **Technique cues**: 
   - Maintain hook grip only if it allows a "settled" wrist cradle without pain or mobility restriction.
   - If releasing: do not relax the hand while pulling against the bar; release the thumb *only* as the hand flips over to transition from pull to push.
   - Avoid early release (loss of connection) and late release (bar weight jams hook).
3. **Exercise recommendations**: Muscle snatches are suggested specifically for practicing the grip release in a slower, controlled environment before attempting full-speed lifts.
4. **Recovery or mobility advice**: Condition joints gradually to handle overhead stress; prioritize proper hand/wrist positioning over grip style if pain exists.
5. **Athlete-specific relevance**: 
   - **Chronic back pain/OHS limiter**: The text implies that forcing a hook grip overhead can restrict wrist mobility, potentially causing the lifter to compensate with poor posture or excessive upper body tension, which could exacerbate back issues.
   - **Push Jerk only**: While focused on snatch, the principle of "releasing the thumb at the right time" applies to any overhead position where stability is compromised by grip tightness.
   - **Male/Hand size context**: As a male lifter (102.5 kg), the text notes men are statistically more likely to find hook grips restrictive or painful due to thicker hands/barbs, suggesting he should prioritize mobility over maintaining the hook if it causes pain.
