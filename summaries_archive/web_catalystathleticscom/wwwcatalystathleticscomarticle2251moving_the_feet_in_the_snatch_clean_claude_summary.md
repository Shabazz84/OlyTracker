# Weightlifting Analysis: Moving the Feet in Snatch & Clean

## Relevant to This Athlete:

### Technique Cues (Snatch & Clean):
- **Foot movement philosophy**: No universal standard—effectiveness depends on individual proportions, mobility, and style
- **Complete foot lift recommended for learning**: Maximizes acceleration down but risks wider/back misplacement
- **Sliding feet alternative**: Requires precision but reduces acceleration loss; easier force absorption (platform connection sooner)
- **Static feet (no movement)**: Legitimate *only* if intentional/consistent—often signals weak pull finish or premature transition (likely this athlete's issue given modest clean/jerk vs. squat ratio)

### Key Red Flags for This Athlete:
- If feet aren't moving during heavy attempts but do during light ones = **strength/power deficit in pull finish or confidence issue**—suggests inadequate leg drive at top of pull
- This may explain the clean/jerk gap (80/65 kg) relative to back squat (118 kg)

### Recommendation:
- **Learn/drill with complete foot lift first** (standard approach)
- Address pull mechanics and leg drive quality before experimenting with alternatives
- Relevant to addressing technical limitations holding back jerk numbers

### Not Addressed:
- OHS limitation (50 kg) or chronic back pain management
- Recovery/programming for night shift worker
- Hypertrophy block design
