# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2104/Ask-Greg-Maintaining-a-More-Upright-Posture-in-the-Snatch-Pu/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Intensity/Volume:** Temporarily reduce loading on snatch pulls if the athlete tips over; prioritize quality posture over heavy weight. Use pauses (2–5 seconds) at specific segments to build strength in the desired position.
*   **Frequency:** Incorporate segment pulls and halting deadlifts regularly to reinforce the "upright" pattern.
*   **Periodization:** Focus on a technical/strength adaptation phase where the goal is strengthening the legs/back to maintain posture, rather than maximizing load immediately.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **"Squat the bar up":** Push with legs as if squatting; avoid "yanking" or ripping the bar off fast initially.
*   **Shoulder Position:** Keep shoulders directly above or slightly in front of the bar at the start, and maintain them there past the knee for as long as possible.
*   **Controlled Separation:** The initial lift must be tight and controlled; avoid letting hips shoot up faster than the chest (which causes the "bump" on the hip).

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Snatch Pulls:** Segment pulls with pauses 1 inch off floor or at the knee; halting snatch deadlifts at mid-thigh/hip.
*   **Accessory Strength:** Snatches from a low hang (below knee to 1 inch off floor); floating snatch pulls.
*   **Back Extension:** Stiff-legged deadlifts, RDLs, good mornings, and weighted back extensions (specifically if the back is failing to maintain extension).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   *Note: The text does not explicitly address recovery cycles, sleep, or mobility drills.* It focuses on mechanical strength deficits causing posture failure.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text directly addresses the possibility that back extension failure is causing the hips to shoot up; recommends specific strengthening (RDLs, good mornings) for this exact issue.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** Emphasizes that an upright posture requires adequate leg strength ("strengthen your squats") rather than just technique cues alone.
*   **Hip Pain/Bump:** Directly addresses the user's complaint about a painful bump on the hip caused by bouncing the bar off the hips; prescribes pauses and controlled starts to fix this.
*   **Limitations:** The advice assumes the athlete can perform squats with an upright posture; if their squat mechanics are poor, they must address that first before heavy pulling.
