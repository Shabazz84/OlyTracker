### 2. Technique Cues
*   **Passive Arm Extension:** Do not actively extend the elbows during the pull. Arms should be straight only because you are *not bending them*.
*   **Avoid Locking:** Actively prevent locking elbows in extension to stop force from driving the bar forward (causing a swing).
*   **Early Pull Mechanics:** Focus on keeping arms relaxed and straight rather than extending them, ensuring force travels vertically through the hips/shoulders rather than rotating around the shoulder hinge.

### 5. Relevance to Athlete Profile
*   **Critical for Back Pain:** The cue "don't lock elbows" directly addresses the athlete's chronic back pain. Locking elbows forces force forward, creating a shear load on the lumbar spine as the lifter tries to "damage control" the bar swing later in the pull. Correcting this promotes vertical bar path and reduces spinal stress.
*   **Relevance to Push Jerk Limitation:** For an athlete using only push jerks (no split), maintaining a tight, stable upper body position is crucial for transferring force. Premature arm bending or locking elbows during the pull can destabilize the rack position, making the subsequent push jerk more difficult.
*   **Note on OHS/Back Squat:** While not explicitly mentioned in this text, correcting the "locking" habit improves overall pulling efficiency, which may indirectly help the athlete's primary limiter (OHS) by allowing better bar control during the transition phases of lifts.

**Summary:** This text is purely a technical correction regarding **arm position during the pull**. It offers no programming or recovery advice but provides a vital cue for an athlete with back pain to reduce lumbar shear forces caused by improper pulling mechanics.
