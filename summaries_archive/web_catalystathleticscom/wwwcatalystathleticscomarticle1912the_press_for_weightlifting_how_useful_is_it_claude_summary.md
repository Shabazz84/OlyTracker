# Weightlifting Analysis: "The Press for Weightlifting" by Greg Everett

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Press priority**: Low for intermediate+ lifters; better used early in development
- **Rep range**: 3–10 reps, light-to-moderate loads (avoid grinding, slow movements)
- **Frequency**: Occasional (1x every 3–4 weeks as variety/recovery)
- **Key principle**: Work capacity is limited—prioritize high-ROI exercises over low-priority ones

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Jerk mechanics**: Legs drive ~90% of upward bar acceleration; upper body guides bar and stabilizes overhead only
- **Why press differs from jerk**: Speed is slower, body is static (not moving under bar), resistance timing is opposite (hardest at bottom, not top), loading capacity is lower
- **Teaching value**: Press is simple for teaching upper body pressing mechanics but won't replicate jerk speed/mechanics

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
**Better alternatives to press for jerk development:**
- **Push press** (top priority): Mimics jerk timing, speed, loading; trains dip/drive timing
- **Jerk support/recovery**: Loads heavier than press; trains total-body stability
- **Lowering jerks to shoulders** (vs. dropping): Builds upper body + leg eccentric strength
- **Overhead holds** (3–5 sec after jerks): Static lockout + stability work
- **Specific accessory work**: Push jerk behind neck (split), jerk balance, tall jerk
