# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1979/Avoiding-Methods-That-May-Ruin-Your-Olympic-Weightlifting-Pr/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** Recommend 3 days/week for non-professionals as the optimal balance between skill acquisition and recovery. Add a 4th day only for technique (empty bar) or GPP if energy permits.
*   **Intensity:** Avoid constantly training maximum weights; use moderate loads to build technique and strength. Reserve max attempts for testing, not daily progression.
*   **Periodization/Ordering:** Prioritize classical lifts (Snatch/C&J) early in the session while fresh. Never place heavy squats immediately before heavy cleans or vice versa. Alternate Snatch and C&J days rather than mixing them heavily in one session.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **High Pulls:** Novices should avoid high pulls initially; they often lead to pulling with the arms. Focus on fast extension, going on toes, and proper shrugging timing first.
*   **Elbow Position:** Keep elbows straight during the pull phase for at least the first year to prevent arm-dominant pulling patterns.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Auxiliary Work:** Do not neglect flexibility, abs work, box jumps, or aerobics. These provide long-term gains even if PRs aren't seen immediately.
*   **Variation Mixing:** When doing pulls, vary the starting position (e.g., do not pull from knee level immediately after snatching from knee level) to avoid inefficient repetition unless approaching a meet day.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Recovery Time:** Acknowledge that recovery time is as critical as training time for learning complex movements.
*   **Flexibility:** Explicitly include flexibility work in the routine; it is a necessary component of Olympic lifting, not an optional extra.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate/Back Pain/Night Shift)**
*   **Night Shift Warning:** The text explicitly warns against "training too seldom" due to fear of overtraining, but emphasizes that 3 days/week is scientifically optimal for recovery. For a night shift worker with chronic back pain, adhering strictly to the 3-day recommendation prevents cumulative fatigue and allows necessary rest between shifts.
*   **Back Pain Context:** The article advises against "blindly copying" methods (like heavy high pulls) that could exacerbate poor mechanics or injury risk in novices/intermediates. It suggests focusing on auxiliary aspects (abs, flexibility) which are crucial for stabilizing a painful back.
*   **Transition Advice:** Since the athlete is transitioning from powerlifting to Olympic lifting, the warning against "constantly working on maximum weights" is critical; they must shift focus from pure strength numbers to technique acquisition and speed before attempting heavy loads again.
