# Analysis: "Keeping the Bar Close" by Matt Foreman

## 1. **Technique Cues for Snatch**
- **Primary issue addressed:** Bar looping (swinging forward during pull completion/transition)
- **Key cue:** "Feel the bar pass close to your face" — Foreman's personal fix for looping
- **Supporting cue:** "Wait until bar clears head before turning wrists over"
- **Related errors:** Insufficient torso verticality before second pull, lack of back tightness off floor
- **Mental approach:** Find individualized cues that resonate (not universal); trial-and-error process

## 2. **Relevance to This Athlete**

**Critical for your profile:**
- **OHS at 50 kg is limiting snatch** — looping exacerbates this by creating unstable receiving position
- **Chronic back pain:** Loose bar path (looping) increases spinal stress; keeping bar close preserves neutral spine
- **Night shift fatigue:** Mental cueing (face proximity) is low-cognitive-load coaching vs. complex positional coaching
- **Push jerk only:** Indicates timing/coordination issues; bar control drills will transfer

**Action:** Start with snatch pulls/tall snatches emphasizing bar proximity to midline before addressing OHS depth.
