# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/106/Correcting-Jerk-Weaknesses/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** High frequency of jerk-specific work is implied (e.g., "cycles with a lot of jerks," complexes).
*   **Intensity/Volume:** Use heavy weights for stability drills (up to 100%+ of max jerk) and moderate loads for speed/elasticity drills.
*   **Periodization:** Focus on specific deficits; if the dip is weak, prioritize dip squats/springs; if overhead support is weak, prioritize supports/recoveries.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Dip & Drive:** Maintain balance over feet; avoid shifting weight too far forward during the dip to prevent driving the bar forward.
*   **Hip Placement:** Keep hips directly under the bar in the receiving position; do not pull hips back out from under the bar (common when torso changes position).
*   **Overhead Stability:** Lock elbows and shoulder blades forcefully; keep arms straight and stable during the drive.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **For Leg Drive/Speed:** Partial front squats, jerk dip squats, jump squats, power jerks.
*   **For Split Stability (Legs):** Walking lunges in split receiving position (heavy loading possible), single-leg squats.
*   **For Overhead Support:** Jerk supports (holding bar overhead from pins for 3-5s), jerk recoveries (standing up from split with bar on pins).
*   **For Upper Body Drive/Pressing:** Push presses, power jerks, split jerks from behind the neck.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   *Note: The text does not explicitly address recovery or mobility.* It focuses strictly on mechanical diagnosis and corrective exercises.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (102.5kg, Push Jerk Only, Back Pain)**
*   **Push Jerk Focus:** The article heavily emphasizes the "dip and drive" mechanics which are critical for a push jerk lifter lacking split technique.
*   **Back Pain Warning:** The text recommends **heavy walking lunges** (100%+ of max) and **jerk supports**. For an athlete with chronic back pain, these high-load spinal compression or unilateral loading exercises require extreme caution or modification; the "softening of the trunk" mentioned as a cause for weakness is particularly relevant to their injury history.
*   **Behind-the-Neck Drills:** The article suggests split jerks from behind the neck to fix hip placement. This may be contraindicated for an athlete with shoulder/back issues or limited mobility, though it addresses the "driving bar forward" cue.
*   **OHS Limitation:** Since 50kg OHS is a limiter, the article's advice on "jerk supports" and "overhead lockout" (locking elbows/shoulders) directly targets the strength deficit preventing the athlete from stabilizing heavier loads overhead.
