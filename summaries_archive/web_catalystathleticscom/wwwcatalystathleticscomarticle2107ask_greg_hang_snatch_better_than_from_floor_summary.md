# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2107/Ask-Greg-Hang-Snatch-Better-Than-from-Floor/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Method:** Use "segment" or "halting" deadlifts to bridge the gap between hang and floor positions.
*   **Tempo/Intensity:** Pause for 3 seconds at specific heights (floor, knee, mid-thigh) with controlled speed; do not rush transitions.
*   **Frequency/Complexes:** Implement complexes like:
    *   *3-Position Snatch:* Mid-thigh → Knee → Floor (descending difficulty).
    *   *2-Position Snatch:* Hang + Full Snatch.
    *   *Segment + Normal:* 1–2 segment snatches followed immediately by a full snatch to prevent "hitching" (compensatory movements).

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Floor Start:** Shoulders directly over the bar; weight slightly more on heels than balls of feet.
*   **Mid-Thigh (Second Pull Initiation):** Shins vertical, shoulders slightly in front of bar, back arched tightly, head/eyes forward, weight slightly behind mid-foot (avoiding full heel dominance).
*   **Common Error Correction:** Avoid shifting weight too far back on heels, which causes compensatory forward shifts or toe-landing.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   Snatch Segment Deadlifts (1-inch off floor, knee, mid-thigh).
*   Halting Snatch Deadlifts (pause 3 seconds at target height).
*   Complexes: Mid-thigh → Knee → Floor snatches; Hang + Full Snatch.
*   Slow-Pull Snatch (controlled movement into mid-thigh position without a pause, then explode).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   *Note:* The text does not contain specific recovery or mobility advice for chronic pain or shift work. It focuses strictly on motor learning and tension awareness through pauses.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** The "controlled speed" and 3-second pauses allow the athlete to reinforce a tight back arch and proper spinal alignment before explosive movement, reducing shear force risks associated with rushing from the floor.
*   **Transition Strategy:** Since the athlete is already proficient at mid-thigh (hang), this text provides a direct roadmap to transfer that strength to the floor without overthinking mechanics.
*   **Caveat:** The recommendation for "weight slightly more on heels" requires caution given the chronic back pain; ensure the posterior chain engages before loading the spine, and monitor if heel-dominant cues exacerbate lumbar strain.
