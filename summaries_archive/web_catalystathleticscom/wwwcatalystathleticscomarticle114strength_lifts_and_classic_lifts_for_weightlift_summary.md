# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/114/Strength-Lifts-and-Classic-Lifts-for-Weightlifting/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** High frequency for squats (4–6 days/week), potentially twice daily; high frequency for classic lifts (snatch/C&J) to build specific strength.
*   **Intensity/Volume:** Avoid "baby weights" and low-volume technique-only sessions for intermediate+ athletes; incorporate heavy loads where the classic lifts represent 60%+ of max strength numbers.
*   **Periodization:** Do not rely solely on general strength (deadlifts) to drive Olympic lift performance; prioritize specific strength in lifting positions over generic hypertrophy unless addressing a specific deficit.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Triple Extension:** Avoid intentional "plantar flexion" or forced shrugs as primary cues; allow angle extension to occur naturally based on individual mechanics.
*   **Shrug/Arm Movement:** The shrug should be integrated into the arm movement of pulling the body down under the bar, not a separate isolated action.
*   **Pop Cues:** Utilize flexible cues like "quad pop" or "hip pop" depending on the athlete's tendency to drift forward; avoid dogmatic application of one cue for all lifters.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Squats:** Essential and should be trained hard (high frequency, significant volume).
*   **Pulls/Halting Deadlifts:** Recommended (e.g., deadlifts to the hip crease with pauses) to improve technique and strength transfer; not all coaches use them regularly, but they are beneficial.
*   **Pressing:** Do **not** prioritize press numbers as a primary method to improve the jerk; the mechanics of supporting weight differ significantly from putting it there (jerk recovery is distinct).

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Jerk Mechanics:** Directly addresses the athlete's "push jerk only" limitation by clarifying that general pressing strength is a poor predictor of jerk performance; focus should be on the specific mechanics of the jerk rather than increasing press numbers.
*   **Strength vs. Skill:** Warns against the assumption that simply deadlifting heavier will automatically fix Olympic lift deficits for an intermediate athlete; emphasizes that seasoned lifters utilize available strength more efficiently in the lifts themselves.
*   **Training Philosophy:** Challenges the "hypertrophy block" approach if it implies neglecting heavy, specific lifting work; suggests that for weightlifters, the classic lifts *are* a primary driver of specific strength development when performed with sufficient intensity.
