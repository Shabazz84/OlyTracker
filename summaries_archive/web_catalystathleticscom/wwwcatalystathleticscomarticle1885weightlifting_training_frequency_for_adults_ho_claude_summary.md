# Analysis: "Weightlifting Training Frequency for Adults"

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Frequency determined by life demands**: Night shift work (Wed–Sun) is a legitimate constraint—design around it, not against it
- **Two viable approaches**:
  - Concentrated: 2–3 sessions/week, 2–3 hours each (5–6 exercises)
  - Distributed: 4–5 sessions/week, 45 min–1.5 hours each (2–3 exercises)
- **Progressive reduction with age**: Foreman went from 5 days/week (teens–20s) → 4 days (mid–late 20s) → 3 days (30s) → 2 days (40s)
- **Universal rules**:
  - Train at the edge of overtraining, not into it
  - Lighter lifters tolerate more volume than heavier lifters
  - Watch subjective markers: sore-but-functional vs. barely-functional = overtrained

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
Sample 1993 session (example only, not tailored): Snatch, clean pulls, back squat, seated good mornings. Article emphasizes **lifting-only programs** (no CrossFit or accessory bloat).

## 4. **Recovery & Mobility**
- Monitor how you *feel* as primary feedback
- Don't add extra work (CrossFit, conditioning) unless accounted for in frequency planning
- Expect soreness; distinguish it from overtraining

## 5. **Relevant to This Athlete**
- **Critical**: Night shift (Wed–Sun) suggests **2–3 days/week concentrated work** is realistic
- Chronic back pain + intermediate status → prioritize recovery quality over frequency
- OHS weakness (50 kg) requires targeted volume but *within* sustainable frequency
- At 102.5 kg (heavier), expect lower frequency tolerance than lighter lifters

**Recommendation**: Start low (2–3 days/week), progress cautiously.
