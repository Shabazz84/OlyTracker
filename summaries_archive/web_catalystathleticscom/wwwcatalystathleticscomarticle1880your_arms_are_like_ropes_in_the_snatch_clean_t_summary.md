# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1880/Your-Arms-Are-Like-Ropes-in-the-Snatch-Clean-True-or-False/

1. **Programming principles:** None specified; text is purely technical/philosophical regarding arm usage timing rather than periodization or volume prescription.
2. **Technique cues:** "Arms are like ropes" (to prevent early pulling) but must transition to active elbow movement ("slightly-upward-slightly-backward") to pull the bar close during the turnover; avoid letting arms stay passive too long which causes the bar to loop forward.
3. **Exercise recommendations:** None specific; implies watching world-record snatch videos for visual learning of the "pull under" mechanics.
4. **Recovery or mobility advice:** None mentioned in text.
5. **Athlete profile relevance:** 
   - **Chronic back pain:** The article warns that over-emphasizing "arms like ropes" can cause the bar to swing forward, leading to a forward jump and potential spinal stress; active elbow movement is required to keep the bar close to the body (reducing shear force).
   - **Intermediate/Transitioning:** Validates that stagnation at 80kg Clean often stems from not knowing *how* to use arms, not just lack of leg power.
   - **Jerk limitation:** The text notes the "pull under" mechanics are identical for Snatch and Clean (bar caught on shoulders vs overhead), implying the same active elbow turnover is needed for a Push Jerk to stabilize the rack position effectively.
