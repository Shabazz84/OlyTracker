### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Intensity/Speed:** For submaximal loads (warm-ups/light technique), apply effort such that the first pull speed mimics a heavy lift; do not "yank" or accelerate too fast for light weights, nor slow down unnecessarily before the second pull.
*   **Tempo Control:** Use controlled speeds and pauses between floor and mid-thigh to ingrain proper position and balance.
*   **Progression:** Start with intentional slowness to learn mechanics; transition to "fast then faster" (first pull is slower than second, but both are high effort) once movement patterns are solidified.

### 2. Technique Cues
*   **Shoulder Position:** Shoulders should be directly above or very slightly in front of the bar at the start; they move forward relative to the bar as hips extend, not because the back angle changes drastically.
*   **Bar Path:** The bar must move backward toward the body slightly during the first pull to rebalance the system (preventing a forward lean/chase).
*   **Knee Clearance:** Do not push knees backward dramatically; they naturally move out of the way as legs extend if hips/shoulders are aligned correctly.
*   **Continuous Tension:** Push continuously against the ground; do not slow down near the end of the first pull to "load up." The bar should never decelerate before the second pull.

*   **Deadlift Variations with Pauses:** Halting snatch/clean deadlifts, segment deadlifts, or lift-offs (floor to mid-thigh).
*   **Slow Eccentrics:** Use slow lowering phases in deadlift variations to strengthen posture and position.
*   **Riser Work:** Snatch/Clean deadlifts on risers (to simulate the first pull mechanics from a higher starting point) or floating deadlifts.
*   **Complexes:** Deadlift + Pull, Lift-off + Pull, or Segment Pull + Competition Lift to reinforce the transition.
