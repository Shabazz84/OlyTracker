# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2079/Technique-Correction-without-Coaching/

1. **Programming Principles**: The text argues against relying solely on verbal coaching cues for complex motor skills; instead, prioritize **exercise selection** that isolates specific movement deficits (e.g., weak third pull) to allow the body to self-correct through repetition and load, rather than conscious mental processing.
2. **Technique Cues**: Explicitly warns that cues like "Legs," "Stay over the bar," or "Don't bend arms" are often ineffective if the athlete lacks physical capability or understanding; advises abandoning verbal cues for specific drills when technique fails.
3. **Exercise Recommendations**: 
   - For weak/imprecise third pull: **Tall Snatches**.
   - To stay over the bar longer (strength/technique): **Halting Deadlifts**, **Segment Deadlifts**, or **Pulls**.
   - For split jerk dip/drive issues: **Power Jerks** or **Power Jerk + Split Jerk complexes**.
   - Specific fixes for rushing hip extension/mis-timing: **Snatch from Power Position**, **Hang Snatches (from knee/higher)**, and **Snatch Pull + Snatch** combinations.
4. **Recovery/Mobility**: Not addressed in this text; focus is strictly on motor learning and exercise selection.
5. **Athlete-Specific Relevance**: 
   - **Chronic Back Pain & Intermediate Status**: The article's core philosophy (using isolated exercises like halting deadlifts or power jerks to fix mechanics) is highly relevant for an athlete with back pain, as it allows strengthening specific positions without the cumulative fatigue of full lifts.
   - **No Split Jerk**: The text specifically recommends using Power Jerks to develop a proper dip and drive before reintroducing the split jerk, directly addressing the user's current limitation.
   - **Night Shift/Recovery**: While not mentioned in the text, the "body is smarter than conscious mind" concept supports using lower-cognitive-load drills (like tall snatches) which may be beneficial for a fatigued night-shift worker who needs to minimize mental fatigue during training.
