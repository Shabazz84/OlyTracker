# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2041/Jumping-Backward-in-the-Snatch-Clean-When-How-to-Fix-It/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency/Volume:** High frequency of technical drills (snatch/clean pulls, tall snatches, snatch balances) to accumulate specific footwork practice without high systemic fatigue.
*   **Intensity:** Use sub-maximal loads for drills; focus on speed and position rather than load.
*   **Periodization:** Not explicitly defined in text, but implies a technical correction phase where specific movement patterns (pulling under, hip extension timing) are prioritized over heavy loading cycles.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **"Lift the knees up":** Actively flex hips to lift knees toward the body; keep soles of feet parallel to the floor during the catch. Avoid pivoting on the knee which causes backward sliding.
*   **Maintain Pressure:** Push against the ground with legs all the way through extension; this acts as an "anchor" preventing feet from sliding back.
*   **Stay Over the Bar:** Do not open hips or pull shoulders back prematurely (at the knee); wait for full hip extension before initiating the upward explosion.
*   **Balance:** Maintain balance over the foot with a *slight* preference for the heel, but never shift weight fully to the heels during the pull.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Drills for Footwork:** Drop snatches, snatch balances, tall snatches/cleans, snatches/cleans from power position or dip positions.
*   **Drills for Timing/Extension:** Slow-pull snatches/cleans, halting snatch/clean deadlifts, segment snatch/clean pulls/deadlifts.
*   **Complexes:** Snatch/Clean Pull + Snatch/Clean (ensure pull mechanics are correct first).
*   **External Cue Tool:** Place a 1/4–1/2" rubber mat behind the heels to physically block backward sliding and force lifting/planting of feet.

*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** While not mentioned in the text, the recommendation for low-fatigue technical drills (pulls, balances) is ideal for a night-shift worker who may have compromised recovery windows; it allows technique work without demanding high CNS output or long sleep cycles required for heavy 1RM attempts.
