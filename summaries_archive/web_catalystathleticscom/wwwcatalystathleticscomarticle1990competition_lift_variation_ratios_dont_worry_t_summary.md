# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1990/Competition-Lift-Variation-Ratios-Dont-Worry-Too-Much/

1. **Programming principles**: The text advises against rigidly adhering to fixed ratios between competition lifts (Clean/Snatch) and their variations (Hang/Block). If a lifter can lift more from blocks/hangs than the floor, this indicates a specific technical flaw in the initial pull (positioning, posture, or rhythm), not superior strength. Training should utilize these stronger partials to build confidence while simultaneously programming pull-specific variations (pulls before lifts, complexes) and technique primers to correct the floor-start weakness.

2. **Technique cues**: Focus on "postural weakness in the early pull" and "incorrect speed or rhythm." The text suggests that if a block lift exceeds the full lift, the athlete is likely losing position or failing to generate proper acceleration from the dead stop at the floor. Cues should target fixing the initial pull mechanics rather than avoiding the partials.

3. **Exercise recommendations**:
   - Hang snatches/cleans (specifically from positions where the lifter performs well).
   - Block snatches/cleans (often knee or just below knee height).
   - Pull variations and complexes that include pulls *before* the lift.
   - Technique primers focusing specifically on the pull from the floor.

4. **Recovery or mobility advice**: None mentioned in this specific text excerpt.

5. **Relevance to athlete profile**: 
   - **Chronic back pain/Back Squat vs. Clean ratio**: The text is highly relevant for diagnosing why an intermediate lifter (102.5 kg bodyweight) might struggle with the full clean despite having a strong squat; it suggests the issue may be in the "early pull" or posture rather than raw strength.
   - **Transitioning athlete**: It validates using hang/block variations as a confidence-building tool while the athlete learns proper floor mechanics, which is crucial for someone new to Olympic lifting who may have developed compensatory patterns.
   - **Night shift/Back pain**: While the text doesn't address sleep or pain directly, it offers a diagnostic framework: if the athlete's back pain flares during full pulls but not partials (or vice versa), it can help isolate whether the issue is mechanical positioning (postural weakness) rather than just load tolerance.
