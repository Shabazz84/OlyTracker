# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2195/Ask-Greg-Slow-Snatch-Turnover/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Prioritize high-frequency practice of specific drills (Tall Snatches, Power Position Snatches) over heavy competition lifts to ingrain timing.
*   **Intensity:** Start drills with weights "you're confident with" and can perform perfectly; do not rush into heavier loads until the movement pattern is corrected, as heavy weight reinforces bad habits.
*   **Periodization:** Use a "fatigue-aggression" method for 3-position snatches (Floor → Knee → Mid-thigh) where subsequent reps are done with more fatigue to force aggression in shorter distances.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Diagnosis Check:** Do not assume the turnover is slow; verify if the issue is actually a late start, overextension in the second pull, or insufficient acceleration/elevation prior to the turn.
*   **Aggressive Extension:** Focus on "snapping the hips" fully rather than partially extending from mid-thigh positions; ensure the trunk remains vertical at the power position.
*   **Timing:** Treat the final upward extension and the pull-under as a single, continuous action (transition and timing).

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Tall Snatches:** The primary drill for turnover speed (ensure proper execution if previous attempts failed).
*   **Hang Snatches:** Specifically from mid-thigh or higher initially to force aggressive extension; avoid lower hangs where partial hip snapping is easier.
*   **Snatch from Power Position:** Recommended over low hang snatches to ensure a fully extended position and vertical trunk.
*   **3-Position Snatch:** A sequence starting at the floor, then knee, then mid-thigh (or upper-thigh) to build aggression through fatigue.

**5. Specifically relevant to this athlete's profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The recommendation for **Snatch from Power Position** is highly relevant; starting with a vertical trunk reduces the shear force and lower back loading associated with deep hip flexion in low hang positions, which may be safer for an athlete with chronic back issues.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning Status:** The advice to "not rush into heavier weights" directly addresses the risk of reinforcing poor technique common when transitioning athletes attempt loads they are not yet technically prepared to handle.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** While not mentioned in the text, the recommendation for high-repetition technical drills (Tall Snatches) allows for shorter, focused sessions that can be integrated into irregular schedules without requiring long, exhaustive training blocks.
