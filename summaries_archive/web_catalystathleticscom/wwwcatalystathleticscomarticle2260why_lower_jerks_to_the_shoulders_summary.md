# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2260/Why-Lower-Jerks-to-the-Shoulders/

### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Implement multiple-rep sets of the jerk (e.g., 3–5 reps) rather than singles to maximize strength stimulus without excessive rest/stripping time.
*   **Intensity:** Start with lighter weights to master control, then incrementally increase load as consistency improves.
*   **Periodization:** Use this technique for hypertrophy and strength accumulation phases; avoid when pain is present or if the athlete cannot perform the movement safely/reliably.

### 2. Technique Cues
*   **Trunk Stability:** Focus on bracing forcefully to prevent collapsing while receiving the bar; a rigid trunk transfers more power into the drive.
*   **Elasticity & Braking:** Absorb and arrest the downward force of the bar with the legs (dip) to improve braking speed, allowing for greater elastic recoil in the bottom position.
*   **Control:** Prioritize "guts" and intimidation management; practice lowering with light loads first to ensure the elbows and wrists remain stable under load.

### 3. Exercise Recommendations
*   **Primary Drill:** Lowering jerks back to the shoulders (re-racking) instead of dropping or using blocks for every rep.
*   **Progression Strategy:** Practice lowering with lighter weights until consistent, then incrementally increase weight within a single workout session.

### 4. Recovery or Mobility Advice
*   **Pain Management:** Do not lower jerks if it causes pain (e.g., medial elbow tendinitis or direct back strain).
*   **Grip Adjustment:** If wide jerk grips cause marginal elbow positioning and strain, adjust grip width to reduce undue stress on joints.

### 5. Relevance to Athlete Profile
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly warns against lowering jerks if it causes pain or if the athlete lacks control, directly addressing your back pain history. You must prioritize safety over this strength stimulus until stability is proven.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** While the article advocates for re-racking to build upper body and trunk strength (beneficial since push jerks lack eccentric work), it notes that if you have limited overhead mobility, you do more muscular work; however, your specific goal of 6-week hypertrophy aligns well with the "free strength" benefit described.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The article emphasizes building a "consistent ability" before increasing load, which is crucial for an athlete managing fatigue from night shifts to avoid injury during high-intensity eccentric work.
