### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Frequency:** Grip training is integrated into a 3-day cycle (Day 1, Day 2, Day 3) alongside main lifts.
*   **Sets/Reps:**
    *   **Gripper Work:** High volume (5 sets x 8-12 reps moderate), endurance focus (5 sets x 15-20 reps light-moderate), and low rep strength (5 sets x 3-5 reps heavy).
    *   **Hang Snatches/Shrugs:** Low volume, high duration (3-5 sets of 10-30 sec holds or hangs) to build endurance without excessive systemic fatigue.
*   **Intensity:** Grip intensity is managed by selecting a tool that allows specific rep ranges; for heavy gripper work, the goal is "squeezing out" reps until failure on the last set.
*   **Periodization:** Progressive overload is achieved by holding the same number of reps until full closure is possible before increasing weight or difficulty.

### 2. Technique Cues
*   **No-Hook Grip Transition:** Perform warm-up sets for cleans and snatches without the hook grip to improve "timing of opening hands during turnover" and maintain connection. Switch back to hook grip only when the bar feels secure at heavier weights.
*   **Grip Positioning (Farmer's Walk):** Grip handles so they rest in the fingers, not crushed high in the palm; mimic the snatch/clean bar position.
*   **Snatch Hangs:** Do not drop the bar after overhead snatches; maintain grip while lowering to maximize tension time.
*   **Elbow Position (Gripper):** Keep elbow straight and arm out to the side at a 90-degree angle (palm back) during gripper squeezes to mimic snatch pulling mechanics.

### 3. Exercise Recommendations
*   **Primary Tool:** Hand grippers (e.g., Iron Mind Captains of Crush).
*   **Snatch Specifics:** No-Hook Hang Snatches (mid-thigh start), Power Snatches, or full Snatches depending on pull-under needs.
*   **Static Strength:** Snatch Grip Hangs/Shrugs using a pull-up bar or high blocks (no hook grip).
*   **Dynamic Load:** Farmer's Walks (using handles, dumbbells, or pinch-gripping smooth plates).
*   **Accessory:** Thumbless or fingertip grip pull-ups/chin-ups; hanging leg raises.

### 4. Recovery & Pain Management
*   **Thumb Taping:** Use **elastic athletic tape** (e.g., Jaybird Jaylastic) on thumbs to increase security and eliminate pain from hook grip volume, rather than standard rigid sports tape.
*   **Equipment Modification:** Reduce chalk usage or use bars with smoother knurling to force a tighter natural grip without excessive strain.
*   **Strap Usage:** Eliminate straps for snatches unless doing high-rep hang work; avoid forcing strap-less pulls on heavy cleans/snatch pulls as it may slow speed and hurt pull training.

### 5. Relevance to Athlete Profile
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The article suggests **No-Hook Hang Snatches** performed with less weight (due to pre-fatigued grip) to reduce recovery demand, which is beneficial for an athlete managing back pain.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** While the text focuses on grip security for snatches/cleans, the "No-Hook" warm-up strategy can help refine hand connection timing even if the jerk technique remains push-dominant.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The program emphasizes pre-fatiguing the grip *before* main lifts to reduce systemic fatigue ("won't take as much out of recovery capacity"), which is crucial for a night-shift worker with limited recovery windows.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The "No-Hook" warm-up sets are specifically recommended to help lifters who have "inadequate grip security in the snatch," addressing a common limiter for athletes transitioning to higher volumes.
