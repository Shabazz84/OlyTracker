# Analysis: Snatch Bar Path/Looping Issue

## Relevant to Athlete Profile
**Highly relevant.** This athlete's OHS (50 kg) is the primary snatch limiter—bar looping/forward drift post-contact directly prevents receiving position stability and OHS depth.

---

## Technique Cues (Snatch-Specific)

**The Problem:** Bar loops forward after contact; arms reverse-curl instead of maintaining vertical pull.

**Root Cause:** Third pull (upper body post-hip contact), NOT the initial pull.

**Correction Sequence:**
1. **Snatch High-Pulls** (power position start) — Elbows to max height, constant vertical arm pull despite aggressive leg drive & hip contact. Teaches bar-body proximity with weight.
2. **Muscle Snatches** (power position start) — Replicate high-pull arm motion; aggressive elbow lift and squeeze shoulder blades/elbows back during turnover to keep bar close to face.
3. **Tall Snatches** — Active, aggressive motion throughout; bar tight to body. Reinforces upper-body control.
4. **Complex:** Snatch high-pull + snatch (with weight) — Trains post-contact bar control and active pull-under.

**Key Cue:** "Lift elbows out and up to maximal height...active, aggressive motion all the way through—preserve as much bar speed as possible."

---

## Programming Notes
- Use exercise series in snatch warm-up: 5 reps each (empty bar)
- Requires significant **time/consistency** to ingrain; motor learning is slow with entrenched habits
- Directly addresses OHS receiving position weakness (bar control = better positioning)

---

## Recovery/Mobility
- **Extreme mobility exception noted:** If shoulder mobility allows, elbows to shoulder height before turnover (relevant given athlete's OHS limitation)
