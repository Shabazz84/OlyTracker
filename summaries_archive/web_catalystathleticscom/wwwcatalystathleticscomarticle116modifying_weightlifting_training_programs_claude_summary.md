# Analysis: "Modifying Weightlifting Training Programs" by Greg Everett

## Relevant to This Athlete

**1. Programming Principles**
- **Frequency modification for constraints**: Everett emphasizes prioritizing needs and scaling down thoughtfully—critical for this athlete's Wed–Sun night shift schedule (only 3–4 available training days/week realistically)
- **Prioritization framework**: Identify primary limiting factor first (here: OHS/snatch technique + chronic back pain) and build around it
- **Recovery-first approach**: "Start on the minimal end, see how you respond, then add." Directly applicable to back pain and night shift fatigue
- **Drop non-essential work first**: Conditioning, accessory exercises, power variations—preserve competition lifts and heavy compounds

**2. Exercise Recommendations**
- Keep squats intact (back + front squat) for leg strength—foundational for clean/jerk
- **Prioritize snatch/clean & jerk fresh** (do them first when CNS is fresh)
- Pulls (snatch/clean) before deadlifts, but after competition lifts
- **Reduce or eliminate:** Muscle snatches, snatch push press (since OHS is limiter—do OHS instead)
- Back extensions or light back work acceptable if needed (avoids excessive spinal loading with chronic pain)
- Drop traditional deadlifts (not essential for weightlifting, adds fatigue)

**3. Technique Cues**
- None explicit in this text

**4. Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- Drop non-sport conditioning work temporarily to preserve recovery capacity
- Flexibility and weekly adjustments critical when fatigued
- "On weeks you're feeling too beat down, strip away more"—fits night shift fluctuation

**5. Chronic Back Implications**
- High squat volume (10×3 back squat) is taxing; consider managing spinal load with exercise order and accessory selection
- OHS work should precede heavy squats for warm-up effect, not after

---

## Key Takeaway for This Athlete
Scale a 3-day template: **Day 1 (Snatch focus) → Day 2 (Clean/Jerk focus) → Day 3 (Squat/OHS emphasis)**. Eliminates conditioning, prioritizes OHS development for snatch, respects recovery constraints.
