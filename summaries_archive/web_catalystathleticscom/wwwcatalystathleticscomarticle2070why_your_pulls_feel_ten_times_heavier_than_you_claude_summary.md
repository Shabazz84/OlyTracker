# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2070/Why-Your-Pulls-Feel-Ten-Times-Heavier-Than-Your-Lifts/

**ANALYSIS:**

This article is about **mental psychology in weightlifting**, not programming or technique coaching.

---

**RELEVANT TO THIS ATHLETE:**

**Mental/Psychological:**
- Addresses the phenomenon of pulls feeling disproportionately heavy post-lift (common for intermediate athletes)
- Key insight: Mental framing dramatically affects perceived difficulty and performance
- Practical cues:
  1. Focus on **movement cues/technique, not weight on bar**
  2. Use **visualization** before lifts
  3. Maintain **long-term vision** (big lift goals) alongside short-term PRs—prevents mental limits

**Why it matters for this athlete:**
- Night shift fatigue + chronic back pain likely amplify mental barriers
- OHS weakness (50 kg) may be partially psychological; reframing as technique work vs. weight struggle could help
- Push jerk plateau (65 kg vs. 80 kg clean) could benefit from confidence-building visualization before PRs
- Hypertrophy block will add load; mental approach to "heavy feels" will impact adherence

**NOT INCLUDED:**
- Programming structure, sets/reps/intensity recommendations
- Specific snatch, clean, or jerk technique cues
- Recovery/mobility protocols
- Exercise selection
