# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2004/Why-Youre-Dizzy-in-the-Clean-or-Front-Squat-How-to-Prevent-/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Intensity/Frequency:** Not explicitly detailed in this text; the article focuses on acute safety and symptom management rather than periodization structures.
*   **Correction Strategy:** If dizziness occurs, immediately drop the bar and take a knee. Do not attempt to "push through" or finish the set if symptoms appear.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Rack Position:** Ensure proper shoulder protraction and slight elevation of scapulae to create space for the bar; prevent the bar from crashing into the neck/throat area which compresses carotid arteries.
*   **Breathing Control:** During the most difficult phase (recovery), release a small amount of air via a controlled hiss to reduce intrathoracic pressure without losing trunk rigidity. Avoid excessive breath-holding/bearing down that triggers vagal nerve stimulation.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Immediate Action:** If dizzy, stop the lift immediately.
*   **Substitution/Modification:** The text suggests reducing intensity (e.g., 50-60% 1RM) only if the athlete is in a caloric deficit or dehydrated, but emphasizes fixing nutrition/hydration first rather than just lowering weight.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Hydration & Nutrition:** Prioritize adequate water intake and electrolyte (specifically salt) consumption before training to prevent dehydration-induced dizziness.
*   **Sleep/Stress Management:** Address sleep deficits and overtraining, as exhaustion is a primary cause of the symptoms described.

**5. Specifically relevant to this athlete's profile**
*   **Night Shift Work:** The text explicitly links "major sleep deficit" (highly probable for Wed–Sun night shift workers) to dizziness during heavy cleans/front squats. This is likely the root cause of the athlete's issue given their schedule.
*   **Chronic Back Pain/Intermediate Status:** While not directly addressed, the article warns against breath-holding combined with high exertion if there is a history of hypertension or cardiovascular issues; the athlete should monitor blood pressure responses carefully during heavy front squats/cleans.
*   **Caloric Deficit Warning:** The text specifically notes that being in a caloric deficit (common during hypertrophy blocks) can trigger dizziness in front squats even when other lifts feel fine, requiring immediate dietary adjustment rather than just technique tweaks.
