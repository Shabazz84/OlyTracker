# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1726/Jerks-From-the-Rack-or-Blocks/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Volume vs. Intensity:** Jerks respond well to volume; blocks allow for multiple reps at weights >80% of max jerk (e.g., 2–3 reps) which is otherwise impossible when lowering the weight safely.
*   **Intensity Limits:** Do not use blocks for heavy singles or max attempts (>90%). The presence of blocks alters dip mechanics due to hyperconsciousness of hitting them and prevents coaches from viewing footwork angles.
*   **Frequency/Usage:** Blocks are a specialized tool, not a requirement. Use them specifically when the athlete can lift more than they can lower safely, or for strength work in the upper body/absorbing force.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Dip Depth Control:** Use blocks as a "depth gauge." Set the block height just below the target dip depth; if the lifter dips too deep and hits the plates, it provides immediate tactile feedback to keep the dip shallower.
*   **Footwork Visibility:** Avoid blocks for max attempts because they obscure the coach's view of foot placement from side/oblique angles (unless using transparent metal frames).

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Jerk Variations:** Utilize Jerks from Rack or Blocks to increase training volume at heavy loads.
*   **Equipment:** Consider building adjustable jerk tables (e.g., 38" and 44" heights) with recessed tops for plywood/rubber matting to minimize bounce compared to light metal stands.
