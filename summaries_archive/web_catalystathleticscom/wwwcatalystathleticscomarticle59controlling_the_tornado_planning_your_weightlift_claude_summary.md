# Analysis: "Controlling the Tornado" by Matt Foreman

## Relevant to This Athlete's Profile

**HIGHLY RELEVANT — Work Schedule & Planning:**
- Athlete works night shift Wed–Sun; article emphasizes "distraction factors" (work, family obligations) must be planned around
- Foreman explicitly states: "Jobs are wonderful at throwing wrenches into your machinery"
- **Implication**: Night shift work requires intentional periodization windows; avoid peaking during heavy work weeks

**RECOVERY/DOWNTIME:**
- Post-competition downtime essential, especially for older/chronically injured athletes
- For this athlete (chronic back pain): Foreman recommends 1–2 weeks of "transition lifting" with variety exercises (kettlebells, pulls, squats) after peaks to spare joints
- Non-barbell recovery (stretching, core work, swimming) explicitly mentioned

---

## Programming Principles Extracted

1. **Competition frequency**: 5–6 meets/year optimal; higher frequency = injury risk (exception: young beginners)
2. **Training cycles**: 10–12 weeks for major peaks; shorter for training meets
3. **Periodization strategy**: Distinguish between "peak meets" and "training meets" (conservative weights, minimal peaking stress)
4. **Recovery cycles**: 3–4 days post-peak for younger lifters; 1–2 weeks for older/damaged bodies
