# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1981/What-Kind-of-Jerk-Are-You-Strength-Elastic-Jerk-Styles/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Focus:** The text advocates for identifying the athlete's natural "jerk style" (Elastic vs. Strength) via a vertical jump test and training that specific rhythm/timing rather than forcing a generic technique.
*   **Frequency/Volume:** No specific sets/reps provided; emphasizes high-frequency technical practice of the identified style ("train to support the type of jerk you're built best for").

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Elastic Jerk (High dip speed, shallow depth):** "Abrupt braking in the dip," "Shorter application of force," engage arms sooner when bar reaches maximal upward speed.
*   **Strength Jerk (Low dip speed, deep depth):** "Slower braking," "Longer application of force," continue driving with legs longer before engaging arms later to match max bar speed.
*   **General:** Listen to which rhythm feels best; do not interfere with natural tendencies unless they are errors.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **For Elastic Qualities:** Countermovement jump variations, jerk dips, "jerk springs," and rebound jerks.
*   **Testing Tool:** Compare a vertical jump from a dead stop (squat) vs. a countermovement jump to determine style suitability.

*   None explicitly mentioned in this text excerpt.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Jerk Style Determination:** Crucial for an athlete using only a push jerk (no split). The text provides the methodology to decide if their "push" should be shallow/quick (elastic) or deep/slow (strength) based on their jump mechanics.
*   **Back Pain Consideration:** A "Strength Jerk" style involves a deeper dip and slower braking, which may increase spinal compression time; an "Elastic Jerk" uses a shallower dip but requires higher speed/force absorption. The text advises testing to see which is biomechanically superior for *this* individual, potentially mitigating pain by aligning technique with natural leverage rather than forcing a specific depth.
