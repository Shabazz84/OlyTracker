# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2158/The-Press-in-Snatch-Mobility-Stability-Diagnosis/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** High volume of low-intensity technical work (e.g., 3–5 sets of 3–6 reps) using light loads or PVC to master range of motion (ROM).
*   **Intensity:** Low intensity; the goal is mobility and stability, not strength. Do not increase weight until full control at the bottom position is achieved.
*   **Frequency:** Can be used as a warm-up accessory or dedicated mobility session 2–3 times per week.
*   **Progression:** Strict linear progression through positions: Standing (behind neck) → Box Squat (above parallel, vertical trunk) → Box Squat (below parallel, slight lean) → Squat with heel elevation → Flat-footed squat. Only advance when the current stage is smooth and pain-free.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Scapular Setting:** Squeeze upper inside edges of shoulder blades together; maintain this position throughout without intentional shrugging (no additional elevation).
*   **Trunk Rigidity:** Keep abs tight to prevent "hinging" at the T/L junction; aim for a continuous arch, not a sudden angle.
*   **Elbow Position:** Keep elbows close under the bar, not behind it.
*   **Bar Path/Control:** Press smoothly with no grinding or bouncing; control the descent actively (pull bar down into position) rather than letting gravity crash it onto shoulders.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Drill:** Snatch Press from Bottom Position (progressing through the box squat hierarchy).
*   **Diagnostic Tool:** Standing Snatch-Grip Press Behind Neck (to assess upper back/shoulder mobility baseline).
*   **Mobility Modifiers:** Heel elevation (for ankle restrictions) and Box Squats (to isolate lower body vs. upper body limitations).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Diagnosis Protocol:** Use the exercise to identify specific restrictions:
    *   Cannot press standing vertical = Upper back/shoulder restriction.
    *   Can press on box but not squat = Lower body restriction.
    *   Can press with heels elevated but not flat-footed = Ankle restriction.
*   **Pain Management:** Stop immediately if grinding, abrupt jerking occurs, or pain is felt at the T/L junction.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (102.5 kg, Back Squat 118kg, Chronic Back Pain)**
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** The text explicitly warns against "hinging" at the T/L junction and emphasizes a continuous arch; this is critical for an athlete with chronic back pain to prevent lumbar shear during overhead work.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** This article directly addresses the primary limiter by prescribing a diagnostic progression to isolate whether the 50kg OHS ceiling is caused by ankle, hip, or thoracic mobility issues.
*   **Transition Strategy:** As an intermediate athlete moving to Olympic lifting, this provides a safe method to build overhead stability without heavy spinal loading, which is safer than jumping straight into high-volume snatch work given the back history.
*   **Night Shift Note:** While not explicitly mentioned in the text, the low-intensity nature of these drills makes them suitable for days following night shifts where fatigue management is crucial, provided sleep quality allows for mobility focus.
