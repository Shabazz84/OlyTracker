# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2160/Why-Shrug-in-Snatch-Clean-Pulls/

1. **Programming principles**: Not addressed; text focuses solely on technical mechanics of the shrug within pulls.
2. **Technique cues**: 
   - The shrug bridges the second and third pulls (transition from hip extension to upper body action).
   - Goal: Squeeze shoulder blades together, not just lift straight up or let shoulders roll forward.
   - Prevents bar path deviation by ensuring upward momentum is met with a vertical bar path close to the body; avoiding "stiff shoulders" prevents the bar swinging forward off the hips.
3. **Exercise recommendations**: Snatch pulls and Clean pulls (specifically high-pulls) are implied as the primary vehicles for practicing this shrug transition.
4. **Recovery or mobility advice**: None mentioned in this specific excerpt.
5. **Profile relevance**: 
   - **Chronic back pain**: The text warns against "stiff shoulders" causing the bar to swing forward, which can increase shear force on the lower back; proper shrugging keeps the bar close and vertical, potentially reducing lumbar strain.
   - **No split jerk**: While this article discusses pulls (not the jerk), the principle of keeping the bar path vertical via shoulder movement is critical for a push-jerk athlete to ensure stability under the bar without relying on footwork adjustments.
