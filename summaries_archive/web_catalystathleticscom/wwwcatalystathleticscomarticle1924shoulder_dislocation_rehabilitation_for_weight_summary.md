# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1924/Shoulder-Dislocation-Rehabilitation-for-Weightlifting/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Injury Modification:** Immediately reduce volume/intensity to "extreme rest" (4 days post-injury: 0 training). Resume with pain-free, low-load accessory work before reintroducing main lifts.
*   **Frequency/Intensity:** No specific sets/reps for the injured shoulder; focus on maintaining lower body strength via alternative movements (SSB squats) at sub-maximal loads (e.g., 40kg x 10).
*   **Periodization:** Shift from competition prep to a rehabilitation block. Do not force competition numbers if training volume is compromised; prioritize healing over immediate PRs.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Grip Awareness:** Be hyper-aware of bar knurling differences between warm-up and competition; grip anxiety can lead to under-pulling and shoulder strain.
*   **Overhead Stability:** Ensure the bar is not "too far back" in the overhead position, as this creates excessive anterior/posterior shear forces on the shoulder girdle.
*   **Grip Width:** Consider a narrower snatch grip if it provides more joint stability (based on athlete anecdote), though individual anatomy varies.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Substitutes for Squats:** Safety Bar Squats (SSB) to reduce spinal shear and allow unilateral loading; SSB Pause Squats; SSB Good Mornings.
*   **Accessory Work:** Lunges, Bulgarian Split Squats, GHRs (Glute Ham Raises), Ankle stability pads.
*   **Core/Back:** Decline sit-ups, crunches, back extensions, Russian twists.
*   **Shoulder Rehab:** Kinesio taping for support; passive abduction and external rotation exercises within pain-free ROM.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Contrast Therapy:** Alternate 10 minutes of heat with 10 minutes of ice daily (after initial acute phase) to manage inflammation and blood flow.
*   **Sleep Positioning:** Use small pillows for support; shoulder pain is often worst in the morning due to poor sleeping positions.
*   **Immobilization Strategy:** Avoid rigid slings if they cause discomfort; prefer bracing the arm against the side with a slight shrug to maintain function while protecting the joint.
*   **Pain Management:** Ice (10-min bouts) and high-dose NSAIDs (ibuprofen) in the first 48 hours.

**5. Specifically relevant to this athlete's profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain & Night Shifts:** The text highlights that back pain can be exacerbated by awkward sleeping positions or rolling over during a fall. For a night shift worker with chronic back issues, strict sleep hygiene and pillow support are critical to prevent re-injury during recovery.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** Since this athlete only uses push jerks (no split), they must ensure the dip and drive mechanics do not place excessive anterior stress on the shoulder, as the lack of a split catch removes the ability to "catch" the bar with legs in a way that might mitigate shoulder strain.
*   **OHS Limiter:** The athlete's 50kg OHS is a primary limiter; however, during rehab, overhead pressing must be avoided until full ROM and stability are restored to prevent re-dislocation. Focus on SSB squats and leg strength while the shoulder heals.
*   **Psychological Factor:** Do not "fight" through pain or strain in the shoulder girdle (as the author did); this leads to catastrophic failure. Listen to the body immediately upon feeling strain.
