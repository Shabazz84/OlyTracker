# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/76/Sample-Weightlifting-Flexibility-Program/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency:** Daily execution (before training or whenever possible).
*   **Volume/Intensity:** Low intensity, high consistency focus; "relatively simple and quick."
*   **Duration:** Accumulate 2 minutes per static stretch; short holds for activation (10 sec).
*   **Periodization:** Prescribed as a daily baseline for athletes lacking mobility; not a periodized strength block.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Hang from Bar:** Hang with shoulder-width grip; actively drive chest through arms; toes can lightly touch floor/box to assist if full hang is impossible (accumulate 2 mins total).
*   **Mini-band Squat:** Unloaded squat; push knees out by driving feet against the ground (not just pushing knees outward passively).
*   **Spiderman Hold:** Lunge position; stretch hip of front leg by reaching torso and arm inside the front leg.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Foam Rolling:** T-spine (arms overhead), Glutes, Adductor/Hamstring origin, Quads.
*   **Static Stretching (Pre-workout exception):** Door jamb pec stretch, Hang from bar, Spiderman hold, Squatting ankle stretch (2 mins each).
*   **Activation:** Superman hold (10 sec), Mini-band squats (15 reps).

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Timing:** Static stretching is generally discouraged pre-workout for advanced lifters but is acceptable here because the need for flexibility outweighs minor performance degradation risks. Best time to stretch is post-training while warm.
*   **Consistency:** The biggest limiter is adherence; programs must be simple enough to do consistently rather than "fancy" routines that are skipped.
*   **Advanced vs. Novice:** Advanced lifters should use dynamic flexibility for prep; this static protocol is specifically for those lacking requisite mobility.

**5. Relevance to athlete's profile**
*   **OHS Limiter:** The text explicitly identifies the "Hang from bar" and overhead mobility work as critical for athletes struggling with Overhead Squat (OHS) mechanics, directly addressing your 50 kg OHS limiter.
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The inclusion of T-spine foam rolling and Superman holds addresses thoracic extension and posterior chain stability, which can mitigate lower back strain during lifts.
*   **Transitioning Athlete:** The program is designed for "relatively new lifters" lacking mobility to perform lifts optimally, fitting your transition phase perfectly.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** While the text notes post-workout stretching is ideal (warm body), it emphasizes that consistency matters more than perfect timing; you can adapt this routine to your night shift schedule as long as it remains daily.
