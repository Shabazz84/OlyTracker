**Text Relevance:** Yes, this is an Olympic weightlifting technique article focusing on clean catching mechanics and mobility.

*   **Not Specified:** The text focuses exclusively on technical cues and injury prevention; no specific sets, reps, intensity percentages, or periodization structures are provided.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Catch Location:** "Catch the bar at the base of your throat" (tucked into the notch between clavicles) rather than on the forward deltoids or sternum to prevent forward bar lurches.
*   **Elbow Position:** Keep elbows up in a proper position while tucking the bar.
*   **Hand Grip:** Maintain a closed fist; minimize opening hands to let the bar roll back. Avoid pinky/ring fingers popping off.
*   **"Pop and Adjust":** If flexibility prevents a perfect catch, master the "pop" (driving bar slightly off shoulders after standing) to adjust hand position for the jerk.
*   **Dip Mechanics:** Keep hips traveling straight down; do not drift forward or back at the bottom of the dip.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Mobility Stretch:** "Clean grip on bar, bar behind neck (back squat position), reach elbows forward and lift high while pushing shoulders forward and up slightly." (Mandatory pre-lift stretch).
*   **Barbell Tricep Rollout:** Rolling triceps out on a barbell on a squat stand to improve front rack flexibility.
*   **Muscle Snatches/Cleans:** Recommended for building accuracy of bar placement and regaining flexibility after injury.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Long-term Flexibility:** Acknowledges that mobility takes sustained, persistent effort over a long period; it does not improve immediately.
*   **Injury Management:** If pain is radiating (e.g., elbow), do not self-diagnose via internet searches; find a manual therapist familiar with Olympic lifting.
*   **Breathing/Blackout Prevention:** Release some air during recovery and elevate shoulders slightly in the rack position to prevent carotid compression/vagal stimulation.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain & OHS Limiter:** The article emphasizes that flexibility deficits (wrists, elbows, upper back) force lifters into poor catching positions (bar on sternum/deltoids), which leads to "long-term pain increases." Addressing the front rack mobility via the recommended stretch is critical for this athlete's specific OHS limitation.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** The text explicitly discusses the necessity of the **"Pop and Adjust"** technique, which is vital for an athlete who cannot achieve a perfect closed-fist catch due to flexibility limits but must still transition smoothly into a push jerk.
*   **Intermediate Status:** The advice to "minimize" hand opening rather than accepting it as permanent failure is relevant for an intermediate athlete aiming to refine technique before the hypertrophy block.
