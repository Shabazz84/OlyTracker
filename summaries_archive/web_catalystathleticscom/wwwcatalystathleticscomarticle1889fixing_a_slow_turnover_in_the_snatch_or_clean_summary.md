# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1889/Fixing-a-Slow-Turnover-in-the-Snatch-or-Clean/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** 3–5 sets of 5–6 reps for isolation strength (e.g., tall snatch high-pulls); lighter loads for technique drills to avoid form breakdown.
*   **Intensity:** Use sub-maximal weights for turnover work; do not load "tall muscle snatches" heavily as they are pure upper-body strength movements.
*   **Frequency/Periodization:** Focus on specific phases of the lift (initial pull, turnover, punch) individually before combining them. Treat the second and third pulls as a single continuous action rather than two separate events.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Elbow Position:** Rotate elbows to point sideways throughout the first and second pulls; keep pulling elbows up and out during the turnover. Do not shrug under the bar prematurely—let the shrug happen naturally with arm pull.
*   **Bar Proximity:** Keep body and bar in immediate proximity ("close enough to smell"); avoid slack in the system.
*   **Timing/Transition:** Initiate the pull-under almost simultaneously with hip/knee extension (finish and start overlap). Do not wait for the bar to reach its apex before moving under; treat the second and third pulls as one fluid motion.
*   **Receiving Position:** Meet the bar wherever it is at lighter weights (power clean height) rather than forcing a low squat, which causes the bar to crash on shoulders.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Tall Snatch High-Pull:** Pure upper body strength for initial pull under; use empty bar or light technique bar/dumbbells if needed.
*   **Snatch High-Pulls (Mid/Upper Thigh):** Simulates actual lift with leg drive, finishing with upper body pull. Stay flat-footed to emphasize upper body.
*   **Tall Muscle Snatch:** Isolates turnover without hip drive; keep loads very light.
*   **Muscle Snatch from Hang/Floor:** Adds weight while maintaining turnover focus.
*   **Snatch Long Pulls:** Maintains tension on the upper body throughout the movement.
*   **Drop Snatches / Snatch Balances:** Strengthens the "punch up" phase; balances improve speed and timing.
*   **High-Hang Snatches / Power Snatches off High Blocks (Mid-thigh):** Focuses on final extension and rapid change of direction.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shifts)**
*   **Back Pain:** The emphasis on "keeping elbows up," "no slack in the system," and "pulling with arms" rather than relying solely on a heavy shrug may reduce spinal shear forces if form is strict. However, the article does not address chronic pain management directly; the athlete must ensure "tall muscle snatches" (light load) do not aggravate existing back issues due to poor posture or over-extension.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** The text focuses on turnover mechanics applicable to both Snatch and Clean. For this athlete, improving the *clean* turnover is critical since they lack a split jerk; a faster turnover allows for a more stable rack position before the push jerk, potentially compensating for the lack of leg drive in the jerk phase.
*   **OHS Limiter:** The "tall muscle snatch" and "snatch high-pull from tall position" directly address upper body strength deficits (shoulders/arms) which may correlate with their 50kg OHS limitation, though these are isolation drills for turnover speed rather than direct overhead pressing.
*   **Night Shifts:** The article emphasizes developing an "aggressive mindset every time you train." For a night shift worker with irregular sleep cycles, maintaining this high-intensity mental focus is crucial to compensate for potential fatigue or reduced recovery quality on Wed–Sun shifts.
