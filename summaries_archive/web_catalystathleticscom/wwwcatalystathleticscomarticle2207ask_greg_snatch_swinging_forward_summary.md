**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency:** Incorporate the suggested drill series (High-pull → Muscle Snatch → Tall Snatch) into the warm-up for snatch workouts (e.g., 3–5 sets of 5 reps with empty bar).
*   **Intensity:** Use light loads (empty bar or very low weight) specifically to isolate and reinforce the upper body mechanics; do not use heavy weights for these technical drills.
*   **Periodization:** Treat this as a long-term correction process ("significant period of time") rather than an immediate fix, acknowledging that ingrained bad habits require sustained repetition before efficacy is realized.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Upper Body Action:** Focus entirely on the "third pull" (post-contact). The arms must continue pulling up aggressively after hip contact; do not stop pulling or reverse curl.
*   **Elbow Position:** Drive elbows to their maximal height (mobility permitting) and keep them high during the turnover.
*   **Bar Path Control:** Squeeze shoulder blades back and pull elbows back *during* the turn-over to keep the bar as close to the face/chest as possible.
*   **Aggression:** Maintain active, aggressive motion throughout the entire lift to preserve bar speed; avoid "slowing down" at contact.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Snatch High-Pulls:** From power position or standing, focusing on maximal elbow height and vertical drive while keeping the bar close.
*   **Muscle Snatches:** From power position, emphasizing the same high-elbow extension and active turnover as the high-pull.
*   **Tall Snatches:** Replicating the upper body motion of the muscle snatch from a standing start to bridge the gap to the full lift.
*   **Complexes:** Pairing Snatch High-Pulls with Snatches (light weight) to train control of the bar immediately after contact and active pull-under.
