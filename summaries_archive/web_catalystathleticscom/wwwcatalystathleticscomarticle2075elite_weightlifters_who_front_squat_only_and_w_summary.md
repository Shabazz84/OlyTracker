# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2075/Elite-Weightlifters-Who-Front-Squat-Only-And-Why-You-Shouldn/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Periodization:** Do not adopt "front squat only" programming during development years (intermediate phase). Use back squats primarily for maximal lower body strength and hypertrophy in early/mid-career phases; transition to front-squat dominance only after years of accumulated leg mass are established.
*   **Frequency/Intensity:** Utilize both exercises. Back squats should handle heavier loads to stimulate glutes, spinal erectors, and core growth. Front squats should be used at moderate-to-high intensity specifically to reinforce clean posture and torso stability.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Front Squat Focus:** The primary technical goal of the front squat is not just leg strength, but strengthening the "north-of-the-waist" muscle groups (torso, shoulder girdle, arms) required to secure a heavy clean on the shoulders.
*   **Posture:** Use front squats specifically to develop the upright torso position needed for the snatch and clean.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Mandatory Inclusion:** Implement both Back Squats and Front Squats in training cycles. Eliminating back squats is a "bad idea" for 99% of athletes, including intermediates.
*   **Exception Handling:** Only consider front-squat-only protocols if the athlete possesses extraordinary genetic gifts (e.g., CJ Cummings level) or has specific joint contraindications to back squats that cannot be managed otherwise.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Joint Management:** If an exercise causes pain (as noted with CJ Cummings regarding knees), modify the training cycle rather than eliminating a fundamental movement pattern entirely; however, do not assume this applies universally without medical assessment.
*   **Longevity Warning:** The author notes that even elite lifters may drop front squats later in their careers if they cause excessive wear ("beating my body up"), suggesting flexibility to swap exercises based on chronic pain or age.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Intermediate Phase:** As an intermediate athlete, you are in the phase where "most lower body strength will be developed through back squats." You must prioritize heavy back squats for muscle growth and spinal erector development before considering a front-squat-only approach.
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text warns against blindly following elite exceptions (like Naim Suleymanoglu) who may have different pain profiles or genetic resilience. Given your chronic back pain, the "front squat only" dogma is dangerous; you need the balanced load distribution of both squats to build a robust posterior chain without overloading specific spinal segments.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** While not explicitly detailed in this text, the author's emphasis on avoiding exercises that "beat your body up" supports your need for sustainable programming given your shift work and pain history. Do not sacrifice recovery for unproven "secret" methods.
