# Analysis: Snatch Overhead Position by Greg Everett

## Relevant to This Athlete

**1. Technique Cues (Snatch Overhead Position)**
- Bar positioned vertically over base of neck (where neck meets traps), head pushed forward slightly
- Grip width: bar should rest in hip crease when hanging at arms' length
- **Upper back (T-spine): extended and locked via pressurization** — *critical for chronic back pain athlete*
- Shoulder blades: retracted + upwardly rotated; squeeze upper inside edges together forcefully (active, not passive)
- Elbows: completely extended (not mostly); arms neutral in internal/external rotation (midway between extremes)
- Wrists: partially extended, heel of palm facing upward more than forward; bar slightly behind forearm midline
- Grip tension: only as tight as needed to control bar

**2. Specific to OHS Limiter (50 kg)**
- **Shoulder blade stability is foundational.** Poor upper back position directly compromises overhead security.
- T-spine extension + shoulder blade retraction will improve OHS comfort and load capacity.
- Elbow extension aggression needed; test for hyperextension limitations.

**3. Relevant to Chronic Back Pain**
- Proper T-spine extension and upper back positioning transfers load away from lumbar spine.
- Emphasize "pressurization and muscular effort" to stabilize trunk overhead—protects lower back.

**4. Practice/Programming Principle**
- Refine overhead position in *every* overhead rep (warm-up included); treat position as primary skill, not afterthought.
- Sloppy warm-up reps = sloppy competition reps.
