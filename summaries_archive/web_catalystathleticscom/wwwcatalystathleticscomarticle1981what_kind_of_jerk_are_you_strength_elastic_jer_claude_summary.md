# Analysis: Jerk Style Classification (Strength vs. Elastic)

## Relevant to Athlete Profile

**Critical Issue:** Athlete uses **push jerk only—no split jerk**. This article focuses on dip/drive mechanics but doesn't address push jerk limitations or transition to split jerk (which would significantly increase competition jerk capacity).

---

## Key Content Extracted

### Technique Classification
- **Elastic Jerk:** Shallow dip, quick rhythm, early arm engagement, relies on barbell elasticity
- **Strength Jerk:** Deep dip, slower rhythm, longer leg drive, later arm engagement

**Identification Method:** Compare countermovement vertical jump vs. dead-stop squat jump. Better countermovement = elastic jerk suited; better squat jump or similar = strength jerk suited.

### Relevant Cues
- Pay attention to: dip depth, dip speed, arm engagement timing, dip/drive rhythm
- Train to *support* your natural style, not fight it

### Exercise Recommendations for Elastic Quality
- Countermovement jump variations
- Jerk dips
- Jerk springs
- Rebound jerks
