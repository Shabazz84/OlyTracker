# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2200/My-3-Biggest-Mistakes-in-Weightlifting/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Simplicity over complexity:** Avoid mimicking advanced lifters' programs; stick to basics appropriate for the athlete's current level.
*   **Volume management:** Begin conservatively with volume and average intensity, then gradually increase to find a "sweet spot." High volume is rarely optimal for most athletes.
*   **Focus:** Prioritize fewer things with high focus rather than doing "a bit of everything."
*   **Evaluation:** Make single variable changes at a time to accurately evaluate effects; avoid introducing multiple new tricks simultaneously.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Fundamentals first:** Aggressively correct "chinks in the armor" immediately (breath control, trunk stabilization, balance over feet, overhead positions, basic postures). Do not allow bad habits to cement even if current performance seems adequate.
*   **Aggression:** Lifters must be aggressive; lack of aggression is a common mistake among those transitioning from other sports or coaches.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   *Note: The text does not list specific exercises.* It recommends focusing on "steady improvement of strength specific to lift postures and motions" and "regular and frequent exposure to the competition lifts and variations."

**5. Relevance to athlete profile**
*   **Transitioning Athlete:** The article warns against rushing intermediate athletes to higher levels without mastering basics, which applies directly to an athlete transitioning from powerlifting/strongman to Olympic lifting.
*   **Chronic Pain/Hypertrophy Block:** The emphasis on "trunk stabilization" and "basic postures" is critical for managing chronic back pain; the text advises taking a step backward to fix these issues now rather than risking injury later.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** While not explicitly addressed, the advice to avoid "overcomplicating programming" and "doing too much" supports an athlete with limited recovery windows due to shift work by prioritizing efficiency over excessive volume.
