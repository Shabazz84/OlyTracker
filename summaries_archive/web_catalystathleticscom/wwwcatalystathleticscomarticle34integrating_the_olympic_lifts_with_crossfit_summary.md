# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/34/Integrating-the-Olympic-Lifts-with-CrossFit/

## Key Points
*   **Isolation Priority:** Olympic lifts must be trained as isolated skill sessions prior to any metabolic conditioning or fatigue accumulation to ensure neurological freshness and prevent reinforcing poor mechanics.
*   **Progression Ladder:** Strict adherence to a progression hierarchy is required: PVC → Light Bar → Regulation Bar. Do not increase load until technique is consistent rep-to-rep with lighter implements.
*   **Frequency & Intensity:** High-frequency skill work (daily light practice) is recommended alongside 2–3 dedicated strength days per week. Skill sessions should remain at 50–60% intensity; strength days utilize intuitive loading based on daily readiness, working up to heavy singles/doubles/triples.
*   **Recovery & Adaptation:** Active recovery via light technique or mobility work is permitted on off-days, provided volume remains low. At least one full rest day per week is essential given the cumulative load of training.

## Technique Cues
*   **Primary Driver:** Execution relies exclusively on "vicious vertically-oriented extension of the legs and hips." The arms and upper back must remain passive; they are not drivers of the lift.
*   **Critical Violation Warning:** Strictly avoid "power-curling" (using arms/upper back to elevate the bar). This bypasses hip power, limits athletic transfer, and significantly increases injury risk for athletes with chronic back pain.
*   **Efficiency Goal:** Technical consistency is the prerequisite for reducing effort, fatigue, and time-to-complete during high-rep scenarios.
*   **Warm-up Protocol:** The Burgener Warm-up is the standard for initial skill practice; it must be performed correctly before introducing dynamic movement.

## Programming Principles
*   **Skill-First Approach:** Implement daily isolated skill work (PVC drills, light barbell drills focusing on lagging segments like turnover/rack position) before integrating lifts into conditioning workouts.
*   **Strength Integration:** Dedicated strength days should prioritize specific weaknesses (e.g., Overhead Squats for the athlete's 50kg OHS limiter, Front Squats to heavy doubles, Push Presses).
*   **Substitutes & Accessories:** Utilize dumbbell snatches/cleans as they do not disrupt barbell motor learning. Include accessory work such as Snatch Balances and Snatch Push Presses to build specific strength without excessive spinal loading.
*   **Flexible Loading:** For night shift workers, abandon rigid schedules in favor of "intuitive loading." Programming must adjust daily based on the athlete's state ("spitting fire" vs. "worn down") rather than a fixed calendar.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain Mitigation:** The strict adherence to leg/hip extension mechanics is a safety imperative for this athlete. Relying on arms/upper back circumvents the intended hip power and exacerbates spinal stress; correcting this is non-negotiable for injury management.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** The "intuitive loading" principle directly addresses irregular recovery windows caused by night shift work (Wed–Sun). Programming must be flexible, prioritizing daily readiness over fixed intensity targets.
*   **Transition Strategy:** As an intermediate athlete moving from general strength to Olympic lifting, the immediate focus is a 6-week hypertrophy block followed by technique/strength isolation. The athlete should not integrate lifts into conditioning (WODs) until proficiency and consistency are achieved under fatigue.
*   **Specific Limiter Addressing:** Programming must prioritize the Overhead Squat (50kg), identified as the primary snatch limiter, through dedicated accessory work like OHS and Snatch Balances within the strength sessions.
