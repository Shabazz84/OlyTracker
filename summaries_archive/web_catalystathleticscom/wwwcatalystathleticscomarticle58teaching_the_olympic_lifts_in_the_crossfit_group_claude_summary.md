# Analysis: Greg Everett - "Teaching the Olympic Lifts in the CrossFit Group Setting: Part 2"

## Relevance to Your Profile
This article is **highly relevant** to your situation as an intermediate Olympic weightlifter, particularly regarding technique progressions and the snatch.

---

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Emphasis on practice volume over shortcuts**: Everett stresses that Olympic lifts require 4–6 days/week practice for years; no quick fixes exist.
- **Progressive complexity**: Teach barbell lifts before odd-object variants (dumbbell cleans before medicine ball cleans).
- **Avoid regressive progressions**: Medicine ball cleans create poor habits that must be unlearned.

---

## 2. **Technique Cues (Most Relevant to You)**

**Snatch-specific concern:**
- The article criticizes medicine ball cleans for teaching poor pull mechanics (elbows back/around instead of up/out).
- **For your low OHS (50 kg)**: Focus on pulling under with proper elbow mechanics to develop upright receiving positions.

**Clean/Jerk mechanics:**
- Change direction explosively after hip extension—avoid hesitation at top.
- Shrug occurs *while pulling under*, not before—it doesn't elevate the bar, the athlete's descent does.
- Barbell work transfers to all implements; medicine ball work does not.

**Front squat vs. Back squat**: Everett warns that low-bar back squat emphasis creates posterior-chain dominance that *limits* front squat and Olympic lift mechanics—relevant given your chronic back pain and need for quad-dominant positioning.

---

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- **Use barbells, not medicine balls**, for clean progressions.
- **Dumbbell cleans** are acceptable intermediate tools (resemble barbell mechanics).
- **High-pulls**: Limited value; teach deadlift, cleans, snatches instead for skill development.
- Avoid sumo deadlift high-pulls; standard deadlift high-pulls are better if used briefly.

## 5. **Critical Application to Your Context**

**Your limiting factor: OHS at 50 kg**
- Everett's emphasis: snatch complexity requires *years* of deliberate practice with proper mechanics.
- Your night shift schedule (Wed–Sun) may limit daily practice—prioritize **quality reps** on available days over volume.
- Medicine ball cleans will *not* transfer; use barbell/dumbbell progressions instead.

**Chronic back pain consideration:**
- The article warns against low-bar back squat creating posterior-chain dominance, which compromises front squat and snatch positions.
- Front squat (quad-dominant) may be safer than low-bar back squat for your 6-week hypertrophy block.

---

**Bottom line**: Invest in barbell snatch/clean progressions with high coaching quality. Your OHS limitation requires dedicated snatch practice, not shortcuts.
