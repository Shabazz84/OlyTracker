# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2069/How-to-Quit-Short-Stepping-Your-Split-Jerks/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Diagnosis First:** Do not program split drills until you confirm the issue isn't a drive/elevation failure. If your Power Jerk is <85% of your Split Jerk (excluding mobility limits), prioritize drive development over footwork.
*   **Intensity/Frequency:** Use complexes to force aggression in the drive (e.g., multiple dip squats followed immediately by a jerk). Focus on high-quality, low-repetition work for timing drills rather than volume grinding.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Drive vs. Split:** If short-stepping occurs only under heavy loads but footwork is perfect with lighter weights, the issue is likely an inability to elevate the bar sufficiently or a timing error where the split happens too early.
*   **Dip Mechanics:** Focus on braking efficiently at the bottom of the dip rather than just depth. Avoid leaning forward excessively into the split; this causes the back foot to lift prematurely and the front foot to land first, shortening the stride.
*   **Timing:** Ensure the transition from the dip to the drive is explosive, not sluggish. The length of the drive must match the time needed for the feet to travel under the bar.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **For Drive/Elevation:** Push press, pause jerk, power jerk, push jerk, concentric jerk, jumping squat, jumping quarter squat.
*   **For Dip/Braking:** Jerk dip, jerk spring, jerk dip squat.
*   **For Split Position (Only if drive is confirmed adequate):** Tall jerk, step to split, drop to split, jerk from split, split squats.
*   **Note on "Split Cleans":** The author advises against using split cleans as a primary tool; they are less effective than light power cleans for reinforcing the position and do not fix drive issues.
