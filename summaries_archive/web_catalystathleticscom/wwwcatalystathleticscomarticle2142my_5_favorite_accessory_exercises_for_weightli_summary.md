# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2142/My-5-Favorite-Accessory-Exercises-for-Weightlifters/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Volume/Frequency:** Treat as "accessory" work; do not prescribe fixed sets/reps. Use a **time cap (e.g., 15 minutes)** to prevent workout extension and manage recovery load.
*   **Intensity/Periodization:** Generally safe for high frequency, but reduce volume/intensity in the final weeks before competition (maintenance phase) or drop completely in the taper week.
*   **Placement:** Can be added after main lifts without significantly impacting recovery capacity, provided the time cap is respected.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Turkish Get-Up:** Use a "high-bridge" variation; lift hips high before lunging. Focus on **constant tension and stability**; move deliberately rather than rushing.
*   **Pull-ups:** Essential for balancing pressing/jerking motions to preserve shoulder mobility.
*   **Single-Leg RDL:** Maintain **control** throughout the descent; do not bounce the weight off the floor. Hold the weight in the arm *opposite* the working leg (contralateral loading).
*   **Overhead Carries:** Establish a **strong fixed scapular position** and strong hand/wrist grip. Focus on stability while moving (walking, lunging, etc.).
*   **Step-ups:** Avoid using the back leg to push off; drive through the front leg only. Start low/light to prioritize form over ego.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
1.  **Turkish Get-Up** (High-bridge variation)
2.  **Pull-up**
3.  **Single-Leg RDL** (Contralateral dumbbell/kettlebell hold preferred)
4.  **Overhead Carries** (Barbell or kettlebell; various directions/terrains)
5.  **Step-ups** (Adjust shin angle for posterior chain vs. quad emphasis; use slow eccentrics)

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Mobility:** The high-bridge TGU specifically targets hip flexor mobility and glute activation. Pull-ups aid shoulder mobility preservation.
*   **Recovery Management:** Use time caps rather than set counts to prevent overtraining. Do not let accessory work extend the total session duration excessively.

**5. Relevance to athlete profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The **Single-Leg RDL** and **Step-ups** are highlighted for stability and unilateral strength without the spinal loading of heavy squats or cleans. However, the text does not explicitly address back pain; the athlete must ensure the "controlled tempo" cues prevent compensatory rounding.
*   **Night Shift (Wed–Sun):** The advice to use a **time cap** is critical here. Night shift workers often have erratic sleep schedules; limiting accessory work to 15 minutes ensures the session remains manageable regardless of fatigue levels, preventing excessive systemic stress that could disrupt recovery sleep windows.
*   **Transitioning Athlete:** The text warns against treating accessories as "magical secrets." For an athlete with a weak OHS (snatch limiter), these exercises build foundational stability but will not directly fix the overhead position; they must be viewed as supportive, not primary drivers of technique.
