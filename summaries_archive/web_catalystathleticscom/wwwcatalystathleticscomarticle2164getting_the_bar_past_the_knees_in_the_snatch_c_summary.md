# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2164/Getting-the-Bar-Past-the-Knees-in-the-Snatch-Clean/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency/Volume:** High frequency of low-intensity technical work (slow pulls) is prioritized over heavy loading for the first pull phase.
*   **Intensity:** Focus on sub-maximal loads to perfect mechanics; no specific rep ranges given, but implies high volume of technique drills.
*   **Periodization:** Emphasizes isolating phases (floor-to-thigh) before integrating into full lifts.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **"Think of the first pull as a squat":** Stand up with the bar; do not actively "push knees back." Knee retraction is a natural byproduct of standing up.
*   **Constant Back Angle:** Maintain the same torso angle from floor to thigh initiation; shoulders must move up commensurately with hips (no tipping over).
*   **Knee Path:** Keep knees pushed out laterally until the bar passes them, then allow them to return to a natural position. Do not push them out further during the pull.
*   **Shin Angle:** Avoid vertical shins at knee height; maintain forward shin inclination and knee bend to prevent early hip extension.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Segment Deadlifts/Pulls with Pauses:** Isolate the first pull with 2–3 second pauses at:
    *   1 inch off floor (Shoulders over bar).
    *   Knee height (Shoulders slightly forward, balance on whole foot).
    *   Mid-thigh (Shoulders in front of bar, bar touching thighs).
*   **Floating Deadlifts/Pulls on Riser:** Use a riser to simulate floor start position while naturally correcting posture.
*   **Slow-Pull Snatches/Cleans:** Perform full lifts at slow speed to integrate balance and position.

**5. Athlete-Specific Relevance**
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** The cue "Think of the first pull as a squat" and maintaining a constant back angle directly addresses the risk of lumbar shear associated with early hip extension (tipping over), which is crucial for an athlete with chronic back pain.
*   **Starting Position Correction:** For an intermediate lifter, ensuring shoulders are directly above the bar at the start prevents the "pulling the bar back into legs" error that often stems from poor setup rather than lack of strength.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** While not mentioned in text, the recommendation for "slow speed" drills allows for high-quality movement practice without excessive systemic fatigue, which is beneficial for a shift worker with potentially disrupted sleep cycles.
