## Analysis: Strap Use in Olympic Weightlifting

**1. Programming Principles**
- Straps allow increased weight/volume but don't build actual grip capacity
- Grip work should be incorporated directly into programming
- Advanced lifters: incrementally reduce strap dependency (warm-ups first, then working sets)
- Beginners: avoid straps entirely—grip/mobility/technique are limiting factors, not strength

**2. Technique Cues**
- **Cleans**: Never use straps (injury risk on misses; harder to release than snatches)
- **Snatches**: Minimize strap use; emphasize proper lowering technique to reduce grip demand
- **Pulls/Deadlifts**: Straps acceptable for advanced lifters to preserve pulling speed/strength; beginners should avoid

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
- Direct grip strength work (program-dependent)
- RDLs/SLDLs without straps (no acceleration demands, lighter loads)
- Pull/deadlift variations: straps justified for advanced lifters, but rotate them strategically

**4. Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- Hand health preservation via judicious strap use in high-volume double-session training
- Example: straps in AM power work, no straps in PM floor snatches
