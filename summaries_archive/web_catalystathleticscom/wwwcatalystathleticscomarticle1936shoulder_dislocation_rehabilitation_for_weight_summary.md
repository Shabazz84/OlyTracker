**No, this text is a personal case study of an elite coach recovering from major shoulder surgery (labrum/rotator cuff repair), not a general coaching guide for an intermediate athlete.**

However, based on the specific details Greg Everett provides regarding his rehabilitation process, here are the relevant extractions for your profile:

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Post-Immobilization:** Expect a "slow start" where strength returns faster than range of motion (ROM). Strength loss from 2 months off is recoverable quickly once loading resumes.
*   **Frequency:** Rehab exercises (isometrics, passive stretches) must be performed **2–3 times daily**.
*   **Progression:** Follow a strict "no more and no less" rule regarding PT recommendations; do not rush loading or ROM expansion before the capsule is ready.

**2. Technique Cues / Movement Patterns**
*   **Grip Modification:** Use a strap on one side for front squats if grip/shoulder flexion prevents holding the barbell normally.
*   **Bar Selection:** Utilize safety squat bars (SSB) or cambered bars initially to reduce shoulder strain during lower body work.
*   **Positioning:** Avoid "unusual strain" and improper positioning; consistent, proper technique is the primary preventative measure against re-injury.

**3. Exercise Recommendations (Rehab Phase)**
*   **Early Stage (Post-Sling):** Assisted active flexion (using good arm), passive external rotation with PVC pipe, wall crawls, prone scapular retraction/depression holds.
*   **Intermediate Stage:** Presses with PVC pipe (partial range), pulley flexion, band scapular retractions, snatch/clean grip flexion on foam roller.
*   **Loading:** Start with unweighted squats/lunges; progress to SSB squats; very light dumbbell curls/shrugs only if pain-free.

**4. Recovery & Mobility Advice**
*   **Capsule Management:** The joint capsule often feels like "concrete" post-immobilization; passive stretching is required to break this stiffness, though it will be painful at end-ranges.
*   **Pain Management:** Expect significant pain during passive stretches and PNF contract-relax techniques; distinguish between "good" rehab pain and injury flare-ups.
*   **Sleep/Rest:** Prioritize sleep hygiene as recovery is difficult (often requiring sleeping on the couch or in uncomfortable positions initially).

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain vs. Shoulder Injury:** While this text focuses on shoulders, the principle of "pain tolerance during rehab" applies. If your back pain flares with heavy loading, reduce volume immediately rather than pushing through.
*   **Night Shift Work:** The text highlights that recovery is a long-term process (10–12 months for full return). For a night shift worker, consistency in daily rehab (2-3x/day) is critical to counteract the fatigue and circadian disruption that may slow tissue repair.
*   **Transitioning Athlete:** Since you are an intermediate athlete with a 50kg OHS limiter, be aware that overhead mobility deficits (like the "concrete" capsule described here) will directly cap your snatch/clean overhead potential until resolved. Do not attempt to force overhead range of motion without addressing the underlying capsular tightness first.
