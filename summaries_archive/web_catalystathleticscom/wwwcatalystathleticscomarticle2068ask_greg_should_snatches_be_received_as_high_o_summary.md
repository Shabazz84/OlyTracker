# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2068/Ask-Greg-Should-Snatches-Be-Received-as-High-or-Low-as-Possi/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Intensity/Force:** Prioritize maximal effort in both the second pull (explosion) and third pull (turnover). Do not reduce force on the second pull to artificially lower the receiving position; this creates bad habits of "not finishing aggressively."
*   **Receiving Depth:** Receiving depth is determined by the relative mass of the bar vs. the lifter, not a conscious decision to squat deeper. As weight increases, the bar travels less upward during turnover, naturally forcing a deeper catch if the pull remains maximal.
*   **Connection:** Focus on securing the bar overhead "as high and as soon as possible" within the squat movement to maintain stability. Avoid dropping out from under the bar (power snatching style) which causes instability.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **"High and Soon":** Aim to fix the bar into a secure overhead position immediately upon turnover, rather than waiting for the bottom of the squat.
*   **Stay Connected:** Do not drop out from under the bar; maintain connection between the lifter and the bar throughout the descent.
*   **Aggressive Turnover:** Execute an aggressive third pull regardless of receiving height; do not confuse "aggressive turnover" with "receiving low."

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   *Not explicitly listed in this specific excerpt, but implied:* Power Snatches (to demonstrate high reception with maximal effort) and Full Snatch Squats (with the caveat to maintain connection).
*   *Note:* The text advises against using lighter weights to practice "receiving low" by limiting second-pull force.

**5. Relevance to athlete profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The emphasis on "staying connected" and avoiding dropping out from under the bar is critical for spinal safety, as it reduces shear forces caused by losing control of the bar's trajectory.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** This clarifies a common confusion: you do not need to sacrifice explosive power (second pull) to achieve a deep squat; the depth will naturally adjust based on the weight and your turnover speed if you maintain maximal effort.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** While this text focuses on the snatch, the principle of "securing overhead high and soon" applies directly to his jerk technique to ensure stability before driving down.
