# Analysis: Grip Strength and Training for Weightlifting (Greg Everett)

## 1. Programming Principles
- **Specificity first**: Focus grip work on snatch/clean grips only (hook grip); avoid generic grip exercises that don't transfer
- **Efficiency over volume**: Don't add extra work; integrate grip training into existing lifts
- **Progression**: Reduce strap dependency gradually across warm-ups → work sets; introduce gripper training at reps of 2–5 with heavy resistance, building to 3–5 sec holds
- **Periodization note**: Gripper progression (move to next level once hitting 5 reps × 5 sec holds for 3 sets)

## 2. Technique Cues
- **Hook grip emphasis** for snatches/cleans
- **Hand positioning**: Ring and pinkie fingers placement mirrors snatch/clean grips (not deeper in palm)
- **Advanced cue**: Practice snatches/cleans WITHOUT hook grip during warm-ups; remove thumbs entirely (thumbs straight out, not gripping)
- **Gripper specificity**: Emphasize ring/pinkie fingers closing the gripper (mimics bar position)

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
- **Slow lifts without straps first**: RDLs, stiff-legged deadlifts, rows (low speed = minimal impact if grip fails)
- **Classic lifts**: Gradually reduce strap use in power snatches, muscle snatches, hang/block snatches
- **Gripper work** (if time permits): 2–5 reps, heavy resistance; 3–5 sec closed holds; warm-up with light gripper (2×5)

## 4. Recovery/Mobility
- **Wrist health noted** but not extensively covered; related article mentions wrist extensions/rubber band finger spreading for golfer's elbow
