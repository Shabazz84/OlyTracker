# Analysis: Everett on Proficiency vs. Efficiency

## Relevance to Athlete Profile
**Moderately relevant.** This is a philosophical/technical article, not a training program. The athlete's chronic back pain and push jerk limitation (no split jerk) suggest technique proficiency issues that directly relate to Everett's core argument.

---

## Key Points Extracted

### 1. **Programming/Philosophy**
- **Proficiency > Efficiency**: Goal is maximizing results from effort, not minimizing effort. Prioritize weight lifted, not energy wasted.
- **Direct application**: The athlete's weak OHS (50 kg vs 80 kg clean) and push jerk-only pattern indicate technical deficiencies to address before hypertrophy block. Build proficiency first.

### 2. **Technique Cues**

**Bar Path (Snatch/Clean)**
- Slight S-curve is a *result* of good mechanics, not a goal itself
- Back→forward→back movement (relative to lifter) is natural; don't force vertical lines
- Curved path allows optimal body-bar interaction and balance
- **Note**: Heavier bars produce flatter S-curves; lighter bars more pronounced

**Overhead Position (Jerk/OHS)**
- Arm internal/external rotation varies by individual structure
- Elbows positioned ~halfway between down and back (neutral-ish rotation)
- Don't chase "new secrets"—focus on your mechanics

### 3. **Exercise/Technical Approach Recommendations**
- **Avoid chasing trends**: Don't copy one lifter's outlier technique
- **Understand mechanics vs. descriptions**: Double knee bend, S-pull, etc. are descriptions of phenomena, not prescriptive techniques
- **Individual variation matters**: Structure/morphology create different optimal positions
- **Tailor to context**: Competitive OL technique ≠ high-rep/CrossFit technique

## **Specific to This Athlete**

- **OHS weakness (50 kg)**: Likely reflects mobility *and* technical proficiency gaps. Everett's framework suggests diagnosing where mechanics break down (shoulder, ankle, thoracic extension?) rather than chasing "new" cues.
- **Push jerk only**: Building split jerk requires technical proficiency *first*; don't force efficient movement patterns that limit receiving position depth.
- **Chronic back pain**: Everett's emphasis on optimal body-bar positioning (balance, mechanics) over arbitrary efficiency may help—poor proficiency often loads the spine unnecessarily.

## **TL;DR**
Prioritize technical proficiency and optimal mechanics (which naturally produce efficiency) over chasing efficiency directly or viral technique trends. Bar path curves slightly as a natural result; don't force vertical lines. Individual variation is real—dial in YOUR mechanics.
