# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2266/Tipping-in-The-Squat-Legs-Not-Hips/

### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Focus:** Prioritize leg strength over posterior chain (hip) strengthening to correct forward tipping.
*   **Tempo/Intensity:** Implement slow eccentrics (3–6 seconds down). Temporarily reduce squat load to ensure perfect posture; quality reps must overwhelm suboptimal ones.
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Limit heavy squats with poor form to one weekly session (top sets only, low reps). The majority of volume should be dedicated to corrective variations (pauses, eccentrics) at lower intensities.
*   **Periodization:** Dedicate an initial phase to rebuilding postural strength before returning to maximal loads.

### 2. Technique Cues
*   **Primary Cue:** "Legs not hips." The body tips forward because the legs are too weak to open the knee joint from a small angle against resistance, forcing the hips to rise prematurely.
*   **Knee Control:** Prevent knees from moving inward (valgus), which forces hips back and chest down; engage adductors to keep knees tracking properly.
*   **Trunk Stability:** Maintain maximal trunk bracing throughout the descent and ascent; avoid "rounding" of the upper back before hip extension occurs.
*   **Bottom Position:** Eliminate bouncing at the bottom of the squat, as rebounding can trigger tipping unrelated to strength deficits.

### 3. Exercise Recommendations
*   **Slow Eccentric Squats:** 3–6 second descent to emphasize leg engagement in the mid-to-lower range.
*   **Parallel (Pause) Squats:** Sit to parallel, pause 2–3 seconds, then stand.
*   **Segment Squats:** Squat to parallel, pause, drop to bottom, and recover with perfect posture.
*   **Adductor Work:** Direct strengthening of adductors to prevent knee valgus.
*   **Pulling Variations:** High-pull from risers or floating pulls if the athlete is pulling with high hips/forward lean (though this article focuses on squat mechanics).

### 4. Recovery or Mobility Advice
*   **Bracing:** Maintain constant tension and bracing throughout the lift; do not relax at the bottom.
*   **Self-Correction:** If back rounding occurs *before* hips move up/back, focus on trunk strength/bracing. If hips move up/back first, focus on leg strength.

### 5. Relevance to Athlete Profile
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The article explicitly links forward tipping and knee valgus to increased spinal moment arms and back rounding. Correcting the "legs not hips" tipping mechanism is critical for reducing load on the lumbar spine, directly addressing the athlete's chronic pain.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The advice to reduce weight temporarily to fix mechanics aligns with the need to build a robust foundation before increasing intensity in a hypertrophy block.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** While not explicitly mentioned, the emphasis on "maximal trunk bracing" and avoiding "lazy reps" is crucial for an athlete managing fatigue from shift work; poor posture under fatigue exacerbates injury risk.
