# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/112/Weightlifting-Technique-Drills-Training-Lifts/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Purpose-Driven Loading:** Differentiate drills (light weight/volume for motor learning) from training exercises (heavier weights for strength/power). Never exceed the load at which technical execution remains perfect; "improper execution strengthens improper patterns."
*   **Progression:** Move from teaching drills (empty bar/PVC initially) to technique drills, then to high-volume training exercises as proficiency increases.
*   **Intermediate Stage Rule:** Once basic skills are learned, weight should be progressively increased but strictly capped at the athlete's technical ability. Technical drills become a minor component used only to address specific issues arising during heavy loading.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Staying Over the Bar:** Use "halting snatch deadlift" (stopping at hips with shoulders over bar) followed immediately by a snatch to correct early hip opening or dropping under too late.
*   **Positional Strength:** If an athlete reverts to bad positions under load, use specific training exercises (e.g., halting deadlifts without the pull-through) to build strength in the *correct* position before reintegrating the full movement.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Teaching Drills:** PVC pipe (for absolute beginners only), empty barbell complexes.
*   **Technique/Training Exercises:** Snatch balance variations, pressing variations, halting snatch deadlifts, classic lifts (snatch, clean & jerk) performed at sub-maximal loads to reinforce patterns.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   *None explicitly detailed in this text.* The article focuses on programming philosophy and movement mechanics rather than recovery protocols or specific mobility routines.

**5. Relevance to athlete profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Management:** The text supports using "halting deadlifts" as a training exercise to build positional strength without the spinal shear of full pulls if form breaks down under load. This allows for strength development while minimizing pain triggers associated with poor mechanics.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** Validates the athlete's need to stop relying on raw strength (which reinforces bad habits) and prioritize "strengthening the correct movement" before increasing intensity.
*   **Night Shift/Chronic Pain Context:** While not explicitly mentioned, the emphasis on "never taking loading beyond what allows proper execution" is critical for an athlete with chronic pain; it mandates listening to the body's mechanical limits rather than pushing through fatigue or pain via high volume/intensity.
*   **PVC Pipe Note:** The author debunks PVC pipe usage for established lifters, suggesting this intermediate athlete should focus on barbell technique drills and complexes rather than light "learning" tools unless used for specific remediation.
