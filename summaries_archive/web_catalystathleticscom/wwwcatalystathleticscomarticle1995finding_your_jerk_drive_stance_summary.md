# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1995/Finding-Your-Jerk-Drive-Stance/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** High frequency of specific jerk variations (jerk dip squats, jerk dips, jerk drives) to experiment with stance width and toe angle.
*   **Intensity:** Heavy weights used during experimentation phases to identify the most powerful bottom position.
*   **Periodization:** Focus on individual adaptation ("adjust to best suit each athlete's build") rather than a rigid template; prioritize finding the optimal stance before increasing load.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Stance Width:** Start at hip-width or slightly wider; widen if legs are long or weak.
*   **Toe Angle:** Turn toes out 10–20 degrees relative to centerline.
*   **Leg Orientation:** Aim for vertical leg alignment in the drive stance (maximizes force transfer), but prioritize a "quick dip and abrupt stop" over perfect verticality if it hinders braking.
*   **Dip Initiation:** Knees must be unlocked with tension already on the quads; do not start from a passive lock or hyperextension to avoid "freefalling" and losing bar connection/balance.
*   **Braking:** The ability to brake abruptly in the bottom of the dip is more critical than perfect vertical leg orientation for generating elastic recoil.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Jerk Dip Squats** (to feel strength in the bottom position).
*   **Jerk Dips** (isolated movement practice).
*   **Jerk Drives** (focus on the transition from dip to drive).
*   *Note:* The text specifically references these for finding the optimal stance rather than standard heavy jerks.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Push Jerk Specificity:** The text's focus on "vertical leg orientation" and "braking" is highly relevant for a push jerk athlete, as the mechanics of stopping the dip are identical regardless of foot placement (split vs. push).
*   **Back Pain Consideration:** The emphasis on maintaining tension in the quads *before* initiating the dip helps prevent the "freefall" that can cause spinal shear or loss of balance, which is crucial for an athlete with chronic back pain.
*   **Stance Experimentation:** The advice to experiment with stance width and toe angle is vital for a 102.5 kg athlete to find a position that minimizes lumbar stress while maximizing force production.
*   **Limitation Address:** While the text doesn't mention the OHS, the principle of "vertical leg orientation" applies to the push jerk drive; ensuring the legs are vertical helps direct force upward rather than forward, which can reduce shear on the lower back during the drive phase.
