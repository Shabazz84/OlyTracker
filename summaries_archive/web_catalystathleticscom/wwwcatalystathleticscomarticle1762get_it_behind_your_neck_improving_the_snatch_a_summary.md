# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1762/Get-it-Behind-Your-Neck-Improving-the-Snatch-and-Jerk-Overhe/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** 5–8 reps per set; avoid heavy singles for these specific movements initially.
*   **Intensity:** Light to moderate; loads must be smooth with "no grinding" at the bottom or middle of the movement.
*   **Frequency:** Beginners/mobility work: daily (low load). Advanced/strength focus: early in training cycles, frequency reduced as load increases.
*   **Progression:** Start with mobility-focused light push presses; increase loading only as mobility improves and upper back strength fortifies.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Overhead Position:** Focus on "straight bar path" from behind the neck to ingrain correct positioning.
*   **Shoulder Girdle:** Force upper back extension and shoulder girdle opening; retract and slightly elevate shoulder blades to create a solid shelf for the bar.
*   **Bar Placement:** Guide the bar carefully into position; ensure it does not slip off the back (critical for wrist safety).

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Snatch Variations:** Snatch push presses, snatch press (seated/standing), and "snatch Sots press" (snatch-grip behind-the-neck press from squat bottom).
*   **Jerk Variations:** Press/push press behind the neck, split push press behind the neck, jerk behind the neck, power jerk behind the neck.
*   **Mobility/Beginner Work:** Women's bar or PVC push presses behind the neck (if full range is unavailable).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Safety Check:** Athletes with extreme inflexibility should not attempt these lifts as they will "beat up the shoulders"; use leg assistance (push press) only if mobility allows safe execution.
*   **Daily Routine:** Beginners with mobility issues should perform behind-the-neck pressing exercises daily (barring pain).
*   **Execution:** Ensure smooth movement through bottom and middle phases; stop immediately if grinding occurs or pain is felt.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **OHS Limiter:** The article directly addresses the primary limiter (Overhead Squat/Press) by prescribing behind-the-neck presses to improve overhead mobility and stability.
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** While beneficial for upper back extension, the athlete must ensure "no grinding" and start with very light loads or PVC/Women's bar to avoid aggravating existing pain; the text warns that inflexible athletes risk shoulder injury if they force the position.
*   **Transitioning Athlete:** The recommended rep ranges (5–8) and focus on technique over heavy singles are ideal for an intermediate athlete refining mechanics before a hypertrophy block.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** Daily low-intensity mobility work is suggested, but given the night shift schedule, the athlete must monitor fatigue closely to avoid overuse injuries in the shoulders/back during daily sessions.
