# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/138/Lift-Before-You-Lift-Weightlifting-Training-Warm-ups/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** 3–5 sets of 3–5 reps for warm-up variations; keep weights light to avoid fatigue before main lifts.
*   **Intensity:** Sub-maximal; prioritize smooth execution and full lockout over load. Avoid "grinding" or using momentum to compensate for mobility deficits.
*   **Frequency/Placement:** Perform immediately prior to the associated lift (e.g., Sots Press before Snatch) or as standalone warm-up drills.
*   **Periodization:** Use these drills throughout the training cycle, adjusting volume/intensity based on daily readiness and specific technical needs.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Sots Press:** Keep feet flat (no rolling to inside edges); maintain upright posture without hunching; ensure elbows are fully locked out overhead.
*   **Muscle Snatch/Clean:** Aggressively pull elbows up high before turnover; internally rotate arms in the start position; stay connected to the bar (don't release hook grip early in clean).
*   **Tall Split Jerk:** Set bar at hairline with head pulled back and elbows spread under the bar; balance over heels; split feet only (no forward chest lean); land 50-50 on feet.
*   **Split Drills:** Hold split position for 3–5 seconds to build comfort/strength; recover by bringing front foot back first, then rear foot up.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Snatch Prep:** Snatch Sots Press (press from bottom of squat with snatch grip), Muscle Snatch.
*   **Clean Prep:** Clean Sots Press, Muscle Clean.
*   **Jerk Prep:** Tall Split Jerk (light press to forehead/hairline), Jump-to-Split or Drop-to-Split (bar on back).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Thoracic/Shoulder Mobility:** Foam roller rollouts (perpendicular to spine, arms overhead with light weights); narrow half-roll on T-spine with elbows bent 90°; hanging from pull-up bar leaning forward into arms.
*   **General:** Do not "abuse" joints by forcing range of motion; if an exercise cannot be performed fluidly, it is not the right choice for that day.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** The article emphasizes maintaining a tight trunk and avoiding "Quasimodo-esque" hunching during Sots presses, which directly addresses chronic back pain by reinforcing spinal stability in the squat position.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** While not explicitly mentioned for night shifts, the advice to keep warm-up weights light (3-5 reps) is crucial for a fatigued athlete (night shift worker) to prevent injury without exhausting limited recovery resources before training.
*   **Jerk Limitation:** The text specifically details the "Tall Split Jerk" and "Drop-to-Split," which are essential for an athlete restricted to push jerks who needs to master foot placement and elbow extension mechanics, even if not performing a split jerk in competition.
*   **Mobility Focus:** The specific thoracic mobility drills (foam rolling/hanging) target the overhead position limitations often seen in intermediate lifters transitioning from powerlifting backgrounds.
