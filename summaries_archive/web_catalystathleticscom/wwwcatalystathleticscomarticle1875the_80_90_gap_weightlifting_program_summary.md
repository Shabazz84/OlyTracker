# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1875/The-80-90-Gap-Weightlifting-Program/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Loading Strategy:** Utilize an "80–90% Gap" range for competition lifts (Snatch, Clean & Jerk).
*   **Intensity:** Daily intensity is self-regulated between 80% and 90% of the athlete's current 1RM based on daily performance state.
    *   *Good days:* Up to 90%.
    *   *Bad days:* Minimum 80%.
    *   *Constraint:* Do not exceed 90% or drop below 80%.
*   **Rep Scheme:** Strictly singles (or multiple singles) for the main lifts. No sets of 5, 3, or doubles within this specific loading range for the competition lifts.
*   **Frequency/Periodization:** Designed as a non-peaking block suitable for general maintenance and development. Best used up to 5–6 weeks before a meet; transition to exact pre-planned weights thereafter.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   *None explicitly detailed in this text.* The article focuses entirely on loading parameters rather than biomechanical cues.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Focus:** Competition lifts (Snatch, Clean & Jerk) and variants (Rack Jerk).
*   **Secondary/Accessory:** The author notes that exercises like pulls and squats should be programmed differently (likely using sets of 5 or 3) as they do not fit the "singles-based" loading model.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   *None explicitly detailed in this text.* The article mentions avoiding overtraining by capping intensity at 90%, but offers no specific mobility protocols or recovery strategies (e.g., sleep, nutrition).

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Strength Level:** The program is suitable for an intermediate athlete looking to refine technique and maintain strength without the high fatigue of heavy singles (>90%).
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The 80–90% cap helps mitigate injury risk compared to maxing out, but the "singles-only" nature for main lifts requires careful warm-up management.
*   **Night Shift Work (Wed–Sun):** The program's reliance on "daily intuition" and self-regulation is highly relevant here. It allows the athlete to adjust intensity based on fatigue levels caused by shift work without rigidly following a schedule that might force high-intensity training during poor recovery windows.
*   **Goal (Hypertrophy Block):** This specific text describes a *strength/technique maintenance* model, not a hypertrophy block. It is **not** suitable for the immediate 6-week hypertrophy goal described in your profile; it should be utilized after the hypertrophy phase or during the transition to technique work.
*   **Jerk Style:** The program applies generally to "Rack Jerk," which aligns with a push jerk focus, though specific technical cues for split vs. push are absent.
