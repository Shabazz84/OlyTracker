## 2. **Technique Cues: Snatch & Clean**

**Hip-Bar Contact (Core Message):**
- Bar should remain **as close to thighs as possible without contact** until hips finish extending
- Shoulders must stay **slightly in front of bar** until bar reaches hips (snatch) or upper thighs (clean)
- **Final explosion occurs late**—prioritize pull-under over bar elevation
- Hips **must contact the bar** at extension completion; separation indicates incomplete hip extension or balance issues

**Extension Mechanics:**
- Hip extension must be **extremely violent**
- Legs push against platform **for entire duration** of hip extension (maintains vertical force, prevents forward bar drift)
- Hip extension should drive bar **up and slightly back into hips**, not drive hips forward excessively
- Goal: keep hips **within vertical plane** (shoulder-hip-ankle alignment)—prevents forward momentum loss
- Bar should **briefly brush upward** along body as hips come through (not dragging; not explosive bounce-away)

**Finishing Position:**
- Extension ends with bar in hip contact, legs ~vertical, hips opened beyond neutral (shoulders behind hips)
- **Body is never vertical at lift finish**—this is emphasized consistently

## 5. **Athlete-Specific Application**

**For this athlete:**
- **Weak OHS (50 kg)** → Focus on receiving position depth and stability; proper hip-bar contact ensures upright torso in turnover
- **Push jerk only** → Understanding hip snap mechanics transfers to jerk drive phase (similar violent hip extension)
- **Chronic back pain** → Maintaining vertical leg drive and avoiding excessive forward bar drift reduces spinal shear
- **Night shift fatigue** → Technique clarity (brush vs. bang is context-dependent, not dogmatic) allows flexible, efficient practice
- **6-week hypertrophy block** → After hypertrophy, refined extension mechanics will maximize strength gains

---

**Key Takeaway:** No single "correct" technique exists; individualize hip-bar contact based on what produces vertical bar elevation without forward drift.
