## Analysis: "The Back-Off Week for Weightlifting Programs" by Mike Gray

**Relevant to this athlete:**

### 1. Programming Principles
- **De-load/back-off week structure**: Reduce intensity (65–80% range) while maintaining technical focus, not complete rest
- **Frequency**: Applies to intermediate athletes cycling through mesocycles (e.g., after 3 weeks of heavy work)
- **Timing**: Schedule proactively every 3–4 weeks; best initiated when training is going well, not when fatigued
- **Relevance to athlete**: After the 6-week hypertrophy block, a back-off week before strength/technique phase would aid recovery and manage chronic back pain

### 2. Exercise Recommendations
Example session shown:
- Snatch: 65% (2), 70% (2), 75% (1), 80% (1) — **3 singles at top load**
- Snatch pulls: 95–100% (2 reps × 2 sets)
- Back squat: 70–80% (3 reps)

**Relevance**: Light snatch work + pulls could address OHS/snatch technique limiter without aggravating back.

### 3. Recovery & Mobility Advice
- Warm-ups and recovery rituals maintained (same rigor as normal weeks)
- Complete rest days respected; avoid "poking around" the gym
- Mental recovery prioritized; keep sessions brief
- **Relevant to night-shift athlete**: Maximize this week's rest quality

### 4. Key Principles for This Athlete
- Don't skip de-loads due to back pain; lighter intensity reduces injury risk
- Trust the program; resist ego-driven additions
- Timing: Plan de-loads strategically, not reactively

**Not addressed**: No specific guidance for chronic back pain or split jerk development.
