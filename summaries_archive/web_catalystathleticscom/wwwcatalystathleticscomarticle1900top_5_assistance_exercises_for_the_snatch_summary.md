# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1900/Top-5-Assistance-Exercises-for-the-Snatch/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** Assistance exercises generally prescribed in low reps (2–5) to prioritize technique and speed over hypertrophy volume. Muscle snatches and snatch balances often used as warm-up couplets or standalone strength sets of 3–5. Tall snatches specifically recommended at 25–30% intensity for 2–3 reps.
*   **Intensity:** Snatch pulls performed at 90–105% of max snatch (adjusted by athlete level); assistance lifts should be weighted to allow perfect technical execution rather than maximal load.
*   **Frequency/Periodization:** Exercises serve as "primers" before main lift training, warm-up components, or standalone finishers. Advanced athletes progress from overhead squats to snatch balances within a cycle.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Snatch Pulls:** Emphasize leg drive ("push with the legs"), elbow elevation ("up and out"), bar proximity, and balance. Use pauses to strengthen specific positions or risers/blocks to emphasize the top of the pull.
*   **Muscle Snatch:** Focus on "aggressive punch" for lockout; keep elbows high during turnover (do not let them drop); ensure leg drive is present.
*   **Snatch Balance:** Emphasize receiving position strength, timing of overhead lockout, footwork precision, and aggressive turnover.
*   **Dip Snatch:** Train final aggressive leg drive, proper balance in the dip phase, bar proximity during the third pull, and avoid overreaching hips through the bar.
*   **Tall Snatch:** Minimize the initial "cheat" (lifting slightly before dropping); move down under the bar as far/quickly as possible; plant feet flat directly under the bar; pull elbows up/out aggressively.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Snatch Pulls:** Variations include high-pulls, paused pulls, riser elevations, and block pulls to target specific phases (leg drive vs. top of pull).
*   **Muscle Snatch:** Use as a technique primer or standalone strength exercise for turnover mechanics.
*   **Snatch Balances:** Includes Drop Snatch (speed focus), Heaving Snatch Balance (foot position focus), and Regular Snatch Balance (strength/confidence focus).
*   **Dip Snatch:** Substitute for Power Snatches to train aggressive finishes with lighter loads; perform without straps to build grip.
*   **Tall Snatch:** Essential for teaching turnover mechanics and foot movement; keep weight very low to ensure speed and precision.
