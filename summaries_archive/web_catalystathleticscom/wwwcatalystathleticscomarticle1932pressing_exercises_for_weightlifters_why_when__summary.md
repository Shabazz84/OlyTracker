# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1932/Pressing-Exercises-for-Weightlifters-Why-When-and-How/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Timing:** Perform pressing work during the **Base Preparation period** (6–14 weeks pre-competition). Drop bench press in the Transitional phase; reduce strict press volume/intensity or switch to low reps (1–2) with high intensity.
*   **Frequency/Placement:** Schedule pressing on days followed by a rest day (e.g., Wed/Sat for Russian model). Perform presses at the **end** of sessions to avoid fatigue affecting speed lifts.
*   **Variation:** Avoid linear progression on one lift; rotate exercises (Push Press, Bench, Snatch Grip Press) and rep ranges (2–3 heavy reps vs. 5–6 higher volume) weekly.
*   **Intensity/Volume:** Work until failure (approx. 5–7 reps) to stimulate hypertrophy, but do not make this the daily protocol due to recovery demands.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Elbow Lockout:** Focus on a stable overhead position; while joints provide primary support, active shoulder muscles ensure comfort and safety in the locked-out rack/overhead positions.
*   **Push Press Integration:** Can replace the Jerk or Clean & Jerk mid-session to utilize leg drive for shoulder strength development.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary List:** Strict Press, Push Press, Snatch Grip Press, Bench Press.
*   **Combinations:** If combining push press with other presses in one session, follow the order: Push Press → (Snatch Grip Press OR Bench Press). Never place strict pressing before push press in a combined session.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Fatigue Management:** Avoid heavy snatching immediately following a pressing day; ensure at least one rest day separates pressing sessions from high-speed lifting to prevent soreness from hindering technique.
*   **Rotation:** Change exercise schemes once adapted to preserve "adaptation reserves" and avoid overuse injuries.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, 102.5kg, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Hypertrophy Goal:** The article explicitly supports the athlete's goal of a hypertrophy block by recommending working sets to failure (5–7 reps) and varying rep ranges to build massive shoulders for better rack positions.
*   **Back Pain Caution:** While the text advocates pressing, it warns that poor timing or protocols can be "harmful." Given chronic back pain, strict overhead pressing must be introduced cautiously; ensure core stability is maintained, as excessive spinal loading during presses could exacerbate pain.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The article's emphasis on scheduling pressing days followed by rest days (e.g., Wed/Sat) aligns with the athlete's night shift schedule (Wed–Sun), provided the "rest day" after a press session is utilized for sleep recovery rather than work shifts, to mitigate fatigue accumulation.
*   **Transition Phase:** Since the athlete is starting a 6-week block now, this falls squarely into the recommended "Base Preparation" window where pressing volume should be highest before tapering later in the cycle.
