# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1700/Knee-Pain-Patellar-Tendonitis-and-Coming-Back-to-Weightlifti/

**Note:** The provided text is a specific case study and Q&A regarding **Patellar Tendonitis**, not a general coaching transcript for an intermediate lifter with back pain or night shift issues. However, the principles within can be adapted to your profile.

### 1. Programming Principles (Sets/Reps/Intensity/Frequency)
*   **Acute Phase:** Complete rest from loading the knees (squats/lunges) until acute pain subsides.
*   **Return to Training:** High-rep, high-volume work at light intensity (**50–60% 1RM**) for **8–10 reps** and **3–5 sets**.
*   **Progression:** Gradual transition: 2 weeks @ 8 reps $\rightarrow$ 2 weeks @ 6 reps $\rightarrow$ lighter lower volume week before returning to heavy 5s.
*   **Olympic Lifts (Snatch/C&J):** Limit to **3 reps per set** maximum during rehab phases; keep weights light. Avoid power variations that require stopping in a partial squat position with maximal quad tension.
*   **Tempo:** Controlled eccentric (lowering) phase with **no bounce**; maintain tension throughout the bottom position.

### 2. Technique Cues
*   **Squatting:** "Controlled speed down, no bounce," "maintain tension all the way down and through the bottom."
*   **Alignment:** Ensure femur and tibia are aligned to prevent patella tracking issues (knees moving inward or riding on inside of feet).
*   **Warm-up:** Do not rush; ensure knees are physically warm before loading.

### 3. Exercise Recommendations
*   **Primary Rehab:** Bilateral squats at low intensity initially; transition to unilateral work (unweighted lunges, high reps 8–12) once pain-free.
*   **Prehab/Accessory:** Balanced strengthening of quads, hamstrings, adductors, and glutes. Anterior tibialis work.
*   **Cardio/Mobility:** Stationary bike or leg press (light weight, perfect form) to increase blood flow. Avoid running/cutting sports until heavy squatting resumes.

### 4. Recovery & Mobility Advice
*   **Soft Tissue Work:** Extensive foam rolling of quads (specifically rectus femoris and lateral quad/ITB connection). Use a lacrosse ball or elbow on the area just above the knee cap (quadriceps tendon convergence) post-workout.
*   **Stretching:** Stretch quads and hip flexors after warming up; repeat between sets.
*   **Thermal Therapy:** Ice immediately post-workout (15–20 mins); consider hot/cold water contrast therapy for stubborn cases. Keep legs warm during training (wear pants).
*   **Isometrics:** Recent expert consensus suggests high-volume isometric holds in mid-range (avoiding deep range) to stimulate tendon remodeling without excessive tension.

### 5. Relevance to Athlete Profile
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text emphasizes **glute activation** and hip mobility. Weak glutes often force the lower back to compensate during squats; strengthening them is crucial for your back pain.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** The advice to "keep legs warm" (wear pants) is vital if training in a cool environment or after a night shift when body temperature may be lower, reducing tissue elasticity.
*   **Transitioning Athlete:** The strict limitation on Olympic lift volume (max 3 reps) during the return-to-training phase protects your connective tissues while you rebuild the specific strength required for snatching/cleaning without aggravating the knees or back.
*   **Hypertrophy Block:** While the text focuses on rehab, the "high-rep, high-volume" approach aligns well with a hypertrophy block if adapted to non-knee-loading exercises (e.g., upper body, core) while maintaining knee health protocols.
