# Analysis: "How to Improve Speed in Weightlifting" by Greg Everett

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- Train at **70–80% intensity** to maintain speed capacity; avoid excessive heavy loads that force slow lifting
- **Execution intent matters most**: move fast consciously, even if absolute speed is modest
- **Jump training**: 1–2x/week, low volume (not 100+ reps weekly); start conservatively
  - Preferred: back squat jumps at 20–25% of best back squat
  - Paired jumps (squats + jumps, no rest) to recruit high-threshold motor units
  - Avoid excessive arm swing; consider pauses over pure countermovement

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Core principle**: Technical proficiency is the primary lever for speed—optimized mechanics transfer muscular effort to bar acceleration with minimal resistance
- Pulls and squats: move fast concentrically unless addressing specific technical faults (e.g., balance shift)

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- Snatch/clean pulls with maximal bar speed
- Power variations or hang/block variations (lighter weights)
- Squat jumps, box jumps, vertical jumps
- Pause variations preferred (mimics Olympic lift demands)

## 4. **Relevance to This Athlete**
- **High priority**: OHS is primary snatch limiter (50 kg vs. 80 kg clean). Fast, technically efficient squatting directly addresses snatch bottleneck
- **Night shift concern**: Jump training is neurologically demanding—monitor recovery fatigue
- **Chronic back pain**: Emphasize pull-ups, paused jumps, and controlled squatting over ballistic movements; avoid explosive loading if symptomatic
