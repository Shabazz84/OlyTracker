# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2177/Ask-Greg-Stabilizing-in-the-Bottom-of-the-Snatch/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency:** High frequency of overhead stability drills (5-second holds) integrated into snatch practice and snatch balances.
*   **Intensity:** Not explicitly defined in this text; focuses on technique over load. Mentions high-intensity work eventually forced adaptation, but the specific advice here is low-load technical drilling.
*   **Sets/Reps:** 3 sets of overhead squats (varying foot widths and heel elevation) for assessment; multiple reps of snatch balances with 5-second holds in the bottom position before recovery.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Torso:** Maintain an upright torso; avoid leaning forward significantly unless mobility restricts it.
*   **Bar Path:** Keep the bar vertically over the back of the neck, not drifting forward ("chasing" the bar).
*   **Shoulders/Arms:** Fully retract and upwardly rotate shoulder blades (squeeze upper inside edges); extend elbows to end range; keep bar in palms over forearms.
*   **Effort:** Be "aggressive and continuous" in holding the position; no part of the overhead stance should be passive. Pressurize the trunk.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Overhead Squat (OHS):** Used for assessment (varying foot width/toe angle) and stability training.
*   **Snatch Balance:** Used to practice active turnover and securing the overhead position quickly.
*   **Heel-Elevated OHS:** Specifically recommended to test ankle mobility limitations.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Mobility Checks:** Perform a diagnostic sequence: OHS with normal feet, wider feet, toes turned out, and finally heels elevated on plates.
    *   *If easier with heels:* Ankle mobility is the limiter.
    *   *If not easier:* Hip mobility or combined ankle/hip restriction.
*   **T-Spine:** Address thoracic spine limitations if they contribute to forward lean (implied by "t-spine mobility" in user comments).

**5. Athlete-specific relevance**
*   **OHS Limitation:** The text directly addresses the athlete's primary limiter (OHS 50kg) by prescribing specific mobility drills and stability holds to improve depth and upright torso position.
*   **Shoulder Stability:** Addresses the "shoulders collapsing" issue common in lifters with less overhead experience, which aligns with an intermediate transitioning to Olympic lifting.
*   **Back Pain Caution:** While the text emphasizes "pressurization" (trunk stability), the athlete's chronic back pain requires careful application of these high-tension cues; the mobility checks are crucial to ensure the pain isn't caused by compensatory forward leaning due to poor ankle/hip mobility.
*   **Night Shift Note:** The text does not address sleep or circadian rhythm issues specific to night shift workers, though the emphasis on "active effort" and "pressurization" requires high neural drive which may be fatigued during night shifts.
