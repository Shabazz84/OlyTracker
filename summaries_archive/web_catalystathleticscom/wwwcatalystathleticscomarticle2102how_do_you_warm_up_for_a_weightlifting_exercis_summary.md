# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2102/How-Do-You-Warm-Up-For-A-Weightlifting-Exercise/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Warm-up Structure:** Start with empty bar drills (5 reps each: high-pull from knee, tall muscle snatch, press, drop snatch, OHS). Progress to first working weight (30-40% max) for 2–5 sets of doubles/triples or complexes.
*   **Progression Strategy:** Use progressively smaller weight increases as intensity rises (e.g., 10% jumps early, dropping to 2–5 kg increments near top weight).
*   **Intensity/Reps:** For working sets, either maintain full volume on warm-ups (conditioning focus) or reduce reps on the way up (fatigue management for max attempts).
*   **Frequency:** No specific frequency mentioned; focus is on *how* to structure the pre-lift routine.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Drill Sequence:** Snatch high-pull from below knee, tall muscle snatch, snatch press, drop snatch, overhead squat.
*   **Focus Areas:** Use warm-up drills to target specific problem areas (e.g., swap "snatch press" for "press in snatch" if needed).
*   **Mental Prep:** Vary warm-up weights and increments occasionally to prevent mental blocks ("Big Dumb Head") regarding unfamiliar loads.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Warm-up Complexes:** Snatch pull + snatch; Push press + jerk.
*   **Strength Drills:** Good mornings (optional addition).
*   **Competition Warm-up Example:** Bar → 35% ×2×3 → 48% ×2 → 62% ×1 → 76% ×1 → 83% ×1 → 90% ×1 → 94% ×1 → 97% ×1.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **General:** Do not rush the warm-up; ensure you feel physically "warm" before heavy lifting to prevent injury and improve performance.
*   **Mobility/Prep:** The article is part of a series on mobility, but specific exercises beyond the standard drill sequence are not detailed in this text.

**5. Athlete-Specific Relevance (Intermediate, 102.5kg, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Caution:** While the text advises using complexes for practice, an athlete with chronic back pain should avoid high-volume "full volume" warm-ups on heavy squats or cleans if they induce fatigue/pain. Stick to the "fewer reps on the way up" strategy to minimize spinal loading before working sets.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** The text suggests varying weights to combat mental blocks; this is highly relevant for a night-shift worker whose circadian rhythm may affect focus, requiring extra mental preparation via varied warm-up loads rather than rigid routines.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** The recommended drill sequence explicitly includes the Overhead Squat and Snatch Press, which directly addresses the primary limiter.
*   **No Split Jerk:** The text mentions "Push press + jerk" complexes; this aligns with a push-jerk focus but requires careful monitoring of shoulder fatigue given the lack of split mechanics to absorb load.
