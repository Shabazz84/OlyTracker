# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1846/Things-You-Can-Get-Rid-of-Things-You-Cant-Weightlifting-Pro/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Prioritization:** Strictly prioritize full competition lifts (Snatch, Clean & Jerk) and Squats (Front/Back). These are the only two non-negotiable categories.
*   **Elimination Strategy:** Immediately discard "variety" assistance exercises (deficit pulls, stone loading, bodybuilding variations, bizarre YouTube drills).
*   **Hierarchy of Assistance:** If time permits a third category, prioritize pulling exercises (Snatch/Clean Pulls) over squats *only if* squats are already covered; otherwise, Squats > Pulls. Direct assistance (Overhead Squat, Push Press) ranks below the core three but above variety work.
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Designed for adults with limited time (3–4 workouts/week). Focus on high-intensity, low-volume efficiency rather than accumulating "cool" exercises.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   *None explicitly detailed in this text.* The article focuses entirely on exercise selection and program architecture, not biomechanical execution cues for specific lifts.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Mandatory Core:** Full Snatch, Clean & Jerk, Front Squat, Back Squat.
*   **Secondary (If time allows):** Snatch Pulls, Clean Pulls.
*   **Tertiary (Direct Assistance):** Overhead Squats, Push Press, lifts from the hang.
*   **Explicitly Discard:** Power Snatch/Clean, Deficit Snatches, "Telephone Pole" variations, Stone Loading, Bench Press, Zercher Lifts, and low-bar back squats (unless specifically needed for a weakness, but generally lower priority than high-bar).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   *None explicitly detailed in this text.* The article mentions that adults have "jobs, families, responsibilities" taking up time, implying recovery is constrained by life logistics rather than prescribing specific mobility protocols.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Night Shift, Back Pain)**
*   **Back Pain Management:** The recommendation to eliminate "variety assistance exercises" and focus on the core lifts/squats reduces total volume and fatigue accumulation, which can be beneficial for managing chronic back pain by avoiding excessive accessory work that compounds spinal stress. However, the text does not address *how* to modify squats or pulls specifically for back pain (e.g., reducing depth or load).
*   **Night Shift/Time Constraints:** The article is highly relevant regarding time management. It validates the need to drop "fluff" exercises due to limited training windows (1–1.5 hours), which aligns with a night shift worker's disrupted schedule.
*   **Hypertrophy Block:** The text argues against high-volume bodybuilding-style lifting ("variety assistance") for general success, suggesting that for an intermediate athlete, the "bang for buck" comes from the main lifts and squats rather than isolated hypertrophy work. This contradicts a dedicated 6-week *hypertrophy* block unless that block is strictly limited to heavy squatting within the core lift framework.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** The text does not address the specific limitation of using only a push jerk; it treats "Clean and Jerk" as a monolithic category without distinguishing between split and push mechanics.
