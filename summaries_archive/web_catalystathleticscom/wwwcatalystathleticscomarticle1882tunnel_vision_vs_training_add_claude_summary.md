# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1882/Tunnel-Vision-vs-Training-ADD/

**Relevant to Weightlifting Training Philosophy**

---

### **Applicable to This Athlete:**

**Programming Principle:**
- **Tunnel Vision vs. Training ADD framework**: The athlete has a concrete 6-week hypertrophy block → technique/strength progression. This calls for **tunnel vision** (focused on one goal) rather than scattered training. Avoid adding competing modalities (CrossFit, powerlifting, other sports) during this block.

**Key Consideration for Night Shift Worker:**
- Foreman notes that extended tunnel vision can be mentally draining. For a night shift athlete with chronic back pain, periodically incorporating **lower-intensity ADD phases** (alternative recovery methods, different movement patterns) between blocks may help prevent burnout and manage pain without derailing the primary goal.

**Recovery/Mobility Angle:**
- The article validates using alternative training methods (powerlifting, throwing, etc.) during *recovery phases* after competition—but emphasizes these must be *intentional and temporary*, not random dabbling. Relevant to managing chronic back pain: structured low-stress alternatives (e.g., sled work, carries, aerobic conditioning) fit this model better than true sport ADD.

**Caution:**
- Don't experiment with new high-risk disciplines (MMA, plyometrics on bad back) without proper coaching. Consistency in the primary program trumps novelty.

---

**Bottom Line:** Stay tunnel-focused on the 6-week block. Save training ADD exploration for planned recovery phases.
