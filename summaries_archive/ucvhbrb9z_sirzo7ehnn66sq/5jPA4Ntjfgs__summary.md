## Key Points
*   **Dynamic Execution Priority:** Training plateaus are often resolved by prioritizing speed and intent over absolute load. If movement feels "sticky" or slow, immediately reduce weight to restore explosive velocity; do not add load until acceleration is consistent.
*   **Intensification Blocks:** Utilize short-term blocks (2–3 weeks) focusing on relative maximums (85–90% 1RM) with low volume to stimulate neural drive, followed by immediate deloads if progress stalls or fatigue accumulates.
*   **Hormonal & Recovery Monitoring:** If performance halts despite adequate nutrition and sleep, suspect hormonal imbalances (specifically low testosterone). Immediate intervention involves cutting training volume or taking a full rest week rather than pushing through.
*   **Movement Quality over Dead Stops:** Avoid "fighting" stuck points in the main lifts. The goal is continuous acceleration; if the bar slows significantly, the load is too heavy for the current technical state.

## Technique Cues
*   **"Charge from the Bottom":** Initiate every pull with an immediate, explosive drive. There must be no relaxation or pause at the bottom of the squat or pull that allows momentum to dissipate before the second pull.
*   **Fixation at Top:** Powerfully fixate the bar and body position at the top of the movement (rack position or overhead lockout) rather than just reaching it.
*   **Explosive Initiation in Accessories:** When performing pause squats, box squats, or overhead work, ensure the transition out of the bottom position is instantaneous. No "dead stops" that turn strength exercises into slow presses unless specifically prescribed for hypertrophy.

## Programming Principles
*   **Frequency & Load Organization:** Aim for 3 sessions per week focused on dynamic execution. If fatigue accumulates, reduce to 2 sessions or take a full deload week immediately; do not sustain high volume indefinitely.
*   **Accessory Selection Strategy:** Prioritize variations that build strength without excessive spinal loading (e.g., Goblet Squats, Front Squats, Box Squats) over heavy Back Squats or conventional deadlifts to accommodate chronic back pain.
*   **OHS Development:** Address the 50kg OHS limiter through high-speed, light-to-moderate volume work (e.g., landmine presses, speed OHS) rather than heavy max attempts that risk aggravating the lower back.
*   **Deload Protocol:** After any intensification block or if hormonal markers suggest fatigue, reduce frequency significantly or take a full week off to reset the nervous system.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain Management:** The standard "intensification" approach requires modification; heavy conventional deadlifts and high-volume back squats must be avoided. Substitute with Goblet/Front Squats and Box Squats to maintain leg strength while sparing the lumbar spine.
*   **Night Shift Schedule (Wed–Sun):** Training sessions should be scheduled on non-consecutive days within the shift cycle (e.g., Wed lift, Thu rest). Do not train during "off" days if sleep is compromised; prioritize recovery over volume consistency.
*   **Push Jerk Only Constraint:** Apply dynamic execution principles strictly to the Push Jerk: ensure the dip and drive are explosive rather than a slow press, as speed is the primary limiter for this variation.
*   **Hypertrophy Transition:** For the upcoming 6-week block, utilize "Accessory Focus" methods (leg press, dumbbell presses) to build mass safely before returning to Olympic lifts for technique refinement. Avoid long-term high-volume hypertrophy without progression.
