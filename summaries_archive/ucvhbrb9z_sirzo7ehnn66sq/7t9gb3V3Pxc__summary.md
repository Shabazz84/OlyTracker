## Key Points
*   **Training Structure:** 4–5 days/week split by movement pattern (Pressing/Jerk, Squatting, Pulling).
*   **Hypertrophy Block Strategy:** Utilize linear progression with high volume (e.g., 3 sets of 10 reps at ~63% of current max) rather than complex periodization. Focus on "current maximum" performance daily, not theoretical 1RM.
*   **"No Struggle" Principle:** If the bar slows, hesitates, or requires isometric locking, the load is too heavy. This applies strictly to the press and squat phases.
*   **Speed over Force:** In pressing movements (foundational for push jerk), generate maximum speed via hip/leg drive. Slow bar velocity indicates technical failure or excessive load.
*   **Recovery Protocols:** Long rest intervals (~20 mins) between high-intensity sets to maintain speed quality. Active recovery (stretching, mobility) during rests. Skip sessions entirely if fatigued, head hurts, or energy is low—critical for night shift workers.

## Technique Cues
*   **Scapular Control:** Initiate press with scapular retraction and depression ("shoulders together") *before* the drive. Maintain a dynamic shelf; avoid static squeezing that creates tension without movement.
*   **Fluidity & Drive:** The lift must be smooth without "stops." If the bar halts mid-movement, it indicates a failure in the initial drive phase (clean) or hip extension (press).
*   **Spinal Safety:** Avoid excessive arching ("bridge") if it causes discomfort. Focus on creating tension through scapular stability rather than spinal extension to protect the chronic lower back.
*   **Elbow Position:** Keep elbows stable during setup; avoid excessive bending in the press phase that compromises the shelf.

## Programming Principles
*   **Intensity Management:** Progress linearly week-to-week based on daily performance. If a lift feels "poor" or struggles, reduce intensity immediately rather than forcing a max effort.
*   **Press/Jerk Specifics:** Use "Attack" sets (3 sets) progressing from 60–75% to 85–91%. The final set should be ~20kg below max, performed with maximum speed and no struggle.
*   **Squat Volume:** Prioritize hypertrophy volume (e.g., 3x10 @ ~63%) for the initial block to build capacity without excessive spinal compression.
*   **Pulling Frequency:** Deadlifts/Pulls performed 1–2 times weekly, often following squat sessions, with no strict cycles—focus on gradual weight increases based on feel.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** Schedule flexibility is key. Do not force training during circadian dips; utilize "psychological activation" (music, caffeine) if energy is low but technique remains sound.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Push Jerk Optimization:** The emphasis on hip drive and scapular stability directly translates to the push jerk mechanics required by this athlete, eliminating the need for split footwork while maximizing vertical force transfer.
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** The strict "no struggle" rule reduces static spinal loading and shear forces, addressing chronic back pain by prioritizing fluid motion over heavy isometric locking.
*   **OHS Limiter Resolution:** The focus on scapular control and bar speed addresses the 50 kg OHS plateau, suggesting technical efficiency (not just raw strength) is the primary driver for improvement.
*   **Hypertrophy Block Alignment:** The recommended high-volume, moderate-intensity squatting protocol perfectly matches the 6-week hypertrophy goal before transitioning to technique/strength work.
*   **Recovery & Circadian Rhythm:** The instruction to skip training when fatigued or experiencing headaches provides a necessary safety valve for a night shift worker, preventing injury during low-recovery windows.
