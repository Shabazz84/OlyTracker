## Key Points
*   **Hypertrophy Block Strategy:** Utilize a 6-week block focusing on mechanical tension via higher loads (70%+) rather than high-rep "trash sets." Target rep ranges of 5–12 depending on fiber type, prioritizing the final set near failure while maintaining 1-2 reps in reserve (RIR) for previous sets to manage CNS fatigue.
*   **Progression Model:** Apply a geometric progression ("2-for-2" rule: add weight if target reps are hit with one rep left for two consecutive sessions). Avoid arithmetic jumps; increase volume by adding reps within a set (e.g., moving from 9-8-7 to 10x10) before increasing load.
*   **Warm-up Protocol:** Eliminate high-rep warm-ups. Use low-rep, high-intensity sequences (5 reps → 3 reps → 1–2 reps) with increasing weight to activate the CNS without inducing metabolic fatigue or acidosis.
*   **Injury & Pain Management:** If chronic back pain or inflammation flares, immediately cease loading and rest until symptoms subside before resuming at very low volumes/weights. Do not train through persistent pain (>2 months); seek diagnostic imaging rather than self-managing spinal issues.
*   **Recovery Constraints:** As a night shift worker with limited recovery capacity, strictly time rest intervals (2–5 minutes) rather than resting "by feel" to maintain training density while preventing CNS burnout. Monitor for overtraining signals: sleep disruption, mood swings, and loss of motivation.

## Technique Cues
*   **CNS Activation:** Execute warm-ups with a "powerful impulse" signal from the brain to recruit high-threshold motor units before heavy work; this is critical for night shift athletes waking up their CNS.
*   **Pressing Mechanics (Push Jerk):** Recognize that in pressing movements, the triceps and biceps act primarily as stabilizers for forearm angle rather than primary movers in specific ranges of motion. Focus on rigid torso stability to compensate for the lack of a split jerk.
*   **Tendon Adaptation:** Alternate heavy loads with "pump" style work (lighter weights, higher reps) to stimulate tendon adaptation without excessive strain, acknowledging that tendons adapt slower than muscle tissue.

## Programming Principles
*   **Volume Management:** Avoid training to absolute failure on every set. The optimal strategy is 2–3 sets per exercise taken close to failure with 1 RIR, reserving true failure for only one final "donation" set.
*   **Fatigue Management:** Eliminate "wasted sets" (sets where significantly more reps could have been performed). Use fixed rest intervals to ensure consistent fatigue management and trackable progression.
*   **Periodization:** Do not change variables until progress stalls. If linear progression fails, introduce variability in rep ranges or load rather than increasing frequency.
*   **Nutritional Note:** Avoid using baking soda/water during training to neutralize acid; this disrupts stomach pH and causes digestive issues that hinder recovery.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain & Night Shifts:** The "don't train through inflammation" principle is critical for your spinal health. Your night shift schedule necessitates strict adherence to the low-rep warm-up protocol to safely activate the CNS without exacerbating fatigue or pain.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** While the text does not provide split jerk cues, the biomechanical insight regarding triceps as stabilizers can help refine your push jerk form to maximize efficiency given your constraint of using only a push jerk.
*   **Hypertrophy Transition:** The focus on finding optimal rep ranges (potentially 8–12+) for specific muscle groups supports your goal of a hypertrophy block, allowing for muscle growth without the excessive systemic fatigue that would hinder recovery as an intermediate lifter with spinal issues.
