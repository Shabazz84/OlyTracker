# Merged Summary

## Key Points

- **Hypertrophy rep ranges:** 6–12 reps produce hypertrophy; fatigue level determines effectiveness within that window.
- **RIR approach preferred:** 2–3 reps in reserve (RIR) is optimal for hypertrophy during a dedicated block; avoid true-failure training until technique/motor control stabilize.
- **5×5 outperforms high-volume schemes:** Builds strength AND hypertrophy simultaneously with better mechanical tension; superior to 10×10 for intermediate athletes, especially those with chronic pain.
- **Tendon recovery is slower than muscle:** Avoid rapid load escalation; balance heavy work with lighter volume days to manage connective tissue stress.
- **Recovery monitoring critical for shift workers:** Sleep quality, mood, and training drive are early fatigue markers; reduce load immediately if compromised—especially relevant given Wed–Sun night shift.
- **Warm-up CNS activation:** Heavy singles (~80%) for 1–2 reps activates high-threshold motor units without fatigue; then drop to work sets.
- **Progression model:** +1 rep in one set per session (or +weight after 2 sessions at same load) with standardized rest periods.

None applicable. Source material contained no snatch, clean, jerk, or OHS-specific coaching.

## Programming Principles

- **6-week hypertrophy block structure:** Use 5×5 or 5×4→5×5 progression (start conservatively, progress reps before load).
- **2–3 RIR sets only** (not to-failure); quality CNS activation over volume.
- **Avoid "junk sets":** Stop when mechanical tension/pump diminishes to prevent CNS fatigue without adaptation benefit.
- **Load management for chronic back pain:** Prioritize mechanical tension and controlled progression over absolute volume; balance heavy days with lighter sessions.
- **Night-shift recovery strategy:** Prioritize sleep monitoring; reduce weekly load if sleep quality drops.
