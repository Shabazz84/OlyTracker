# COACHING PHILOSOPHY DOCUMENT
**Athlete:** 102.5 kg, BS 118 kg | Clean 80 kg | Jerk 65 kg (push jerk) | OHS 50 kg | Chronic back pain | Night shift (Wed–Sun)  
**Goal:** 6-week hypertrophy block → technique/strength phase

---

## 1. CORE TRAINING PHILOSOPHY

**Five Operating Principles:**

1. **Recovery dominates progress for shift workers.** Sleep quality, mood state, and non-training stress directly predict adaptation. A single poor sleep night or mood suppression warrants load reduction or session cancellation—forced training during CNS fatigue is counterproductive. For a Wed–Sun night-shift worker, this is non-negotiable.

2. **Mechanical tension under control > metabolic tonnage.** Use 5×5 or wave-loaded schemes (5×5 → 4×4 → 3×3 weekly progression) with 2–3 reps in reserve (RIR). Avoid true-failure training until technique stabilizes. This balances strength and hypertrophy while protecting connective tissue and the chronic back.

3. **Tendon recovery is slower than muscle.** Load escalation must be gradual; balance heavy work days with lighter volume sessions. Don't repeat identical weight/reps across weeks—vary rep/load schemes to prevent accommodation and CNS staleness without inducing overuse injury.

4. **Technique specificity precedes absolute load.** Snatch limiter is OHS (50 kg), jerk limiter is split mechanics (push jerk only). Hypertrophy block must build positional strength in weak ranges (overhead stability, split position depth) before chasing max loads.

5. **Individualization trumps formulas.** Programming adjusts daily based on sleep quality, back pain flare-ups, and training drive. RPE/feel-based autoregulation and training logs are essential; rigid percentages fail chronic pain athletes and shift workers.

## 2. PROGRAMMING APPROACH

**6-Week Hypertrophy Block Structure:**

| Week | Squat Pattern | Press/Jerk | Accessory Focus | Rationale |
|------|---------------|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| 1–2 | 5×5 @ 72–75% | Push jerk 4×5 @ RPE 7 | OHS 3×6, split stance work | Establish baseline, technique prep |
| 3–4 | 4×4 @ 78–80% | Push jerk 3×4 → jerk intro 2×3 @ RPE 6 | OHS 3×8, dip variations, split hold | Volume accumulation; split jerk foundation |
| 5–6 | 3×3 @ 82–85% | Jerk (mixed) 3×3, push jerk 2×3 | OHS 2×8, jerk dip, front squat 3×5 | Deload week 6; transition to technique phase |

**Key rules:**
- Progress reps before load (+1 rep/session until target, then increase weight 2–5 kg, reset reps).
- **Volume reduction protocol if suppressed:** Drop to 2–3 exercises/session for 2–3 weeks if sleep/mood collapse, then reintroduce progressively.
- Metabolic "accent" sets (1×8 @ moderate load) only in lower-volume sessions (weeks 5–6); skip in high-volume weeks.
- Extensive warm-ups non-negotiable before overhead/loaded spine work (aerobic activation, motor pattern prep, low HR).

**Periodization philosophy:** Undulating within session (wave loading) and across weeks (varied intensity/rep schemes) prevent psychological plateau and reduce overuse while building hypertrophy and positional strength for technique transition.

## 3. KEY TECHNIQUE CUES & EXERCISE PREFERENCES

**Primary Lifts:**

- **Back Squat:** 5×5 foundation; depth/stability non-negotiable for OHS transfer. Reduce load if back pain flares; pause 1–2 sec at bottom to reinforce position.
- **Push Jerk:** Continue as primary jerk work weeks 1–3; master dip mechanics, leg drive, overhead lockout. No split jerk until split position strength improves (see accessory work).
- **Overhead Squat:** Primary snatch limiter—**this is the hypertrophy block's technical anchor.** Start weeks 1–2 at 3×6 @ manageable load (40–45 kg if needed); focus on thoracic extension, core stability, and elbow position. Progress reps to 3×8 by week 5; load only after position quality improves. Film weekly; prioritize depth and upright torso over load.
- **Clean:** 3–4 reps/session, submaximal (RPE 6–7). Focus on: triple extension timing, hip extension, rack position. Do NOT chase max singles during hypertrophy block; technique reps only.

**Accessory Circuit (2–3×/week):**
- Split squat or split-stance work (3×8 each leg) — builds split jerk foundation and unilateral stability for back pain management.
- Front squat or Zercher squat (3×5–6) — builds leg strength, core stability, reduced spinal loading vs. back squat alone.
- Jerk dip or push press (3×6–8) — develops lockout strength and overhead position without split demand.
- Prone cobra or bird dogs (3×10) — anti-extension spinal stability work, critical for chronic back pain during pressing.
- Chest-supported rows or seal rows (3×8) — posterior chain activation, scapular stability, reduced lumbar stress.

**What NOT to do:**
- Max-effort singles on snatch/clean/jerk during hypertrophy block (neural fatigue + recovery debt for shift worker).
