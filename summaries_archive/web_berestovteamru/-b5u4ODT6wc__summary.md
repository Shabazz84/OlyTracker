**2. Technique cues (Push Jerk / Shvung Zhimovoy)**
*   **Grip & Rack:** Grip slightly wider than shoulders or shoulder-width. Bar must rest firmly on the palms, clavicle, and shoulders; do not let the bar roll onto fingers/palms only.
*   **Elbows:** Rotate elbows down and back to create a stable shelf for the bar; do not drop them so low that the bar loses support from the shoulders.
*   **Core/Bracing:** Lift the diaphragm (do not take a full breath on the floor), pull chest up, tuck belly button in to increase intra-abdominal pressure, and brace the lower back.
*   **Head Position:** Slightly lift head and tuck chin to create vertical space for the bar; do not jut chin forward.
*   **Dip (The "Chair" Cue):** Initiate dip by pushing hips back as if sitting on a chair behind you. This engages the strongest hip muscles rather than just bending knees. Keep torso upright; do not lean forward excessively.
*   **Drive & Transition:** Drive powerfully upward using legs and ankles (rising onto toes is acceptable for maximal effort). The transition from drive to press must be synchronized: "push then immediately catch and press." Do not start pressing too early (loses leg drive) or too late (bar flies up, loses momentum).
*   **Bar Path:** Drive the bar straight up, slightly behind the head, avoiding a forward arc.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Primary Focus:** Push Jerk (*Shvung Zhimovoy*).
*   **Variations Discussed:**
    1.  *Classic/Explosive:* Allow rising onto toes during the drive to utilize full body mechanics.
    2.  *Strict/Strength-focused:* Keep feet flat on the floor (no toe rise) to force more time under tension and emphasize stability, though this limits max weight lifted.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Mobility:** Emphasis on ankle mobility for rising onto toes; thoracic extension to keep chest up during the dip.

**5. Relevance to athlete's profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Warning:** The text explicitly warns against "over-arching" or leaning the back excessively backward to press heavy weight (a common Soviet-era standing jerk mistake), noting this causes significant spinal injury risk. This is critical for an athlete with chronic back pain.
*   **Technique Correction:** The "chair dip" cue helps prevent knee-dominant movement, which can sometimes compensate for poor hip mobility or weak posterior chain, potentially reducing lower back strain if executed correctly.
*   **Limitation:** The text does not address the specific constraints of night shift work (sleep hygiene) or the athlete's specific weakness in the Overhead Squat (OHS). It focuses entirely on the mechanics of the Push Jerk.
