# Merged Weightlifting Summary

## Key Points

- **Technique precedes load:** Establish optimal movement patterns before progressive intensification
- **Individual variation is normal:** Grip width, dip depth, and rack position vary by athlete morphology and mobility
- **Hip-driven dip is foundational:** Initiates the push jerk; knee-dominant dipping compromises bar stability and force application
- **Transition speed matters:** Minimize pause between dip and drive to preserve momentum and bar contact
- **Shoulder/thoracic mobility gates progression:** Insufficient mobility forces compensatory patterns (wrist stress, forward lean, spinal rounding) that limit load capacity

## Technique Cues

**Rack Position & Grip:**
- Shoulder-width grip (thumb to shoulder measurement) optimal baseline; adjust based on mobility
- Elbows high and back; open chest to create stable anterior deltoid platform
- Bar rests evenly across shoulders, not rolled forward

**Dip:**
- **Initiate with hip drive first**—NOT knee-dominant
- Feet shoulder-width, slight toe-out; keep torso vertical throughout
- Test dip depths (shallow, medium, deep) to find individual preference
- Controlled, smooth descent initially; avoid sharp bounces that separate bar from shoulders

**Drive:**
- Maintain exact same angles from dip into drive; minimize pause
- Full foot contact; no early heel lift or toe-in
- **Arms are secondary**—legs drive, arms follow; avoid premature pressing with hands
- Preserve rib/core position; prevent forward trunk lean

**Head Position:**
- Slight chin tuck; pull head back as bar passes
- Neutral head carriage; no aggressive nodding (causes deceleration)

## Programming Principles

- Use **half-squat pressing variations from different dip depths** to identify optimal position and build positional strength across ranges
- Prioritize variable squat depths (half, medium, deep) before loading progression
- Emphasize **vertical torso mechanics throughout** to minimize spinal stress

## Athlete Relevance

**Chronic back pain → Critical Application:**
Vertical torso + hip-driven dip + controlled tempo directly reduce lumbar strain. Forward lean during dip or drive exacerbates low-back stress—avoid at all costs.

**Push jerk only (no split jerk):**
Content directly applicable; extensive rack position and drive mechanics transfer directly to competition movement.

**OHS 50 kg limiter:**
Shoulder/thoracic mobility work is prerequisite. Test grip width and shoulder mobility before heavy loading; narrow grip demands superior thoracic extension to prevent rounding.

**102.5 kg bodyweight:**
Moderate loading allows 6-week hypertrophy block focus on movement quality without compensatory patterns.

**Action item:** Assess shoulder and thoracic mobility to determine grip width, dip depth, and mobility work priorities before intensity escalation.
