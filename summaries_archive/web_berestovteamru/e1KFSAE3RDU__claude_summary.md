## Key Points

- Push jerk technique relies on a loose grip during rack position (locked only during dip/drive), bar positioned mid-palm behind clavicle with elevated chest and slightly raised elbows
- Dip must be vertical and even-paced; excessive hip extension forward is a common error that shifts load away from quads/glutes and risks lower back collapse
- Overhead position requires bar slightly behind ear line with elevated shoulders and rigid wrist
- Shoulder mobility is the primary prerequisite for proper bar positioning; insufficient ROM limits push jerk progression
- Push jerk variations (power jerk, mid-dip jerk, low-dip jerk) exist on a spectrum of technical/physical demand; lower-dip variations require exceptional shoulder/hip/lower back mobility and leg strength
- Push jerk serves as effective supplemental work for front squat strength development

## Technique Cues

**Rack Position:**
- Grip loose on chest; lock grip only during dip/drive phase
- Bar on mid-palm behind clavicle
- Chest elevated, elbows slightly raised

**Dip & Drive:**
- Vertical, even-paced descent (avoid jerky or excessively slow tempo)
- Hip extension: controlled—avoid driving hips forward excessively, which collapses lower back and shifts load away from prime movers

**Overhead Position:**
- Bar slightly behind head (ear line)
- Shoulders elevated
- Wrist rigid throughout lockout

## Programming Principles

- Online coaching format with weekly programming, video submission, and detailed feedback enables progression tracking
- Push jerk variations should be selected based on athlete mobility/strength profile rather than forcing one variation

## Athlete Relevance
