## Key Points
*   **Primary Focus:** The content details auxiliary drills specifically for the **Push Jerk**, emphasizing leg drive mechanics over arm usage.
*   **Critical Safety Warning:** Athletes must maintain core rigidity at the bottom of the dip; "sitting" or relaxing on a box/bench causes lumbar spine compression, posing a high risk for those with chronic back pain.
*   **Movement Mechanics:** The dip requires a controlled descent followed by an immediate acceleration upward ("touch and reverse"). Full knee extension is mandatory to maximize power transfer, avoiding compensation via toe-dominant rising or bent-knee pushes.
*   **Loading Strategy:** Drills utilize variable loads from empty bar/PVC up to 100–110% of the athlete's Push Jerk max when used as a standalone strength movement.

## Technique Cues
*   **The Dip (Half-Squat):** Move slowly down, do not drop quickly. Maintain heel contact with the floor throughout. Use a "box touch" rhythm: lightly touch the surface and immediately reverse direction without relaxing or sitting fully on it.
*   **The Drive:** Legs are the primary drivers; hands act only as guides/stabilizers. Keep the bar path vertical (no forward/backward drift). Ensure complete knee straightening during extension, not just rising onto toes with knees bent.
*   **Tempo Control:** Execute a "slow down, fast up" cadence to reinforce technical control and manage fatigue, particularly useful for night-shift workers requiring high-quality movement patterns despite potential sleep disruption.

## Programming Principles
*   **Integration:** Use as auxiliary work combined with the main Push Jerk in complex structures (e.g., 3 half-squats + 1 full jerk) to reinforce dip mechanics before the primary lift.
*   **Exercise Selection:**
    *   *Half-Squats on Chest/Shoulders:* For loading strength while maintaining specific dip depth.
    *   *Box/Bench Assisted Half-Squats:* Use a box/bench 5–10 cm away to provide tactile feedback for correct dip depth (light touch only).
    *   *Half-Push Press:* To isolate leg drive and bar path control.
*   **Intensity:** Range from technical drills with light loads to heavy loading (up to 110% of max) depending on the specific goal of the session.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** Directly addresses your constraint of using only a Push Jerk, providing the necessary auxiliary work to maximize efficiency without relying on split jerk mechanics.
*   **Back Pain Management:** The strict requirement to avoid "sitting" on the box is critical for your chronic back pain; it ensures lumbar tension is maintained throughout the dip, preventing injury during high-load transitions.
*   **Strength Transfer:** Mastering full leg extension and avoiding toe-dominant compensation will improve power transfer, reducing reliance on shoulder strength (currently limited by a 50 kg OHS) to move heavier weights.
*   **Fatigue Management:** The emphasis on controlled tempo drills allows for effective technique refinement even during the night shift schedule (Wed–Sun), where recovery and energy levels may fluctuate.
