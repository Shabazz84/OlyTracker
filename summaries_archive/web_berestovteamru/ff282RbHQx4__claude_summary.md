## RELEVANT EXTRACTIONS FOR THIS ATHLETE:

### 1. **Programming Principles**
- Snatch pulls from platform are positioned as a **high-value accessory** for building pulling power and speed
- Emphasis on **controlled tempo** early in learning; progress to explosive tempo only after mastery
- Recommendation: perform with straps to focus on pulling mechanics

### 2. **Technique Cues - Snatch Pull from Platform**
- **Starting position**: spine extended, hip position below or well above knee (NOT at knee level)
- **Path**: bar stays close to body, pulled in vertical line
- **Lowering**: controlled descent along shins/legs—**never loose/dropped**
- **Bounce technique**: intentionally use bar elasticity; bar bends, rebounds, and you catch that energy into the next pull
- **Precision**: bar must return to same spot it departed (midpoint of platform); loss of bar path = loss of center of gravity
- **Common error**: dropping bar uncontrolled or simply throwing weight at platform (ineffective, risks damage)

### 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- **Setup alternatives**: official platform, bench + plates, low plyo box, kettlebell
- **Load adjustment for lighter lifters**: hang plates on bar ends to increase deflection (enhances elastic rebound)
- **Works equally** at 60 kg, 100 kg, 200+ kg if setup is correct

### 4. **Relevant to THIS Athlete's Profile**
- ⚠️ **Chronic back pain concern**: snatch pulls demand heavy spinal extension + isometric control. Requires **careful progression** with posterior chain stability work (not detailed here)
- **Night shift context**: recovery demands are high; high-CNS exercises like snatch pulls should be programmed early in session
- **OHS limiter (50 kg)**: snatch pulls build power but won't directly solve overhead weakness—mobility/positioning work still needed
- **No split jerk**: no mention of jerk-specific pulls, but protocol transfers

**Verdict**: Useful accessory for snatch pulling development; safe if executed with control. Requires modifications given chronic back pain.
