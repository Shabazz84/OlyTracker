## Technique Cues
*   **Grip Width:** Start at shoulder-width. Avoid narrow grips (causes thoracic rounding) or excessively wide grips (requires prohibitive mobility). Optimal width minimizes the vertical distance from chest to lockout.
*   **Elbow & Chest:** Keep elbows up and out to create a stable "platform." Rotate the chest open; do not let elbows drop (bar slides off) or flare too high (reduces power transfer).
*   **Dip Mechanics:** Dip into the hips ("sit on a chair") while keeping the torso upright. If flexibility is limited, spread knees slightly outward to prevent forward fall. Avoid knee-dominant dipping; drive from the hips.
*   **Depth & Transition:** Execute a medium-depth, smooth dip initially. The transition from dip to push must be instantaneous with no pause at the bottom.
*   **Push Mechanics:** Maintain all angles established during the dip. Do not lean forward or rise onto toes prematurely. Keep head slightly lifted and chin tucked back to prevent bar contact with the chin.
*   **Arm Action:** Arms are passive until leg drive is complete. Do not engage arms early; they should only "accompany" the bar after the initial push. Squeezing hands too soon kills momentum.

## Programming Principles
*   **Drill Progression:** Utilize Half-Dips and Push Jerks at varying depths (short, medium, low) to identify individual optimal mechanics before loading.
*   **Sequencing:** Prioritize "smooth" dipping drills to establish control before introducing explosive/short dip variations.
