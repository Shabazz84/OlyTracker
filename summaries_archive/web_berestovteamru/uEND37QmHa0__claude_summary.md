# Merged Summary: Half-Squat & Half-Push Programming for Jerk Development

## Key Points

- **Half-squats** serve dual purposes: technical work at light weight with controlled tempo, or strength-focused at heavy weight
- **Half-pushes** require leg drive as primary driver, not arm pressing; arms are guides only
- **Combination work** (3 half-squats + 1 full jerk) integrates patterns effectively at ~70–80% intensity
- Half-push fault indicator: if bar doesn't reach eye level on heavy weight, load is excessive
- Box half-squats should only touch lightly—avoid full relaxation to prevent spinal deload

## Technique Cues

**Half-Squat:**
- Depth: match jerk dip depth (not full squat)
- Bar placement: **chest position for jerk specificity** (not shoulders)
- Full foot contact; never lift heels
- Hip drive backward like sitting into a chair
- Keep elbows low and forward
- Common errors: forward collapse, knee cave, torso fold

**Half-Push:**
- **Drive with legs, not arms**—arms guide only
- Vertical bar trajectory to eye level
- Two foot variations: full contact (emphasizes leg extension control) or with heel rise (allows natural triple extension)
- Tempo options: controlled or continuous (no pause at catch)
- Light bar/stick drills build leg-drive pattern with minimal arm engagement

## Programming Principles

- **Variable tempos**: slow descent/fast ascent (advanced) or slow descent/slow ascent (beginner)
- **Standalone half-pushes**: up to 100–110% intensity
- **Combination half-pushes**: ~70–80% intensity
- **Hybrid blocks**: use half-squats on shoulders (higher volume, leg hypertrophy) during 6-week hypertrophy phase; transition to chest position for jerk-specific work

## Athlete Relevance

| Limiting Factor | Application |
|---|---|
| **Chronic back pain** | Half-squats safer than full squats; maintain upright torso; avoid box relaxation |
| **Weak overhead (50 kg OHS)** | Half-squat + half-push combinations build leg drive—essential jerk prerequisite |
| **No split jerk** | Half-pushes improve dip/drive mechanics before learning split technique |
| **Night shift recovery** | Tempo-controlled variations reduce fatigue accumulation |
| **6-week hypertrophy block** | Half-squats on shoulders + jerk combination work (3+1 structure) |
