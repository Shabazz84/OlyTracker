## Key Points
*   **Simultaneous Extension:** The second pull must be a single explosive moment where legs, back, and traps fire together ("one surge"). Sequential extension (legs first, then shrug) wastes energy and increases spinal shear forces.
*   **Pre-acceleration Phase:** Initiate movement with an upward trajectory from the knees *before* bar contact to avoid "grabbing." The explosive pull must occur specifically at the **mid-thigh**. Pulling too low indicates early arm engagement; pulling too high indicates a lack of pre-acceleration.
*   **Arm Position:** Arms must remain straight throughout the pull. Avoid initiating an upward pull with the arms before hip extension is complete.
*   **Bar Path & Contact:** The bar must stay close to the shins/knees during the knee phase without bouncing or shifting weight between heels and toes. Knees track parallel to toes; no valgus collapse.
*   **Start Position:** Hips should be higher than a snatch pull (due to narrower grip) but not excessively high. Avoid starting too low if leg strength is insufficient, as this forces the back to compensate.

## Technique Cues
*   **Stance & Grip:** Feet shoulder-width with slight toe turnout (prevents knee valgus). Wraps must fully encircle the wrist without creasing; grip tight against bar with fingers free once strapped.
*   **Spinal Alignment:** Maintain a neutral lumbar curve and flat back throughout the pull. Avoid rounding in thoracic or lumbar regions to mitigate chronic back pain risks. Shoulders directly over the bar.
*   **Knee Mechanics:** Keep a slight bend at the knees during the transition; do not fully extend legs before the second pull. Use lats to keep the bar close to shins/knees.
*   **Foot Contact:** Maintain full foot contact (no rising onto toes or heels) until the moment of extension. Create a "spring" angle at ankle, hip, and back simultaneously.

## Programming Principles
*   **Focus on Timing over Volume:** Prioritize the *sequence* and *timing* of the pull rather than specific set/rep schemes. Emphasize the "single explosive moment."
*   **High-Frequency Technical Repetition:** Use high-frequency drills to ingrain reflexive motor patterns ("reflexive" vs. "analytical"). This reduces cognitive load, which is critical for a night-shift worker managing fatigue.
*   **Avoid Over-Analysis:** For intermediate/transitioning athletes, avoid complex technical nuances (like knee retraction) that disrupt rhythm. Focus on uniform bar path and simultaneous extension.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain Mitigation:** The emphasis on synchronized leg/back engagement reduces spinal shear forces compared to back-dominant pulls. Avoiding "pulling with the back first" is critical for pain management.
*   **OHS Limitation (50 kg):** Correcting the "mid-thigh" contact point and eliminating early arm grabs will improve bar path efficiency and vertical velocity, potentially aiding overhead stability without immediately overloading the OHS.
*   **Night Shift Adaptability:** The shift toward reflexive, automatic movement patterns reduces the cognitive load required during training sessions, accommodating potential fatigue from Wed–Sun night shifts.
*   **Transition Strategy:** As an intermediate athlete moving to Olympic lifting, focusing on the "pre-acceleration" and simultaneous extension builds a solid foundation before introducing complex technical drills or heavy loads.
