# Merged Summary: Standing Press for Olympic Weightlifting

## Key Points

- Standing press is an **accessory exercise** (~1x/week frequency); do not overuse
- **Primary benefit for this athlete:** shoulder mobility/stability and spinal stability under load
- Limited direct carryover to jerk (leg-driven movement), but valuable for confidence under heavy shoulder load
- Shoulder mobility deficit is the **critical limiter**—identical to OHS/snatch rack position requirements

## Technique Cues

**Bar Position:**
- Bar on **collarbone + both deltoids** (three contact points), NOT fingertips
- Grip through middle of palm for force transfer
- Requires significant shoulder mobility; poor mobility forces bar too high/forward

**Body Position:**
- **Completely straight back**—no forward/backward lean or lumbar extension (critical for chronic back pain)
- Legs straight (no micro-squat dip)
- Neutral spine with brief pelvic tuck
- Diaphragm raised → rigid torso → intra-abdominal pressure

**Elbow Position:**
- Elbows slightly raised behind head, NOT flared to sides (kills pressing mechanics)
- Position maintains bar on shoulders/clavicle

**Movement Pattern:**
- Powerful full-body tension contraction (NOT isolated arm strength; similar to snatch pull mechanics)
- Vertical bar path
- No excessive backward arch

**Breathing:** Small inhale, forceful exhale on drive

## Programming Principles

- Accessory work should improve classic lift technique, not replace it
- Use same grip width as clean/jerk
- Regulate load/volume appropriately for accessory status
- Avoid micro-squats or leg drive (defeats press stimulus)

## Athlete Relevance

**Critical Issue:** OHS 50 kg is snatch limiter → shoulder mobility deficit is primary technical barrier

**Recommendation for 6-week hypertrophy block:**
- Standing press: **3–4x/week, 6–8 reps** with mobility emphasis
- **Primary objective:** thoracic/shoulder mobility gains (extension, external rotation)
- **Secondary benefit:** spinal stability work suits chronic back pain (rigid stance = safer loading pattern)
- **Limited jerk carryover** (jerk is leg/back-driven; press is shoulder-driven), but builds confidence under heavy shoulder load given push-jerk-only profile

Mobility work should be the focus; expect snatch/OHS improvements after ROM gains.
