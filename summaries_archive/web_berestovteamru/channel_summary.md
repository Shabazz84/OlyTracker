# Coaching Philosophy: Berestov Team Adaptation

## 1. Core Training Principles
*   **Mobility Over Raw Strength:** Lower body strength (e.g., 118kg squat) is irrelevant if overhead stability fails due to shoulder mobility limits. The 50kg OHS ceiling indicates a mechanical bottleneck that must be resolved before increasing load.
*   **The "Dip-Drive" Connection:** The jerk drive must be an explosive, elastic movement. The bar must bend (deflect) via the speed of the dip; if the dip is slow or controlled, the bar will not leave the chest efficiently, forcing the lifter to compensate with spinal torque.
*   **Posterior Chain Protection:** "Shooting hips forward" prematurely during the drive phase shifts load from the posterior chain to weak spinal erectors. This specific error must be eliminated to mitigate chronic back pain.
*   **Grip Economy:** Grip tension should be minimal during the rack position (relax wrists) and maximal only during the explosive pull. Unnecessary upper-body stiffness hinders bar path and increases injury risk for heavy lifters.

## 2. Programming Approach
*   **Structure:** Utilize an online asynchronous model to accommodate night shift schedules (Wed–Sun). Training blocks are structured as:
    *   **Phase 1 (Weeks 1-6):** Hypertrophy focus with technical refinement. Low volume, high intent.
    *   **Phase 2 (Weeks 7+):** Technique and strength integration.
*   **Intensity & Load:** Prioritize speed and "feel" over maximal load in early phases. Use light-to-moderate loads specifically to elicit bar deflection rather than force production.
*   **Frequency:** Technical drills (Push Jerk focus) performed 1–2 times weekly. Heavy loading is introduced only after the elastic dip-drive connection is mastered.

## 3. Key Technique Cues & Exercise Preferences
**The Push Jerk (Primary Focus)**
*   **Rack Position:** Bar rests in the center of the palm, supported by the clavicle ("behind the collarbone"). Elbows raised and spread to form a rigid shelf; chest up, diaphragm engaged. *Cue:* Relax wrists while resting; lock grip only on pull.
*   **The Dip:** Execute a sharp, explosive "rescue movement," not a slow squat. The speed of the dip must bend the bar. Maintain vertical torso; avoid shifting weight to the front foot or premature hip extension.
*   **The Drive & Transition:** Bar leaves chest at max drive height. Immediately after deflection, accelerate body downward to meet the bar ("switch speed"). Do not brake under the bar. Arms remain rigid behind the head (no forced retraction).

**Exercise Selection for Deflection**
*   **Primary Drill:** Push Jerk using thin bars (15kg women's or 10kg child bars) with plates loaded at the outermost sleeves to maximize deflection.
*   **Alternative:** Standard barbell with plates on the very ends of the sleeves if a thinner bar is unavailable, provided the athlete can accommodate the width without hitting the collar.

## 4. Recovery and Mobility Approach
*   **Mobility as Non-Negotiable:** Shoulder joint mobility is the prerequisite for overhead stability. Without it, progress stalls regardless of leg strength. Daily attention to shoulder range of motion is required before attempting heavy OHS or Jerk work.
*   **Pain Management:** The coaching model explicitly targets back pain by correcting the "shooting hips" error. By ensuring the drive comes from a vertical dip and not lumbar extension, load transfer to the spinal erectors is minimized.
*   **Fatigue Management:** For night shift workers with fluctuating energy levels, utilize low-systemic-fatigue drills (light barbell deflection) on days where fatigue is high, maintaining technical quality without exacerbating pain or exhaustion.

## 5. Application to Athlete Profile
**The Context:** Intermediate lifter (102.5 kg), Back Squat 118kg, Clean/Jerk limited by OHS (50kg). Chronic back pain. Night shift worker. No Split Jerk.

*   **Addressing the Mobility Ceiling:** The athlete's strong squat is currently wasted due to a mobility ceiling preventing overhead stability. The program prioritizes shoulder mobility drills and light barbell deflection work over heavy squats or jerks until the 50kg OHS limit is broken.
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** The chronic pain is likely exacerbated by poor jerk mechanics (shooting hips forward). By enforcing a vertical dip and strict posterior chain engagement, we remove the dangerous lumbar torque associated with the athlete's current technique.
*   **Schedule Adaptation:** The online coaching model fits the Wed–Sun night shift window perfectly. Video feedback allows for asynchronous correction of the "dip" speed and rack position without requiring real-time gym presence.
*   **Transition Strategy:** Since the athlete cannot yet perform a Split Jerk, mastering the Push Jerk with perfect elastic timing is the critical path to increasing load safely. The focus on "feeling" the bar bend allows for high-quality technical repetition even when systemic fatigue from night shifts is present.
