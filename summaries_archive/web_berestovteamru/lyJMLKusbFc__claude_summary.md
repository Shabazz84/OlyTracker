# Merged Summary: Back Squat Technique & Programming for Intermediate Olympic Weightlifter

## Key Points

- Back squat is a **supplemental exercise** to reinforce snatch/clean receiving position, not a primary goal
- Bar position on trapezius (not low/powerlifting style) reduces lumbar shear stress — critical for chronic back pain management
- Depth must prioritize **spinal integrity over range** — vertical spine and rigid core are non-negotiable
- Knee sleeves (7mm) recommended for joint stability; belt use at 80%+ intensity, secured at lumbar level
- Mobility deficits (especially shoulder/thoracic) are root causes of compensation — address directly rather than widening grip

## Technique Cues

| Element | Cue |
|---------|-----|
| **Bar Position** | On trapezius + shoulders; slightly below clavicle; never on neck or low |
| **Rack Height** | Match bar position at trap level |
| **Grip Width** | Shoulder-width starting point; wider only if mobility allows (reduces forward lean) |
| **Stance** | Match snatch/clean receiving — parallel or slight toe-out; NOT wide (powerlifting style) |
| **Movement** | Hip-back first; controlled descent; powerful ascent with acceleration |
| **Spine/Core** | Vertical spine non-negotiable; rigid core brace; prevent excessive forward lean |
| **Knee Tracking** | Parallel alignment; prevent inward collapse (valgus) |
| **Breathing** | Inhale ~50% before descent; hold breath; brace during squat |
| **Depth** | As deep as possible **while maintaining vertical spine & full foot contact** — not maximum range |

## Programming Principles

- Back squat reinforces receiving position mechanics for snatch/clean — program as supplemental accessory, not primary
- Use belt at 80%+ intensity; secure at lumbar level (not too high)
- Knee sleeves maintain joint warmth during heavy work
- Always unrack/rerack facing rack with small, controlled steps

## Athlete Relevance

**CRITICAL FOR CHRONIC BACK PAIN:**

- **Vertical posture + rigid bracing** directly mitigates lumbar stress vs. powerlifting (low bar) style
- **Controlled eccentric** (no bouncing) reduces sudden spinal loading
- **Bar on traps** reduces shear on lumbar spine
- **OHS severely limited (50 kg vs. 80 kg clean)** — likely mobility + back stability issue; back squat depth must prioritize stability over range to avoid reinforcing poor positioning

**6-Week Hypertrophy Block Application:**
- Use back squat as supplemental volume to build receiving position strength while protecting back
- Pair with thoracic/shoulder mobility work (address root cause of limited OHS)
- Moderate intensity, controlled tempo, emphasis on bracing cues
- Consider night shift fatigue — keep volume sustainable on recovery days (Wed–Sun schedule)
