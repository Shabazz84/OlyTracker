# Jerk exercises. Weightlifting, amateurs

# Weightlifting Coaching Summary

## Key Points
- **Weekly structure (amateur):** Mon (snatch-focused) | Wed (clean/jerk-focused) | Fri (base/squat-focused)
- **Intensity caps:** Max 80% on compound complexes; 90–100% on pulls
- **Volume:** 3 sets of compound work at submaximal intensity, adjusted weekly based on recovery
- **Periodization:** Off-season programming adjusted session-to-session, not competition-cycle driven
- **Fatigue protocol:** Complexes tire relevant muscle groups before technical work to reinforce technique under load

## Technique Cues

**Clean (foot contact):**
- Keep heels planted throughout pull—do NOT lift off early
- Early toe-off limits hip/back/glute extension power
- Requires full foot contact for maximal force production

**Front rack/grip:**
- Locked grip throughout descent (not just catch) for shoulder/elbow stability
- Reduces muscular effort lowering; requires substantial shoulder/elbow mobility
- Poor mobility → cannot execute clean technique; mobility work is inseparable from technical progression

**Jerk:**
- Maximum-effort upward drive with aggressive heel drive and full extension before footwork
- Common error: arms bend/curl during pull instead of staying straight, wasting bar height and speed
- Aggressive dip/drive matters more than pulling high

## Programming Principles
- Self-assess readiness during warmup; willingness/recovery status determines session intensity
- Complexes (clean without foot spread + push jerk → pull to extension → clean → squat → jerk @ 80%) fatigue target muscles before technical jerk work
- Mid-week (Wed) should emphasize recovery; heavy technical work requires adequate preceding sleep
- Conditioning additions must not compromise primary lifting recovery

## Athlete Relevance

**Critical for your profile (102.5 kg, chronic back pain, 50 kg OHS weakness):**
- **Foot-contact pull principle** reduces compensatory spinal loading from early toe-off
- **Shoulder/elbow mobility gap:** Your 50 kg OHS vs. 80 kg clean indicates front-rack/mobility limitation—prioritize mobility work before jerk sessions
- **Wednesday jerk fatigue protocol is risky** without adequate mobility prep and core stability; assess front squat/clean mechanics for back safety first
- **Night shift schedule (Wed–Sun):** Sleep compromise before mid-week technical work; ensure recovery buffer before heavy jerk sessions
- **Hypertrophy block strategy:** Use base-building phase to address mobility constraints and back stability before intensity increases
