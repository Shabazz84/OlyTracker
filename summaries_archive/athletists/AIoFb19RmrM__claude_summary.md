# Merged Weightlifting Coaching Summary

## Key Points

**Programming Structure:**
- Competition prep (2 weeks out): High-frequency loading at 80–90% before deload
- Amateur athletes recover faster than elites; can maintain higher loads closer to competition
- Weekly structure includes jerk-specific days with supplemental exercises and dedicated rest days
- Clean + jerk combined when loads are lighter; split when substantial (jerk 90%+)
- **Critical principle:** Always stand up every clean/jerk rep to drive leg development—contrasts with pull-focused approaches

**Load Management:**
- Lower weights (70–80%) allow flexible positioning; heavier loads (90%+) expose technical weaknesses
- Use bands/pin positions to target specific weaknesses (slow pull, weak leg drive) rather than heavy deadlifts alone
- Jerk from rack/blocks preferable to split jerk variants for athletes transitioning to Olympic lifting

## Technique Cues

**Front Rack Position (Most Critical):**
- Bar on shoulders/hands, not fingertips (weak base causes wrist/elbow pressure)
- Elbows high and forward; rotate elbows forward to create shoulder platform (don't force shoulders back unnaturally)
- Maintain upright chest/back posture; individual lumbar lordosis varies by anatomy

**Jerk Dip & Drive:**
- Dip initiation is the most crucial phase—athlete must feel explosive power from starting position
- Hips should not rise excessively; controlled, shallow dip prioritized
- Avoid forward lean in dip; maintain vertical torso
- **How you dip determines bar path; poor dip = bar flies forward**

**Power vs. Split Jerk:**
- Power jerk: shorter ROM, faster recovery, less ankle/knee mobility demand, reduces lower-body fatigue
- Split jerk: requires extended time in split position (higher posterior chain load)

**General Principle:** "Don't lift the bar—fall under it" (applicable to all three lifts)

## Programming Principles

**Exercise Sequencing:**
- Clean to front rack + power jerk (1+2 rep pattern common)
- Jerk from blocks/rack with push/power jerk variants (primary for this athlete)
- Power jerk + dip combinations at 1+2 rep schemes
- Low-dip front rack work at 70–75% for shoulder mobility + movement speed
- Front rack holds/position work (critical for arm/elbow comfort)
- Clean variations from pins/bands at moderate weight for acceleration work targeting weak points

**6-Week Hypertrophy Block Application:**
- Include jerk from rack 1–2x/week at moderate intensity (70–75%)
- Emphasize starting position perfection and shoulder mobility over load increases
- Prioritize standing every clean/jerk rep to maximize leg development
- Warm-up protocol: Start light (50–70 kg) even after prior warm-up work; emphasize leg activation before heavy jerk/clean work

**Recovery Considerations:**
- Shoulder mobility: Essential for this athlete (OHS limiter); requires 3–4x/week dedicated work
- Avoid deep dip work until shoulder mobility improves (transfers lumbar spine stress)
- Adequate mobility work before heavy dips; stretch shoulders adequately

## Athlete Relevance

**High Priority Issues:**
- **Chronic back pain + night shift fatigue:** Recommend conservative loading on low-dip jerk variants; avoid excessive lumbar stress. Warm-up protocol becomes critical when entering sessions in cold state.
- **OHS 50 kg (confirmed shoulder mobility deficit):** Prioritize front rack mobility work and high-frequency shoulder activation before increasing jerk intensity
- **Split jerk not yet trained:** Power/push jerk currently appropriate; defer split jerk to dedicated technique phase
- **Clean 80 kg relative to 118 kg back squat:** Suggests pulling/catching weakness → prescribe clean variations from pins at 60–75 kg for acceleration work
- **Jerk 65 kg (power/push only):** Build leg drive via heavy back squat work + jerk dips; progress to split jerk once squat strength ≥100 kg

**Specific Recommendations:**
1. Master front rack position (elbow rotation, shoulder platform) before driving intensity
2. Always stand up every clean/jerk rep during hypertrophy block to maximize quad/posterior chain development
3. Add shoulder mobility work 3–4x/week (essential given night shift fatigue and OHS limitation)
4. Use power jerk exclusively; delay split jerk training until strength/technique phase
5. Keep jerk work moderate (70–75%) during hypertrophy block; prioritize movement quality and starting position perfection over load
6. Use band/pin clean variations to target pull acceleration weakness rather than heavy deadlifts
7. Avoid deep dip work; prioritize shallow, controlled dip position with vertical torso alignment
