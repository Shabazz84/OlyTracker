# Survive the training camp and finish with a record!

## Key Points
*   **Profile Mismatch Warning:** The source material consists of general coaching anecdotes and youth training logs; it lacks specific periodization, load management protocols, or recovery strategies tailored to an intermediate adult with chronic back pain and night shift work.
*   **Jerk Mechanics:** Success relies on driving the bar slightly *behind* the head (not forward) and moving the entire torso under the bar simultaneously. Relying solely on deltoid strength ("hanging" on the front leg) leads to instability and compensatory lumbar arching, which exacerbates back pain.
*   **Squat Safety:** Avoid "dropping" or "crunching" into the bottom of a squat; focus on controlled descent (amortization) and vertical torso alignment to protect the spine and knees.
*   **Individualization:** Technique variables (scissors distance, grip width) must be adapted to individual anatomy rather than rigid rules. Narrowing jerk grip may be necessary if shoulder mobility limits overhead stability.

## Technique Cues
**Push Jerk (Primary Focus)**
*   **"Push Behind the Head":** Drive the bar slightly backward so the body can move under it; pushing strictly forward shifts load to shoulders and causes loss of control.
*   **Torso Travel:** Move the entire torso under the bar ("fall forward with the body"), not just the arms. The bar must travel *with* you.
*   **Front Leg Action:** Actively extend the front leg to create an open angle; do not "hang" on the front knee or let it touch the floor (unless specific conditioning).
*   **Grip Adjustment:** If overhead stability is compromised, narrow the grip for comfort and shoulder safety rather than forcing a wide position that increases bar travel distance.
*   **Hand Position:** Keep wrists relaxed (like a boxer's punch) during the push; avoid clenching fists to ensure explosive release from the palm base.

**Back Squat**
*   **Descent Control:** Lower slowly and calmly; do not "hit" or drop into the bottom position. Focus on absorbing force rather than creating impact noise in the knees.
*   **Torso Alignment:** Maintain a vertical torso over the line of force to prevent excessive forward lean that strains the lumbar spine.

**Conditioning (Recovery)**
*   **"Goose Walk":** Low-squat walking for stability, but use with caution if back pain is acute.
*   **Active Recovery:** Light running at high cadence (~180 spm) to keep heart rate low; avoid high-impact footwear like basketball shoes.

## Programming Principles
*   **No Prescribed Schemes:** The source contains no specific sets, reps, or intensity percentages for a 6-week block.
*   **Quality Over Volume:** Prioritize movement quality and "pleasure" in training over high-volume accumulation.
*   **Intensity Management:** Avoid attempting max attempts immediately after heavy sessions; allow recovery between high-intensity efforts to prevent fatigue-induced technical breakdown.
*   **Individualized Load:** Adjust grip width and foot placement based on immediate comfort and stability rather than theoretical ideals.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The emphasis on vertical torso alignment and avoiding "hitting" the bottom of the squat is critical to prevent lumbar exacerbation. The warning against "deltoid hanging" in the jerk prevents compensatory spinal extension under load.
*   **Night Shift Work:** While no specific shift protocols exist, the advice for low-intensity active recovery (conversational pace running) aligns with managing fatigue from irregular sleep schedules without inducing excessive systemic stress.
*   **OHS Limitation (50kg):** The instruction to prioritize shoulder comfort over theoretical grip width is vital; narrowing the jerk grip may improve stability if overhead mobility is a primary limiter, even at the cost of increased bar travel distance.
*   **Transition Phase:** The focus on individualized mechanics (e.g., scissors distance) supports an intermediate lifter finding their specific technique rather than adhering to rigid dogma during the transition to Olympic lifting.
