# Survive the training camp and finish with a record!

## Key Points
- Jerk grip width should prioritize comfort and mobility over convention; tension in hands during grip reduces explosive drive
- Back squat technique should emphasize controlled descent (amortization phase) over rebound/bounce style, especially critical given athlete's chronic back pain and relative inexperience
- Light aerobic work (low-intensity running) does not compromise strength outcomes
- World-class lifters use unconventional positions based on individual comfort/anatomy rather than strict form templates

## Technique Cues
**Jerk:**
- Grip width: find position where bar rests comfortably on shoulders/wrist with minimal tension; avoid gripping/tensing the hand during drive phase
- Relaxed hand position enables better explosion off shoulders

**Back Squat:**
- Prioritize smooth, controlled descent over rapid rebound
- Powerful ascent with heavy loads; avoid bouncing at bottom
- Spinal safety emphasis: controlled movement protects lower back

## Programming Principles
- Individual anatomy and comfort should guide technical adjustments rather than standardized cues
- Light aerobic conditioning (conversational pace, ~180 bpm reference cadence) can be included without detriment to strength development

## Athlete Relevance
**Critical for this athlete:**
- **Jerk grip adjustment**: Current discomfort with wider grips aligns with coaching principle of comfort-first positioning; experiment narrower to find optimal shoulder-bar contact without hand tension
- **Back squat mechanics**: Controlled descent is non-negotiable given chronic back pain; avoid rebound style entirely during hypertrophy block; emphasize eccentric control
- **Push jerk readiness**: Current 65 kg push jerk can progress; ensure hand relaxation during drive phase is established before split jerk introduction
- **Recovery compatibility**: Night shift schedule may benefit from low-intensity conditioning; ensure it doesn't interfere with primary lift recovery
