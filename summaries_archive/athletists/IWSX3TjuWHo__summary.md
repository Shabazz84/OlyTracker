# Соревновательная модель тренировки. О великих штангистах

## Key Points
*   **Training Model:** The source describes a "competition-model" session designed for peak performance, not hypertrophy development. It prioritizes high-intensity simulation over volume accumulation.
*   **Intensity Cycling:** The protocol cycles between 70% (active recovery/maintenance) and 90–100% (max-effort attempts). Athletes drop to 70% immediately after heavy sets to maintain muscle temperature without fatigue accumulation.
*   **Lift Sequence:** Strict adherence to Snatch first, followed by Clean & Jerk. No auxiliary exercises are performed during these specific sessions; the focus is exclusively on the competition lifts.
*   **Recovery Constraints:** The text explicitly advises against using rigid tape or plasters for finger protection; elastic bandages wrapped to a comfortable thickness are preferred to prevent blisters without restricting grip.

## Technique Cues
*   **Snatch Execution:** Prioritize speed and explosive power over pull height. Avoid the error of "chasing height" (trying to lift the bar too high), which can trigger lumbar extension issues; instead, focus on dropping under the bar quickly while it flies up.
*   **Clean & Jerk Dynamics:** Recognize that the Clean is not as fast as the Snatch. Focus specifically on the acceleration of the "second pull" before initiating the drop under the bar.
*   **Common Errors:** Prevent "dropping the bar early" (not finishing the lift), which leads to knee/ankle instability and excessive forward lean. Ensure equal focus is applied to both attempts within a paired set.

## Programming Principles
*   **Session Structure:** Warm-up progressively to 70% (2–3 sets), ascend to 90%, perform max-effort cycles up to 100%, and utilize 70% sets as active recovery between heavy attempts.
*   **Auxiliary Work:** Zero accessory exercises are recommended for this specific session type; the coach explicitly omits them during competition-model days to preserve energy for the main lifts.
*   **Drill Integration:** Use "Snatch to rack" (high pull) specifically as a speed and feel drill prior to full lifts, rather than as a hypertrophy movement.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Critical Mismatch:** The source is **low relevance** for the current 6-week hypertrophy block. It focuses on peak competition preparation (100% intensity) which contradicts the goal of building muscle mass and managing chronic back pain through volume.
*   **Pain Management Caveat:** While the text advises against "chasing height," this specific cue regarding excessive lumbar extension may be beneficial for an athlete with chronic back pain, provided it does not compromise the necessary pull mechanics for technique refinement.
*   **Missing Variables:** The source offers no guidance on sleep hygiene for night shift workers (Wed–Sun schedule) or specific mobility protocols to address lower back pain, making it insufficient as a standalone plan for this athlete's current constraints.
