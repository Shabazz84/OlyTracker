# Чего не нужно делать перед соревнованиями. Тяжелая атлетика

## Key Points
*   **Psychological Approach:** Prioritize light technical training over complete rest before competition phases to manage anxiety; avoid "obsessing" over weight cutting (suishing) if it compromises strength gains and recovery.
*   **Anthropometric Adaptation:** Block height must be customized to the athlete's leverage; do not force flexibility by using blocks that are too low, as this risks injury or compromised form.
*   **Muscle Recruitment:** The primary driver of the pull is leg drive, not hip elevation. The torso must remain stable during the initial phase to maintain spinal tension.

## Technique Cues
*   **"Do Not Hike Hips":** A critical cue for athletes with chronic back pain. Prevents lifting the torso/hips prematurely ("задирает таз"), which reduces lumbar shear and ensures force is generated through the legs rather than the lower back.
*   **"Pull Back Tight" (Spinal Tension):** Before initiating movement, establish full spinal tension. If flexibility limits range of motion, adjust block height upward to maintain a neutral spine rather than rounding or over-extending.
*   **Short, Explosive Pull:** From the blocks, focus on a rapid transition into the second pull ("четкое короткое движение") to maximize vertical velocity without excessive bar travel time.
*   **Stability Under Height:** Use drills (like Push Jerk from blocks) to overcome psychological resistance to height and build stability under load.

## Programming Principles
*   **Drill Selection:** Utilize **Snatch/Clean from Blocks** as primary technical drills for the hypertrophy/technique block. These allow high-intensity leg drive practice with reduced spinal loading compared to full-range pulls, ideal for managing back pain while building coordination.
*   **Intensity Management:** While advanced texts suggest 4 sets at 80% intensity, this volume/intensity is likely too high for an intermediate athlete in a hypertrophy phase; prioritize technical quality and lower loads initially.
*   **Weight Class Strategy:** Do not implement weight cutting protocols during the growth/strength phase. Focus on increasing absolute strength (e.g., Clean >80kg) before considering class reduction.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Back Pain Management:** The "Do Not Hike Hips" cue directly addresses the athlete's chronic back pain by enforcing a leg-dominant pull pattern, reducing lumbar strain caused by compensatory hip lifting.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** Block work bridges the gap between raw strength and Olympic technique, specifically targeting the intermediate need for better bar path control without the high injury risk of heavy full-range pulls.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** While the text lacks specific sleep protocols, it highlights that psychological barriers (fear/anxiety) often hinder performance more than physical fatigue. Technical drills from blocks can build confidence and motor patterns even when energy levels fluctuate due to shift work.
*   **Equipment Constraints:** The advice to adjust block height based on comfort and flexibility is crucial for an athlete with limited mobility (OHS 50kg limiter), ensuring the pull starts safely without forcing range of motion that exacerbates pain.
