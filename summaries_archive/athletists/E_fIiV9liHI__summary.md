# Обводим колени, делаем ножницы. Много разных ошибок! Это сборы!

## Key Points
*   **Mobility-Limited Overhead:** The primary limiter for the Overhead Squat (OHS) and Jerk is not pure strength, but restricted shoulder flexion and tight lats causing compensatory lumbar hyperextension ("arching").
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** Chronic back pain is exacerbated by "hugging" the bar with arms early, rushing the pull, or arching to compensate for poor overhead mobility. Stability must come from intra-abdominal pressure (IAP) and lat engagement, not spinal extension.
*   **Jerk Technique Evolution:** While currently using a push jerk, the analysis suggests transitioning toward split jerks ("scissors") as they are more forgiving of shoulder limitations and allow better recovery under load. However, if sticking to push jerks, strict overhead stability is non-negotiable.
*   **Pull Mechanics:** Snatch/Clean pulls require maximizing leg drive while keeping hips low initially to protect the lumbar spine. The "meet" (catch) point must be reinforced through explosive transitions from half-squats or blocks.

## Technique Cues
*   **Overhead Stability (Jerk/Snatch):** Keep the spine "flat against a wall" during overhead movements; do not arch the lower back to gain range of motion. Engage lats ("wide muscles") to pull arms behind the head without rib flare.
*   **Pull Initiation:** Avoid lifting hips too early ("не задрали"). Control the descent to the block/stand, then explode. Do not "hug" the bar with arms; use traps and legs only. Keep elbows high but do not drag the bar away from the body.
*   **Bar Path & Contact:** Maintain constant contact between the bar and the body ("прижимай штангу к себе"). Prevent the center of gravity from drifting backward.
*   **Knee Alignment:** Actively push knees out during the pull and transition phase.
*   **Jerk Recovery (if transitioning to split):** Keep split steps short; shift center of gravity backward and "climb" out of the split by driving the front foot forward rather than letting the bar pull you down.
*   **Core Engagement:** Create IAP by lifting the diaphragm and protruding hips/tailbone slightly, avoiding excessive glute clenching which can increase shear forces.

## Programming Principles
*   **Drill Progression:** Prioritize isolated pattern work (pause jerks, half-squat snatches) before full-intensity lifts to ingrain motor patterns. Use sub-maximal loads for technique refinement.
*   **Specific Variations:**
    *   **Snatch Pulls from Blocks/Stands:** Use small plates or stands to simulate the bottom position, emphasizing leg drive over arm pull.
    *   **"Half-Squat" Snatches (Polupod):** Execute sharp, explosive movements from a half-squat to force an earlier catch and sharper arm action.
    *   **Mobility Drills:** Daily wall scapular slides (heels/glutes/back against wall) to monitor shoulder mobility without lumbar compensation. Stop immediately if the lower back peels off the surface.
*   **Grip Management:** Use lifting straps during pull variations and snatch work from blocks to eliminate grip fatigue, forcing trapezius and deltoid engagement instead of forearm failure.
*   **Tempo Control:** Avoid rushing the initial pull; prioritize controlled transitions before the explosive phase to manage fatigue (critical for night shift workers).

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The cues to avoid early hip lift, prevent lumbar arching during overhead work, and utilize IAP directly address the athlete's pain source. Controlling the descent and maintaining a flat spine reduces shear forces on the lumbar region.
*   **OHS Limitation (50 kg):** The analysis confirms the OHS failure is likely due to thoracic/shoulder mobility and lat tightness rather than leg strength. Addressing this via wall slides and lat engagement will unlock overhead depth before increasing load.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** The emphasis on "calm," controlled execution over speed helps manage fluctuating energy levels common in night shift workers (Wed–Sun). Using straps allows the athlete to focus on spinal stability without grip fatigue when recovery is suboptimal.
*   **Transition from Push Jerk:** While the athlete currently uses only a push jerk, the technique cues regarding shoulder mobility and split-step mechanics provide a roadmap for safely introducing split jerks if overhead flexibility remains a bottleneck.
*   **Hypertrophy Block Prep:** The focus on "heavy leg loading" via pulls and controlled technical drills provides a foundation for the upcoming 6-week hypertrophy block, ensuring the athlete builds strength without exacerbating back pain.
