# .   90%,  100!

## Key Points
- Limit heavy attempts to **3 max attempts maximum**; additional attempts accumulate fatigue without benefit
- Progress with **incremental jumps** (5 kg increments, e.g., 80→85→90) rather than large jumps to build confidence
- **Bar path control is critical**: bar should stay close to body; excessive forward displacement (>15 cm) indicates technical failure
- **Pull strength and leg drive** must be balanced; weak legs limit snatch pull height and receiving position depth
- Asymptomatic disc issues are not training contraindications; controlled resistance work with proper form strengthens stabilizers
- Different athletes respond to different cues—avoid avoiding uncomfortable positions; train through them to build capacity

## Technique Cues
**Snatch:**
- Keep bar close ("lick yourself with the bar")
- Maintain center of gravity over bar at start
- Engage pull with hip/leg drive into the pull
- Control descent; don't drop excessively low

**Clean/Jerk:**
- Smooth descent into dip, then explosive drive (avoid hesitant/jerky movements)
- Prevent forward collapse in dip

**Back Squat:**
- Maintain neutral spine; don't collapse forward
- Proper bar placement prevents forward shift

## Programming Principles
- 90% intensity signals readiness to attempt maxes **only if executed with confidence**
- Listen to body on heavy days; fatigue management is non-negotiable
- Avoid excessive volume at near-maximal loads
- Mental readiness matters as much as physical capacity
