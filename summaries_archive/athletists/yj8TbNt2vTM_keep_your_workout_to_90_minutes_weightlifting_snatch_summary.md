# Keep your workout to 90 minutes. Weightlifting. Snatch.

## Key Points
*   **Session Duration & Volume:** Limit sessions to 1.5–2 hours (4–5 exercises). Exceeding this shifts focus from Olympic power development to "strength endurance," reducing quality.
*   **Intensity Management:** Begin main movements with a light barbell warm-up regardless of feeling fresh. Progress to ~80% intensity based on daily readiness ("listen to your organism"). If fatigue or pain is present, reduce load rather than skipping the session entirely.
*   **Frequency vs. Quality:** While 5–6 days/week is mentioned, quality takes precedence. Missing a day due to poor recovery (common for night shift workers) is superior to forcing a session that risks injury.
*   **Periodization Flow:** Combine movements logically (e.g., Classic Snatch followed by Hang Above Knee) to reinforce the final drive without losing drop-under speed.
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** Utilize "Snatch Pulls from Groin" with no leg drive and no back involvement to isolate pulling mechanics and grip strength while unloading the lumbar spine.

## Technique Cues
*   **"Drop Under" Speed:** The primary cue for beginners is dropping under the bar quickly without pushing up first (unlike a clean pull). Practice this with just the barbell or light weights (30–40 kg) to master speed before adding load.
*   **Foot Strike Audibility:** On initial learning stages, "hit" the floor hard ("beat your legs") to create an audible sound. This ensures subconscious explosive power and proper timing; subtlety can be introduced once mastered.
*   **Bar Path & Friction:** Keep the bar within 1mm of the body. Friction against thighs/knees aids trajectory control during the pull; avoiding all contact often leads to a wider path and power loss.
*   **No Premature Push:** When practicing drops, do not push the bar up with arms first. Let gravity take you down while keeping hands active to guide the bar.

## Programming Principles
*   **Warm-up/Prep Strategy:** Use "Snatch Pull from Groin" (approx. 30 kg) for 6 sets as a pure arm-driven pull to warm up tendons without spinal loading. For overhead pressing limitations, isolate this movement or start with just the barbell before increasing load.
*   **Main Lift Sequencing:** Pair the Classic Snatch with Snatch from Above Knee in a single session (e.g., 2 sets at 50%, then 1 set at 85%) to reinforce drive speed without full lift complexity.
*   **Grip Development:** Include Snatch from Floor (no straps) as an accessory for grip strength.
*   **Mobility & Protection:** Prioritize ankle dorsiflexion and hip mobility work to enable proper squat/snatch depth. Use tape or patches on shins/knees to prevent friction burns rather than relying solely on knee sleeves.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The "Snatch Pull from Groin" (no leg drive) directly addresses the athlete's back pain by allowing technical work without lumbar loading.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** Starting main lifts at 50% or just the barbell prevents reinforcing bad habits under heavy loads, crucial for an athlete transitioning to Olympic lifting with existing strength patterns.
*   **Night Shift Worker (Wed–Sun):** The emphasis on "listening to your organism" and adjusting intensity based on daily readiness is vital for managing fluctuating sleep cycles. Skipping a hard session when fatigued aligns better with irregular schedules than forcing performance.
*   **OHS Limitation (50 kg) & Push Jerk:** The coach's notes on the difficulty of pressing from behind the neck due to flexibility correlate with the athlete's OHS limiter. This suggests isolating overhead mobility work and using lighter weights before attempting heavier loads, supporting the transition to a push-jerk-only style.
