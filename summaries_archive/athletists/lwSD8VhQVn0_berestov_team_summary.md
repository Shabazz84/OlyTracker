# Базовая тренировка по тяжелой атлетике. Berestov team

## Key Points
*   **Periodization Strategy:** Prioritize a "base" phase (strength and hypertrophy) before intensive technical refinement. Alternate high-intensity days with lighter technique work to manage fatigue.
*   **Load Management:** Utilize variable intensity based on daily readiness rather than rigid percentages. Cap heavy sessions at 70–80% if fatigue or joint stiffness is present; prioritize "listening to the body" over hitting arbitrary targets.
*   **Exercise Selection:** Heavy squats and deadlifts form the foundation of "base" days. Snatch pulls from blocks are preferred for isolating back speed and trap engagement without exhausting the legs prior to squatting. Push jerks and overhead squats (OHS) are essential for developing shoulder stability and overhead strength.
*   **Equipment:** Wooden lifting blocks are recommended over metal for durability and practicality during pull variations.

## Technique Cues
*   **Snatch Pulls from Blocks:** Use block height to isolate the final acceleration phase above the knee, ensuring the bar is at the "power position" immediately after the initial pull. Keep the bar close to the legs and maintain optimal angles for leg drive; avoid over-compensating with upper body mechanics.
*   **Torso & Head Position:** Strictly avoid throwing the head back during pulls or cleans, as this strains the lower back. Maintain a stable torso and keep traps engaged ("pull into the trap") throughout the movement.
*   **Footwork & Base:** Drive through the full foot to ensure stability; establish a solid base before initiating any lift.
*   **Jerk Mechanics (Push Style):** Utilize leg drive to initiate the movement, followed by a lockout of the arms. This aligns with push jerk mechanics where shoulder stability is prioritized over split footwork complexity.

## Programming Principles
*   **Frequency:** 3 sessions per week (e.g., Monday for Snatch focus; Wednesday/Friday for Clean & Jerk or Base work).
*   **Session Structure:**
    *   **"Base" Days:** Prioritize heavy squats, deadlifts, and pulls to build strength capacity.
    *   **Technical Days:** Focus on snatch/clean mechanics using variations like snatch from blocks or snatch balance to reduce leg fatigue while maintaining technical focus.
*   **Adaptive Loading:** Adjust daily intensity based on subjective feedback regarding joint stiffness (knees/ankles) or muscle spasms. If the body feels stiff, reduce intensity immediately rather than forcing a heavy session.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain Management:** The emphasis on keeping the head neutral and avoiding "throwing the back" directly addresses the athlete's chronic pain history, reducing lumbar strain during pulls.
*   **Night Shift & Variable Energy:** The programming philosophy of training based on daily readiness rather than a fixed schedule accommodates the fatigue fluctuations inherent in night shift work (Wed–Sun). It allows for intensity reduction when energy is low without derailing progress.
*   **Push Jerk Specialization:** The athlete's specific limitation to push jerks aligns with the methodology of using "push" movements to build overhead stability and strength, bypassing the technical complexity of split jerks while addressing OHS limitations through targeted shoulder work.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** Using snatch pulls from blocks allows an intermediate lifter to handle heavier loads safely, bridging the gap between raw strength (118kg squat) and Olympic technique without the high fatigue cost of full lifts on every rep.
