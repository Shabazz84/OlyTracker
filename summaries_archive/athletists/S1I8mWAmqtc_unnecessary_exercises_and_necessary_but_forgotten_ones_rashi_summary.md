# Unnecessary exercises and necessary but forgotten ones. Rashid Itsaev

## Key Points
*   **Isometric Strength:** High-value static holds (5–20 seconds) are prioritized over pure volume to build tendon strength, spinal stability, and "feel" for the bar without excessive shear force. This is critical for managing chronic back pain.
*   **Neural Priming Strategy:** Max effort front squats should be performed at the **beginning** of a session (single attempts) rather than at the end to stimulate neural drive before fatigue accumulates, essential for night-shift athletes with limited recovery windows.
*   **Mobility over Static Stretching:** Dynamic warm-ups like "Goose Walks" and "Frog Walks" are superior to static stretching for preparing hips and the lower back for lifting.
*   **Blood Pressure Caution:** Isometric holds carry a risk of hypertensive spikes; progression must start at 5 seconds, and breathing control is mandatory.

## Technique Cues
*   **"Feel the Bar":** Execute slow, controlled movements (squats, holds) to sense bar position and body tension rather than rushing ("bam-bam"). This builds proprioception essential for a push-jerk-only athlete who lacks the dynamic transition of a split jerk.
*   **Scissor Snatch Balance:** Drop into a scissor/snatch balance pattern rather than a full lunge. Focus on maintaining a "square" base and keeping weight centered over one foot for 1 second before recovery to improve stability under load.
*   **Goose Walk (Гусиный шаг):** Perform dynamic walking drills holding the barbell in a low squat position (approx. 50kg) to activate hips and stabilize the spine. This serves as a primary warm-up for back pain mitigation.

## Programming Principles
*   **Isometric Progression:** Incorporate heavy deadlift holds or rack pulls with maximum tension but no movement. Start at 5 seconds, progressing to 20 seconds only if blood pressure remains stable. Use these to build structural integrity during the hypertrophy block.
*   **Front Squat Sequencing:** Schedule max effort front squat attempts (1 rep) as the first main movement of the session. This leverages fresh neural drive for strength gains without fatigue interference, aligning with the goal of transitioning from intermediate strength to Olympic lifting.
*   **Sub-Maximal Volume:** Avoid frequent maximal testing ("going for limits"). Prioritize sub-maximal work combined with long isometric holds to build structural integrity before competition phases or heavy loading.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain Management:** The emphasis on isometrics and "Goose Walks" provides a mechanism to strengthen spinal erectors and lats statically, reducing shear forces associated with dynamic heavy lifting while maintaining stability.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation (Wed–Sun):** Performing high-neural tasks (max front squats) at the start of the session ensures peak performance despite late-night fatigue. The structured warm-up (Goose Walks) specifically targets hip mobility issues common in shift workers with sedentary periods.
*   **Push Jerk Constraint:** Since a split jerk is not used, the "Scissor Snatch" and "Feel the Bar" cues are vital for developing the fine motor control and balance required to stabilize the overhead position without the dynamic recovery of a split stance.
*   **Hypertrophy Block Prep:** The strategy of neural priming (max squats early) followed by controlled work supports the transition into a 6-week hypertrophy block, ensuring the athlete is structurally sound before increasing volume.
