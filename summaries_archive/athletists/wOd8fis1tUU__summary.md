# К большим весам через тяги и приседания. Рашид Ицаев

## Key Points
*   **Accessory Necessity:** Competition lifts alone are insufficient for growth; heavy pulling and squatting are mandatory to build the strength required for high-level performance.
*   **Pre-Competition Loading:** A 3–4 day taper should not involve complete rest; instead, lift at ~95% intensity or perform specific heavy pulls to "feel" the weight before competition.
*   **Spinal Safety First:** For athletes with chronic back pain, prioritize spinal strengthening (good mornings/bends) and ROM work *before* loading heavy squats.
*   **Mental Discipline:** Distraction is a primary cause of injury; strict focus on the barbell and leaving external stress outside the gym is critical for safety and performance.
*   **Recovery Reality:** Recovery time increases with age (7–8 months for full restoration after layoff); night shift workers must prioritize sleep quality to match physical recovery needs.

## Technique Cues
*   **The "Triangle" Pull Path:** Do not pull the bar in a straight vertical line from the floor. Initiate by moving slightly away from the center to create leverage, then pull back tight ("pull away, then pull in").
*   **Arm Relaxation:** Keep arms straight and relaxed during the first pull; do not grip or bend elbows until the second pull (dip). Gripping too early causes shoulder collapse and excessive lumbar arching.
*   **Toe Drive & Heel Lift:** Rise onto the toes/forefoot ("base of the toes") immediately upon initiating the pull, catch, and press. This engages calves/traps for a powerful upward drive.
*   **Push Jerk Specifics:** Since split jerks are not used:
    *   The bar must travel high (above forehead) before the dip.
    *   Use a sharp, explosive leg drive; do not "sit" deep in the squat before pressing.
    *   Maintain finger engagement ("clamping") at lockout even if grip is slightly released to manage fatigue.
*   **Stability:** Once overhead, fixate for 3 seconds to prove stability before racking or dropping.

## Programming Principles
*   **Structure:** Integrate heavy pulling (130–140kg for a 102.5kg lifter) and squats into every cycle; do not rely solely on snatch/clean/jerk variations.
*   **Intensity Management:** Use high-intensity singles (90%+) to build confidence, but only after establishing spinal strength through accessory work.
*   **Frequency:** Maintain a consistent routine (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri) rather than sporadic training; consistency is required for the "order in the head" needed for heavy lifts.
*   **Warm-up Protocol:** Mandatory daily morning dynamic warm-ups; avoid aggressive static stretching before lifting as it reduces strength potential.
*   **Progression Logic:** Heavy accessory work (pulls/squats) makes the competition lift feel lighter and builds the confidence to trust the body under load.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The coach's specific directive to strengthen the back via "bends" before heavy squats is a critical protocol for your injury profile. Strict arm relaxation during pulls is non-negotiable to prevent lumbar hyperextension.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** Your reliance on push jerks makes the "toe drive" and high bar path cues essential, as you lack the split technique to compensate for low bar height. The focus must be on explosive leg extension rather than arm strength.
*   **Night Shift Schedule (Wed–Sun):** The text emphasizes that a strict daily routine is vital; your irregular schedule conflicts with optimal recovery. You must prioritize sleep hygiene and mental decompression to mitigate the injury risk associated with fatigue.
*   **Transition Phase:** As an intermediate athlete returning after a gap, the "muscle memory" of heavy pulling will return quickly, but you require a gradual ramp-up (not jumping straight to max) to rebuild confidence without aggravating old injuries.
*   **Hypertrophy Block Goal:** The emphasis on heavy pulls and squats aligns perfectly with your 6-week hypertrophy block; this is the necessary foundation to support future technique/strength work.
