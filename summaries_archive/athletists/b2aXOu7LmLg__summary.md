## Key Points
*   **Intensity Management:** For intermediate athletes transitioning to Olympic lifting, prioritize technique over load; avoid training above 70% intensity until movement patterns are solidified. Heavy loading without mobility or stability significantly increases injury risk, particularly for those with chronic back pain.
*   **Volume vs. Intensity:** Adopt a "high volume, low intensity" approach during the hypertrophy/technique block. Focus on drilling movements with light loads to ingrain motor patterns before increasing spinal compression.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Utilize self-recording (video analysis) as a primary feedback mechanism, especially for night-shift workers with limited access to live coaching. Review every session to ensure bar path and posture remain correct before adding weight.
*   **Mobility Prerequisite:** Do not attempt overhead loading or heavy squats until shoulder mobility is sufficient; forcing the position without adequate range of motion leads to acute injury.

## Technique Cues
*   **Snatch/Clean Pulls:** Maintain a neutral spine ("keep back straight") throughout the pull. Avoid excessive lumbar arching; focus on keeping the bar close to the body and stabilizing the torso rather than pulling with the arms.
*   **Jerk Mechanics (Push Jerk Focus):** Since the athlete uses only a push jerk, prioritize the leg drive and dip mechanics over foot placement variations (split). Cue "push knees out" and "drive hips back" to generate vertical force. Ensure the bar does not drift forward during the transition.
*   **Overhead Stability:** For the OHS limiter, focus on active shoulder engagement and thoracic extension rather than forcing the weight overhead with a compromised posture.

## Programming Principles
*   **Phased Approach:** Structure training in blocks: begin with a 6-week hypertrophy/technique phase focusing on light weights (under 70% 1RM) to build tissue tolerance and movement efficiency, followed by a technique/strength block.
*   **Accessory Selection:** Incorporate plyometrics (jumps with varied foot placements) to develop explosive leg drive without the fatigue of full lifts. Use snatch pulls/high pulls as speed drills rather than strength builders.
*   **Load Progression:** Increase load only after consistent technical execution is verified via video review. Do not progress if back pain or mobility restrictions impede form.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Back Pain Management:** The "light weight, high volume" philosophy directly mitigates chronic back pain by avoiding high spinal compression loads typical of heavy singles/doubles. This aligns with the goal of a hypertrophy block to strengthen supporting musculature before heavy loading.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** The emphasis on self-recording and asynchronous coaching allows for effective training despite irregular sleep schedules (Wed–Sun shifts). It removes the dependency on real-time supervision, which is difficult during night hours.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** The focus on mastering the dip and drive mechanics supports a push-jerk-only style by ensuring the athlete generates force from the legs rather than relying on upper body strength to stabilize an unstable overhead position.
*   **OHS Limiter:** The explicit warning against heavy loading without shoulder mobility directly addresses the 50 kg OHS limitation, suggesting that mobility work must precede further load increases in the snatch.
