# Ты знаешь, когда можешь поднять! Тяжелая атлетика

## Key Points
*   **Block Pulls for Mechanics:** Lifting from blocks (elevated platforms) forces a "stretched back" position and prevents rounding, but requires strict hip engagement to avoid the "butt rising" error where hips shoot up before the second pull.
*   **Overhead Stability & Lockout:** For push-jerk-only athletes, heavy overhead presses with static holds from high positions (near eye level) build maximal lockout strength and stability without split mechanics.
*   **Load Management:** Prioritize sub-maximal loads when mental fatigue or physical "bad feelings" occur; forcing PRs during low-energy states leads to injury.
*   **Footwear & Base:** Soft running shoes hinder stability; hard-soled lifting shoes or a flat, firm base are essential for force transfer and reducing compensatory strain on the lower back.

## Technique Cues
*   **Starting Position (Blocks):** Maintain a straight spine from the elevated start height; initiate the pull with leg drive, not arms. Watch for early hip rise ("butt rising") which kills second-pull power.
*   **Bar Path:** Ensure the bar stays close to the body during the transition; if it drifts away, the lift becomes inefficient ("like driving nails").
*   **Overhead Lockout (Push Jerk):** Lift the head and keep eyes forward/up to maintain spinal alignment; do not look down. Keep the torso rigid ("don't fall back") with an immediate, hard lockout.
*   **Mental State:** Distinguish between psychological fear and lost movement patterns. If angles feel "off" or confidence is low, reset rather than forcing heavy attempts.

## Programming Principles
*   **Block Variations:** Use cleans/snatches from blocks to overload legs and force proper hip extension mechanics without full fatigue. Adjust block height based on flexibility; too high may reduce leg drive, too low risks spinal rounding for those with limited mobility.
*   **Overhead Strength:** Incorporate heavy overhead presses with static holds (10 reps or fewer) at varying heights (high position to lockout) to address the 50 kg OHS limiter. Focus on quality and stability over metabolic conditioning.
*   **Fatigue Management:** Schedule heavy block sessions carefully around night shifts; avoid high-intensity work immediately following shifts when recovery is compromised.
*   **Exercise Selection:** Prioritize lifts from blocks, pulls, and static overhead holds over standard squats if leg volume is already high from pulling variations.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** Block lifts can prevent lumbar rounding (a common pain trigger) but require perfect spinal neutrality from a lower start height; revert to floor pulls or higher blocks if back pain flares during the initial pull phase. Overhead cues (lifting head, rigid torso) help reduce excessive lumbar extension.
*   **Push Jerk Constraint:** The heavy overhead hold drills directly target the push-jerk limitation by building lockout strength and stability without requiring split mechanics.
*   **Night Shift & Recovery:** Acknowledge that work interference affects recovery; prioritize sleep quality and sub-maximal technique work on low-energy days rather than chasing volume or PRs.
*   **OHS Limiter (50 kg):** The static overhead holds and high-position presses provide the specific time-under-tension needed to strengthen the shoulder girdle and triceps for the OHS bottleneck.
*   **Equipment Note:** Ensure access to a "totem" setup or stable blocks to replicate the varying heights discussed; standard bumper plates are optimal for block height, but improvised platforms must be safe and level.
