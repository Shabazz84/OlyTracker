# Rhythm of Movement in Weightlifting. Tim Berestov

## Key Points

- **Static hold training** builds connective tissue resilience and competitive stability; static jerk holds (1 min @ 80% best jerk) preferred over pure dynamic reps
- **Snatch foot positioning**: Maintain full foot contact until late extension phase; premature toe rise loses support base and disrupts mid-pull positioning
- **Pull sequencing critical**: Full foot drive → complete leg extension → *then* rise onto toes (not simultaneous); timing errors cascade through receiving positions

## Technique Cues

**Snatch (No Foot Spread Drill)**
- Keep feet planted through full leg extension; delay foot rise to final phase
- Common error: premature toe rise at mid-thigh pull loses stability and positioning
- Sequencing fix: Drive through full foot → extend completely → rise onto toes
- Benefit: Corrects timing across all phases; improves front rack position carryover

**Jerk**
- Static split position hold: 1 min @ 80% best jerk (post-catch)
- Builds split stance stability under load

## Programming Principles

- Emphasize **static/isometric work** over high-rep dynamics for stability development
- Static holds address competitive failure modes (stability > strength deficits)
- Use challenge protocols (e.g., timed holds @ submaximal %) to expose and fix sequencing errors

## Athlete Relevance

**High priority:**
- **OHS limiter (50 kg)**: Snatch foot-spread drill directly improves pull sequencing → front rack position → OHS carryover
- **Chronic back pain**: Full-foot stability phase may reduce compensatory lumbar arching vs. premature toe-rise mechanics
- **Push jerk focus**: Static jerk hold protocol (1 min @ 80%) is directly applicable and coach-endorsed for stability work
- **Split jerk deficit**: Static holds in split stance build connective tissue resilience needed for future split jerk training

**Gap**: No guidance on shift work recovery or periodization strategy integration.
