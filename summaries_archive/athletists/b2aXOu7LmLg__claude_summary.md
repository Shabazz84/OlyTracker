## Key Points

- **3 sessions/week structure** with snatch and clean/jerk alternated across sessions (avoid consecutive snatch + jerk days due to CNS fatigue)
- **Final exercise protocol:** light, slow technical work after heavy main lifts to reinforce technique on fatigued organism and "shake out" fatigue
- **Critical snatch pull fault:** premature hip/back rounding after knees pass causes forward bar drift (triangle path) and pubis contact
- **Fix:** maintain back angle through mid-thigh, extend legs only, let bar settle naturally into power position before explosive finish

## Technique Cues

**Snatch Pull — Hip/Back Positioning:**
- After knees pass horizontal, **"терпеть" (hold/be patient)** at mid-thigh—resist collapsing hips/rounding back
- Cue: **"накрыв" (maintain posture/cover the bar)**—keep chest up, let gravity bring bar into power position
- Common fault: Early back collapse → premature knee re-bend → bar strikes pubis
- Error pattern: Pulling back instead of pulling through creates forward bar path deviation

## Programming Principles

- Alternate snatch and clean/jerk days (consecutive pairing creates excessive CNS/lower-body fatigue)
- Cap main work intensity; reserve final exercise for technical reinforcement
- Use light loads (≤70% max) for self-taught athletes prioritizing technique over load
- Place technical finishers last when fatigue naturally reduces collapsing tendency

## Athlete Relevance

**Chronic back pain + OHS limiter (50 kg):** Back angle collapse in snatch pull directly destabilizes overhead position and strains lumbar spine. Postural cue fix is foundational before intensity increases.

**Night shift fatigue:** 3×/week structure with CNS-respecting session alternation and light technical finishers fits disrupted recovery window.

**Hypertrophy block context:** This snatch pull pattern correction prevents compensation injuries during higher-volume phases.
