# MERGED SUMMARY: Weightlifting Coaching Insights

## Key Points

- **Train weak points, not around them.** Address OHS (50 kg) and split jerk deficits directly through targeted accessory work rather than avoidance.
- **Bar path precision matters significantly.** Small positional shifts create large movement deviations; use varied starting heights (blocks/rack) to develop bar trajectory feel and sensitivity.
- **Snatch/pull difficulty is leg-strength dependent.** Pulling from above knee/hang is easier than full floor pull for athletes with weaker legs; split snatch work transfers better than classical snatch when leg drive is limiting.
- **Jerk readiness correlates with front squat strength.** Expect jerk ~front squat max; jerk from rack isolates jerk strength without clean fatigue.
- **Manage cumulative joint stress.** Avoid excessive holding/fixation in bottom positions under load; drop early reps, fix only final rep in a set—especially relevant for chronic back pain.
- **Exercise sequencing affects fatigue distribution.** Position jerk later in session (fatigued state) to mimic competition; prioritize heavy compound work before ancillary exercises.
- **Weight cuts must be gradual.** Avoid rapid cuts (3–5 kg/month safe; rapid cuts invite injury). Preserve strength during transitions.
- **Individuality in recovery varies.** Monitor personal response to recovery modalities (sauna, swimming, active recovery); no universal protocol.

## Technique Cues

**Clean/Front Squat:**
- Keep elbows **high and rotated forward** to maintain upright torso and reduce back flexion (critical for chronic back pain).
- Wider grip reduces starting position depth but lengthens bar path; narrower grip risks trunk flexion—prioritize mobility-driven grip width choice.
- Catch in proper "rebound" position with strong leg drive; elbows alone cannot support weak legs.

**Snatch/Pulls:**
- **Bar path precision:** Vertical exit from pull (not arc); bar should contact at hip, not swung forward.
- Pull from **above knee (reduced range)** easier than floor pull for leg-limited athletes.
- Mark target landing zones (chalk lines) for feedback on bar trajectory.

**Jerk:**
- Begin with **power jerk from rack** (no split training yet). Expected power jerk ≈ 65–75 kg based on 80 kg clean.
- Stable dip position + controlled foot placement critical; catch with control, not passive absorption.
- Position **late in session** (fatigued) to train competition conditions; use early sessions for technique/strength focus only if planned.

**Overhead Squat (OHS limiter):**
- Address shoulder/thoracic mobility and bar trajectory precision before strength increases.
- Apply block variation (low/medium/high) to develop stability and bar feel.
- Perform post-session accessory work to avoid fatigue interference with main lifts.

---

## Programming Principles

- **Base intensities on competition lifts** (clean, jerk, snatch), not estimated maxes.
- **Substitute, don't stop.** Modify movements during pain/injury (e.g., rack pulls instead of snatch pulls, front squat instead of full clean).
- **Fatigue-driven training:** Jerk placement later in session mimics competition structure; some sessions may prioritize jerk exclusively on fresh CNS for technique work.
- **Hypertrophy block sequencing:** Higher volume, later jerk placement, reduced running volume (low-intensity conditioning preferred over aerobic work >10 km).
- **Training intensity vs. competition mindset:** Train hard to make competition feel easier; intentional fatigue management in specific sessions.
- **Weak point priority:** Dedicate extra time/sessions to OHS and split jerk before strength block begins.
