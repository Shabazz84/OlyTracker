## Key Points
*   **Training Block Strategy:** Execute a 6-week hypertrophy block focused on building work capacity (20–30 working sets per session) before transitioning to higher-intensity technique/strength phases.
*   **Schedule Constraints:** Due to night shifts (Wed–Sun), training must occur Mon/Tue or Thu/Fri, ensuring a minimum 48-hour buffer between the final training session and the start of the next shift to manage cumulative fatigue.
*   **Pain Management:** Do not train through compromised technique; chronic back pain requires avoiding heavy axial loading immediately post-shift. Prioritize unilateral leg work (lunges/split squats) and core stability during the hypertrophy phase to support the spine before reintroducing high-volume bilateral lifting.
*   **Nutrition & Recovery:** As a night shift worker, prioritize small, frequent meals over large post-workout loads to manage digestive sensitivity. Aim for a slight caloric surplus (+300 kcal) using easily digestible macros to support hypertrophy without bloating.

## Technique Cues
*   **Overhead Limiters:** Address the 50 kg OHS limitation by prioritizing shoulder mobility drills before overhead work. Do not force range of motion; if flexibility is restricted, avoid heavy overhead squats or split jerks to prevent back exacerbation.
*   **Jerk Mechanics:** Adhere strictly to the **Push Jerk** (no split jerk) due to current limitations. Focus on strict pressing mechanics and vertical drive rather than footwork complexity until mobility improves.
*   **Posterior Chain:** Substitute traditional heavy Deadlifts with **Trap Bar Deadlifts** or **Rack Pulls** if spinal shear force aggravates chronic back pain, maintaining posterior chain strength while reducing lumbar load.
*   **Form Priority:** Maintain strict technique over ego lifting; "home training" principles apply equally to the gym setting—correct mechanics are non-negotiable for injury prevention.

## Programming Principles
*   **Volume vs. Intensity Balance:** During the hypertrophy block, prioritize moderate volume (20–30 sets) rather than low-volume powerlifting styles (<15 sets). Cycle into a strength phase later where intensity increases and volume decreases slightly.
*   **Frequency Management:** Utilize a 2-day or 3-day split to accommodate recovery needs. If training only twice weekly, extend sessions to 1.5–2 hours; if three times, keep sessions under 1 hour.
*   **Load Selection:** Avoid heavy axial loading (heavy back squats/deadlifts) on days immediately following high-stress work shifts. Monitor the Back Squat (118 kg) closely but substitute or reduce load if pain signals increase.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Profile Fit:** The program is tailored for an intermediate lifter (102.5 kg) transitioning to Olympic lifting, specifically addressing the gap between current strength (Clean 80kg/Jerk 65kg push) and technical requirements.
*   **Shift Work Adaptation:** The schedule explicitly respects the Wed–Sun night shift constraint, preventing training fatigue from spilling into work performance or sleep quality.
*   **Injury Mitigation:** The plan directly addresses chronic back pain by shifting focus to unilateral stability and mobility during the hypertrophy block, reducing the risk of injury while building the necessary tissue capacity for heavier Olympic lifts later.
