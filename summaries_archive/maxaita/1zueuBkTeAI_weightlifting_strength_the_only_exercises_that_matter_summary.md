# Weightlifting Strength: The ONLY Exercises That Matter
**Channel:** Max Aita | **Date:** 2025-12-16 | **Language:** en

## Key Points
- Not a tall-lifter video — a **methodology** for selecting exercises: a minimum exercise list per lift, then a fault-driven process for choosing accessories that actually fix your technical problems.
- Core philosophy: **"1 + 1 ≠ 2"** — don't map one fault to one magic exercise. Build a *pool* of similar exercises that probabilistically address the root causes, then prioritize by overlap and rotate through them.
- **Variability is the key to skill acquisition** (free-throw analogy: only ever shooting from one spot makes you bad in a game). Practice many similar-but-slightly-different variations to groove the correction under varied conditions.

## The Minimum Exercise List (per lift group)
- **Snatch:** classic snatch + power snatch (highest correlation/transfer, lets you train lighter without losing specificity) + an overhead-strength lift (overhead squat or snatch balance — overload the overhead position).
- **Clean:** classic clean + power clean + front squat (leg strength is the prerequisite to improving the clean; if you do one squat, front squat).
- **Jerk:** power jerk (simpler, trains drive capacity/power) + push press (builds overhead strength, drive power, and the lockout that jerks alone don't).

## Fault-Driven Exercise Selection (4-step method)
1. **Identify the fault** (example used: jumping forward in the snatch).
2. **Break down root causes** — bar path, loss of position, physical weakness (e.g. back too weak to hold over the bar, or legs too weak relative to back), balance (too far forward/back). Then map **which phase/position** it affects: first pull (ground→knee), at the knee, finish/turnover.
3. **Create a priority list** — generate a broad pool (could be ~20 movements) across two buckets:
   - *Phase-specific:* tempo snatch (first-pull speed if rushing/yanking), complexes (pull-to-knee + snatch), pause variations (at / above / below knee — same skill from different angles).
   - *Bar-path / balance / position:* no-feet snatch (literally can't jump forward), no-contact (stops over-kicking the hip and swinging the bar out), pause lifts (fix hip height at the knee).
   - Find which movements **appear repeatedly** across categories → move those to the top. End with an ordered ~10–15 movement list; train from the top of it.
4. **Progression & sequence** (across weeks/mesocycles):
   - **Target the issue first** — pick the most intense corrective movements as the primary work.
   - **Move toward specificity** — progress from corrective drills toward the classic lift across the cycle (you can't only do drills and expect to be good at the full lift).
   - **Move complex → simple** — as bar weight rises, the exercise must replicate the real movement; shed heavy-constraint drills.
   - **Variability** — rotate close variations (weekly: 1–2 + classic lift; or mesocycle: pause-below one week, pause-above next, pause-at next) for varied exposure.
