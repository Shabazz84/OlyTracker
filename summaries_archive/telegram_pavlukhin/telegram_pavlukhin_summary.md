## Key Points
*   **Athlete Profile & Goals:** An intermediate lifter (102.5 kg) transitioning from general strength to Olympic weightlifting. The immediate objective is a 6-week hypertrophy block focused on accessory volume and muscle mass, followed by a technique/strength phase.
*   **Performance Disparity:** A significant gap exists between raw strength (Back Squat 118 kg, Clean Deadlift potential >160 kg) and Olympic performance (Clean 80 kg, Jerk 65 kg). The primary technical limiter is the Overhead Squat (OHS) at 50 kg, driven by shoulder mobility and core stability.
*   **Health Constraints:** Chronic back pain and shoulder/ligament sensitivity are critical constraints. Heavy conventional deadlifts and high-volume axial loading are contraindicated due to spinal shear forces. Night shift work (Wed–Sun) creates sleep deficits that directly correlate to perceived weight heaviness, slower pull speeds, and recovery failure.
*   **Jerk Strategy:** Currently restricted to Push Jerks; the program integrates specific drills for Classic Split Jerk mechanics to facilitate a future transition while maintaining current stability.

## Technique Cues
*   **Snatch Mechanics:** Prioritize speed in pulls ("pulling with snatch grip") and an active "snatch in the hole" position. Maintain rigid arms (cables, not ropes) to prevent early bending; keep elbows high and wrists extended to stop bar drift, which strains ligaments.
*   **Jerk Execution:** For Push Jerks, focus on vertical leg drive and full lockout without foot separation. Avoid "pushing forward," which indicates weak legs or poor stability. Integrate Classic Split Jerk drills (1/1 @ 95kg) to build receiving position stability for future transition.
*   **Squat & Bar Path:** Monitor for forward lean ("завалился вперед") and bar projection over toes; ensure heels remain planted to maintain center of gravity. If feet fold inward at high intensity, utilize weightlifting shoes for fixation rather than forcing structural symmetry through stretching.
*   **Pulling Dynamics:** Accelerate the pull through the belt line; if the bar feels light at the knee, turnover speed improves naturally. Initiate lifts with legs/glutes while keeping shoulders in front of the bar to prevent rounding or "flying out."

## Programming Principles
*   **Frequency & Split:** 3–4 sessions per week (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri/Sat). Avoid daily heavy lifting models; adopt a "short and frequent" approach (30–60 min) to manage CNS fatigue. Structure follows an "Olympic Lifts + Accessory" model.
*   **Block Periodization:**
    *   **Hypertrophy Block (Weeks 1–6):** Moderate volume for accessories using machines/isolation (e.g., leg press, cable rows, bench) to build mass without axial spinal loading. Use controlled tempo (30–40s time under tension).
    *   **Technique/Strength Phase:** Low volume, high intensity main lifts (1–2 reps at top sets) with progressive loading. Use "wave loading" logic: if a heavy single fails, drop immediately to lighter weights for volume rather than forcing the max.
*   **Intensity & Progression:** Do not use linear progression for main lifts; adjust based on daily bar speed and subjective tension ("if easy, go heavier"). Prioritize posterior chain stability (hyperextensions, rows) over axial loading where possible.
*   **Cardio & Conditioning:** Cycling is strictly warm-up/cool-down. Separate heavy lifting and endurance work to prevent leg fatigue; avoid long cardio sessions immediately prior to lifting.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Back Pain Management:** The program explicitly excludes conventional deadlifts and high-volume axial loading. Hypertrophy work must utilize machines/cables (e.g., leg extensions, cable rows) to build mass without aggravating the spine. Coach monitors "stomach/back" discomfort during front squats for real-time adjustments.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** Training is flexible around the Wed–Sun shift schedule. Heavy lifting days are scheduled only during high-sleep windows; low-sleep days are reserved for technical drills or rest, acknowledging that fatigue makes weights feel heavier and increases injury risk.
*   **Shoulder/Elbow Health:** Due to ligament pain from snatch mechanics, heavy snatching volume is reduced until stability improves. Focus shifts to "comfortable angles" and controlled pulling variations.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** Avoids elite-level daily max attempts ("Bulgarian style") which are dangerous for this profile. The structured block periodization (Hypertrophy → Technique) addresses the OHS limiter via upper body mass before returning to heavy Olympic lifting.
