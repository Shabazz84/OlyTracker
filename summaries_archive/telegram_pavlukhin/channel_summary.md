# Coaching Philosophy: Pavlukhin Methodology
**Target Profile:** Intermediate Strength Athlete transitioning to Olympic Weightlifting.

## 1. Core Training Philosophy
*   **Safety-First Hypertrophy:** Mass must be built without compromising spinal integrity. We prioritize machine and isolation work for hypertrophy to eliminate axial loading on a chronically injured back, reserving heavy compound lifts only when pain-free.
*   **Speed Over Force:** The primary driver of Olympic success is bar speed, not raw force application. Lifts are initiated with explosive intent ("pulling with snatch grip") rather than grinding through resistance. If the pull feels slow or heavy, the load is too high for the current technical state.
*   **Structural Stability Before Mobility:** We do not chase extreme flexibility at the expense of joint stability. If structural symmetry fails (e.g., feet folding), we utilize equipment (weightlifting shoes) to fix the base rather than forcing painful stretching that compromises ligament health.
*   **Fatigue-Aware Periodization:** Training volume and intensity are dictated by sleep quality and CNS recovery, not a rigid calendar. Night shift work is treated as a physiological variable; heavy days occur only during high-sleep windows.

## 2. Programming Approach
**Structure & Frequency:**
*   **Model:** "Olympic Lifts + Accessory" split.
*   **Frequency:** 3–4 sessions per week (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri/Sat). Sessions are kept short and frequent (30–60 mins) to manage CNS fatigue.
*   **Cardio:** Strictly warm-up/cool-down only. No endurance work immediately preceding lifting to prevent leg fatigue.

**Periodization Strategy:**
*   **Phase 1: Hypertrophy Block (Weeks 1–6):** Focus on accessory volume and muscle mass using machines/isolation (leg press, cable rows, bench). Tempo is controlled (30–40s time under tension) to build tissue without spinal shear. Heavy conventional deadlifts are contraindicated.
*   **Phase 2: Technique/Strength:** Low volume, high intensity main lifts (1–2 reps at top sets).
    *   **Wave Loading Logic:** If a heavy single fails, immediately drop the weight for volume rather than forcing a max attempt.
    *   **Progression:** Non-linear. Adjust load daily based on bar speed and subjective tension ("if easy, go heavier").

**Intensity Management:**
*   Avoid "Bulgarian" style daily max attempts.
*   Prioritize posterior chain stability (hyperextensions, rows) over axial loading where possible.

## 3. Key Technique Cues & Exercise Preferences
**Snatch Mechanics:**
*   **Arms as Cables:** Maintain rigid arms; do not bend elbows early. Keep elbows high and wrists extended to prevent bar drift, which strains ligaments.
*   **Active Hole:** Prioritize an active "snatch in the hole" position.
*   **Pull Dynamics:** Accelerate through the belt line. If the bar feels light at the knee, turnover speed will improve naturally. Initiate with legs/glutes while keeping shoulders *in front* of the bar to prevent rounding.

**Jerk Execution (Push & Split):**
*   **Push Jerk Focus:** Vertical leg drive and full lockout without foot separation. Avoid "pushing forward" (indicates weak legs/poor stability).
*   **Split Transition:** Integrate Classic Split Jerk drills (e.g., 1/1 @ 95kg) to build receiving position stability for future transition, even while currently restricted to Push Jerks.

**Squat & Bar Path:**
*   **Center of Gravity:** Monitor for forward lean ("завалился вперед") and bar projection over toes. Heels must remain planted.
*   **Foot Fixation:** If feet fold inward at high intensity, use weightlifting shoes for fixation rather than forcing structural symmetry through stretching.

## 4. Recovery and Mobility Approach
**Sleep & Shift Work Adaptation:**
*   Night shift workers (Wed–Sun) face inherent sleep deficits. Training is flexible: Heavy lifting days are scheduled *only* during high-sleep windows. Low-sleep days are reserved for technical drills or rest. Fatigue makes weights feel heavier and increases injury risk; we do not train through sleep deprivation.

**Back Pain Management:**
*   **Contraindications:** No conventional deadlifts, no high-volume axial loading (heavy back squats).
*   **Monitoring:** Coach monitors "stomach/back" discomfort during front squats in real-time for immediate adjustments.
*   **Shoulder/Elbow Health:** Heavy snatch volume is reduced until stability improves. Focus on "comfortable angles" and controlled pulling variations to prevent ligament flare-ups.

**Mobility Strategy:**
*   Mobility work supports the lift but does not replace strength. We address OHS limitations (shoulder mobility/core stability) through specific accessory work rather than aggressive stretching that compromises joint integrity.

## 5. Application to Athlete Profile (102.5 kg, Intermediate)
**The Gap:** The athlete possesses significant raw strength (Squat 118kg, Clean Deadlift >160kg) but lacks Olympic transfer (Clean 80kg, Jerk 65kg). The primary limiter is the Overhead Squat (OHS) at 50kg.

**Immediate Action Plan:**
1.  **Hypertrophy Block (Weeks 1–6):** We will not attempt to fix the OHS with heavy overhead squats immediately, as this aggravates back pain and shoulder ligaments. Instead, we will build upper body mass and core stability via machines/cables. This creates a stronger "shelf" for the bar without spinal shear.
2.  **Jerk Transition:** While currently using Push Jerks (65kg), we will integrate Classic Split Jerk drills to prepare for future transition, ensuring the receiving position is stable before increasing load.
3.  **Back Pain Protocol:** All heavy pulling and squatting variations are modified to eliminate axial loading. We will use leg press and cable rows to build the posterior chain required for a strong pull without aggravating the spine.
4.  **Shift Work Integration:** Training sessions will be scheduled around the Wed–Sun night shift. If sleep is compromised, the session defaults to technical drills or rest. We acknowledge that fatigue increases perceived weight heaviness; we do not push through it.
