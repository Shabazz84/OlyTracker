# Weightlifting Coaching Analysis for Intermediate Athlete

## Key Points

- **Light training (70% intensity)** calculated from current muscular readiness, not historical maxes; adjust for tendon pain or incomplete recovery
- **Single-rep work** preferred over high-rep sets to minimize fatigue and inflammation—critical for chronic back pain management
- Hard:easy training ratios: 2:1 (intermediate), escalating to 3:1 (advanced); this athlete should use **1:1 or 2:1 given night shift + back pain**
- **Technique improves above 85%**; 70% enables recovery and controlled error correction only
- Light weeks indicated post-overtraining, post-illness, post-injury, and between hypertrophy blocks
- **Core/abdominal work non-negotiable**—weak abs = spinal instability, rib cage collapse risk, increased belt dependency

---

## Technique Cues

**Snatch/Pull:**
- Full-foot pressure throughout; avoid premature toe rise
- Vertical shin at start; shoulder coverage at top of pull
- Smooth, even pull-off from platform
- On 70%: practice controlled pull-off and explosive knee drive *separately* (lighter loads don't demand simultaneous execution)
- Bar path check: keep bar close to body; film to identify forward jump

**Snatch Pull (Ryvkovaya Tyga):**
- **Not a deadlift variant**—performed from snatch stance, not conventional deadlift position
- Goal: speed/acceleration, not max strength
- Moderate weight with explosive intent; equal pull-off + full-foot stance throughout
- Avoid heavy snatch pulls (cause lower back fatigue, reduce speed)

**Core/Pressing:**
- Mandatory abdominal bracing to maintain intra-abdominal pressure under load
- Integrate leg raises and core-specific work

---

## Programming Principles

**Exercise Selection:**
- Prioritize **front squat over heavy snatch/clean pulls** (safer, less spinal fatigue for back-pain athletes)
- Front squat load ≤10% above snatch load
- Avoid heavy conventional deadlifts (unsafe spinal loading under Olympic demands)
- Minimize accessories; progression removes snatch pulls later

**Recovery Assessment:**
- Monitor squat ascent speed via video: 70% should take ~1 sec; >2–3 sec indicates overreaching
- Typical recovery phase: 1–2 months to normalize inflammation
- Sleep quality and adequate nutrition critical (undereating multiplies perceived fatigue)

---

## Athlete Relevance

| Factor | Application |
|--------|-------------|
| **OHS 50 kg (snatch limiter)** | 70% light work ingrain snatch pull speed and front squat positioning before heavy blocks; technique improvements available even at light intensity |
| **Chronic back pain** | **Avoid heavy snatch/conventional pulls**; emphasize front squat + mandatory core work; use light weeks aggressively for inflammation management; single-rep work prevents excessive muscle damage |
| **Night shift Wed–Sun** | Sleep disruption reduces recovery tolerance; adopt **1:1 hard:easy ratio minimum** (vs. standard 2:1 intermediate); light weeks more critical |
| **Hypertrophy block planned** | Light weeks (70%) serve as recovery bridges between hypertrophy blocks; prevents back pain exacerbation |
| **Push jerk only** | No split jerk mechanics yet; focus current block on front squat strength and core stability as foundation before split jerk progression |

**Primary recommendation:** Rotate aggressive light weeks (70% singles) given back pain + night shift. Use light sessions for snatch pull speed development and front squat positioning refinement before transitioning to 6-week hypertrophy block.
