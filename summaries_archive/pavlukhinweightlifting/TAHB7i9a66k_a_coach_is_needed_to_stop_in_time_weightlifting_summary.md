# A coach is needed to stop in time. Weightlifting

## Key Points
*   **Recovery Over Percentages:** Performance is strictly tied to sleep quality; training intensity must be adjusted based on actual rest rather than a calendar schedule or percentage targets. For night shift workers, missing sleep necessitates reduced volume or rest days to avoid "sticky" movements and injury.
*   **Ballistic Mechanics:** Olympic lifts are "metathrow" (ballistic) actions where the bar is thrown upward by the legs/core; arms only engage at the top for fixation. The movement must be vertical, not a drag or pull with the back.
*   **Shoulder Lag:** The primary limiter for this athlete (OHS 50kg) indicates weak deltoids, which compromises the receiving position in both snatch and jerk. Faster shoulder development is required to fix the "dip" and drive mechanics.
*   **Micro-Loading:** Progression should utilize small increments (0.5–1 kg plates) rather than large jumps to fine-tune strength and technique without overwhelming the nervous system or joints.

## Technique Cues
*   **Vertical Bar Path:** The bar must travel strictly vertically upward. If it drifts backward, it creates a "dug" path that prevents heavy loading; if it goes up, it engages the lats for stability.
*   **Leg Drive vs. Back Pull:** Avoid "pulling with the back" or dragging the bar. The drive must originate from the legs (explosive extension). For an athlete with chronic back pain, this distinction is critical to prevent spinal loading during the pull phase.
*   **Elbow Rotation:** Focus on turning elbows faster (*быстрее провернуть локоть*) to secure the rack position immediately upon bar contact, rather than waiting for the bar to reach the top.
*   **Dip Mechanics (Jerk):** Execute a short, fast dip ("short squat"). Do not over-bend; the goal is an immediate vertical drive. For push jerks specifically, let the bar fly up while driving under it without hesitation or mental "stopping."
*   **Mental State:** Maintain "absolute calmness" (*хладнокровие*). Hesitation or ego-driven aggression leads to failure. If the athlete feels they are "dying under the bar," it indicates a timing issue, not just strength deficit.

## Programming Principles
*   **Sleep-Dependent Intensity:** Do not use rigid percentage-based programming. Training load must be dictated by sleep quality; if a night shift worker is fatigued, training should consist of low-volume "feel" reps or rest, rather than grinding sets.
*   **High Frequency/Low Volume (Fatigue Management):** When sleep is compromised, switch to high-frequency, low-volume sessions (e.g., testing feel with few reps) to maintain neural pathways without accumulating excessive fatigue.
*   **Combined Sessions:** Due to time constraints typical of night shift workers, do not separate Snatch, Clean & Jerk, and Squats into distinct days if time is limited; combine or "chop" movements to fit the schedule while managing total volume.
*   **Aerobic Base for Recovery:** Incorporate aerobic conditioning (specifically leg-focused) to improve recovery density between sets and accelerate inter-session recovery, allowing for higher training frequency without burnout.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** The text explicitly links poor performance and "sticky" movements to lack of sleep. For a Wed–Sun night shift worker, this validates the need to prioritize recovery windows over hitting specific weights on tired days.
*   **Chronic Back Pain Mitigation:** The cue to avoid "pulling with the back" is vital for an athlete with chronic pain. It reinforces that the drive must come from the legs to minimize spinal shear and compression during the pull phase of the Clean and Snatch.
*   **Addressing the OHS Limiter:** With a 50kg Overhead Squat, the athlete's shoulder strength is lagging significantly behind their lower body (118kg squat). The advice to prioritize "faster deltoid development" directly targets this specific bottleneck for both snatch and jerk stability.
*   **Intermediate Plateau Breaking:** The distinction between "ego lifting" and trusting the process ("animal feeling") addresses the intermediate lifter's tendency to force PRs before technique is solidified, which is a common cause of stagnation at 80kg Clean/65kg Jerk levels.
