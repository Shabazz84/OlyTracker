# Frequent vs. Infrequent Training. Weightlifting

## Key Points
*   **Training Frequency:** Prioritize high frequency (daily or near-daily) short sessions (~45 mins) over infrequent, long blocks to accumulate volume without systemic fatigue. For night shift workers, a 3x/week "every other day" model is also viable to manage irregular sleep schedules.
*   **Volume vs. Intensity:** Accumulate total heavy lifts by spreading them across the week (e.g., multiple sessions of 95kg) rather than single high-intensity days. Use linear progression ("steps") rather than rigid percentages; increase weight gradually while keeping rep schemes constant.
*   **Periodization Strategy:** Implement alternating "Heavy" and "Light" weeks. A "light" week is not a rest week but a recovery phase where the athlete lifts adapted weights, serving as a marker of progress. Avoid full hypertrophy blocks in favor of skill/strength adaptation.
*   **Recovery & Communication:** Strict adherence to 2–3 minute rest intervals between heavy sets for nervous system reset (no talking during rest). Daily communication regarding sleep and stress is mandatory; reduce load if not rested. Short sessions allow for faster physical recovery compared to grueling blocks.

## Technique Cues
*   **Chest & Posture:** "Hold chest up" (*Держим грудь*) throughout the lift to maintain spinal alignment. Focus on a straighter spine during the catch phase ("spine less bent").
*   **Leg Drive & Footwork:** Pull with legs stronger; drive through the "whole foot" into the floor. Avoid falling forward or rushing the pull.
*   **Speed & Fixation:** Execute movements explosively but with focus. Prioritize faster fixation in the bottom position (catch) to improve stability.
*   **Bar Path:** The bar must rotate easily; if it resists, mechanics are flawed. Ensure every repetition is "quality and beautiful" rather than just heavy.
*   **Stability:** Maintain a "hard/strict" hold (*Жёстко терпеть*) during the catch phase to absorb force effectively.

## Programming Principles
*   **Session Structure:** Limit sessions to 1–2 exercises (e.g., Snatch or Clean & Jerk) per day to minimize friction and time loss, ideal for night shift workers with limited energy windows.
*   **Exercise Selection:** Prioritize **Front Squats** over Back Squats for Olympic specificity due to better transfer to the clean/jerk posture and reduced spinal compression (critical for chronic back pain).
*   **Progression Model:** Use a "step" approach (+5kg or +10kg) rather than fixed percentages. This allows natural adaptation without the psychological stress of rigid targets.
*   **Hypertrophy vs. Skill:** While the athlete desires hypertrophy, the primary focus must remain on skill acquisition and neural drive. Accumulate volume through high-frequency practice rather than bodybuilding-style accessory work.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The emphasis on Front Squats and "short daily sessions" reduces spinal compression compared to heavy back squats or long, grueling blocks. Frequent low-dose loading is safer for managing chronic pain than weekly spikes in intensity.
*   **Night Shift (Wed–Sun):** The flexible frequency models (daily short sessions or 3x/week every other day) accommodate irregular sleep patterns better than rigid morning/evening block schedules. Training close to home/work minimizes friction.
*   **Push Jerk Constraint:** While the philosophy supports "Clean & Jerk in competition style," the current limitation of push-jerk only is acceptable for this transition phase. The focus remains on front-rack stability and leg drive, which transfers directly to the push jerk mechanics.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The "discipline over genetics" mindset addresses the psychological barrier of transitioning from powerlifting (high intensity) to Olympic lifting (skill frequency). This prevents burnout ("roof goes off") common in high-intensity blocks.
