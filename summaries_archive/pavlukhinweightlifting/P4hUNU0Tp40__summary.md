# Одноповторная Работа и Тяжелая Атлетика #тяжелаяатлетика

**1. Programming principles:**
*   **Intensity/Frequency:** Perform 80–90% of max lifts regularly, but avoid doing them in a single set. Instead, accumulate volume through multiple sets (e.g., 2–3 sets) to develop the creatine phosphate system without excessive lactate accumulation.
*   **Squatting Strategy:** Limit heavy back squats to one repetition per session rather than three consecutive heavy reps. Perform more *sets* of singles at this intensity; the total volume remains similar, but injury risk is significantly reduced compared to multiple heavy consecutive reps.

**2. Technique cues:**
*   None explicitly mentioned in this excerpt (focus is on load management and recovery).

**3. Exercise recommendations:**
*   **Snatch & Clean & Jerk:** Perform these daily or regularly as the primary stimulus, focusing on singles at 80–90% intensity.
*   **Back Squat:** Include heavy squats but strictly limit to single repetitions per set (1 rep max effort), increasing total sets rather than reps per set.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice:**
*   **Injury Prevention:** The primary recovery strategy mentioned is mechanical: reducing injury risk by avoiding consecutive heavy back squat repetitions ("less traumatic to squat once than three times in a row").

**5. Relevance to athlete profile:**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** This advice is highly relevant. Limiting the back squat to single reps with higher set volume reduces spinal compression fatigue and acute injury risk, which is crucial for an athlete with chronic back pain.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The focus on accumulating 80–90% intensity through multiple sets aligns well with building a strength base while managing fatigue during the transition to Olympic lifting.
*   *Note:* This text does not address the specific constraints of night shift work or the push-jerk-only limitation, but the squat modification is universally beneficial for this profile.
