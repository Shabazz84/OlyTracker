# Unified Weightlifting Coaching Summary

## Key Points

- **Recovery between max attempts:** 2–3 minutes required for hydrogen ion clearance; maintain HR >100 bpm via light aerobic activity (walking) rather than sitting/lying down
- **Inter-lift timing:** 15–30 min between snatch and clean/jerk mirrors competition; 10 min walking + 10–20 min glucose/electrolyte intake optimizes metabolic recovery
- **Heavy single protocol:** "Attack" method uses 90–95% competition weights (not 50 kg jumps) to maintain CNS engagement during peak phases
- **Competition simulation:** Friday platform sessions (separate bar/platform, pre-announced weights, 2-min rest between attempts) reduce adrenal stress on meet day through nervous system acclimation
- **Active recovery days:** Light technical work (e.g., 1–2 rep back squats) superior to complete rest for hormonal support
- **Technique errors stem from muscular weakness in stabilizers, not mental misunderstanding**

## Technique Cues

**Snatch/Clean Pull:**
- Knee angle: Avoid excessively acute position (weakens quads, causes forward bar drift)
- Knee tracking: Must track over toes; lateral deviation causes loss of verticality and missed lifts
- Foot position: Stay on full foot—avoid rolling to lateral edges

**Clean:**
- "Grip chest, don't collapse"—maintain rack position stability with relaxed upper arms/shoulders
- More dynamic standup from receiving position to preserve jerk energy
- Don't pull bar with torso off platform prematurely

**Jerk:**
- Keep bar close to body; avoid head extension forward (compensates for arm tension/mobility deficit)
- Lat and long triceps head must "cover" the bar—shoulders slightly ahead, bar pressed into body ("Don't release from yourself")
- Relaxed arms in dip/drive (they assist trajectory, not create tension)
- Shorter, more explosive drive preferred over deep pause
- If jerked forward early, chase bar with hip drive rather than abandoning lift
- Receiving: Chest up, back leg bent, stay vertical under bar

**General Cues:**
- "Master the bar" (psychologically own the lift)
- "Drive straight, calmly" (avoid panic jerking)
- Vertical drive before catching

## Programming Principles

- **Periodization context:** Peak training → deload/taper toward competition
- **Rest protocol during training:** Avoid sitting/lying between sets—maintain circulation via low-intensity movement
- **Active recovery emphasis:** Light technical work on recovery days superior to complete rest
- **Volume tolerance:** 7–8 sets in snatch phase appropriate for intermediate athletes
- **Between-set nutrition:** Glucose for CNS/brain fuel; electrolytes for nerve conduction and H+ ion clearance
- **Mental management:** Coach balances push vs. back-off based on CNS fatigue (not just muscular fatigue); missed lifts are learning events for nervous system

## Athlete Relevance

| Factor | Application |
|--------|-------------|
| **Chronic back pain** | Vertical bar path and lat engagement reduce lumbar shear; avoid forward lean in snatch/clean pull. Diagnosis: forward bar drift from quad weakness and inadequate lat positioning. |
| **OHS limiter (50 kg)** | Tendon elasticity deficit in quads (insufficient elastic recoil during standup) and lat/upper back weakness constrain receiving position stability. Address positioning and quad strength before volume increase. |
| **Push jerk only (65 kg)** | Arm relaxation + mobility constraints identified as ceiling limiters (~40 kg from chest observed). Forced arm turnover and head extension indicate shoulder/thoracic mobility deficit, not strength deficit. Prioritize arm relaxation cues and elastic quad work before heavy jerk loading. |
| **Night shift (Wed–Sun)** | Active recovery protocol and light technical work support hormonal recovery despite sleep disruption. Glucose/electrolyte intake during heavy sessions essential. |
| **6-week hypertrophy block goal** | Prioritize back squat depth, quad, and trap development to address receiving position weakness. Follow with technique/strength block using "attack" method (90–95% singles) and competition simulation sessions. |
| **Intermediate status** | Volume tolerance (7–8 sets) appropriate; mental coaching on missed lifts valuable for confidence and CNS adaptation. |

**Key Takeaway:** Technique errors (forward bar drift, receiving position collapse, jerk height ceiling) stem from quad/lat/upper back weakness and mobility constraints—not mental misunderstanding. Hypertrophy block should emphasize back squat and upper back development before competition phase. Arm relaxation and elastic tendon work must precede heavy jerk loading.
