# Weightlifting Analysis Summary

## Key Points

- **Current focus**: Leg strength & posterior chain development (back squat, Romanian deadlifts, glute bridges) as foundation before technique refinement
- **Periodization strategy**: 6-week hypertrophy block (accessory-heavy) followed by technique/strength phase; deliberately conservative progression to avoid overload
- **Primary technical limiters**: 
  - Snatch: forward jump, weight shifting to toes, lacks vertical bar path and proper elbow extension
  - OHS: insufficient shoulder stability/overhead mobility (50 kg is limiting factor)
- **Load progression**: Volume > intensity approach; avoid training to exhaustion/nausea; tendons need adaptation time during technique learning
- **Recovery emphasis**: Conservative intensity suits night shift fatigue; session termination when CNS state low; accessory work on lower-intensity days

---

## Technique Cues

**Snatch:**
- Bar travels close to body; no forward bounce/jump
- Maintain vertical jump pattern, not forward
- Vertical elbow extension ("проворот локтей вертикальных")
- Weight distribution: mid-foot/full foot contact; avoid shifting to toes
- Lighter weights (50 kg = 95% intensity) reveal technical errors clearly

**Back Squat:**
- Full foot contact throughout descent
- Controlled, vertical descent; minimize forward knee migration

**Glute Bridge (accessory):**
- Support on upper back/thoracic spine only; head/neck neutral (no forward lead)
- Vertical shin angle; no lateral bar drift
- Drive via hip extension, not lumbar flexion (critical for chronic back pain protection)

---

## Programming Principles

- **Exercise order**: Squat-first until snatch/clean technique stabilizes, then prioritize Olympic lifts
- **Accessory strategy**: High-rep glute bridges, Romanian deadlifts (quad-dominant imbalance correction), single-arm vertical pulls target full posterior chain during hypertrophy block
- **Volume progression**: Gradual extension ("плавно растягиваем"); avoid aggressive volume without pharmaceutical support
- **Set/rep structure**: Snatch 2 reps at working load; RDLs to high reps (40–50 sec work/20–30 sec rest); glute bridges time-based (40 sec work/10 sec rest) with drop sets
- **Intensity management**: Hold conservative loads (120 kg squat vs. 125–130 kg capable); load progression secondary to technical consistency
- **Recovery consideration**: Don't train to nausea; extend session duration only if recovery allows

## Athlete Relevance

| Issue | Application |
|-------|-------------|
| **OHS 50 kg (primary limiter)** | Back/trap development via snatch/jerk work + accessory shoulder stability focus addresses overhead weakness; tendon elasticity training (RDLs) improves elastic recoil needed for OHS |
| **Chronic back pain** | Squat-first + accessory-heavy programming reduces injury risk; explicit lumbar protection cuing on glute bridges; avoid excessive lumbar extension errors |
| **Push jerk only (65 kg)** | Posterior chain strength (RDLs, glute bridges) prepares legs for split jerk mechanics; leg strength foundation essential before jerk technique advancement |
| **Night shift (Wed–Sun)** | Conservative intensity + early session termination on low CNS days fits fatigue management; volume progression gradual to match recovery capacity |
| **Intermediate status** | Hypertrophy block + accessory-heavy approach suits capacity building without heavy loaded compound stress; technique errors resolved before competition entry (May/June comps deferred) |
| **Back squat 118 kg** | Current focus; conservative load holding (120 kg) until technique perfects; leg strength is foundation for snatch/clean carryover |

**Key coaching principle**: Build maximal reserve capacity via hypertrophy & posterior chain development while perfecting movement patterns—don't chase competition prematurely with technical errors.
