# Мне 35 и сильнее я не был!

## Key Points
*   **Training Philosophy:** Prioritize dynamic movement quality over static grinding; heavy loads require fast turnover driven by relaxation, not tension. Competition drives strength better than isolated gym work.
*   **Fatigue Management:** Avoid "grinding" to failure (e.g., high-rep sets like 6x3). Stop sets immediately when muscles feel tired or technique begins to degrade to prevent injury and preserve motor patterns.
*   **Load Selection:** Focus on sub-maximal loads during technique sessions; a weight that feels manageable can be deceptive ("100 kg feels like 120") if speed is lost.
*   **Injury Prevention:** Explicitly avoid landing on knees/shins or "clamping" arms, which creates shear force and joint stress.

## Technique Cues
*   **The Pull (Snatch/Clean):**
    *   **Knee Path:** Fully extend the knee to generate upward momentum; do not let it wrap around or stay bent. The hip must snap like a spring immediately after knee extension.
    *   **Bar Path:** Allow the bar to travel vertically into the groin/pelvis naturally without artificial pulling by the arms.
    *   **Foot Pressure:** Press through the whole foot, not just the toes.
*   **The Catch (Snatch):**
    *   **Absorption:** Land "butt down" (hips low) to absorb impact in the glutes and muscles; never land on knees or shins.
    *   **Stability:** Maintain vertical balance; avoid leaning forward onto the toes during the catch phase.
*   **The Jerk (Push Jerk Specific):**
    *   **Elbow Rotation:** Rotate elbows quickly under the bar *after* the drive is complete, rather than forcing them down prematurely.
    *   **Relaxation:** Keep arms relaxed during the turnover to allow the bar to fly up freely; avoid "clamping" or tensing the upper body.
    *   **Drive:** Rely on leg drive and elbow speed (critical for push jerks without split mechanics).

## Programming Principles
*   **Volume & Frequency:** Utilize low volume per session with high frequency ("two sets of one," "one more and stop"). Focus on daily or frequent practice of specific movements rather than high-volume grinding.
*   **Exercise Selection:** Emphasize Snatch Pulls/High Pulls to teach the leg "spring" mechanism without full lift complexity. Use light weights or empty bars for speed drills.
*   **Progression Logic:** Discard beginner drills (e.g., jumping with sticks) for intermediate athletes; focus instead on refining complex motor patterns under load.
*   **Strength Foundation:** Maintain Back Squat as a foundational metric to support Olympic lifts, but avoid high-rep squatting that induces fatigue-induced spinal rounding.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The "stop before failure" rule is critical for this athlete. Avoiding high-rep grinding prevents the fatigue-induced lumbar rounding that caused previous injuries. Prioritize cues to land "butt down" and avoid artificial pulling to reduce shear force on the spine during the catch.
*   **Night Shift Worker (Wed–Sun):** The emphasis on mental focus ("train in silence," "breathe and feel muscles") helps maintain proprioception and technique quality despite irregular sleep cycles and fatigue.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** Since split mechanics are absent, strict adherence to the "relaxation" cue is vital; arms must not clamp, relying entirely on leg drive and rapid elbow turnover for the lift.
*   **Transition from Powerlifting:** The athlete must shift mindset from static strength (grinding) to dynamic elasticity. Heavy weights require fast turnover driven by relaxation, contrasting with powerlifting's reliance on tension.
