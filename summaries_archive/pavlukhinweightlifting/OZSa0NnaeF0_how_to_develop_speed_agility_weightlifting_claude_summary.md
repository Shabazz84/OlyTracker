# Merged Summary: Russian Olympic Weightlifting Coaching Analysis

## Key Points

- **Speed intention over actual speed**: Lift all weights (20 kg–95% max) with maximal intent to accelerate, even if movement slows due to load. This drives neural adaptation independent of actual bar velocity.
- **Neural adaptation timeline**: ~8 weeks for nervous system restructuring under heavy load.
- **Motor unit recruitment follows Henneman's Size Principle**: Small→large motor units recruited progressively as load increases. Motor unit recruitment ≠ technical mastery—these are separate adaptations.
- **Stop-gap rule**: End session when bar speed drops ~10% (indicates technical decay and CNS fatigue).
- **Hypertrophy-then-strength periodization**: 
  1. Hypertrophy block (controlled tempo, bodybuilding approach on squats)
  2. Rest/deload
  3. Realize hypertrophied muscle via heavy neural work with speed intent
- **Avoid slow hypertrophy loading on heavy compounds** (snatch, jerk) → tendon injury risk in unprepared athletes. Use controlled-tempo squats instead.

---

## Technique Cues

- **Center of gravity management**: "Move it back if it falls forward"
- **Common catch error**: One leg drifting in catch → poor base → missed lift + excess energy waste
- **Better technique → fewer misses, lower physical cost, more reps possible**
- **Squat descent philosophy**: Smooth, controlled descent + maximal intent on drive up

---

## Programming Principles

- **Session structure**: Single-exercise focus, long rest periods (20 min between snatch and jerk in comp-sim), phosphagen system emphasis (ATP-PC), minimal metabolic acidosis
- **Friday model**: Heavy singles/doubles imitating competition conditions
- **Recovery non-negotiable**: Do not train when fatigued/sleep-deprived—useless and injurious. Must demonstrate bar speed to justify heavy session.

## Athlete Relevance

| Profile | Application |
|---|---|
| **Chronic back pain** | Prioritize technique over load; avoid excessive volume; use controlled-tempo squats in hypertrophy block (safer than heavy snatch/jerk volume) |
| **Night shift (Wed–Sun)** | Train heavy only Mon–Tue when fresh enough to demonstrate bar speed. Mid-shift training is CNS-fatigued training and contraindicated. |
| **OHS 50 kg limiter** | Anterior shoulder/thoracic mobility work required; build back/front squat strength first (hypertrophy block), snatch technique second. Technique refinement must precede aggressive loading. |
| **Push jerk only (no split)** | Split jerk development must precede heavy jerk peaking; currently a bottleneck. |
| **BS 118 kg / Clean 80 kg gap** | Suggests weak leg drive in pull and/or poor catch mechanics. Prioritize technique and catch position strength before pursuing max load. |

**Your 6-week hypertrophy block should:**
1. Use controlled-tempo back/front squats (Mon–Tue, fresh CNS)
2. Light snatch/jerk technique work at 50–60% max with speed intent
3. Avoid high-volume snatch/jerk loading (back pain + tendon injury risk)
4. Post-block: Reintroduce heavy neural work emphasizing speed + mobility
