# Weightlifting Analysis Summary

## Key Points
- Back squat strength directly drives Clean improvement; prioritize squat gains during hypertrophy block (athlete's 118 kg squat has clear upside relative to 80 kg clean)
- Jerk is **tendon-driven**, not muscle-limited; frequent heavy jerking causes tendon fatigue (not muscular fatigue)
- Shoulder/lat mobility and elbow positioning are primary limiting factors for this athlete's weak OHS (50 kg) and jerk catch
- Short, explosive dip maximizes elastic recoil; deep dipping loses momentum and bar trajectory
- Minimize competition lift warm-ups (approach quickly, take attempts sooner)

## Technique Cues
**Snatch/Clean:**
- Drive knees forward aggressively while controlling foot placement (prevent outward drift)
- Pull bar closer to body (horizontal pull, not vertical push-off)
- Full-foot contact during pull (not heel or forefoot only) prevents forward bar path drift

**Jerk (relevant for push jerk):**
- Short, fast dip is superior to deep dip (reference: Murata's minimal dip)
- Elbows must rotate early and aggressively forward to secure catch position
- Drive through entire foot for maximum force production

**Back pain management:**
- Avoid lumbar spine extension during clean catch ("breaking at lower back")
- Find comfortable spinal position during dip warm-up; lat tension/engagement supports core stability

## Programming Principles
- Squat volume during hypertrophy block directly transfers to clean numbers
- Minimum 10 min rest between competition lifts (snatch → clean & jerk)
- Grind toward competition maxes via multiple attempts (avoid prolonged technical warm-ups)
- Rest day mobility work (lat/shoulder) maintains function during training phase
- Manage jerk frequency to allow tendon recovery despite muscular readiness
