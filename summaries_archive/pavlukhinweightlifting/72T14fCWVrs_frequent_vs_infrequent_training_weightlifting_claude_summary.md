# Frequent vs. Infrequent Training. Weightlifting

# Unified Weightlifting Coaching Summary

## Key Points

- **Frequency over volume**: 6–7 days/week of short (~45min) sessions with 1–2 heavy lifts per session produces superior adaptation versus single large-volume sessions. Accumulated nervous system stress across days outpaces single weekly peaks.
- **Step-based loading**: Use fixed increment jumps (e.g., +10kg) rather than percentage-based progressions. Athletes adapt to the step size; this suits fatigued/night-shift states better than load-relative scaling.
- **Light/heavy week cycles**: Same lift feels easier on light weeks over time—a readiness signal for load increases in heavy weeks.
- **Bulgarian principle**: Target ~100 quality reps per movement per day via high frequency, not single-session volume.
- **Quality over quantity**: Reduce between-set talking (disrupts breathing/CNS prep). Ensure adequate rest (2–3min for heavy singles).

## Technique Cues

- **Front squat**: Full foot pressure into floor; explosive concentric; prioritize clean execution over speed.
- **Barbell spin**: Smooth rotation indicates efficient grip/setup.
- **General**: Movement quality and motor pattern refinement trump everything; competition simulation during training builds mental resilience under distraction.

## Programming Principles

- **High-frequency skill work**: Snatch, clean, jerk, and front squat practiced every other day for technical refinement.
- **Competitive movement focus**: Prioritize snatch, clean, jerk, and front squat (sport-specific variants only).
- **Front squat over back squat**: More sport-specific and safer for chronic back issues; load tolerance higher.
- **Minimal accessory work during strength blocks**: Hypertrophy/accessory work deferred to post-competition phases.
- **Spread heavy lifts across the week**: Multiple sessions per movement (e.g., 3 heavy snatches/session × 2–3 sessions) = 6+ quality reps/week without fatigue accumulation.
- **Recovery communication**: Athlete reports under-sleep, injury, or fatigue → coach adjusts load or shifts session. CNS/hormonal depletion (cortisol/adrenaline) prevented through adequate rest and consistency.

## Athlete Relevance

**Strengths of this approach for your profile:**
- **Night shift (Wed–Sun)**: Short daily sessions feasible with nearby gym access; avoids marathon 2hr sessions on inconsistent sleep.
- **Step-based loading**: Better than percentage-based progression when sleep/recovery varies week-to-week.
- **Front squat emphasis**: Safer alternative to back squat given chronic back pain; addresses load tolerance without exacerbating spinal issues.
- **Discipline > genetics principle**: Consistency across 6 weeks matters more than talent—directly applicable to hypertrophy block goal.

**Critical gaps NOT addressed in source:**
- **Back pain management**: No mobility, core stability, or load management protocols for chronic pain. This must be added before/during hypertrophy block.
- **OHS deficiency (50kg)**: Primary snatch limiter not discussed. Requires targeted shoulder mobility, trunk stability, and overhead position drills.
- **Split jerk progression**: Push jerk ceiling ~65kg. Source doesn't outline split jerk introduction—essential for jerk ceiling gains beyond current level.
- **Hypertrophy-specific loading**: Source mentions hypertrophy ≠ strength focus but provides no rep ranges, rest periods, or exercise selection for your 6-week block.

**Recommendation**: Integrate back pain mitigation work (mobility/anti-rotation drills), initiate split jerk technical progression, and structure hypertrophy block with 6–8 rep front squat work + targeted overhead mobility before transitioning to strength phase.
