# Block 3 Exercise Cues — Where Every Line Comes From — 2026-10-18

Block 3 (weeks 17–24) puts 33 exercises on its cards. On 2026-10-18 only 8 of
them had a coaching cue in the app's `EXERCISE_CATALOG`; this document covers
the other 25. Each cue line in the app ends with the coach it came from, and is
listed below with the passage it rests on and a verbatim quote.

**The pack.** `python main.py evidence --catalog cues` asked one question per
exercise (`synthesis/questions.py::CUE_QUESTIONS`, ids 101–125) and wrote the
retrieved passages to `evidence/2026-10-18-cues/` (gitignored — transcript
text). `docs/programs/2026-10-18-cue-citations.json` pins each handle to a
content hash. Handles are pack-local: `E117.4` is the fourth passage retrieved
for question 117 (`front_squat`). Ids start at 101 so these handles can never be
read as the program packs' E1–E42, which the app's older cue text cites.

**The check.** `python -m tools.check_citations docs/programs/2026-10-18-block3-cues.md evidence/2026-10-18-cues`
proves every handle resolves *and* every quote below appears in the passage it
cites (case, spacing and punctuation ignored; `...` is an elision, `[word]` an
editorial correction of the transcript).

**Rules the cues follow.**

- A cue says only what its passages say. Where a passage left something out,
  the cue does too (one Dozer block-clean line was dropped because the source
  said *heavy* block cleans and Block 3's are 65–70%).
- **Speaker checked.** The pack flags passages that run into a reader-comment
  thread. Every flagged passage used here was read for who is speaking: either
  the article's own closing paragraph (before "Related Articles"), or a reply
  signed by Greg Everett. No reader comment is cited.
- **Transfer.** A few cues apply a passage about one lift to a close relative.
  Each one is labelled where it occurs.

## Snatch family

### Snatch — `snatch`

1. *Off the floor, shoulders and hips rise together; shins vertical by the time the bar reaches the knee.* (Dozer · Sika)
   - Dozer: "with getting the Shins to Vertical it's really easy to let those hips go up too soon if you can do both of these things at the exact same time" [E101.1]
   - Sika: "when the barbell is at the knee joint ... Shin is pretty much perpendicular with the floor" [E108.6]
2. *Arms long and shoulders down until the bar reaches the hip; then keep the elbows above the bar until it passes your chest.* (Dozer)
   - "keep try to keep the elbows higher than the barbell until the barbell passes your chest" [E101.2]
   - "if we start pulling with the arms before we make contact ... the bar stalls so what I like to think about is keep those shoulders down and the arms long" [E101.2]
3. *Settle the bottom position before you stand. Don't rush out of the squat.* (Everett · Dozer)
   - Everett: "rush out of the squat and drop the bar partway up" [E101.3]
   - Dozer: "sit in the bottom catch it hold it once you have it then you can stand up" [E119.6]

### Power Snatch — `power_snatch`

1. *Finish the pull, then get under fast. Having less time and distance to get under the bar is what trains second- and third-pull speed.* (Everett)
   - Everett's exercise library, quoted by Matt Foreman: "The power snatch can be used to train speed and force production in both the second pull and the third pull by limiting the amount of time and distance the lifter has available to get under the bar" [E102.11]
2. *The bar has to go higher than in a squat snatch.* (Webster)
   - "When you power snatch, you have to pull the bar higher in order to receive it than you do when you do a squat snatch" [E102.7]

### Snatch from Blocks — `snatch_from_blocks`

1. *Start in the position you'd pass through from the floor. Blocks only carry over to the floor if the position matches.* (Sika)
   - "you need to make sure that your positions are remaining largely similar" [E103.2]
   - "technique that's very useful for Block snatches but that doesn't transfer to lifts from the floor" [E103.2]
2. *Above the knee the job is speed: no early arm bend, then a fast, vertical extension.* (Sika)
   - "above the knee is very very useful for Speed" [E103.9]
   - "limit that amount of early arm Bend" [E103.1]
   - "focus on a bit more of a vertical extension" [E103.1]
3. *It's easier to commit under the bar off blocks than off the floor. Use that to practise going under.* (Dozer)
   - "If you're not going under the bar, it's a mental thing and you need to utilize some block work" [E103.5]
   - "It's much harder to have a bad day off the blocks than it is to have a bad day off the floor" [E103.5]

### 3-Position Snatch — `three_position_snatch`

The program lists the positions as floor, knee, mid-thigh. Everett's own
articles give the order.

1. *Top down: mid-thigh first, then knee, then floor. Start where it's easiest and add distance each rep.* (Everett)
   - "Work on 3-position snatches from the top down - mid-thigh, knee, floor" [E104.1]
   - "This will start you off in the most comfortable position for you, then gradually add more distance" [E104.10]
2. *Every rep passes through the same mid-thigh position: shins vertical, shoulders slightly in front of the bar, weight just behind mid-foot, not on the heels.* (Everett)
   - "what you need to work on is moving from the floor into the proper position at mid-thigh" [E105.8]
   - "your shins should be vertical, shoulders at least slightly in front of the bar" [E105.8]
   - "weight slightly behind the middle of the foot—don't get all the way back on your heels" [E105.8]

### 2-Position Snatch — `two_position_snatch`

1. *Mid-thigh rep first, then the floor.* (Everett)
   - "Another is a 2-position snatch , or hang snatch + snatch, again at mid-thigh first, then the floor" [E105.2]
2. *Use the hang rep to feel staying connected in the turnover and meeting the bar at the right height, then copy that from the floor.* (Everett)
   - "focus on staying connected to the bar in the turnover and meeting it at the correct height, which is easier to do from the hang than from the floor" [E105.5] — flagged passage; speaker checked: the article's closing paragraph.
   - "Then in the snatch that follows, you can attempt to mimic the movement from the hang" [E105.5]

### Snatch + 2 Overhead Squats — `snatch_ohs_complex`

1. *After the snatch, keep the bar locked and still for both squats: shoulder blades squeezed up and together, bar over the back of the neck.* (Everett)
   - "lock it in forcefully keeping it as stable as possible" [E106.8]
   - "shoulder blades retracted and upwardly rotated (squeeze the upper inside edges together forcefully)" [E125.7]
   - "the trunk should be inclined forward very slightly to place the bar over the back of the neck" [E125.7]
2. *Overhead squats straight after the snatch train you to lock the bar out harder in the bottom.* (Sika)
   - "I added in a strict overhead squat with my snatching" [E106.3]
   - "you're just training your ability to lock out the bar harder and more powerfully" [E106.3]

### Overhead Squat — `overhead_squat`

1. *Lock the bar in forcefully, then keep upper back, shoulders, arms and bar motionless for the whole rep.* (Everett)
   - "the goal is keeping the upper back shoulders arms and bar as motionless as possible with continuous active effort to stay tight" [E106.8]
2. *Shoulder blades squeezed up and together; bar over the back of the neck, in the palm just behind the forearm; grip only as tight as control needs.* (Everett)
   - "The bar should be in the palm slightly behind the center of the forearm with the heel of the palm facing up and the hand gripping the bar only tightly enough to maintain control and position" [E125.7]
3. *The goal is a bottom position so routine you never hesitate to receive a snatch there.* (Everett)
   - "mentally to make it so routine and comfortable that you never think about it or hesitate to put yourself there" [E106.12]

### Snatch Deadlift — `snatch_deadlift`

1. *Same positions as your snatch, done slowly. Feel the back angle so you can carry it into the lift.* (Torokhtiy)
   - "A short deadlift is done for us to feel the angle. And transmit it to the movement further on" [E108.1]
   - "the deadlift can be done slowly for us to feel the movement and only after transfer it to the snatch" [E108.1]
2. *Keep one back angle through the first pull; at the knee, shins vertical and shoulders in front of the bar.* (Everett · Sika)
   - Everett: "We want to maintain a fairly constant back angle through the first pull" [E108.11]
   - Sika: "Shin is pretty much perpendicular with the floor ... in relation to the shoulders which are in front of the" [E108.6]
3. *This isn't a max deadlift: at snatch weights you can keep the back set, so keep it set.* (Everett)
   - "even with a maximal effort clean, an athlete will still be able to a large degree to control the position from which he or she pulls the bar from the floor—this includes things like relative hip and shoulder height and the extension of the back" [E108.7]

### Snatch Push Press — `snatch_push_press`

1. *Dip and drive straight down and up with the knees only. Don't push the hips back.* (Everett)
   - "dip and drive completely vertically, flexing and extending at the knees only" [E109.4]
   - against: "instructing clients to dip by pushing the hips back somewhat" [E109.4]
2. *Treat it as a leg exercise: drive hard enough that your heels come up.* (Everett)
   - "I tell athletes to think of the push press as a leg exercise rather than an upper body exercise" [E109.4]
   - "if the heel remains perfectly flat, it indicates that the athlete has not put as much upward force into the bar with the legs as possible" [E109.4]
3. *No grinding reps.* (Everett)
   - "Sets of 5-8 or so is usually what we'll do and loading such that there are no grinding reps" [E109.2] — flagged passage; speaker checked: Everett's signed reply about snatch-grip and behind-the-neck pressing.

## Clean & jerk family

### Power Clean — `power_clean`

1. *Finish the extension, then pull yourself down onto the bar, keeping the torso upright.* (Sika)
   - "prioritizing keeping your torso in a vertical position or as close to Vertical position as possible and pulling yourself aggressively down onto the [bar]" [E110.8]
2. *Land in your squat stance with hips back over the heels, not a wide starfish, so a heavy one can ride down into a full clean.* (Sika)
   - "he keeps his hips back behind the level of his heels to catch that power clean" [E110.8]
   - "feet then scoot out into a very wide starfish like catch position" [E110.8]
   - "what you need is the ability to miss a power clean into a full clean" [E110.2]
3. *Rack it on shoulders pushed forward and slightly up.* (Everett)
   - "the shoulders should be protracted and slightly elevated" [E110.6] — flagged passage; speaker checked: Everett's signed reply.

### Clean from Blocks — `clean_from_blocks`

1. *Above the knee the work is the second pull and the turnover: explode, then get under fast.* (Sika · Torokhtiy)

2. *Start in the position you'd pass through from the floor.* (Sika)

### 3-Position Clean — `three_position_clean`

The corpus returned no clean-specific passage on position order.

1. *Top down, the same order as the 3-position snatch: mid-thigh, then knee, then floor.* (Everett)
   - **Transfer** from the snatch: "Work on 3-position snatches from the top down - mid-thigh, knee, floor" [E104.1]
2. *Make contact with the knees still bent and drive the legs into the bar. Don't stand up first and then bang it.* (Dozer)
   - "when we make contact we're not standing up and then making contact" [E113.3]
   - "we want to make that contact with a deep knee bend that way we can take the legs and pump them into the bar" [E113.3]
3. *Catch with the elbows high.* (Dozer)
   - "when you catch these get those elbows really high" [E113.3]

### 2-Position Clean — `two_position_clean`

1. *Mid-thigh rep first, then the floor, as in the 2-position snatch. Copy the hang rep's contact from the floor.* (Everett)
   - **Transfer** from the snatch: "again at mid-thigh first, then the floor" [E105.2]
   - "Then in the snatch that follows, you can attempt to mimic the movement from the hang" [E105.5]
2. *Be patient: the legs push, the knees get out of the way, and contact comes with the legs still bent.* (Dozer)
   - "we pull from the floor we push with the legs knees get out of the way and we get back with bent legs" [E113.3]
   - "so you want to be patient" [E113.3]

### Clean + 2 Front Squats + Jerk — `clean_fs_jerk_complex`

1. *After the front squats, stand tall: straight, braced torso before the dip, not an over-arched one.* (Sika)
   - "i feel like i'm a little bit too arched before i get into the jerk" [E114.7]
   - "everything locked down sit like this nice and straight" [E114.7]
2. *The front squats make the jerk harder, so keep its dip and drive vertical anyway.* (Sika · Everett)
   - Sika: "that front squat before the jerk makes it hard to make your legs makes it hard for you to change that direction" [E114.7]
   - Everett: "it needs to be straight down, straight up and complete" [E115.5]

### Power Clean + Power Jerk — `power_clean_power_jerk`

The clean half is covered under Power Clean; question 115 asked about the jerk.

1. *Before the dip: knees unlocked, quads tight, full breath. Dipping from locked knees shifts you forward.* (Everett)
   - "Unlock your knees and create tension on your quads" [E115.5]
   - "If you begin the dip of the jerk from the passive knee lock you normally stand with, there will be slack in the system" [E115.5]
   - "this will often shift you forward" [E115.5]
   - "Make sure that you're getting a big, full breath" [E115.5]
2. *Dip at a speed that keeps the bar on your shoulders, then drive straight up.* (Everett)
   - "Initiate the dip with a controlled enough acceleration that the bar remains connected to your shoulders completely" [E115.5]
3. *Catch in your squat stance, thighs above parallel, bar pushed back over the back of the neck.* (Everett)
   - "feet in the squat stance and thighs above horizontal" [E115.4]
   - "the bar must move back behind the neck as the trunk inclines forward slightly to complete the proper overhead position" [E115.11]

### 2 Power Jerks + 1 Split Jerk — `power_split_jerk_combo`

1. *Same dip and drive for all three: straight down, straight up, complete. Only the feet change on the split.* (Everett)
   - "should be identical to that of the power jerk—that is, it needs to be straight down, straight up and complete. The lift only varies with the splitting of the feet into the receiving position" [E115.5]
2. *The power jerks groove a balanced, vertical dip and drive. They only help while they stay that way.* (Everett)
   - "The biggest benefit of the power jerk is training a balanced, vertical dip and drive" [E115.11]
   - "Power jerk + split jerk is another good complex as long as you're able to maintain your balance and vertical dip and drive in the power jerk" [E116.3] — flagged passage; speaker checked: the article's closing paragraph.
3. *Go smoothly from the dip straight into the drive, with no pause at the bottom.* (Sika)
   - "if you're pausing too long in the bottom you're going to dissipate a lot of those stored energies" [E116.9]
   - "essentially looks like there's zero [pause] in the bottom" [E116.7]

## Squats and pulls

### Front Squat — `front_squat`

1. *Rack: shoulders forward and slightly up, bar sitting on them, and a full hand around the bar, not two fingers.* (Everett · Foreman)
   - Everett: "the shoulders should be protracted and slightly elevated" [E117.9] — flagged passage; speaker checked: Everett's signed reply.
   - Foreman: "he changed his hand position and sealed more of his hand on the bar" [E117.2], in an article on front squatting with a closed fist instead of two fingers.
2. *Knees travel forward over the toes and the pressure stays in the quads, with the torso upright.* (Sika/Telander)
   - "you're in a front squat position so you're going to have to stay upright" [E117.4]
   - "reinforces the technique of pushing your knee over your toe and keeping the pressure in your quads the whole time" [E117.4]

### Back Squat — `back_squat`

1. *Set your lower back's natural arch, not an exaggerated one, and keep it for the whole rep: no rounding at the bottom, no hip thrust at the top.* (Sika)
   - "[Thrust] at the top of the rep is never good" [E118.1] (the passage chunk begins mid-word)
   - "I'm going to set my hips in that natural level of lumbar extension and they're going to stay there throughout the entire squat" [E118.1]
   - "I'm not going to lose this position at any point in my set" [E118.1]
   - "I'm not allowing you to overextend or go into this level of hyperextension" [E118.1]
2. *Go heavy only as deep as that arch holds.* (Everett)
   - "If an athlete cannot maintain proper spinal extension in the lowest position" [E118.2]
   - "he should be careful to not lift very heavy weights to this depth" [E118.2] — flagged passage; speaker checked: Everett's signed reply.
3. *Knees forward over the toes, hips close to the heels. Standing up, keep driving the knees forward so the hips don't shoot back.* (Sika)
   - "We're driving the knees forward over our toes. As we hit the bottom, we're trying to keep our hips close to our heels. And as I stand up, I'm pushing my knees forward" [E118.9]
   - "keep my knees forward keep my quads engaged and stop my hips from shooting back as I stand up" [E118.11]

### Pause Back Squat — `pause_back_squat`

1. *Hold the full 3-second pause, then stand without a bounce. Taking away the stretch reflex is the point.* (Torokhtiy)
   - "Pause squats primarily develop maximum strength since there is not a stretch-contraction response throughout the movement" [E119.11]
   - "It is recommended to take a 3-second break in the bottom position" [E119.11]
2. *Use a weight you can stand up in good position. A slow rep in the right position beats a fast one that tips forward.* (Everett)
   - "Pause squats are even better as long as you're using weights that allow you to stand with the proper position" [E119.8]
   - "a slow but properly positioned squat is superior to a fast but improperly positioned squat" [E119.7]

**Contested.** Dozer disagrees about long pauses: "I have seen no convincing evidence that prolonged pause squats, so sitting in the bottom for two, three, five seconds does anything at all" [E119.1]. The cue follows the program, which prescribes a 3-second pause, as does Torokhtiy [E119.11]. This is the same dispute the Block 2 Judgment Ledger recorded.

### Stiff-Legged Deadlift — `stiff_legged_deadlift`

Torokhtiy's video is a Romanian deadlift from a 10-kg plate. **Transfer:** the
stiff-legged deadlift is the same hinge with the knees held in place.

1. *Knees slightly bent and fixed, back straight. The movement is all in the hinge.* (Torokhtiy)
   - "мы сгибаем немножко ноги в коленях" ("we bend the legs slightly at the knees") [E120.3]
   - "насчет прямой спины держал прям спиной прямое" ("with a straight back, held straight") [E120.3]
2. *It builds the lower back, hamstrings and glutes that keep the trunk solid in the jerk.* (Torokhtiy)
   - "тренирую первое поясница заднюю поверхность бедра и ягодицы" ("training, first, the lower back, the hamstrings and the glutes") [E120.3]
   - "в толчке это очень важно чтобы здесь было всё крепко и стабильное" ("in the jerk it is very important that everything here is strong and stable") [E120.3]

### Barbell Row — `barbell_row`

1. *Pendlay style carries over most: bar from the floor every rep, hips high as in a deadlift, pull to the ribs, plates back to the floor each time.* (Dozer)
   - "the one that carries over to the lifts the most more than any other sort of row is going to be our penl row" [E122.1]
   - "the bar starts on the floor our hips are pretty high we're in more of a deadlift position" [E122.1]
   - "we're just going to pull to the ribs upper abdomen and the plates are going to come back to the floor every single time" [E122.1]
2. *Train the back directly. Pulls and squats alone don't do it.* (Sika)
   - "make sure you're training your back in a good and directed manner and not leaving up to pulls and our squats" [E122.7]

### Plank — `plank`

1. *Brace as you would under a bar: lower abs tight and pushed down, not sucked in.* (Everett)
   - **Transfer** from the jerk dip: "try focusing on lower ab tension specifically when you brace" [E115.12]
   - "Don't suck your abs in. Imagine pushing them down as you brace" [E115.12]
2. *Hold the position still. In the lifts the trunk's job is to be a rigid support.* (Everett)
   - Cited from the Block 2 pack, not this one: Everett, *Back Training for Weightlifting*
     (https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/62/Back-Training-for-Weightlifting/) — in
     the lifts the trunk is *to simply act as a rigid support structure*, so static
     strength is the priority. The Block 2 document quotes and cites it in its Trunk section.

### Ab Wheel — `ab_wheel`

1. *Roll out only as far as you can still control the position, hold it there, then roll back.* (Gregory, via Sika)
   - Stephane Gregory, physio to the Chinese team, on Sika's channel: "we prescribe barbell roll outs" [E123.2]
   - "go to where they control and then just hold it" [E123.2]

### Trunk — Static Strength — `trunk_static_strength`

Question 125 retrieved Everett on overhead holds rather than trunk work, so both
lines come from the article the Block 2 pack retrieved for its trunk question.

1. *Static holds, not crunches: in the lifts the trunk's job is to act as a rigid support.* (Everett)
2. *Aim at the front and sides. The lifts, squats and pulls already load the back.* (Everett)
   - Both: Everett, *Back Training for Weightlifting*
     (https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/62/Back-Training-for-Weightlifting/) — the
     lifts and squats and pulls load the lower and middle back, so that area needs less
     specific work than *the anterior and lateral aspects of the torso*. Quoted and cited
     in the Block 2 document's Trunk section.
