# Block 1: Live vs. Evidence Rebuild — 2026-08-11

Per-prescription comparison of `PROGRAM_B1` (`docs/src/app.jsx:300`) against
`2026-08-11-block1-rebuild.md`, which was written blind from
`evidence/2026-08-11/`.

**The live program is not modified by this document.** The athlete is in week 6
of 8 and finishes weeks 7–8 as written.

## Verdicts used

- **AGREES** — the live prescription is supported by a retrieved passage.
- **DIVERGES** — the corpus says something materially different.
- **OLD-UNSUPPORTED** — no corpus support; not contradicted, just unfounded.
- **ABSENT-FROM-LIVE** — the corpus supports something the live block omits.
- **NEW-UNCITED** — the rebuild needed judgment here too; neither is evidence-based.
- **NOT ASSESSED** — the relevant question was not read in this pass.

**Deviation from plan, recorded:** the plan specified four verdicts. Two more
were needed. `ABSENT-FROM-LIVE` because several of the most useful findings are
things the live block *lacks* rather than gets wrong — collapsing those into
DIVERGES would hide them. `NOT ASSESSED` because 14 of 30 question files were
not read, and silence must not read as agreement.

## The finding that matters most

**The overhead-squat dosing in the live program is the reverse of what the
sources say — and it got there through a document built from those same
sources.**

Dimensions 8, 9 and 10 are one finding. The live block runs overhead squats on
four days a week, 11–12 sets, including a standalone slot in D1's primary
position. The corpus says: 1–2× per week, 5–7 total sets of 2–3 reps, *"never
excessive"*, and *"it's not a standalone overhead squat"* even in a program
written specifically for a weak overhead [E10.8].

The reasoning behind that is not incidental:

> *"If we start fatiguing ourselves with an overhead squat — say if I was doing
> a five by five progression or a five by three progression from week to week —
> I'm going to really really sacrifice a lot of my recovery capital that I need
> for the snatch and for the jerk."* [E10.5]

And more fundamentally:

> *"The overhead squat is rarely an issue of strength. You usually have weak
> shoulders or poor mobility in the shoulders, neither of which need to be
> trained with an overhead squat."* [E10.5]

Corroborated independently by Everett: assuming insufficient strength is the
natural response, but *"there are other elements to consider that may be
preventing the athlete from properly using what may be adequate strength"*
[E11.2].

**How the live program got here is the point.** Versions v3.5.2–3.5.6 *added*
overhead-squat volume, closing what `master_synthesis.md` identified as a gap:
"OHS under-dosed at the #1 limiter." That synthesis is a single LLM pass over
seven generic topics covering ~5% of the corpus. Asked the specific programming
question directly, the sources say the opposite.

This is not a retrieval failure and not a hallucination in the usual sense. It
is a **plausible inference from a thin, topic-shaped view of the corpus**, which
then shipped. It is exactly the class of error the evidence rebuild was designed
to catch, caught on its first real run — and it is a stronger argument for the
`ask`-over-`synthesize` rule in `CLAUDE.md` than any argument made in advance.

**What the corpus prescribes instead**, for the same limiter: diagnose the
position first [E11.11], check that the effort is genuinely aggressive rather
than passive [E11.11], use snatch balance and behind-the-neck pressing to carry
the load at lower cost [E10.3], keep OHS as a 5-second isometric hold inside a
complex [E11.3], and note that forward lean overhead points at *lower-body*
mobility rather than shoulders [E11.10].

## Second finding: the pain gate is invented

Dimension 26. The live block repeats, on D1 and D3 of every loading week:

> *"Pain-gate: back pain >3/10 pre-session → drop load ~40% or sub
> machine-equivalent."*

**Neither number appears anywhere in this corpus.** Question 22 returned four
passages; two are usable. They provide a *method* — identify your own flare-up
triggers by matching trends, keep what reduces pain and *"do it diligently"*,
and see a professional [E22.3] — and one precedent: an athlete with a bad lower
back moved to block pulls, with front squats preferred over back squats for the
upright posture [E22.2].

That is not a protocol. The "3/10 / 40%" rule has the same shape as the archived
synthesis's *"stop if pain >3/10"*, which retrieval already showed to be
unsupported and which `STATUS.md` records as a cautionary example. A variant is
still in the shipping program.

Chronic back pain under load needs a clinician. The corpus correctly refuses to
supply a number, and so does the rebuild.

## Tall-Lifter Reproduction Verdict: **PASS**

The v3.2.0 squat protocol was derived from this corpus months ago from Sika and
Telander. A blind rebuild, querying only *"what do these coaches say about squat
rep ranges and technique for tall or long-limbed lifters?"*, reproduced it —
from the same two independent sources, plus Greg Everett arriving separately.

Reproduced:

- Load migrates to the back at **both** extremes — very high reps and very heavy
  singles — because a long-limbed lifter's back is stronger than their quads
  [E20.6]
- Live in the rep range where technique stays perfect [E20.6]
- Attack the quads with separate, back-sparing exercises [E20.6, E20.7]

**And it came back sharper than the shipped version**, supplying numbers the
original protocol lacked:

- The position-holding zone is **~65–70% of 1RM and ≤3–5 reps**; above either,
  positions break down [E20.10]
- Adopting the correct pattern **temporarily costs 30–40% of the old 1RM**
  before it is surpassed [E20.7]
- Hips shooting back is trainable, not morphological: *"if any of the reps can
  be done with less or no tipping, then all of them can be"* [E20.9]

That second number is directly actionable and was not previously in the program.

**Interpretation.** The method reproduces a known-good result unprompted, which
is the strongest available evidence that its *other* outputs — including the
uncomfortable overhead-squat finding — are trustworthy rather than artifacts of
question phrasing. A rebuild that had missed the tall-lifter protocol would have
been a retrieval finding requiring investigation before anything else could be
believed. It didn't.

## Recommended follow-ups

Not actions taken — the live program is untouched by design.

1. **Reconsider the overhead-squat dosing** (dims 8–10). The strongest single
   result here, and it reverses a shipped change.

   guidance and cites nothing.

   (dim 13), both absent and both cited.

   marked NOT ASSESSED, and mobility dosing is unclaimed.
