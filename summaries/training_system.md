# OLY Training System
**Athlete:** 102.5 kg | Intermediate powerlifter → OLY | Chronic back pain | Push jerk only | Night shifts Wed–Sun
**Sources:** Pavlukhin · Berestov/Athletists · Torokhtiy · Catalyst Athletics · Dozer · Sonny Webster · Golovinsky/LMS · Atletisty

---

## Part I: Foundation Principles

These are the non-negotiable truths extracted from all 8 sources. Everything in the program is downstream of these.

### 1. The Back Constraint Is Absolute
The spine is a **stabilizer, not a mover**. Every coach agrees: power comes from the legs and hips; the erector spinae must remain rigid throughout pulls. This is not a style choice — it is the single most important rule given chronic back pain. Consequences:
- Any lift that causes lumbar shear is immediately substituted, not reduced
- "Pushing through" back pain is universally condemned across all 8 sources
- The program is designed around the back, not merely modified for it

### 2. Speed and Explosive Intent Over Load
For a strength athlete transitioning to OLY, the brain defaults to tension-based grinding. This is wrong for Olympic lifting. Across all sources: **explosive intent at sub-maximal loads (65–85%) builds the motor pattern**; heavy slow pulls entrench bad mechanics permanently (Everett: "practice makes permanent"). Speed comes first, weight follows.

### 3. Auto-Regulation Is Not Optional
Rigid percentage-based programming fails night shift workers. Every source that addresses the night shift schedule reaches the same conclusion: daily intensity must be dictated by CNS status, sleep quality, and pain level — not a calendar. The plan gives structure; the athlete gives the daily reading of readiness.

### 4. OHS Stability Is the Snatch Ceiling
The overhead squat at 50 kg is the current hard limit on snatch development. This is not a strength problem — it is a position problem (thoracic mobility, shoulder external rotation, active lat engagement). Until the OHS ceiling rises, snatch weight cannot follow. Every block must dedicate specific work to this.

### 5. The Jerk Is the Priority Weak Point
Clean: 80 kg. Jerk: ~65 kg. This 15 kg gap is the biggest opportunity in the entire program. The jerk must be treated as the primary technical skill, not an afterthought after squats and pulls. It gets scheduled first, gets the best energy, and gets the most focused cue work.

### 6. Touch the Competition Movement Every Session
From Golovinsky's LMS framework: even on light days, even on hypertrophy days — the competition lifts are touched. 2–3 sets of muscle snatch or hang power snatch at 50% on "non-snatch" days keeps the motor pattern alive. The pattern degrades if left for days. This takes 5–10 minutes and is non-negotiable.

### 7. High Frequency, Low Volume Per Session Over Low Frequency, High Volume
Olympic lifting is a skill. Skills require frequent repetition, not infrequent exhaustion. 4–5 sessions per week at moderate volume beats 2 sessions per week with high volume. The nervous system needs consistent input to build the pattern.

### 8. Eight-Week Cycles With Testing
Berestov's model, confirmed by Golovinsky: 8-week macro-cycles ending in a test or simulation. Within each cycle: a 4-week accumulation, 1-week unload, then 2–3 weeks of intensity consolidation before testing. This creates rhythm and gives the athlete regular feedback without the burnout of indefinite intensity progression.

## Part II: Periodization Architecture

### The Long Arc: Four Blocks

| Block | Duration | Goal | Intensity Zone |
|-------|----------|------|----------------|
| **Block 1: Hypertrophy Foundation** | 6–8 weeks | Build muscular base, tissue resilience, fix OHS | 60–75% TM |
| **Block 2: Technique Consolidation** | 6–8 weeks | Ingrain motor patterns under moderate load, lead-up exercises | 70–82% TM |
| **Block 3: Strength & Load Development** | 6–8 weeks | Raise average training weight 4% (Torokhtiy rule → +10 kg total) | 75–90% TM |
| **Block 4: Competition Prep / Peaking** | 4 weeks | Reduce volume, preserve intensity, test maxes | 85–95% TM |

Each block ends with a deload week (volume –40%, intensity maintained) and a brief test of key lifts before the next block begins.

### Block 1 Detail: Hypertrophy Foundation (Current Block)
**Goal:** Build the tissue that will carry the load. Per Pavlukhin: hypertrophy creates the structural base; intensity extracts from it. Do not rush this.

- **Phase 1 (Weeks 1–3):** 65–72% TM. Higher reps (4×6–8 on accessory lifts). Focus on posterior chain, OHS stability, and learning the correct positions.
- **Phase 2 (Weeks 4–6):** 72–80% TM. Introduce daily max singles on jerk. Begin feeling out top-end singles on snatch and clean without grinding misses.
- **Deload (Week 7):** Volume –40%, intensity maintained. Light technique work only.
- **Test (Week 8):** 1RM attempts on snatch, clean, jerk, OHS, front squat.

### Block 2 Detail: Technique Consolidation
**Goal:** Berestov's lead-up exercise system — every session begins with positional work before the competition lift. Movement patterns become automatic.

- Session structure shifts: more time on hang variations, segment pulls, snatch balances, and pause work
- Load climbs back from ~70% into 80–85% range by end of block
- Jerk focus: daily max singles continue, but now with a back-off volume set (e.g., heavy single + 3×3 at 85% of that single)

### Block 3 Detail: Strength & Load Development
**Goal:** Torokhtiy's principle — raise average training weight by 4% to add 10 kg to the total. This requires deliberately loading the moderate intensity zone (not just hitting maxes).

- Track weekly average training weight (total kg lifted ÷ total reps)
- Each week, the average must trend upward by ~1%
- Heavy singles appear 1–2× per week (Golovinsky's daily max protocol for jerk; 2× weekly for snatch/clean)

### Block 4 Detail: Peaking
**Goal:** Translate accumulated strength into competition-ready performance.

- Volume drops 35–40% from Block 3
- Intensity rises to 90–95% on key days
- Competition movement every session; no variation chasing
- Schedule 2–3 mock competition sessions with full warm-up protocols

## Part IV: Loading Methodology

### The Daily Max Single Protocol (Jerk — Golovinsky/LMS)
Replace prescribed sets/reps on jerk with the following:
1. Warm up through percentages (50%, 60%, 70%, 80%)
2. Continue adding weight in 2.5–5 kg increments
3. Stop at the **heaviest single that feels solid and fast**
4. No grinding misses. If a weight felt maximal but was completed, that is the day's max.
5. Back-off: 2–3 sets at 85% of that single

This produces more quality reps near maximal load than fixed percentages, and auto-regulates based on daily readiness.

### Feel-Based Loading for Snatch and Clean
From Torokhtiy and Everett: work up to the **heaviest weight that feels good that day**. Do not force a PR. Do not chase a number. If 85% feels heavy today (night shift, poor sleep), stop at 85%. If 90% feels light, explore 92–95%.

### Average Training Weight (ATW) Tracking
From Torokhtiy's research (r=0.904 correlation between ATW and performance):
- Each session: log every lift (kg × reps)
- Weekly: calculate ATW = total kg lifted ÷ total reps
- Goal per block: ATW rises 1–2% per week over 6 weeks
- Raising ATW by 4% predicts a ~10 kg increase in competition total

The ATW is tracked in the OlyTracker app automatically.

### Load Distribution by Session Quality

| Session Quality | Snatch/Clean Target | Jerk Target | Notes |
|----------------|---------------------|-------------|-------|
| ⭐⭐⭐ Best (Mon/Tue) | Up to 90–95% | Daily max | Full capacity days |
| ⭐⭐ Good (Wed/Thu) | 80–87% | Daily max | Solid but not pushing |
| ⭐ Compromised | 65–75% | 70–75% | Technique only |
| Pain >3/10 | Substitute or skip | Skip | Non-negotiable |

### The LMS Phase System (Golovinsky)
For longer periodization, the program cycles through three modes:

1. **High Volume Mode (Accumulation):** Moderate intensity (65–78%), higher reps/sets, metabolic stress. Builds hypertrophy and work capacity.
2. **High Intensity Mode (Intensification):** Near-maximal loads, low volume, daily max singles. Extracts strength from hypertrophy base.
3. **Micro-Weight Mode (Deload/Recovery):** Very light loads, technique focus, active recovery. Used after pain flares or after each intensity block.

Never mix modes within the same week. Cycle through them systematically.

## Part V: Exercise Library

### Primary Competition Lifts
- **Snatch** — full lift, from floor
- **Clean & Jerk** — full lift, from floor
- **Jerk from Rack** — jerk training without clean fatigue

### Lead-Up Exercises (Berestov's "Подводящие Упражнения")
These precede the competition lift every session. They ingrain positional mechanics.

**Snatch lead-ups:**
- Muscle Snatch — arm-only pull with turnover; trains speed under bar
- Power Snatch — receive above parallel; trains extension timing
- Hang Snatch (knee) — eliminates first pull; trains second pull position
- Snatch from blocks — isolates specific pull phase
- Snatch Balance — drop under actively; trains receiving speed and OHS stability
- Overhead Squat — positional strength in catch

**Clean lead-ups:**
- Muscle Clean — trains front rack turnover without leg assistance
- Power Clean — receive above parallel; trains extension timing
- Hang Clean (knee) — isolates second pull
- Clean Pull — trains posterior chain, bar path, extension without turnover
- Romanian Deadlift — posterior chain, hamstring, spinal safe

**Jerk lead-ups:**
- Push Press — drives overhead strength from leg initiation
- Jerk from rack — dedicated jerk without clean
- Jerk Balance / Dip & Drive — trains dip control specifically
- Rack Holds (Dozer) — 30–60s at 105–120% of max jerk; builds shoulder/lockout strength
- Pogo Jumps (Atletisty) — develops hip drive pattern for jerk without spinal load

### Squat Hierarchy (Back Pain Priority)
1. **Front Squat** — primary squat. Less spinal compression, better Olympic transfer.
2. **Overhead Squat** — OHS development and snatch receiving position.
3. **Back Squat** — used at 65–75% on Day 5 only, during pain-free periods.
4. **Belt Squat / Leg Press** — substitute during back flare-ups. Maintains leg strength without axial loading.
5. **Pause Front Squat** — positional strength in bottom; builds tolerance for receiving heavy cleans.

### Posterior Chain & Back Health
- **Romanian Deadlift (RDL)** — primary hamstring/glute builder, spinal safe
- **Good Morning** — use only pain-free, light, as mobility tool
- **Reverse Hyperextension** — back health staple; decompresses spine
- **Back Extension / GHD** — slow eccentric, pause at top; builds erector endurance
- **Snatch/Clean Pull** — posterior chain strength with competition-specific bar path

### Overhead Stability (OHS Development)
- **Overhead Squat** — primary; 3–4 sets every Day 1 and Day 3
- **Snatch Balance** — develops speed into receiving position; trains the "drop" under bar
- **Rack Hold (Dozer)** — heavy holds (70 kg+) to release tension and build shelf stability
- **Band OHS / Stick OHS** — mobility and position practice without load
- **Landmine Press** — shoulder strength with reduced spinal loading
- **Face Pull** — posterior shoulder, external rotation strength

### Upper Back & Shoulder Support
- **Lat Pulldown / Chest-Supported Row** — upper back without lumbar shear
- **Strict Press** — overhead strength foundation for jerk
- **Dumbbell Row** — unilateral balance, back health
- **Band Pull-Apart** — daily shoulder health, rotation cuff support
- **Bicep/Tricep work** — elbow support under heavy overhead loads

## Part VI: Technique Cue Index

### Setup (All Lifts)
- **Shoulders over the bar** — shoulder joint directly above the barbell at start (Atletisty/Vorobyev Rule 2)
- **Shins close to bar** — minimize shin-to-bar distance to reduce torque moment (Atletisty Rule 1)
- **Full foot contact** — entire sole on floor; lifting heel or toe compromises bar path and shear force (Pavlukhin)
- **Static back** — visualize the erector spinae as a rigid column that does not move during the pull (Pavlukhin)
- **Scapulae retracted and set** — create the "shelf" before the bar moves (Torokhtiy)

### First Pull
- **Lead with the chest / maintain back angle** — chest rises first; hips and shoulders rise at the same rate (Webster)
- **Do not straighten the legs early** — the "stripper snatch" fault; legs straighten before hips are engaged (Webster)
- **Bar stays close** — "drag" the bar up the thighs; bar contact is maintained through the entire pull (Pavlukhin/Athletists)
- **High start alternative** — for back pain: start with higher hips (vertical shins) to shorten pull path and reduce lumbar shear (Pavlukhin)

### Second Pull / Extension
- **Full hip extension before turnover** — hips must reach full extension before the elbows pull (Torokhtiy "no jump" snatch)
- **Drive elbows up immediately** — after contact/extension, elbows travel up and out simultaneously
- **Thoracic drive, not lumbar arch** — power comes from extending the thoracic spine (lifting the chest); hyperextending the lumbar spine shifts stress to the injury site (Torokhtiy)
- **Rapid relaxation** — after explosive extension, immediately relax to allow the drop under; tension at the wrong moment kills bar height (Atletisty)

### Receiving / Catch
- **Active shoulders / drive into the bar** — do not passively receive; drive scapulae up and out to create a rigid platform (Torokhtiy, Catalyst)
- **Elbows turn out and up** — immediately upon receiving, elbows rotate forward (clean) or up and out (snatch)
- **Elbow tension (OHS)** — actively pull the hand toward the elbow to engage lats; prevents lumbar hyperextension at the top (Pavlukhin)
- **"Sit and relax"** — in the squat receiving position, distribute load through hips rather than lumbar extension (Dozer)
- **"Beat the bar"** — actively decelerate (meet the bar) at peak height; do not let gravity drop the weight onto the spine (Webster)
- **"Bone lock"** — elbows into slight hyperextension + pinched scapulae in overhead; creates structural integrity without muscular effort (Catalyst)

### Jerk (Push Jerk Specific)
- **Load front half of foot before dip** — weight distribution shifts forward before the dip initiates (Dozer)
- **Controlled dip** — do not rush the dip; fight against the bar's momentum downward before driving (Webster)
- **Dip-drive synchronization** — dip must be absorbed fully before the drive initiates; no "early drive" (Torokhtiy)
- **Vertical bar path** — feet remain neutral or minimally turned; bar travels straight up, not forward (Torokhtiy)
- **Drop under immediately** — do not wait for peak height; drop as bar leaves contact (Athletists)
- **Immediate bar release / drop** — after lockout, release the bar immediately; do not absorb it with the biceps/shoulders (Pavlukhin)
- **Spine whip** — for advanced application: aggressive pull-down generates upward momentum; only applicable once dip control is mastered (Dozer)

### OHS / Overhead Stability
- **"Drive knees through the bar"** — cue for active squat descent in the overhead position (Catalyst)
- **"Shrug into the rack"** — actively elevate the bar via scapular elevation to stabilize the receiving position (Catalyst)
- **Active lats** — pull hands toward elbows; engages lats to prevent hyperextension and stabilize the bar (Pavlukhin)
- **Thoracic extension** — lift the chest from behind, not from lumbar (Webster, Torokhtiy)

## Part VII: Mobility & Recovery Protocol

### Daily Mobility (10–15 min, every session warm-up)

**Thoracic (highest priority):**
- Foam roller thoracic extensions — 10 reps over foam roller at mid/upper back
- PVC Wall Slides — 10–15 reps; trains shoulder external rotation in overhead (Webster)
- Stick Rotation / Band Elbow Press — 2×10; shoulder mobility for front rack (Torokhtiy)
- Cat-cow + thoracic rotation — 10 reps each direction

**Ankle (critical for OHS and squat depth):**
- Half-kneeling ankle rocks — 10 reps per side (Webster's "Duck Rocks")
- Weighted hip glides — 10 reps per side (Webster)
- Toe-elevated calf stretch — 30s per side

**Hip Flexors:**
- Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch — 30–60s per side
- Turtle Squats / Duck Walks — 10–15 reps (Webster)

**Shoulder/External Rotation:**
- Band pull-aparts — 2×15
- External rotation with band — 2×15
- Snatch grip overhead hold (bar only) — 2×30s; trains external rotation under bar load

### Weekly Recovery Schedule (Around Night Shifts)

| Day | Recovery Action |
|-----|----------------|
| Mon | Full session — best day, full recovery |
| Tue | Full session — second best |
| Wed | Light session, stop by 3pm |
| Thu | Post-shift nap → train if 5+ hours sleep |
| Fri | Rest — post-shift sleep only |
| Sat | Light session if rested; mobility if not |
| Sun | Full rest — recover from last shift |

**Klokov Recovery Protocol (adapted for night shift):**
- **Sauna:** Every 10–14 days. Schedule for Mon afternoon (post-training, long before next session).
- **Massage:** 1–2× per month, preferably before or after sauna day.
- **Video review:** After every session; identify one error per session only — do not overwhelm.

### Back Pain Management Protocol

**Traffic Light System:**
- 🟢 No pain: Train as planned
- 🟡 Dull ache (1–3/10): Reduce back squat → front squat; reduce pull weight 15%; maintain technique work
- 🔴 Sharp/acute (4+/10): Stop axial loading entirely. Substitute leg press, belt squat, chest-supported rows. Maintain OHS mobility work and jerk from rack if pain is location-specific.
- 🚫 Numbness or radiating pain: No training; seek medical assessment

**During 🔴 flare (retreat microcycle):**
- Reduce load 40% across the board
- Machine isolation replaces barbell compounds
- Maintain mobility — it will reduce the duration of the flare
- Return to full training only after 2 consecutive pain-free training days

## Part VIII: Weak Point Protocols

### Protocol 1: OHS Development (Primary Snatch Limiter)
Current ceiling: 50 kg OHS. Target by end of Block 2: 65–70 kg OHS.

**Every Day 1 and Day 3:**
- Snatch Balance × 3 sets — trains receiving speed; use 55–70% of snatch max
- OHS × 3–4 sets — progressive; aim to add 2.5 kg every 10–14 days
- Rack Holds × 2 sets (Dozer) — hold at 100–115% of current OHS max for 30–60s; builds shelf stability

**Daily mobility prerequisite:** Thoracic foam roller + PVC wall slides + ankle rocks before any overhead work.

**Progress marker:** When OHS reaches 65 kg with solid position, snatch loads can rise to match.

### Protocol 2: Jerk Development (Primary C&J Limiter)
Current ceiling: ~65 kg jerk vs. 80 kg clean (15 kg gap).

**Day 4 is dedicated jerk day. Additionally:**
- Touch jerk every Day 2 after cleans (3×3 at 80% of best jerk) — Golovinsky's "touch the movement" principle
- Daily max singles on Day 4 — auto-regulating, no grinding misses
- Rack Holds on Day 3 — build overhead lockout without full jerk stress

**Progress markers by milestone:**
- 70 kg jerk: focus on dip control (the limiting factor now)
- 75 kg jerk: introduce spine whip concept (Dozer)
- 80 kg jerk = cleans. Gap closed. Shift to 85/90 kg clean targets.

### Protocol 3: Back Health Maintenance (Ongoing)
The Demon Back Protocol (Dozer) as adapted for this athlete:

**Weekly essentials (regardless of training load):**
- Reverse Hyperextension × 3×15 (or back extension as substitute)
- Dead Bug × 3×10 per side
- McGill Big Three (curl-up, side plank, bird dog) — done together in 10 min
- Daily: Bird Dog × 10 per side as morning activation

**What to avoid during training:**
- Heavy conventional deadlifts from floor during back pain periods
- Back squat with significant axial load during flare-ups
- "Good morning" with any meaningful weight
- Any explosive movement when back is stiff/cold

## Part VIII-B: Golovinsky OLY-Specific Contributions

*Note: 12 Golovinsky YouTube OLY videos were extracted and are currently being summarized. This section will be expanded with specific cues and programming details from those videos. What follows is based on his LMS methodology (power35.ru/last_manorg) + initial video summary analysis.*

### Golovinsky's Core OLY Transfer from Powerlifting
Golovinsky holds the world record raw bench press (302.5 kg at 127 kg BW). His LMS system was built for powerlifting but the principles transfer directly to OLY:

**What transfers:**
- **Touch the competition movement every session** — even 2 sets of hang power snatch on a non-snatch day. In bench, he never goes a session without touching the bench. Same principle. The motor pattern degrades within 48–72 hours of no practice.
- **Daily max singles** — instead of prescribed 5×3 on jerk, climb to the heaviest single that feels solid. No grinding misses. This is his core intensity methodology for the main lift. Applied to jerk: the single is the session's primary achievement.
- **Accessory work is structural, not cosmetic** — every accessory exercise must have a named purpose and direct carryover. "Face pull for rotator cuff health under jerk load." Not "arms because symmetry."
- **High intensity + low volume option** — fewer heavier singles may outperform more sets at moderate load, especially for the jerk. 3–5 heavy singles beats 5×3 at 78%.
- **Phase cycling** — do not mix intensities in one week. A high volume week is a high volume week. A high intensity week is a high intensity week. Mixing causes neither adaptation to complete.

**Golovinsky OLY Cues (from video summaries — to be expanded):**
- **"Charge from the bottom"** — explosive initiation from the bottom of any position; no pause that allows momentum to dissipate. Applies to receiving position, dip of the jerk, bottom of the squat.
- **"Fixation at top"** — powerfully fix the bar and body at the receiving position; reaching it is not enough, owning it is the goal.
- **Dynamic execution priority** — if bar speed slows significantly, the load is too heavy for the current technical state; reduce immediately rather than grinding.

### Golovinsky's Intensity Blocks (LMS Applied to OLY)

**Intensification Block (2–3 weeks):**
- Main lift at 85–90% with low volume (3–5 singles)
- Followed by immediate deload if progress stalls or fatigue appears
- Do not sustain high volume indefinitely — the intensification block is short by design

**Volume Block:**
- 65–78% range, more sets, metabolic emphasis
- Builds work capacity and hypertrophy base
- Jerk: more sets at 80–85% of recent max single vs. pushing for new maxes

**Deload / Micro-Weight:**
- After any intensification block
- Full week off or 50–60% only
- Priority: hormonal reset, CNS recovery, tissue repair

## Part IX: Progression Markers — When to Advance Blocks

### Block 1 → Block 2 (Hypertrophy → Technique)
Move when ALL of the following are true:
- [ ] OHS: 60 kg or more with solid position
- [ ] Snatch: Consistent technique at 75–80% of TM
- [ ] Back pain: Stable at 🟡 or better for 2+ consecutive weeks
- [ ] 8 weeks of Block 1 completed

### Block 2 → Block 3 (Technique → Strength)
Move when:
- [ ] OHS: 65 kg or more
- [ ] Snatch: Clean bar path and receiving position at 85% TM
- [ ] Jerk: Gap to clean reduced to 10 kg or less
- [ ] 8 weeks of Block 2 completed
- [ ] Tested maxes in Block 2 deload week show progress

### Block 3 → Block 4 (Strength → Peaking)
Move when:
- [ ] ATW has risen 4%+ from start of Block 3
- [ ] PRs set in at least 2 of the 3 competition lifts
- [ ] Competition or test event within 4–8 weeks
