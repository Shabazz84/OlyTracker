# How & Why Athletes' Ability for Reps & Intensity Varies | Training Programs - Olympic Weightlifting

# Analysis for This Athlete

## Programming Principles
- **Rep ranges by lift complexity**: Higher reps tolerable in back squat (lower tech demand); snatch/clean/jerk require lower reps at high intensity (90%+) to preserve technique
- **1-3 rep range** appropriate for 90%+ intensities in technical lifts to avoid fatigue-induced technical breakdown
- **Individual variation matters**: Training history, gender, neurological efficiency, and technical proficiency all affect rep capacity at a given %
- **Technical proficiency advantage**: Advanced athletes complete more reps at high intensity on Olympic lifts due to preserved precision and lower energy waste on corrections

## Key Relevance to This Athlete
1. **OHS (50 kg) is primary snatch limiter** → Keep technical snatch work in **lower rep ranges** (1-3 reps) at high intensity to avoid compounding poor positions from fatigue
2. **Intermediate level** → Expect *fewer* reps possible on snatch/clean/jerk than guidelines predict; track data rather than assuming Prilepin's table applies
3. **Chronic back pain** → Higher reps in back squat may be better tolerated (lower tech demand) than pushing high-rep snatch work
4. **Night shift schedule** → Fatigue management critical; technical precision will degrade faster—favor lower rep schemes on technical lifts

## Immediate Implication
During 6-week hypertrophy block: prioritize **squat volume** for hypertrophy; keep snatch/clean work in moderate intensity/lower reps to build precision before strength phase.
