# Your Knees Should NOT be Caving In During the Pull!

**Relevance Assessment:** This text addresses knee valgus (inward collapse) during Olympic lifting movements—directly applicable to this athlete.

---

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- No explicit sets/reps/intensity given
- Emphasis on **deliberate, controlled tempos** (slow eccentrics, slow snatches/cleans)
- **Accessory integration**: depth work + main lifts with band cues

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Knee position**: Actively push knees outward throughout entire rep (especially deadlifts, cleans, snatches)
- **Landing/absorption**: Knees out during landing (jumps, cleans)
- **Deceleration control**: Slow eccentrics + slow snatches/cleans to reinforce knee stability

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- Depth drops/depth jumps with mini band
- Vertical jumps with mini band (knees out during jump AND landing)
- Mini band work in: poles/deadlifts/snatches/cleans (full range)
- Hip and foot strength/stability work (unspecified)

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- **Foot support**: Orthotics or supportive insoles for arch collapse (immediate + long-term training)

## 5. **Athlete-Specific Relevance**
- **High priority for this athlete**: Knee valgus correction is critical given low OHS (50 kg)—likely a stability/positioning issue, not pure strength
- **Back pain consideration**: Proper knee tracking reduces compensatory lumbar stress
- **Integrates well with hypertrophy block**: Banded accessory work + tempo training fits 6-week structure
