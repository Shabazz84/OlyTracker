# Stop Overcomplicating It - Staying Over The Bar in the Snatch & Clean

1. **Programming Principles:** The text advocates for **slow first pulls** (floor to knees), **pauses** above the knee, and **slow eccentrics** during deadlift/pull variations. It suggests using pauses between the break-off and just above the knee to ingrain a static back angle. No specific sets/reps or periodization structures are provided in this excerpt.

2. **Technique Cues:**
   *   **Static Back Angle:** Maintain approximately the same back angle from the start position until the bar passes the knees; do not lean shoulders forward further than necessary.
   *   **Hip Timing:** Only begin opening hips (bringing shoulders back) once the bar is above the knees to initiate the second pull.
   *   **Positioning:** Aim for an "evenly balanced" stance over the whole foot with shins not quite vertical and shoulder joints directly above or slightly in front of the bar at the knee height.
   *   **Avoid Early Extension:** Do not extend hips too soon, as this shifts balance back prematurely and reduces leg drive power.

3. **Exercise Recommendations:** The text recommends practicing the correct posture using any **pull variation** or **deadlift variation**, specifically utilizing pauses on the way up and slow eccentrics. It also suggests incorporating these cues into **snatches and cleans** via a slow first pull.

5. **Profile Relevance:**
   *   **Back Pain:** The emphasis on maintaining a "static back angle" and avoiding excessive forward lean or early hip extension directly addresses the athlete's chronic back pain by promoting spinal stability during the first pull.
   *   **Transitioning Athlete:** The advice to find a "solid base" for experimentation is valuable for an intermediate lifter refining their transition from powerlifting (squat/deadlift) mechanics to Olympic lifting.
   *   **Clean vs. Snatch:** The text notes that the clean has a larger margin of error, which may offer a slightly safer technical entry point for this athlete compared to the snatch while they manage back issues.
