# CATALYST ATHLETICS COACHING SYNTHESIS

## Intermediate Strength Athlete | Olympic Weightlifting Development

**Athlete Profile:**
- 102.5 kg bodyweight | BS 118 kg | Clean 80 kg | Push Jerk 65 kg | OHS 50 kg
- Primary limiter: OHS (30 kg gap to clean suggests overhead stability/mobility deficit)
- Chronic back pain | Night shift worker (Wed–Sun)
- Goal: 6-week hypertrophy block → technique/strength phase

---

## 1. CORE TRAINING PHILOSOPHY

**Five Core Principles:**

1. **Body Over Bar** – Vertical leg extension drives the lift; minimize arm tension. This reduces compensatory back strain, improves pulling consistency, and establishes efficient power transfer. For this athlete: cleaner cleans, better OHS positions.

2. **Consistent Technique Precedes Load** – Correct positioning + skill accumulation over time. Discomfort during technical refinement is normal as stabilizers adapt. The 50 kg OHS demands technical precision before load progression; hypertrophy block builds supporting strength.

3. **Stability Before Intensity** – Secure rack positions (jerk, clean) and locked overhead mechanics protect the spine and enable force transfer. Non-negotiable for chronic back pain management. Test stability drills (behind-the-neck jerks, scapular pulls) before heavy attempts.

4. **Frequency Over Single Sessions** – Consistent short/moderate sessions maintain skill and confidence better than infrequent heavy days. Night shift fatigue argues for 4–5 shorter focused sessions rather than 1–2 brutal days. Snatch/clean/jerk practice benefits from repeated exposures.

5. **Progressive Adaptation Through Periodization** – Training:recovery ratio must balance stimulus with adaptation time. Carefully manage volume during hypertrophy block; avoid accumulating fatigue that masks actual capacity. Deload weeks (50% volume reduction) allow physiological catch-up.

## 2. PROGRAMMING APPROACH

### Structure & Periodization

**6-Week Hypertrophy Block (Mesocycle):**
- **Objective**: Build leg/trunk/shoulder strength to support snatch/clean/jerk capacity
- **Volume distribution** (Prilepin-based): Week 1–3 escalate (e.g., 250–300 reps/week); Week 4 deload (150 reps); Week 5–6 reaccumulate at moderate intensity
- **Rep ranges**: Prioritize 6–8 reps (compound lifts), 10–12 reps (accessory/OHS-focused work), 15–20 reps (mobility/GPP)
- **Frequency**: 4–5 training days/week built around Wed–Sun night shift schedule

**Week Template (Example):**
- **Monday**: Snatch emphasis (technique-light loads 60–75%) + Front squat (6×4–6 @ RPE 7–8) + accessory
- **Tuesday**: Clean emphasis + Back squat (3×8 @ RPE 7) + OHS strength work (paused OHS, 5×5–6)
- **Thursday**: Jerk skill (push jerk + dips, no heavy eccentrics) + Pendlay rows + mobility
- **Saturday**: Light snatch/clean complex + Heavy front squat (3–5 reps near RPE 8) + leg work
- **Optional Sunday**: Recovery session (mobility, light technique, GPP)

**Intensity Management:**
- Snatch/Clean/Jerk: 65–85% of estimated max (avoid grinding singles during hypertrophy block)
- Squat/Deadlift: 75–85% RPE (moderate-heavy to drive strength)
- Avoid back-heavy eccentrics (re-racking heavy jerks) due to chronic pain; prioritize concentric emphasis

**Transition to Strength Phase (Weeks 7+):**
- Reduce volume 25–35%, increase intensity (80–95% singles/doubles)
- Emphasize snatch/clean/jerk frequency with reduced accessory load
- Introduce split jerk progression (once push jerk is stable at 70+ kg)

### Periodization Rationale

- **Macrocycle**: 12-week build (6-week hypertrophy → 6-week strength/technique peak) for first comp prep cycle
- **Mesocycle placement**: High-frequency, moderate-intensity hypertrophy addresses weak OHS by building shoulder/thoracic/core capacity; then strength block leverages new capacity
- **Deload strategy**: Weeks 4 and 8 (50% volume + lower intensity) allow CNS/back recovery critical for night-shift worker

## 3. KEY TECHNIQUE CUES & EXERCISE PREFERENCES

### Snatch
- **Starting position**: Shoulder blades down/tight; engage lats to prevent bar drift (addresses OHS stability)
- **First pull**: Static starts to build positional consistency
- **Extension to catch**: Finish full triple extension *before* feet move; complete pull prevents early squat entry
- **Receiving position**: Sit aggressively into full OHS depth; relax legs and lock overhead position (critical given weak 50 kg OHS)
- **Core exercise**: Snatch pulls (static start) + OHS holds + light snatch complexes (3–5 reps, 60–70%)

### Clean
