# Every Rep Counts

**Weightlifting coaching content — Quality/intent focus.**

---

**1. Programming Principles**
- Emphasis on training volume distribution: majority of work is lighter loads, so practice quality there determines heavy lift outcomes
- Every rep compounds skill development (positive or negative)

**2. Technique Cues**
- Overhead position: must be "soft" ❌ → "aggressive" ✓
- Bracing: minimal ❌ → intentional ✓
- Bar control: rushing to drop ❌ → controlled descent ✓

**3. Relevance to This Athlete**
- **High priority**: OHS only 50 kg (major snatch limiter). This principle directly applies—sloppy warm-up OHS reps (likely common with weak overhead stability) reinforce poor positions. Must enforce aggressive bracing and intentional overhead position even at light loads.
- **Back pain consideration**: Intentional bracing ties directly to spinal stability. Lazy warm-ups likely worsen pain patterns.
- **Night shift fatigue risk**: Extra motivation to stay focused when fatigued; sloppy reps compound poor recovery state.

- None provided in text.

**Key takeaway**: This athlete should treat every warm-up rep—especially OHS—as a technical practice session with aggressive positioning and bracing, not a "just moving weight" phase.
