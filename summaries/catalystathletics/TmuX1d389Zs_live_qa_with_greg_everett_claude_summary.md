# Merged Weightlifting Analysis Summary

## Key Points

**Programming & Load Management:**
- Heavy singles acceptable 1x/week after high-volume blocks; stop when "crushed"—leave 1–2 reps in tank
- Back-off weeks every 3–4 weeks essential
- Avoid excessive volume (context-dependent; 250 reps/week appropriate for this athlete, not 600)
- Wave loading (95% → back off → 98% → back off → 101%+) desensitizes mental barriers near technical limits
- Majority of training should be successful lifts accumulated near (not beyond) technical limits
- Miss frequency should be minimal (countable on hands over 8–12 weeks); frequent missing indicates programming mismatch or mental issues
- Competition warmup: light technical sets (3–5 @ 60%), then large jumps; singles over doubles at 60%+

**Recovery Hierarchy (Non-Negotiable):**
1. Sleep (prioritized above all—critical for night-shift worker)
2. Nutrition quality
3. Stress management
4. Monthly PT/chiro check-ins for chronic pain monitoring
- Training philosophy: "Stimulate, not annihilate"
- Sleep deprivation exacerbates fatigue from heavy singles

## Technique Cues

**Snatch:**
- Shoulders over bar; maintain back angle
- Missing behind: jump forward away from bar (release grip, open hands, keep arms straight, let bar roll)
- Catch on toes/losing forward: Most common cause is **jumping backward with feet landing on toes** (not pulled forward or bar swing)—check video
- **Overhead position (shoulder blades)**: Fully retract AND upwardly rotate simultaneously; squeeze upper inside edges together. Elbows neutral (bony point halfway between down/back)—avoid full internal/external rotation
- Rib flare: Lock spine extension with circumferential pressure (abs, obliques, back, diaphragm); stay tight throughout pull-to-catch
- Vertical arms: Default until ~knee, then shoulders move farther over as bar moves back slightly (arms behind vertical once bar passes knees)

**Second Pull Contact:**
- **Continuous solid contact** with legs driving, hips extending, bar rising—not wimpy brush or explosive bang
- Ideal finish: legs vertical, hips slightly hyperextended, shoulders behind hips
- **Best drill: snatch high pulls**—teaches sustained leg drive + fluid arm pull transition

**Clean/Jerk:**
- **Split jerk advantages over push jerk**: Broader base, more forgiving balance, lower elevation required, easier recovery from depth—strongly prioritize split jerk training
- Split jerk depth: Front shin ~vertical; front thigh 20–40° to floor; back knee bent, behind hip. Dip below this depth, drive bar up, lock out at that depth
- Short stepping (split): Caused by insufficient drive height/explosiveness and forward head/chest lean. **Fix**: Drive more explosively; maintain upright torso
- Missing behind (jerk): Guide bar behind, get out of the way
- Sideways pulls (split): Check front-view video; diagnose unbalanced drive, asymmetric shoulders, or foot placement. Consider **diagonal foot placement** (not just forward/backward)
- **Push press**: Valuable for jerk strength only (not snatch/clean)

**Knee Valgus (Snatch/Clean Pull):**
- Primary cause: Slack in system between first and second pull
- **Fix**: Continuous leg drive against ground start-to-finish; slow eccentric on pulls/squats to maintain alignment
- Add unilateral work: single-leg squats, lunges, step-ups, single-leg RDL, glute bridges, abduction

**Squat:**
- Combine front + back squat sets in same session
- Avoid box squats; prefer direct down positioning
- No boxes or targets in OHS (prevents sitting back, reduces tension demand)
- Squat to depth maintaining proper back extension + balance; hold 2 sec, active stretch slightly lower, progress fractionally

## Programming Principles

**Volume & Frequency:**
- Standard recommendation: 3x/week minimum (appropriate for night-shift worker—allows recovery between sessions)
- Individual variation critical: High-volume protocols work for responders only; Smolov not universal
- For tall/intermediate lifters: Experiment between high intensity/low volume + high frequency OR high volume/low frequency/moderate intensity
- Add volume gradually rather than reducing from initially high volume
- Returning athletes: Long-term training history = rapid strength regain (higher injury risk); short-term history = slower comeback

**Squat Training Approaches:**
- 6+ program options available; trial-and-error essential to match individual response
- Back-off weeks non-negotiable in hypertrophy phases

**Drill Integration:**
- Incorporate 1–2 technical primers as warm-up (not separate sessions)
- Example warm-up: 3–4 sets × 3 light snatch pulls (70%) → 3–5 sets × 3 tall snatches (empty bar) → prescribed main lift
- Keep rest <1 min; integrates technique work without adding training days

**Power Development:**
- Jump squats (full depth) or quarter-squat jumps off jerk blocks (concentric-only)
- Excludes push presses for explosiveness work

**Receiving Position Work:**
- Snatch teaching sequence: OHS → snatch balance series → snatch from hang (mid-thigh) → 6 segmented drills → starting position → pull to mid-thigh
- Power snatch to full snatch: Stop and squat it anyway even if stood up first; sit bottom 3 seconds (full count). Accumulate time/reps in bottom—no shortcuts
