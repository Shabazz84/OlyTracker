# Coaching Philosophy: Olympic Weightlifting for Long-Limbed Athletes with Back Constraints

## 1. Core Training Philosophy

**a) Leverage demands structure, not genetics excuse**
Long limbs create mechanical disadvantage in the squat—load naturally shifts posteriorly onto the back. This isn't a weakness to hide; it's a constraint that demands *specific* solutions. Train around it, not through pain.

**b) The rep-range sweet spot matters more than intensity**
Avoid both extremes: grinding heavy singles migrate load to the back; very high reps (15+) fatigue the back before the legs respond. Find and live in the rep range where **perfect technique is sustainable**—typically 3–6 reps for squats. This is where long-limbed lifters build leg strength without back penalty.

**c) Split squatting from maximal lifting**
Main squats (front/back) practice *technique and strength at correct intensity*. Accessory work *isolates and builds muscular qualities* the main lifts cannot. These are separate jobs; using one for both is inefficient.

**d) Back-sparing accessories are not "extra"**
For long-limbed athletes with back pain, belt squat and single-leg squat are *primary leg-building tools*—not luxury work. They force the legs to work harder because the back is unloaded. Light weight + perfect position = maximum quad stimulus.

**e) Clean position feeds squat quality**
Front squat under tension (especially paused) reinforces the upright torso and forward knee travel demanded in the clean rack. This carryover is bidirectional: squat work improves rack stability.

## 2. Programming Approach

**Periodization structure:**
- **Block 1 (6 weeks): Hypertrophy + Technique Foundation**
  - Primary: Pause front squat 4×4–5 @ RPE 6–7 (moderate weight, flawless position)
  - Secondary: Back squat 3×5–6 @ RPE 6 (same rep range logic)
  - Tertiary: Belt squat 3×8–10 or single-leg squat progression (BW → DB → front-rack) 3×6–8 per leg
  - Clean/jerk technique work 3–4× per week at submaximal weight; snatch basics on 2–3 days

- **Block 2 (4 weeks, post-hypertrophy): Technique & Strength**
  - Reduce squat volume; raise intensity to RPE 7–8 @ 3–5 reps
  - Maintain one accessory squat weekly (belt or single-leg)
  - Increase clean/jerk barbell work; add snatch pulls and OHS progression

**Intensity logic:**
Never chase maximum reps or singles on the main squat. The goal is *maintaining position under load*, not grinding. If form breaks, weight is too heavy for that block.

**Night shift accommodation (Wed–Sun):**
- Training days should cluster before longest work stretch or immediately after recovery day
- Prioritize compound movements when alertness is highest
- Accept reduced volume on mid-shift days; don't force intensity

## 3. Key Technique Cues & Exercise Preferences

**Pause front squat (primary squat tool):**
- Front rack enforces upright torso
- Pause at mid-depth (knees ~90°): eliminates bouncing, forces quads to sustain load
- Cue sequence: *"Toes down, hold—hold—out of the hole, knee over toe"*
- Forces lifter to push knees forward and maintain quad tension throughout

**Back squat (secondary, reinforces leg drive):**
- Tempo descent: deliberate, controlled lowering to maintain quad engagement
- Cue: *"Sit slower, stay upright, drive legs out of the bottom"* (prevent hip shoot-back and chest collapse at lockout)
- Toes slightly out, knees tracking forward, hips low but torso upright

**Belt squat (quad isolation, back-unloaded):**
- Manipulate the machine: slow eccentric, drive from the legs, keep pressure on quads throughout
- Lighter weight is *correct*—the machine removes back demand, forcing legs to work harder
- 8–10 reps per set; can accumulate fatigue safely

**Single-leg squat progression (back-sparing, leg-forcing):**
1. Bodyweight goblet or box hold
2. Dumbbell front-rack (low weight, balance focus)
3. Barbell front-rack (when control is solid)
4. Barbell back-rack (last; early loading risks "good morning" collapse)

**Snatch limitation (OHS weak at 50 kg):**
- OHS is the limiter, not the clean
- Prioritize: paused front squat (rack position) → OHS tempo work 3×5–8 at moderate weight → snatch balance → snatch pulls before full snatches
- Build mobility + quad/core stability before chasing OHS load

## 4. Recovery and Mobility Approach

**Back pain management (non-negotiable):**
- Never load the squat into pain; intensity RPE ≤7 on main lifts
- Prioritize belt squat and single-leg squat (unload the spine while building legs)
- Avoid high-rep back squats; they migrate load posteriorly and fatigue the back prematurely

**Sleep & circadian rhythm (night shift):**
- Control napping; protect one solid sleep window per week if possible
- Sleep debt accumulates; recovery blocks may underperform—expect this, don't force intensity
- Light mobility work on longest work days (Wed–Thu) to maintain position quality
