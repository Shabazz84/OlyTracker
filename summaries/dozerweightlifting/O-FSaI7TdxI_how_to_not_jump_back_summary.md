# How to not jump back

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency:** Perform "yo-yo" drills (snatch deadlift + low hang snatch) in *every* session where the snatch is programmed, prior to working sets.
*   **Sets/Reps:** Not specified; implied high repetition of the drill pattern ("over and over again") followed by standard working sets.
*   **Intensity:** Drills are likely sub-maximal focus on mechanics; working sets follow standard intensity protocols.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Primary Cue:** "Stay in the middle" (do not be forward or back).
*   **Root Cause Correction:** Prevent hips from rising too soon, which causes shoulders to shoot out over the bar and weight to shift forward.
*   **Drill Focus:** Use legs while maintaining a constant torso angle throughout the pull.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Primary Drill:** "Yo-yo snatches" (Snatch Deadlift + Low Hang Snatch).
*   **Purpose:** To teach leg usage and maintain torso angle to prevent backward jumping.

*   *None provided in this text.*

**5. Relevance to athlete profile**
*   **Back Pain:** The cue "maintain your torso angle" is critical for an athlete with chronic back pain, as it reduces shear forces caused by hips rising too early.
*   **Transitioning Athlete:** Addresses a fundamental technical flaw (jumping back) common in athletes transitioning from powerlifting to Olympic lifting.
*   **Limitation Context:** While the text focuses on the snatch pull, the principle of "staying centered" applies universally to preventing compensatory movements that could exacerbate the athlete's OHS weakness or jerk instability.
