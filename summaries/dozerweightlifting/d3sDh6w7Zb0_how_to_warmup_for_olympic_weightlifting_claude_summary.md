# How To Warmup For Olympic Weightlifting.

# Weightlifting Coaching Analysis

## Programming Principles
- **Warmup efficiency**: 10-15 min total (5 min foam roll + mobility, 5 min dynamic work, 5 min barbell)
- **Avoid static stretching pre-lift** (creates instability for Olympic lifts)
- **Dynamic/active stretching preferred** (maintains stability needed for overhead work)

## Technique Cues

**Snatch Warmup:**
- Tall snatch: 3×5 (elbows high, snap under)
- Strict press: 3×5 (standing → lean forward → bottoms-up position)
- Overhead bar work: twist to mobilize thoracic spine

**Clean/Jerk Transition:**
- Front rack mobility: PVC reverse grip curls (armpits to floor, 15-20 reps) — opens thoracic spine
- Wrist prep: side-to-side rotations (fingers forward/out/down)
- Strict press clean grip: 2-3×8 (primes front rack)

## Exercise Recommendations
- Foam roll **problem areas only** (1 min each) — avoid full-body rolling pre-workout
- Half-kneeling hip flexor bounces: 15-20 reps alternating sides
- Deep bodyweight squats + quad/hip openers
- ATG split squats: 3×5 both sides
- PVC/barbell overhead twists (warms lower back + thoracic spine)

## Recovery/Mobility Advice
- **Remove phone during warmup** (focus >compliance)
- Don't hold stretches >1 second (Olympic lifting requires stability, not laxity)
- Save **static stretching for cooldown**
- Mobility = part of training; don't overdo it pre-workout

## Athlete-Specific Relevance
- **Chronic back pain**: Overhead twists, thoracic spine mobility, and strict pressing in warmup directly address spinal mobility
- **Weak OHS (50 kg)**: Tall snatch + strict press focus will improve overhead stability and shoulder positioning
- **Push jerk only**: Front rack mobility work will improve receiving position efficiency
- **Night shift (Wed–Sun)**: 10-15 min efficient warmup critical to maximize limited training time

---
**Summary**: Focused, time-efficient warmup protocol emphasizing dynamic mobility and position-specific activation over passive stretching.
