# DozerWeightlifting Coaching Philosophy
**For Intermediate Strength Athletes Transitioning to Olympic Weightlifting**

---

## 1. CORE TRAINING PHILOSOPHY (5 Principles)

**1.1 Technique First, Intensity Second**
Poor technique, once learned, is extremely difficult to correct. An intermediate athlete with obvious technical deficits (low OHS relative to squat, push jerk only, weak snatch positioning) must prioritize position consistency and mobility restoration before heavy loading. Max-effort training without foundational positioning creates injury risk, especially with chronic back pain. Tall snatches, strict pressing, and movement-specific warm-ups lock in positioning before intensity scales.

**1.2 Individualize Technique Around Anthropometry**
There is more than one way to execute a lift successfully. Long-limbed athletes require greater bar spacing, more compression/reach in the setup, and forward-lean squat mechanics (not forced chest-up posture). The athlete's own feedback ("feels light," "felt weird") is real data—adjust cues, not the athlete.

**1.3 Frequency Over Max Intensity for Development**
Both snatch and clean appear in most sessions. High frequency (5–6x/week) with submaximal, high-quality singles maintains technical competency and neurological stimulus without excessive CNS fatigue. Max-effort training (daily heavy singles) is appropriate for elite lifters; intermediate athletes benefit more from consistent, moderate-intensity movement repetition with small loading increments.

**1.4 Load Positioning Before Load Magnitude**
Loaded mobility drills (front rack holds, overhead squat holds, rack pressure) are *training*, not prerequisites. Holding heavy iron in weak positions builds stability and teaches the body to own positions under stress. Static/passive stretching belongs post-training; pre-training mobility must preserve stability for Olympic lifts.

**1.5 Periodize Deliberately—Avoid Concurrent Overload**
Structure periodization as: (1) Hypertrophy/volume blocks with high squat frequency, then (2) reintroduce Olympic lift intensity when squat fatigue stabilizes. Do not max out squats *and* max out snatches/cleans in the same week. Strength loses 15+ kg within 2–3 days off squats—maintain minimum weekly frequency rather than clustering rest days.

## 2. PROGRAMMING APPROACH

### Structure & Frequency
- **5–6 days/week** (or 4–5 minimum, spread across available time slots)
- **Both snatch and clean in most sessions** (snatch 3x, clean 3–4x per week)
- **Friday = max-out day** (heavy singles/doubles after lighter work earlier in week)
- **Mid-week intensity**: 75–85% singles/doubles without maxing; generate fatigue for adaptation
- **Monday or Thursday**: Deload/technique-emphasis session (bar work, tempo, movement drills)

### Periodization: 6-Week Hypertrophy Block + Strength/Technique Phase

**Weeks 1–6: Hypertrophy Block**
- **Primary focus**: High-frequency squat work (front squat doubles/triples, back squat complexes, tempo squats)
- **Goal**: Build posterior chain relative to squat (118 kg back squat vs. 80 kg clean indicates anterior-dominant strength profile)
- **Olympic lift work**: Maintains frequency but moderate intensity (70–80%); emphasizes positioning and bar path
- **Accessory**: Strict press (3×5–8), push press, clean-grip SSB press (pause holds for thoracic mobility)
- **Volume tolerance**: Accumulate fatigue deliberately; expect first 7–10 days to feel heavy/slow

**Weeks 7–12: Strength/Technique Phase**
- **Squat frequency reduced** (2–3x/week)
- **Olympic lift intensity increases**: Heavy singles, small jumps (5–10 kg increments)
- **Jerk progression**: Introduce split jerk mechanics via pause jerks, snatch-grip split jerks, power splits
- **Mobility emphasis**: Continue front rack holds + overhead work; address chronic back pain through loaded neutral-spine positions

### Intensity & Volume Management

| Phase | Snatch | Clean | Jerk | Squat |
|-------|--------|-------|------|-------|
| **Hypertrophy (Weeks 1–6)** | 3×2 @ 75%, 2×1 @ 80–85% | 3×2 @ 75%, 2×1 @ 80–85% | 3×2 (push) @ 75%, 1×1 @ 80% | 5×5 @ 80%, 3×3 @ 85% |
| **Strength (Weeks 7–12)** | 2×1 @ 85%, 1×1 @ 90–95% | 2×1 @ 85%, 1×1 @ 90–95% | 2×1 (push) @ 85%, split mechanics work | 3×3 @ 85%, 2×2 @ 90% |

## 3. KEY TECHNIQUE CUES & EXERCISE PREFERENCES

### SNATCH

**Foot Pressure & Balance**
- Spread toes, create arch pressure (not heels)
- Full foot engagement prevents balance loss and forward drift
- Correct early: missing forward vs. back tells different technical stories (address cause, not symptom)

**Shoulder Rotation (Critical for OHS Limiter)**
- **Internal rotation > external rotation** for shoulder stability and bar control
- Cue: "Front side in, back gets long"—maintains shoulder integrity in catch
- External rotation causes unhinging, early drop in turnover, and disconnection from bar
- Tall snatches reinforce this position before heavy loading

**Overhead Squat Position**
- Thoracic extension (not shoulder laxity) is primary limiter for most athletes
