# ZACK TELANDER - Training w/ Dozer Ep. #7

## Key Points
*   **Squat Mechanics:** The "Chest Up" cue is counterproductive for long-limbed athletes with back pain; it forces lumbar extension under load. The preferred approach is a natural hinge ("Sit and relax") to distribute load through the hips and posterior chain, reducing shear force on the spine.
*   **Training Philosophy:** Avoid elite-level "Russian method" tactics (constant maxing out at 80-90% with no ramp-up) during an intermediate transition. This approach risks injury without building a necessary base.
*   **Jerk Mechanics:** For push jerk specialists, focus on the "spine whip" mechanism—using a wide grip and aggressive pull-down to launch the bar—rather than relying solely on arm strength.
*   **Programming Structure:** High-intensity singles/doubles are effective for technical practice but should not replace hypertrophy work during a transition block. Pure strength endurance (hybrid training) is less effective than dedicated strength work for Olympic progression.

## Technique Cues
*   **Back Squat:** **"Sit and relax"** or **"Go down, then come up."** Prioritize a deep, relaxed bottom position over forced verticality to prevent lumbar strain.
*   **Snatch/Clean (80%+):** Switch from timing drills to pure effort ("game on"). Aggressive descent is required once intensity passes the 80% threshold.
*   **Push Jerk:** Execute a **"spine whip"** with hands wide; pull the bar down aggressively to generate upward momentum before the dip and drive.

## Programming Principles
*   **Block Structure:** Do not utilize constant max-effort protocols for an intermediate athlete. Prioritize a dedicated hypertrophy block (6 weeks) to build tissue resilience before introducing high-intensity technique work.
*   **Warm-up Strategy:** Begin with empty bar squats focusing strictly on the "sit" position and hip hinge mechanics. Progress immediately to heavy singles/doubles without excessive volume accumulation in warm-ups.
*   **Exercise Selection:** Anchor the program around Back Squat (foundational), Front Squat (limiter for clean), and Push Press/Jerk variations. Incorporate Snatch-Grip Push Presses as a "fun" accessory to maintain engagement without high systemic fatigue.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The "Sit and relax" squat cue is critical for this athlete's long-limbed frame, allowing a hinge pattern that mitigates the lumbar shear forces caused by forcing verticality.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** The "spine whip" technique aligns with the specific mechanics required to maximize height in a push jerk without split footwork.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The advice warns against premature maxing (common in elite circles) which is unsuitable for an athlete needing to build a base. The focus on dedicated strength work over "hybrid" concepts ensures proper progression.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** While the text does not address shift work directly, the recommendation to vary training methods ("weird lifts," accessory variations) helps maintain psychological engagement and prevents burnout during irregular schedules.
