# OLEKSIY TOROKHTIY - Training w/ Dozer Ep. #17

## Key Points
*   **Squatting Strategy:** Heavy, slow grinding squats are not mandatory for elite performance and can compromise bar speed and recovery. If back pain or knee issues exist (as in this athlete's case), alternative pulling variations (snatch/clean pulls) can maintain strength without aggravating the spine. Avoid "pushing through" structural pain; rest is superior to training on injury.
*   **Power Jerk Utility:** Frequent Power Jerk training builds stability and confidence, directly translating to better Split Jerk mechanics if mobility allows later. It is a valid primary variation for athletes with limited ankle/hip mobility or those transitioning from powerlifting.
*   **Recovery & Fatigue Management:** Strict adherence to rest periods (90s–2min) is less effective than training by "feel." Mental fatigue and sleep deprivation (common in night shift work) significantly degrade performance regardless of physical readiness. Avoid external distractions (phones) during lifts to maintain focus.
*   **Mobility Maintenance:** Dedicated, low-intensity evening mobility sessions are critical for maintaining range of motion and injury prevention, particularly as training volume increases or age advances.

## Technique Cues
*   **Jerk Stability:** Focus on distributing an "even amount of weight in both legs" during the catch phase rather than obsessing over the step-back mechanics alone.
*   **Jerk Transition:** Keep the dip and drive short and explosive; avoid excessive depth that compromises balance or speed.
*   **Snatch Grip:** Grip width is individualistic. Do not adhere strictly to statistical norms if a wider or narrower grip improves comfort, stability, and bar path efficiency.
*   **Mental Discipline:** Eliminate distractions (e.g., mobile phones) during the lift to ensure full cognitive engagement with the movement pattern.

## Programming Principles
*   **Frequency & Volume:** Avoid high-volume heavy squatting sessions if they induce pain or slow down bar speed. Prioritize quality over quantity; 2x/day splits are elite-level and likely unsustainable for an intermediate athlete with back issues, but low-frequency heavy lifting is acceptable if mobility permits.
*   **Warm-up Protocol:** Implement a prolonged warm-up (60–90 minutes) including dynamic stretching and mobility work before the main session to prepare joints and prevent injury.
*   **Rest Periods:** Do not rely on strict timers for rest; use subjective readiness ("feel") to determine recovery, typically capping heavy sets at 2 minutes or 90 seconds for high-intensity work.
*   **Accessory Focus:** Prioritize upper back activation and mobility drills over heavy accessory loading, especially during hypertrophy blocks or when managing chronic pain.

*   **Power Jerk Constraint:** The interview explicitly supports the athlete's use of Power Jerks as a primary variation to build stability and confidence, noting it aids Split Jerk development even if mobility is currently lacking.
