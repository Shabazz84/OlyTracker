# ALEX LEE - TRAINIING w/ Dozer Ep. #3

## Key Points
*   **Foundation Before Load:** Do not max out daily. Prioritize re-organizing the body (fixing imbalances, restoring posterior connection) before aggressive loading. Heavy lifting should only resume once technical "connection" is felt.
*   **Continuous Kinetics:** Eliminate pauses between the Clean and Jerk. The movement must be a single kinetic chain; resetting breath or tension kills stored potential energy required for an efficient push jerk.
*   **Posterior Chain Dominance:** Back pain and disc issues often stem from "sneaking under" weights using only quads/anterior chains without posterior engagement. Proper foot pressure and hip activation are non-negotiable for spinal safety.
*   **Strength vs. Technique Transfer:** High raw strength (118kg squat) can mask poor technique. Relying on brute force leads to injury; technical efficiency allows heavier loads with less systemic stress.

## Technique Cues
*   **Foot Pressure & Dip:** Use the **full foot** during the jerk dip. Do not stay exclusively on heels or toes. "Slam forward" into a grounded position and immediately lock the posterior chain back. This prevents the spine from taking the load.
*   **Rotation (Catch):** Utilize **internal rotation** of the shoulders in the catch position. External rotation creates an "unhinged" state, causing front shoulder loading errors and spinal hyperextension. Internal rotation connects the upper back to the lower body, keeping the bar close and the spine long.
*   **Spinal Positioning:** Avoid excessive arching (hyperextension). Maintain a neutral or slightly rounded brace to balance anterior/posterior chains. A "pivot" where both sides of the chain load simultaneously is safer for chronic back pain than a hyperextended position.
*   **Jerk Shelf & Elbows:** Create a shelf by moving elbows slightly wider and down/out, engaging lats/upper back rather than relying on front delts. This stabilizes the bar without compromising the spine.
*   **Start Position Alignment:** Avoid crowding the bar (if long limbs) or starting too far back (prone to dumping forward). Align center of mass with the bar so shins are at 90° and tension is maintained on toes/midfoot during setup.

## Programming Principles
*   **Phased Approach:** Begin with "slow strength movements" and technical drills rather than max attempts. Re-organize the body for ~3 months before heavy loading resumes, even if peak performance can be regained quickly later.
*   **High Frequency, Low Intensity:** Train skill frequently (daily or near-daily) using light weights to build neural pathways. Reserve high intensity for when the "connection" is solidified.
*   **Specific Drills for Re-entry:**
    *   **Back Squat Thrusters:** Use for balance checks and feeling movement adjustments at the top.
    *   **Front Squats (Doubles/Triples):** Preferred over back squats for re-organization; they demand perfect bracing, preventing "ugly" compensations common in heavy back squats.
    *   **Hip Raise to Back Squat:** Teaches tension distribution between toes and heels, ensuring counterbalance is maintained.
    *   **Contact Muscle Snatch:** A technical drill for returning athletes to check rotation, brace, and hip balance without heavy loading.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain Management:** The athlete's pain likely stems from poor posterior connection or "sneaking under" loads. Implementing the neutral/rounded brace and full-foot pressure cues is critical before increasing Clean/Jerk loads.
*   **Push Jerk Specificity (No Split):** Since the athlete does not use a split jerk, maintaining kinetic energy through a continuous dip and drive is vital to absorb force without leg action. The "no pause" rule between clean and jerk is essential for this style.
*   **Night Shift & Sedentary Risks:** Night shift work often mimics sedentary desk job limitations (tight hip flexors, low back structural loss). Prioritize mobilizing hips and re-engaging posterior chains in warm-ups to avoid low back compensation during lifts.
*   **Strength Profile Adjustment:** With a 118kg squat but significantly lower Clean/Jerk numbers, the athlete risks injury by relying on raw strength. The program must focus on transferring this leg strength via "ground-up connection" rather than brute force.
