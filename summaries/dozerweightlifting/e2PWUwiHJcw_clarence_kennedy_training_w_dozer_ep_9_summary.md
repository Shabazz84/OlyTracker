# CLARENCE KENNEDY - Training w/ Dozer Ep. #9

## Key Points
*   **Frequency & Skill Acquisition:** Olympic lifts require high frequency (5–6 days/week) for skill acquisition; low-frequency training is less effective for technique development in transitioning athletes.
*   **Strength-First Periodization:** Adopt a "Squat First, Lifts Second" approach. Prioritize raw strength and work capacity via high-volume squatting phases (e.g., 10x3 or 5x5) before shifting focus to technical refinement.
*   **Recovery Dynamics:** Lower back strength is highly resilient and retains well after breaks, whereas leg strength drops rapidly (~15kg loss in 2–3 days). However, high-volume squatting combined with daily heavy Olympic lifting risks "tanking" recovery; volume must be managed carefully to avoid burnout.
*   **Mobility as a Limiter:** For heavier athletes (102.5 kg), the front rack is often the primary mobility constraint affecting overhead stability and snatch depth.

## Technique Cues
*   **Front Rack Stability:** Address OHS limitations (50 kg) and rack issues with heavy "rack holds" (e.g., 70 kg for ~30 seconds) prior to sessions to release tension and build shoulder stability.
*   **Snatch Speed:** Use bar speed as the primary technical indicator; if the bar lacks speed, the technique is flawed regardless of depth or position.
*   **Depth vs. Form:** Prioritize squat depth even if it compromises "textbook" powerlifting form, provided injury history (chronic back pain) allows for safe execution.
*   **Warm-up Efficiency:** Avoid incremental "nervous" middle weights before max attempts; transition directly from warm-ups to the target intensity to maintain focus and reduce fatigue accumulation.

## Programming Principles
*   **Phased Structure:** Execute a dedicated hypertrophy/strength block focusing on high-volume squats first, then transition to technical work once strength peaks and back resilience is established.
*   **Volume Management:** Balance high squat volume with Olympic lifting frequency; do not combine heavy daily Olympic lifting with high-volume squatting in later stages without deloading.
*   **Accessory Selection:** Incorporate weighted pull-ups (high volume/intensity blocks) to maintain posterior chain strength, particularly when Olympic lifting frequency is reduced for recovery.
*   **Footwear Strategy:** Utilize weightlifting shoes for serious sessions and heavy lifts but occasionally train barefoot or in flat shoes to build ankle stability; consistency is key to adapting to the shoe's geometry.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain & Safety:** While lower back strength is resilient, the recommended high-frequency squatting model poses a risk for an athlete with chronic pain. Implement significant deloading and prioritize mobility work before increasing volume. Do not force training if motivation wanes to prevent burnout.
*   **OHS Limiter (50 kg):** The athlete's low OHS aligns with the "front rack mobility" diagnosis; the 70kg rack hold drill is a direct intervention for this specific weakness.
*   **Night Shift & Recovery:** As a night shift worker, environmental consistency and sleep hygiene are as critical as training frequency. Inconsistent circadian rhythms can exacerbate the "tanking" effect described in recovery dynamics. Maintain a strict sleep routine to support the high-frequency demands of Olympic lifting.
*   **Transition Strategy:** The "Squat First" philosophy supports the 6-week hypertrophy goal, but the transition to technique work must be gradual to ensure back resilience matches the increased technical load.
