# Short term fatigue = Long term results

**1. Programming Principles**
- Fatigue management principle: Added exercises should meaningfully tax the athlete. If supplemental work doesn't impair main lift performance, it won't transfer to strength gains.
- Corollary: Seeking exercises that create noticeable fatigue on main lifts = better transfer potential.

**2. Technique Cues**
- None provided.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
- Isolated hamstring work emphasized as creating sufficient fatigue to impact barbell lifts (positive indicator of transfer).

**4. Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- None provided.

**5. Relevant to This Athlete**
- **Highly relevant**: Isolated hamstring strengthening could address chronic back pain (hamstring tightness commonly contributes to lumbar dysfunction). However, monitor that fatigue from hamstring work doesn't compromise lift quality or recovery on night shift schedule.
- Consider: Hamstring strength may improve snatch/clean positioning (hip extension demand), but should be timed post-session or on lighter days given back history and work fatigue.
