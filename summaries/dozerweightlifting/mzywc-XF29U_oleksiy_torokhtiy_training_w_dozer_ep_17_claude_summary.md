# MERGED SUMMARY: Olympic Weightlifting Coaching Analysis

## Key Points

- **Jerk > Clean principle**: Jerk should be 5–10 kg heavier than clean (trained under fatigue). Power jerk dominance requires maintenance work: 2–3x/week power jerk + 1–2x/week split jerk to prevent mobility loss.
- **Squat philosophy during competition prep**: Avoid heavy eccentric/slow grinds (limits recovery, reduces power output for Olympic lifts). Prioritize speed/dynamic work instead.
- **Upper back activation**: Heavy emphasis pre-session. Critical for shoulder stability and OHS development.
- **Rest periods**: 90 seconds to 2 minutes maximum; adjust by feel.
- **Training frequency**: Elite model uses 2x/day splits (AM: snatch + pulls; PM: clean & jerk + squat) allowing extended recovery between modalities. Single quality session may be more appropriate for your night shift schedule.

---

## Technique Cues

**Snatch:**
- "No feet" warm-up focus (minimal foot movement)
- Pair with deadlifts (no contact) to groove positioning

**Jerk (Power vs. Split):**
- **Power jerk**: Dip and drive shorter (avoid going too deep, losing control); pause in catch (2–3 seconds) for stability; hit floor with shoes at catch
- **Split jerk**: Even weight distribution across both legs; don't collapse into power position; pause to stabilize
- Power jerk 2–3x/week improves split jerk mobility/stability

**General:**
- Grip width varies individually—follow what works for you, not statistics
- Mental clarity critical: avoid phones during lifting

---

## Programming Principles

- **Warm-up structure**: 40–60–80–100 kg, then working sets. Alternate single deadlifts (no contact) with single snatches (minimal volume).
- **Session architecture**: 2x/day model separates modalities (snatch AM, clean & jerk PM) or single quality session per day.
- **Squat placement**: Front squat, back squat, or box squat variations during peak. Consider placing squat *between* snatch and clean/jerk to increase jerk fatigue/adaptation.
- **Mobility work**: Active shoulder work integrated *during* sessions (not just stretching). Evening mobility work separate from training.
- **Recovery protocol**: 30–60 min warm-up → stretching → session → evening mobility. Extended break (5–6 months) after competition to manage mental fatigue.
- **Injury management**: Avoid heavy loading on painful joints; use exercise selection (classic snatch, C&J) rather than surgery/complete rest.

## Athlete Relevance

| Factor | Your Profile | Application |
|--------|---|---|
| **OHS limiter (50 kg)** | Primary snatch bottleneck | Upper back activation (pre-session) + pausing in catches for shoulder stability. Mobility work is ongoing, not a quick fix. Prioritize active shoulder work. |
| **Push jerk only, no split jerk** | Technical gap | Power jerk dominance requires: (1) shorter dip/drive + pause stability drills; (2) split jerk maintenance 1–2x/week; (3) power jerk 2–3x/week to prevent mobility loss. Build confidence progressively. |
| **Chronic back pain** | Load management constraint | Avoid heavy eccentric/slow squats (increase fatigue, delay power). Use speed/dynamic squat work instead. Exercise selection > loading; manage via variation, not avoidance. |
| **Night shift (Wed–Sun)** | Recovery/scheduling constraint | Elite 2x/day model may not suit you. Focus on *one quality session/day* rather than high volume. Emphasize upper back activation and mobility integration within sessions. |
| **6-week hypertrophy block** | Your planned phase | Caution: slow grinding squats conflict with Olympic lift power output. Shift hypertrophy work to *accessory lifts* (pulls, front squat variations) at moderate tempo/volume instead. |
| **Intermediate strength** | Development stage | Don't chase heavy back squats yet. Technique and power development (snatch, C&J) first. Heavy eccentric squats delay power output. |

**Priority for you:** Upper back activation, pausing in positions for stability, integrated mobility work, and power-biased (not heavy/slow) squat variations will serve your snatch/jerk development and chronic back management better than traditional hypertrophy loading.
