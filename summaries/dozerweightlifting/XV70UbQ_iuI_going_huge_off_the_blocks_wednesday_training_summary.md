# Going huge off the blocks. Wednesday Training.

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** Train both lifts (Snatch/C&J) and Squats in the same session, multiple times per week to build a "rhythm" before specific periodization.
*   **Volume vs. Intensity:** Snatches require higher volume at lighter weights due to technical demand; C&Js require less volume but focus on strength development once technique is stable.
*   **Block Work:** Prioritize block pulls/snatches early in training phases to build confidence and allow for heavier loads than floor lifts (mental/technical benefit).
*   **Progression Strategy:** Establish a "minimum" target weight before the workout; if missed, do not push further to avoid damage. If close, attempt one more time only once a month during peak battles.
*   **Periodization:** Start with general habit-building (training frequently without specific goals) for the first month of a new cycle before introducing specific strength or technique cycles.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Block Snatches:** "Don't be patient." Unlike floor pulls, block pulls require immediate aggression; struggle is often mental ("headcase") rather than positional.
*   **Overhead Stability:** Comfort and stability in the transition/catch come from frequency of practice, not just raw strength.
*   **Front Rack (Thoracic Extension):** If the front rack is tight/locked down due to fatigue, perform a "Big Fix": 3 sets of 8 reps at ~20% of Clean & Jerk max while sitting in a squat and pressing overhead to force thoracic extension under load.
*   **Front Squat Grip:** Use straps on front squats (pulling bar deep into wrists) to establish a better rack position if the grip is weak or uncomfortable.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Block Snatches & Low Block Cleans:** Essential for overcoming mental blocks and handling heavier loads safely.
*   **Banded Dead Bugs:** Core activation warm-up (static trunk, moving hips) specifically for those with back pain history.
*   **"Big Fix" Presses:** Light weight overhead press from a squat position to wake up the thoracic spine.
*   **Strapped Front Squats:** To force deep grip and improve rack stability.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Warm-up Duration:** Limit active warm-ups to 15–20 minutes. If unable to feel ready for the bar within this window, increase mobility work outside of gym sessions.
*   **Post-Workout:** Address tightness from upper body accessory work (bench/rows) with stretching or yoga classes.
*   **Fatigue Management:** Recognize that fatigued muscles lock down; use activation drills to "wake up" the thoracic spine before heavy lifting.

*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The transcript explicitly recommends the "core active/hip moving" warm-up and banded dead bugs as a proven strategy for back pain management (referenced from 2016).
*   **Front Rack/Wrist Issues:** The athlete mentions wrist pain and tight lats; the recommended "Big Fix" press is directly applicable to opening up a locked front rack.
*   **Mental Approach:** The advice on block work being a "mental thing" is crucial for an intermediate transitioning to Olympic lifts who may struggle with confidence or timing off blocks.
*   **Training Structure:** The emphasis on building habits and frequency before specific cycles aligns well with the athlete's goal of a 6-week hypertrophy/technique block, suggesting they should focus on consistency first rather than over-analyzing deficits immediately.
