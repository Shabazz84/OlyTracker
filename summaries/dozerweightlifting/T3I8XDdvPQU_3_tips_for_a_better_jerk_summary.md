# 3 TIPS FOR A BETTER JERK

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency/Volume:** High frequency of specific lifts (Snatch doubles, Clean & Jerk triples) with a focus on "heavy fun" rather than high volume grinding.
*   **Intensity:** Based on 200kg max; working at ~85% for cleans/jerks and heavy singles/triples for squats.
*   **Periodization/Adaptability:** Emphasizes "audible" adjustments based on daily readiness. If performance is poor or fatigue is high, the recommendation is to take a rest day immediately rather than plowing through. Consistency is prioritized over hitting specific numbers on bad days.

**2. Technique Cues (Jerk Focus)**
*   **Breathing:** Do not exhale between the clean and jerk. Maintain the brace; inhale only when the bar leaves the shoulders, then re-brace immediately before the dip to ensure maximum torso stability.
*   **Rhythm:** Use a "two-whip" rhythm after standing up from the clean (adjust -> two whips -> dip/drive) to utilize oscillation and eliminate hesitation.
*   **Torso Position:** The torso must drop straight down with zero horizontal displacement; hips stay under shoulders throughout the movement.
*   **Footwork:** Feet should move equal distances (front and back). Think of both feet hitting the floor simultaneously (even if the back foot technically lands first) to prevent "donkey kicking" or pushing the torso forward.
*   **Movement Efficiency:** Feet should slide against the floor, not be picked up and stomped down; minimize vertical movement to save time.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Snatch:** Heavy doubles (as opener).
*   **Clean & Jerk:** Two clean sets at 85% followed by heavy jerks.
*   **Back Squat:** Heavy triple or moderate volume with no drop sets.
*   **Drills:** Floor line drills for jerk footwork (toeing up and dropping to check equal distance) without weight.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Rest Days:** If training is terrible due to stress, sleep issues, or overtraining, take a full rest day immediately. This prevents digging a deeper hole and preserves long-term consistency.
*   **Injury Management:** Stop immediately if an acute injury (e.g., torn hand) occurs; do not train through it to avoid worsening the condition before a meet.
*   **Mental Reset:** Acknowledge that "bad days" happen; resetting mentally is as important as physical recovery.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The emphasis on maintaining a rigid brace and not exhaling between lifts directly addresses spinal stability, which is crucial for an athlete with back pain.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The "two-whip" rhythm cue helps standardize the transition from clean to jerk, reducing inconsistency common in athletes new to Olympic lifting.
*   **Night Shift Worker (Wed–Sun):** The advice to take a rest day when feeling "beaten down" or recovering poorly is critical for shift workers whose sleep cycles may be disrupted; it validates listening to the body over a rigid schedule.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** The detailed footwork and torso stability cues apply perfectly to the push jerk, specifically regarding keeping the torso upright and moving feet equally without horizontal drift.
