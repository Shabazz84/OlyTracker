# Coaching Philosophy: Max Aita Methodology for the Intermediate Weightlifter

## 1. Core Training Philosophy

**Principle 1: Exercise Selection is Fault-Driven, Not Random**
Don't chase one magic exercise per problem. Identify the root cause (e.g., weak back vs. weak legs vs. bar-path breakdown), then build a pool of 10–15 similar-but-varied movements that probabilistically address it. Prioritize by overlap—movements that appear across multiple diagnostic categories rank first.

**Principle 2: Variability Over Repetition**
Repeating the same movement from the same position teaches you to be good at that one thing. Rotate through close variations (pause above/at/below knee, tempo, no-feet, no-contact) to groove corrections under different conditions and transfer them to the full lift. This is how skill actually sticks.

**Principle 3: Sequencing Matters—Corrective → Specific**
Within a mesocycle, front-load the heaviest corrective constraints (pauses, tempo, complexes, no-feet). As weeks progress, shed constraints and move toward the classic lift. By the end, you're doing heavy, simple, specific work. This is the opposite of "throw in a drill at the end"—it's the spine of your structure.

**Principle 4: The Minimum Exercise List is Non-Negotiable**
- **Snatch:** classic snatch + power snatch + overhead strength (OHS or snatch balance)
- **Clean:** classic clean + power clean + front squat
- **Jerk:** power jerk + push press

Everything else is accessory. Everything else is corrective. Don't add exercises without a diagnosed fault.

**Principle 5: Self-Diagnosis is a Skill**
Film yourself, identify *where* the lift breaks (first pull, knee, turnover, lockout), hypothesize *why* (strength weakness, positional loss, timing), then test the hypothesis with corrective variations. This closes the loop between training and improvement.

## 2. Programming Approach

**Periodization Structure:**
- **Block 1 (Weeks 1–6): Hypertrophy** — Higher volume, moderate intensity, accumulate movement variability and positional work. Use corrective variations heavily; build work capacity for future blocks.
- **Block 2 (Weeks 7–12): Technique Consolidation & Strength** — Reduce volume, increase intensity, sequence corrective → specific. Primary movements (classic snatch, clean, jerk) dominate; accessories are targeted to observed faults on video.
- **Ongoing:** Film weekly, update the corrective pool based on what you see.

**Weekly Structure:**
- Minimum 3 sessions/week for technical sports; 4–5 ideal for an intermediate transitioning to weightlifting.
- Each session includes one primary lift (snatch, clean, or jerk) with its minimum-list companions, plus 1–2 sequenced correctives.
- Accessories are fault-driven and rotated (e.g., Week 1: pause-below knee; Week 2: tempo; Week 3: no-feet; Week 4: back to classic).

**Intensity:**
- Block 1 (Hypertrophy): 60–75% of 1RM, higher reps/sets, focus on movement quality.
- Block 2 (Strength): 75–90% of 1RM, lower reps, priority on the classic lift and heavy push press/front squat.

## 3. Key Technique Cues & Exercise Preferences

**Minimum Exercise List (Already Validated for This Athlete):**
- **Snatch:** Classic snatch, power snatch, OHS (or snatch balance—OHS is the stated limiter, so OHS it is).
- **Clean:** Classic clean, power clean, front squat.
- **Jerk:** Power jerk, push press (no split jerk yet; power + push press builds drive and lockout strength without split-specific demands).

**Corrective Toolbox (Fault-Driven):**
| Fault | Phase | Correctives (ranked) |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| Jumping forward | Snatch finish | No-feet snatch → pull-to-knee + snatch complex → tempo snatch |
| Bar swinging out | Turnover | No-contact → pause-above-knee → power snatch |
| Back weak at knee | 1st/2nd pull | Tempo snatch (1–2 sec pull) → pause-at-knee → front squat |
| Legs weak under load | Receiving | Front squat (heavy) → pause-below-knee → clean from blocks |
| OHS collapse | Overhead | OHS (heavy + pauses) → snatch balance → push press |

**Self-Review Diagnostic:**
When filming, ask:
1. Where does the lift break? (First pull / knee / turnover / receiving / lockout)
2. Is it timing, positional (bar path), or strength?
3. Is this a back-weakness issue (can't hold position) or a leg-weakness issue (can't stay upright)?

## 4. Recovery & Mobility Approach

**Chronic Back Pain Considerations:**
- Prioritize front-loaded strength work (front squat, push press) over back-dominant loading early in blocks.
- Use pauses and tempo lifts to reduce ballistic stress; they teach position without impact.
- No-feet and no-contact variations reduce reactive spinal load compared to full snatches—use these heavily in corrective phases.
- OHS, despite being technically difficult, is a *strengthening* tool for spinal stability; don't avoid it, but progress it carefully (start light, use pauses).

**Night-Shift Adjustment:**
- Prioritize sleep quality over volume. One consistent, well-structured session is better than two scattered ones.
- Train when alertness is highest (early in your sleep window if possible; consistency matters more than timing).
