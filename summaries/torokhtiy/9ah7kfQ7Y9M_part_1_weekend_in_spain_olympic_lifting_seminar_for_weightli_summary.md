# Part 1: Weekend In Spain / olympic lifting seminar for weightlifters &  fitness athletes / Torokhtiy

The text is a Russian-language transcript of a weightlifting seminar by Alex Torokhtiy focusing on the snatch technique (specifically the "snatch in squat" vs. "snatch in standing"). It does not contain specific programming, recovery advice, or mobility protocols tailored to your profile; it is purely technical instruction.

**1. Programming principles:** None mentioned.
**2. Technique cues for Snatch:**
*   **Snatch in Squat (not Standing):** Essential for lifting heavy weights; allows catching the bar at a lower height, adding ~20-25% to potential lift.
*   **Shoulder Position:** Keep shoulders "free" and actively squeeze shoulder blades together when leaning the chest forward for stability.
*   **Bar Control:** The goal is a specific "vibration/sound" upon catch; this indicates proper impulse transfer. Avoid over-exerting energy during warm-ups (save it for heavy lifts).
*   **Footwork/Connection:** Stay on the full foot during the pull (do not rise onto toes prematurely); imagine glue on the soles of your feet to prevent lifting them too early.
*   **Descent:** Land on the full foot with a distinct "impact" sound; if silent, the landing is incorrect. Move down quickly after the pull.
**3. Exercise recommendations:**
*   Snatch from hang (above and below knees).
*   Snatch pulls with pauses.
**4. Recovery or mobility advice:** None mentioned.
**5. Relevance to athlete profile:**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly warns against "CrossFit style" snatching where athletes lift the hips first while the bar is on the floor, engaging only the lower back (lumbar) rather than the legs. This specific error must be avoided by your athlete.
*   **Transition to Olympic Lifting:** Emphasizes that beginners often waste energy in warm-ups; advises conserving energy for heavy lifts.
*   **Snatch Limiter (OHS):** The text argues that catching lower (in a squat) is the key to lifting more weight, which indirectly addresses your OHS limitation by suggesting technique over pure overhead strength for now.
