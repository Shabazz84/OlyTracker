# Technique: SNATCH PULL / A.TOROKHTIY

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Intensity:** Snatch Pulls should generally stay at or below 100% of the athlete's max pull (unlike Clean Pulls which can exceed 100%).
*   **Execution:** Focus on developing speed and amplitude rather than maximal load.
*   **Progression:** Master the movement *without* rising onto toes first; add toe elevation only after technique is solid to increase amplitude/altitude.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Grip Width:** Determine via a 90° PVC angle test (one hand parallel, one straight); project this width from the pubic area to the barbell. Note: Changing grip by >1cm significantly alters technique.
*   **Starting Position:** Knees pushed out; torso positioned *between* legs (sit deeper on thighs, not just hips back) to achieve a lower start.
*   **Angles:** Maintain a constant angle between shoulder, knee, and barbell throughout the pull ("keep the angle"). Avoid "pelvis flying out" or leaning back early.
*   **Bar Path:** Bar should move slightly *toward* the athlete during the explosion; do not move toward the bar to hit it. Keep knees slightly ahead of elbows at start.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Snatch Pulls (from floor):** Primary focus for angle control and speed.
*   **Snatch Pull from Platform:** Recommended variation.
*   **Hang Snatch Pull:** Recommended variation to ensure bar flies up in a straight amplitude with minimal unnecessary movement.

**5. Relevance to athlete's profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text emphasizes keeping the lower back tightened and maintaining a straight line from shoulder-knee-bar at the start. It warns against the pelvis "flying out," which is a common cause of lumbar shear in lifters with back issues.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The detailed grip width tutorial (90° PVC test) is crucial for an athlete transitioning to Olympic lifting, as improper grip width is a primary technical limiter.
*   **Jerk Limitations:** While the text focuses on Snatch Pulls, the emphasis on "explosion" and standing on the full foot without cutting off the base of support applies directly to improving the Push Jerk stability required for this athlete's specific goal.
