# Back SQUAT / A.TOROKHTIY (weightlifting)

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** 2–3 times per week.
*   **Volume/Intensity Periodization:**
    *   *Preparatory Phase:* High volume (4–6 sets), moderate intensity, focus on total work.
    *   *Pre-competition Phase:* Reduced volume (1–3 sets), higher intensity, focus on maximal strength ("breaking through").
*   **Tempo:** Slow eccentric (descent) to engage all muscle fibers and prevent "dropping" into the hole; explosive concentric (ascent).
*   **Breathing:** Inhale before descent, exhale after standing up (repeat cycle per rep) rather than holding breath for multiple reps.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Start Position:** After unloading from racks, retract hips back slightly and bend knees to create a slight forward torso lean. This mimics the starting position of the pull and trains spinal maintenance under load.
*   **Knee Tracking:** Keep knees "covered" by shoulders; do not let them drift forward excessively. Spread knees outward laterally during descent to ensure even load distribution and hip engagement.
*   **Descent Control:** Do not drop or crash into the bottom. Maintain a slight forward lean throughout the squat to simulate the pull mechanics.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Movement:** Back Squat (on shoulders) or Front Squat (on chest), alternating weekly.
*   **Footwear:** Use shoes with heels; if using flat shoes, place a plate under the heel to mimic heel height and reduce ankle mobility demands.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Equipment Warning:** Avoid weightlifting belts ("belt is like a death sentence" for core stability) as they cause muscle atrophy in the core zone.
*   **Knee Sleeves:** Express skepticism regarding commercial sleeves (plastic/metal inserts); suggests they restrict blood flow and are too constricting. Recommends traditional knee wraps if support is needed, or no support at all.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The specific cue to "retract hips slightly" and maintain a forward lean *before* descending directly addresses spinal loading mechanics for athletes with back issues. It trains the athlete to keep the torso angle stable, reducing shear force on the lumbar spine during the descent.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The emphasis on "slow descent, explosive ascent" builds the specific neuromuscular control required to transition from powerlifting (where dropping is common) to Olympic lifting (where starting position stability is critical).
*   **Night Shift Worker:** The advice to breathe rhythmically per rep rather than holding breath for multiple reps may help manage intra-abdominal pressure and fatigue management during potentially lower-energy night shifts.
*   **Limiters:** While the text focuses on squats, the "slow descent" technique is crucial for an athlete with a 50kg OHS limit, as it builds tendon stiffness and control needed to stabilize overhead positions without collapsing.
