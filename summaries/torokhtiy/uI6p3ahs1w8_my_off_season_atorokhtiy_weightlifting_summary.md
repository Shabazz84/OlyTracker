# My Off-season / A.TOROKHTIY (weightlifting)

The text is a general coaching transcript focused on off-season recovery, mobility, and low-intensity technique work for an experienced lifter with shoulder/elbow issues. It does not contain specific programming or advice tailored to the intermediate athlete's profile (102.5 kg, back pain, night shift, push-jerk only).

However, **relevant general principles** from the text that apply to this athlete are:

1.  **Programming Principles:**
    *   **Volume/Intensity:** High volume of low-intensity work (3–4 sets, 8–10 reps) at **50–70% max**.
    *   **Frequency:** Daily training is maintained during the off-season ("carnival"), but with reduced load.
    *   **Structure:** Alternate between squat-focused days and pull/jerk-focused days; use complexes (e.g., Squat + Push Jerk) to circulate blood without heavy loading.

2.  **Technique Cues:**
    *   Focus on "clean" technique at low weights to maintain motor patterns without joint stress.
    *   Use "snatch pulls from the hang" or "squat cleans" as high-repetition technical drills (3 reps x 3 sets).

3.  **Exercise Recommendations:**
    *   **Warm-up:** Resistance band work for shoulder health; dynamic stretching.
    *   **Back Health:** Hyperextensions (3–4 sets of 8–10) and "tilts" with a barbell to keep the spine toned.
    *   **Cardio:** 2km run or similar low-intensity cardio pre-workout if gym access allows, to improve circulation.

4.  **Recovery/Mobility Advice:**
    *   **Supplements:** Joint support supplements (specifically "Animal Flex" and "Arthro Complex") for ligament/joint health.
    *   **Therapeutics:** Localized injections (e.g., "Plasma," "Traumeel") for acute pain points; 7–10 massage sessions per week.
    *   **Mobility:** Dedicated stretching sessions (like gymnasts) targeting problem areas (hips, back, shoulders) before and after training.

5.  **Relevance to Athlete Profile:**
    *   **Back Pain:** The emphasis on hyperextensions, daily mobility, and avoiding heavy loads directly addresses the chronic back pain.
    *   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The strategy of "training more often but with less load" is ideal for a night-shift worker needing consistent movement without high systemic fatigue that disrupts sleep cycles.
    *   **Shoulder Health:** The specific recommendation to use resistance bands for shoulder stability is crucial given the athlete's history and potential overhead limitations (OHS 50kg).
