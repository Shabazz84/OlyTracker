# Complex for the SNATCH / Рывковый комплекс - A.TOROKHTIY

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Structure:** Complex training sequence: Snatch Pulls (no dip) → Snatch Drop (dynamic movement under bar) → Overhead Squat.
*   **Reps/Intensity:** Start with 4 sets of 2 reps per exercise; progress to 2 sets of 3 as weight increases. Emphasize "slow and smooth" execution for pulls, accelerating only at the transition point.
*   **Frequency:** Suggested as a supplementary drill within a session (e.g., after main lifts), not necessarily a standalone daily routine given the chronic pain context.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Snatch Pulls:** Keep knees wide, shoulders over bar, hips back; maintain this angle until the "dip" zone without actually dipping. Accelerate with arms and back only at the transition point (no explosive dip). Arms must be relaxed ("like ropes"), not gripping tight.
*   **Snatch Drop:** Simultaneously press bar up from shoulders while dropping under it. Hips back, knees wide, bar behind head. Squeeze shoulder blades together to create a stable "shelf" and lockout. Avoid elbows pointing straight up; rotate slightly to engage lats for better fixation.
*   **Overhead Squat:** Balance is key. If shoulders/elbows aren't aligned, the bar shifts. The body automatically adjusts posture to catch the weight. Focus on stability rather than depth initially.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Snatch Pulls (No Dip):** To practice the pull path and acceleration without joint stress from a dip.
*   **Snatch Drops:** Dynamic movement under the bar to improve speed under load and hip mobility.
*   **Overhead Squats:** Used as a finishing drill to lock in balance, shoulder stability, and core fixation. Can be done immediately after snatch attempts or as a standalone accessory.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Mobility Focus:** Hip extension (hips back), knee abduction (knees wide), and thoracic/shoulder mobility for the "shelf" position.
*   **Relaxation:** Explicitly instructed to keep arms loose ("ropes") during the pull phase to prevent tension buildup that hinders speed.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly recommends doing pulls *without a dip* (no explosive drop) and moving "slowly/smoothly" to reduce spinal shear forces, which is highly relevant for back pain management.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** While the text focuses on Snatch technique, the cue to "squeeze shoulder blades" and create a stable shelf directly addresses the need for stability in a push jerk (which relies heavily on overhead lockout).
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The progression from 2 reps to 3 reps with increased weight suggests a methodical approach suitable for an athlete building a base before heavy loading.
*   **Note on Night Shifts:** The text does not address sleep or recovery scheduling, which is critical given the athlete's night shift work and chronic pain; this must be managed externally by the coach.
