## 1. **TECHNIQUE CUES FOR SNATCH** (Primary focus)

**Grip Setup:**
- Standard snatch grip: one arm parallel to floor, other at 90° angle overhead
- Bar should lower to navel level
- Individual variations acceptable once fundamentals learned

**Key Phases:**

| Phase | Cue |
|-------|-----|
| **Starting Position** | Hips back, knees out (not forward), shoulders cover bar/knee, full foot contact, feet shoulder-width, toes angled out |
| **Pull (Traction)** | Drag bar along legs, shoulders staying over bar, maintain knee-out position |
| **Extension/Drive (Подрыв)** | "Most important element for heavy weights" — push with legs, finish with hips/thighs, arm pull secondary |
| **Bar Path** | Pull elbows high, bar to chest, then rotate/press overhead |
| **Overhead Squat** | Receive with knees out, hips back, full squat position, shoulders locked overhead |
| **Lockout Cue** | Retract scapulae, elevate shoulders, elbows fully extended |

**Common Errors:**
- Knees caving inward ❌
- Knees moving ahead of elbows (poor hip mobility signal) ❌
- Hips rising too early ❌

---

## 2. **EXERCISE PROGRESSION** (Highly relevant)

Author recommends **learning snatch via progression exercises BEFORE full barbell snatch:**
1. Press from shoulders → lockout position
2. Overhead squat (full depth)
3. Pull to chest + arm turnover drill
4. Drive from rack position (bar in "pocket"/high pull position)
5. Full snatch pulls (bar returns to floor)
6. Complete snatch

## 3. **SPECIFIC TO THIS ATHLETE**

⚠️ **HIGHLY RELEVANT:**
- **OHS 50 kg limiter** → This video's focus on overhead squat depth & stability directly addresses this
- **Chronic back pain** → Cues emphasize: "hips back" (not spinal compression), "shoulders cover bar" (vertical loading), knees-out mechanics (reduces lumbar shear)
- **Push jerk only** → Snatch footwork/drive patterns transfer; overhead stability work applies

**Recommendation for 6-week hyp block:**
- Prioritize overhead squat progressions (light weight, high reps) to build OHS capacity
- Include snatch pull variants (no transition) for leg drive work
- Use rack positions to isolate pull/extension without spinal load from floor
