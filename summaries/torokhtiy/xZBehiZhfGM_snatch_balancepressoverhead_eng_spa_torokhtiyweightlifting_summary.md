# SNATCH Balance/Press/Overhead [ENG SPA] Рывковый комплекс/TOROKHTIY(weightlifting)

The text is a Russian coaching transcript focused on Olympic weightlifting accessory exercises (Snatch Balance, Snatch Press, Overhead Squat) and overcoming psychological barriers to heavier weights. It does not explicitly address the specific athlete's profile (night shifts, back pain, push-jerk limitation), but contains highly relevant technical principles for their goals.

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Intensity:** Start at 70% of max Snatch when introducing new movements; progress to 50% for technique/flexibility work once a foundation is built. For overcoming fear, use 105–110% of current max (overhead holds).
*   **Sets/Reps:** Structure as complexes: 2x Snatch Press + 2x Snatch Balance + 2x Overhead Squat. Later stages reduce to single reps at higher intensities (up to 90-100%) after the initial warm-up sets.
*   **Frequency:** High frequency is acceptable due to rapid recovery; can be used more than once a month, even weekly for specific phases.
*   **Progression:** Begin with deep squats to build flexibility/fundamentals, then transition to shorter squat depths as weight increases to conserve energy.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Hand/Wrist Locking:** Do not rely on finger strength alone. "Lock" the wrist by turning elbows forward (palms facing up/forward) so the bones form a rigid chain capable of holding heavy loads without bending fingers.
*   **Foot Placement:** Feet should be set at the width used during the jump after the pull. Knees must flare outward; toes point slightly out, not parallel. This allows the torso to fit between the legs in the catch position.
*   **Elbow Position:** Actively turn elbows forward *before* dropping under the bar. This engages shoulders and stabilizes the overhead lock.
*   **Drop Safety:** When dropping the bar from overhead, keep arms straight until the very last moment; do not bend elbows during the drop to prevent injury.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Focus:** Snatch Balance (Snatch Drop) as the core accessory for flexibility and catch stability.
*   **Complex Structure:** Combine Snatch Press (from shoulders), Snatch Balance, and Overhead Squat in one session.
*   **Progression Tool:** Use a bench or blocks to elevate the starting position of the overhead squat if full depth is not yet achievable; gradually lower the height as flexibility improves.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Flexibility Focus:** The primary goal of these exercises is improving mobility in the "catch" (squat) position.
*   **Warm-up:** Perform light pressing movements first to coordinate the movement and feel the squat depth before loading heavily.
*   **Safety Check:** Do not attempt deep squats with heavy weight unless you are 100% confident in your overhead stability; otherwise, the bar will force you forward or backward.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** The text explicitly targets Overhead Squat depth and flexibility as a primary solution for athletes who cannot "fit" their body under the bar, directly addressing the 50kg OHS ceiling.
*   **Fear of New Weights:** Suggests using overhead holds at 105-110% to build psychological confidence before attempting heavier cleans/snatches.
*   **Back Pain/Chronic Issues:** The emphasis on "locking" elbows and keeping arms straight during drops is critical for spinal safety, though the athlete must ensure their specific back pain does not contraindicate heavy overhead loading without medical clearance.
*   **Split Jerk Absence:** While the text focuses on Snatch techniques, the principles of overhead stability (elbow rotation, wrist locking) apply universally to any overhead movement, including the Push Jerk.
