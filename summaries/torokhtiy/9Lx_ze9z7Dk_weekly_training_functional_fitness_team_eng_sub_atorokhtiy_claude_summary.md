## **EXTRACTED ANALYSIS FOR THIS ATHLETE:**

### **1. Programming Principles**
- **Front squat with straps** → push jerk → full push jerk progression
- **Volume approach:** 3–4 sets with empty bar, then loaded (emphasis on rep quality over intensity)
- **2-second pause/hold at lockout** for each rep initially, then add dynamics
- Progressive complexity: full squat jerk → half squat jerk → short dip jerk

### **2. Technique Cues (PUSH JERK)**

**Receiving Position (Dip):**
- Knees ~90° max, NO forward collapse
- **Feet angle:** CRITICAL—toes out, load on all 5 toes (NOT pigeon-toed)
- Torso upright; rear glute/hamstring engagement ("as if pregnant")
- Wide stance > narrow (stability > complexity)

**Drive & Leg Mechanics:**
- Drive primarily with legs, NOT upper body
- Keep torso angle constant throughout (minimal forward lean)
- Simultaneous hip + knee extension
- Leg spread timing: spread legs while pressing up, NOT after

**Split Jerk Alternative Mentioned:**
- Coach prefers **split jerk (scissors)** for stronger progression
- Athlete currently does **push jerk only** → this aligns with coach's recommendation to eventually train split jerk for heavier loads

**Full vs. Short Dip:**
- Full squat jerk = deeper catch, easier load distribution
- Short dip jerk = uses bar whip/elasticity, faster, less joint demand

### **3. Exercise Recommendations**
- **Front squat (strapped)** – prerequisite for proper dip mechanics & shoulder mobility
- **Push press variations** – emphasize hip drive over upper body
- **Short dip drills** – isolate leg power without full squat demand

### **4. Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- **Shoulder mobility critical:** "If you can't press from low squat position, develop this—you won't press properly otherwise"
- Mobility work before attempting loaded jerk
