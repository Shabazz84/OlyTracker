# How to lift / Episode #2: Squat Clean / Clean Pull / Back Squat training session

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Structure:** Warm-up (PVC stretches, shoulder mobility) → Main Lifts (Deadlift variants, Pulling variations, Push Jerk to 65-70% of max jerk) → Accessory/Drills (Hyperextensions, Lunges, Elbow rotations).
*   **Intensity:** Primary focus on technique at low-to-moderate intensity (50-70%). Heavy loads (>80%) are used sparingly for specific drills.
*   **Reps/Sets:** Low volume per movement (e.g., 2x3 reps, 1 rep before snatch/clean pulls). High repetition of sub-maximal movements to build motor patterns.
*   **Progression:** Intensity increases gradually over weeks; early weeks focus on "small weights" to fix mechanics before adding load.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Snatch/Clean Pulls:** "Pull up with shoulders" (reach toward ears) rather than just lifting arms; keep elbows high and active; do not let the bar drift forward or back.
*   **Posture:** Maintain a flat, neutral spine ("imagine a PVC pipe inside you"); avoid leaning the torso back during the dip/shrug phase.
*   **Legs vs. Arms:** "Forget arm strength"; arms are like "ropes" (relaxed) used only for holding; power must come from legs and hips.
*   **Dip/Drive:** Knees out, hips back; explosive drive ("push hard") followed immediately by a high pull; do not use arms to pull the bar up after the initial drive.
*   **Receiving Position (Jerk):** Toes of front/back foot turned inward; lunge angle <90 degrees.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Warm-up:** PVC elbow stretches, floor shoulder stretches, Hyperextensions (2x8), Lunges (bodyweight or empty bar).
*   **Main Lifts:** Deadlift variants (Deadlift to Clean pull from hang/floor), Push Jerk (65-70%), Snatch Pulls.
*   **Drills/Accessories:** "Snatch/Clean Pulls" following a single dead rep, High pulls with contact points, Elbow rotation drills (seated or standing).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Mobility:** Prioritize shoulder and elbow mobility via PVC stretching before lifting.
*   **Spine Health:** Emphasis on keeping the back neutral; if shoulders feel "off," rest and focus on pulling/leg work instead of overhead pressing.
*   **Load Management:** Build movements with small weights first to ensure proper mechanics before attempting heavy loads (which can cause injury if form is flawed).

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly warns against "leaning back" during the dip and emphasizes a flat spine ("PVC pipe") to protect the lower back. It advises resting if shoulder/back pain occurs rather than pushing through.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The coach identifies common errors for athletes with strong arms (like this athlete likely has given the 102kg squat) who rely on arms instead of legs, advising them to "forget arm strength" and use legs more aggressively.
*   **Jerk Technique:** Specific cues for the push jerk (toes inward, <90-degree lunge angle) align with the athlete's current limitation of using only a push jerk.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The text emphasizes "working slowly" and not rushing to fix errors, which is crucial for an athlete with fatigue management needs due to night shifts.
