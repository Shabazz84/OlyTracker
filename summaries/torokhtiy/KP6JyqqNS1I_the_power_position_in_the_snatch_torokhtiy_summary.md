# The Power Position In the Snatch / Torokhtiy

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Structure:** The text advocates for **progressive skill acquisition** (breaking complex movements into isolated components) rather than standard linear periodization sets/reps.
*   **Methodology:** Use "step-by-step" drills: start with PVC/bars without arms, add arm action later, then integrate leg drive.
*   **Intensity/Frequency:** High focus on **low-to-moderate loads** (using discs for feedback) to master trajectory and timing before adding weight. The session is a single technical block focusing on specific error correction (leaning back).

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Snatch/Clean Start:** Shoulders must cover the knees at the start; maintain this "shoulder-over-knee" position as long as possible during the pull to maximize leg drive potential.
*   **The Pull:** Avoid leaning back early ("fake opening"). Keep shoulders in front of knees until full extension is initiated by legs, not arms.
*   **Extension:** Straighten legs completely; do not use arms to initiate the lift. Arms should only bend/elbows-up *after* contact with the barbell during the pull.
*   **Receiving (Drop Under):** Do not "hang in the air" waiting for the bar. Drop under the barbell as fast as possible into a full squat, not a half-squat.
*   **Balance:** Use a slight backward inclination *only* at the very end of the pull to stabilize center of gravity and add final speed; do not start with this angle or you lose leverage for leg drive.

**3. Exercise Recommendations (from text)**
*   **Muscle Snatch (No Power Position):** To isolate shoulder/knee coverage.
*   **PVC/Barbell Leg Extensions:** 1-2-3 extension drills without arms to feel the "shoulder over knee" position and full leg straightening.
*   **Hit Drills (5 hits):** Static hold at power position, then "contact and elbows up" to train arm activeness after the strike.
*   **Snatch Balance:** From toes or dip-and-drive, focusing on speed of dropping under into a *full* squat without bouncing.
*   **Deadlift + Snatch/Clean Combo:** One slow deadlift (to feel angle) followed immediately by one hang snatch/clean to transfer the "shoulder over knee" inclination.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, 102.5kg, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Warning:** The text explicitly warns against "leaning back" early or "opening up" too soon, as this creates a "fake" movement that wastes time and can strain the lower back by removing leg drive leverage. *Correction:* Maintaining shoulders over knees protects the lumbar spine during the pull phase.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The text emphasizes "slowly down, faster up" and relaxation of shoulders/scapula to reduce unnecessary tension. This is crucial for a night-shift worker who may have accumulated fatigue; focusing on relaxation prevents compensatory muscle tightness.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** While the text focuses heavily on Snatch/Clean mechanics (pulling under, leg drive), these principles apply directly to the athlete's **Jerk**. The "full squat" receiving and rapid drop-under are essential for a push jerk to ensure stability before the dip/drive.
*   **OHS Limiter:** The emphasis on "shoulders covering knees" and full leg extension is critical for an athlete with a 50kg OHS; it ensures the back is not over-relied upon for overhead stability, shifting load to the legs/core.
