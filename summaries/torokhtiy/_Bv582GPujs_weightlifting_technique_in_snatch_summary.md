# Weightlifting - technique in snatch. Техника в РЫВКЕ / А.ТОРОХТИЙ

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency:** High repetition of specific movements (start pull, high pull, jump drills) within a single session to build muscle memory.
*   **Intensity:** Start with light loads (30 kg barbell mentioned) or empty bar; focus on speed and technique over load.
*   **Sets/Reps:** Not explicitly defined as sets/reps, but emphasizes "slowly" removing the weight after high pulls and repeating the movement without hesitation ("do not think").
*   **Periodization:** Implicitly suggests a technical block focused on drilling specific phases (start, pull, turnover) before combining them.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Snatch Grip:** Establish a 90° angle between the straight arm and the bar; grip width is determined by how high you can pull with one hand while keeping the other straight.
*   **Stance & Knees:** Feet shoulder-width apart, toes pointed out ~15°. Knees must track outward in the direction of the toes during the squat and pull.
*   **Posture:** Hips back (not just down), slight lumbar arch ("prolapse" the lower back) to find a center of gravity; shoulders must cover the barbell at the start.
*   **The Pull/Second Pull:** Keep knees out, hips back, and pull the bar close to the body. The power comes from extending legs and torso (hinge at hips), not lifting with arms alone.
*   **Turnover:** Elbows drive up; imagine showing the barbell direction with elbows. Do not rush to "catch" or chase the bar; let the bar come to you.
*   **Footwork Error:** Keep weight on the **full foot** (flat) as long as possible during the pull. Do not rise onto toes early, as this kills leg power transfer.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Barbell High Pulls/Jumps:** With relaxed arms, jump up while pulling elbows high to simulate the second pull without catching.
*   **Snatch Balance (High Pull variation):** Start with bar already moving upward from a slight dip; jump and let the bar float down to catch it, emphasizing the "float" time on full feet.
*   **Overhead Squat/Press Drill:** 30 kg overhead press/pull-up motion focusing on shoulder mobility and locking out.
*   **Single-Arm Pull Test:** One hand straight up, one pulling max height to determine grip width.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Shoulder Warm-up:** Essential before any snatch work; specifically "propelling" the shoulders back and warming them thoroughly.
*   **Mobility Focus:** Hip flexibility (to allow knees out while hips back) and ankle stability for full-foot contact.

**5. Relevance to athlete's profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly instructs a slight lumbar arch ("prolapse the lower back") to find a center of gravity, which contradicts standard "flat back" cues often used for back pain protection; this requires careful monitoring given the user's history.
*   **No Split Jerk:** While the text focuses on Snatch, the principle of keeping weight on the full foot and driving with hips/legs is critical for a Push Jerk athlete to maximize power without relying on leg drive from toes.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The heavy emphasis on drilling the "start position" and "second pull" separately before combining them is ideal for an athlete transitioning to Olympic lifting who needs to correct foundational mechanics (like knee tracking and foot placement) before adding load.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** No specific sleep advice, but the focus on "relaxed arms" and "not thinking" during drills suggests a need for low-stress technical repetition, which fits well with fatigue management strategies.
