# How to lift / Episode #5: Power SNATCH & Back SQUAT

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Intensity:** Strictly capped at **70%** for the main session described; avoid training to failure on every session ("fear of lifting light weights" is counterproductive).
*   **Frequency/Structure:** Two-session block (Session 4 focuses on Snatch, Session 2 focuses on Clean/Jerk).
*   **Volume:** Low volume at high intensity (1 rep sets up to 90% in general plan, but capped at 70% for this specific athlete due to pain).
*   **Periodization:** Emphasizes mastering body mechanics and tension before increasing load.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Snatch:** "Crank elbows fast" (rotate under quickly), "Shoulders to ears," "Press the lower back into tension from start through pull."
*   **Reception:** "Pause 2 seconds in the receiving position" (to ensure stability and depth).
*   **Grip/Posture:** Grip width should be wider in the future to prevent excessive shoulder movement; keep shoulders tight ("pressing the lower back") during the first pull.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Snatch Focus:** Snatch from hang to overhead (snatch balance), Hang snatch, High pull with contact, Snatch drop (receiving).
*   **Accessory/Strength:** Squat on shoulders (50-90%, 1 rep warm-up style), Clean and Jerk variations (Clean & Jerk, Push press).
*   **Specific Modification:** If pain occurs at a specific weight, stop the heavy lifting immediately and revert to **Power Cleans** only.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Warm-up:** Dynamic movement with barbell: Bent-over rows + Squats (to 50%).
*   **Pain Management:** Do not train through back pain; if discomfort exists in the lower back during reception, reduce load to Power Cleans only.
*   **Mindset:** Prioritize understanding body mechanics over lifting heavy weights on every session.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Protocol:** The text explicitly advises stopping heavy pushing/pulling if the lower back hurts during reception and reverting to Power Cleans. It identifies "rounding the spine" as a likely cause of pain due to rushing elbows or poor tension.
*   **Tension Cue:** Emphasizes "pressing the lower back" (creating intra-abdominal pressure) from the start position, which is critical for an athlete with chronic back issues.
*   **Load Management:** Warns against the mindset that one must be at their limit every session; suggests capping intensity at 70% if the athlete feels "uncoordinated" or in pain, allowing recovery and technique consolidation.
*   **Night Shift Note:** While not explicitly mentioned in this text, the advice to "save energy for the next training" aligns with managing fatigue for a night-shift worker who may have disrupted circadian rhythms.
