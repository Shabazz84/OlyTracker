## Key Points
*   **Training Model:** Online coaching via weekly plans and video feedback is effective for transitioning intermediate athletes (functional training background) to Olympic lifting, accommodating flexible schedules like night shifts.
*   **Movement Focus:** The Push Jerk (*shvung tolchkovoy*) is prioritized as a foundational movement before mastering the Split Jerk or Snatch.
*   **Mobility Bottleneck:** Shoulder joint mobility is non-negotiable; without it, overhead stability fails regardless of lower body strength (e.g., 118kg squat).
*   **Injury Mechanism:** "Shooting hips forward" during the drive phase shifts load from the posterior chain to weak spinal erectors, a critical risk factor for athletes with chronic back pain.

## Technique Cues
*   **Grip Management:** Do not maintain a locked grip while the bar rests on the chest; relax the wrist slightly to allow free bar movement. Engage a lock grip only during the explosive pull phase if necessary.
*   **Rack Position:** Bar must rest in the center of the palm (not fingers) and supported by the clavicle ("behind the collarbone"). Elbows should be raised and spread to form a rigid shelf; keep chest up with diaphragm engaged.
*   **Dip & Drive:** Execute a smooth, even dip. Maintain a vertical torso; strictly avoid premature hip extension or shifting weight onto the front foot, which creates instability and weakens the drive.
*   **Overhead Stability:** For athletes with adequate mobility, keep the bar directly over the center of gravity with rigid arms behind the head rather than forcing shoulder retraction/retreat.

## Programming Principles
*   **Progression Strategy:** Prioritize gradual technical refinement (moving from poor alignment to perfect) over immediate intensity or specific set/rep schemes in early stages.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Utilize self-recording and video analysis to identify specific flaws, such as incorrect bar placement on the fingers versus palms.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** The strict cue against "shooting hips forward" is critical for this athlete's chronic back pain, preventing dangerous load transfer to the lumbar spine during the jerk drive.
*   **Strength vs. Mobility Mismatch:** Despite a strong squat (118kg), the 50kg OHS indicates a mobility ceiling that will stall Clean/Jerk progress until addressed; strength alone cannot compensate for poor overhead mechanics.
*   **Grip Tension Reduction:** Relaxing the grip during the rack position reduces unnecessary upper-body tension, beneficial for a heavy lifter (102.5kg) prone to stiffness.
*   **Schedule Compatibility:** The online coaching model aligns with night shift constraints (Wed–Sun), allowing for asynchronous video submission and feedback without rigid gym hours.
