# berestovteam

## Key Points
*   **Bar Placement:** Critical for spinal safety; bar must rest on the upper traps/shoulders (never cervical spine or lower scapula) to maintain an upright torso and reduce lumbar shear forces, essential for athletes with chronic back pain.
*   **Squat Depth vs. Stability:** Prioritize "correct angles" over maximum depth; stop immediately if lumbar rounding, heel lift, or core tension is lost. Do not sacrifice stability for range of motion.
*   **Descent/Ascent Mechanics:** Execute a slow, controlled eccentric (descent) followed by an explosive concentric (ascent). Use caution with "rebound" mechanics under heavy loads to protect knee joints; prioritize elastic recoil only when load allows.
*   **Equipment Usage:** A lifting belt is recommended specifically for intensities between 80–90% and above; optional for lighter loads. Knee sleeves are advised for joint warmth and stability.

## Technique Cues
*   **Stance & Feet:** Match squat stance width to the bottom position of a snatch or clean (narrower than powerlifting, wider than standing). Keep feet parallel.
*   **Knee Tracking:** Maintain strict knee alignment; knees must track forward in line with toes to prevent valgus collapse (knees caving inward).
*   **Torso Position:** Keep chest up and shoulders pulled forward to lock the bar securely on the traps. Avoid rounding the lower back at any depth.
*   **Breathing Strategy:** Inhale approximately 50% of a breath prior to descent to lift the chest and establish intra-abdominal pressure; hold this brace throughout the rep.

## Programming Principles
*   **Tempo & Control:** Emphasize "slow down, powerful up." Focus on eccentric control during the descent and maximal acceleration during the ascent rather than arbitrary volume prescriptions.
*   **Load Management:** Utilize a belt only when intensity reaches 80–90%. Avoid heavy rebounding/bouncing under maximal loads to mitigate knee joint damage risk.
*   **Technical Mastery:** Prioritize technical precision of movement patterns over linear load progression phases in the early stages.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain Mitigation:** The protocol explicitly mandates high-bar placement (upper traps) rather than low-bar, which shifts the center of gravity forward and increases lumbar shear; this directly addresses the athlete's spinal safety needs.
*   **Overhead Limitation (OHS 50kg):** The emphasis on achieving deep squat angles without rounding the back builds the necessary core stability and thoracic mobility required to improve overhead squat performance later in the block.
*   **Transition Phase:** Advises establishing a solid rack setup and grip width before attempting advanced rebound techniques, suitable for an intermediate athlete transitioning from powerlifting foundations to Olympic weightlifting mechanics.
*   **Note on Constraints:** While this text provides critical biomechanical safety cues for back pain and technique, it does not address the specific hypertrophy volume requirements or night shift recovery protocols; these must be integrated separately by the coach.
