# berestovteam

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** High volume, low intensity (sub-maximal). Focus on speed and technique rather than load.
*   **Frequency:** Use as a primary technical drill within the snatch session; can be performed multiple times per week depending on fatigue management.
*   **Intensity:** Light to moderate weight (focus on bar path and explosiveness), not maximal effort.
*   **Periodization:** Ideal for technique blocks or pre-competition sharpening phases.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **The "Pull" Phase:** Execute a short, sharp dip (5–10 cm max) to engage legs and back *before* the explosive upward drive. Do not drop the bar below this point.
*   **Common Errors to Avoid:**
    *   Dropping the bar too low (turns it into a deadlift pull).
    *   Pulling the bar away from the body to create momentum.
    *   Pushing hips forward or leaning back excessively with the spine during the drive.
    *   Over-rotating the spine backward; energy must go vertically up, not backward.
*   **The Catch (Low Squat):**
    *   Feet: Wide but stable; knees track over toes. Full foot contact.
    *   Torso: Vertical spine, slight lumbar arch ("prognathous" lower back), do not lean forward or backward.
    *   Arms/Shoulders: Shoulders slightly behind the bar; wrists straight (no "bending back"), creating a rigid line from wrist to shoulder to torso.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Primary Drill:** Snatch Pull from Thighs (Standard Version): Dip 5-10cm, explosive drive, catch in low squat.
*   **Variation A (Back Focus):** "Snatch Pull from Thighs - Finish": Keep tension on the bar at the thigh, emphasize vertical back extension without dropping the bar further.
*   **Variation B (Leg Focus):** "Snatch Pull from Thighs - Legs Only": Keep spine perfectly vertical, dip only with legs, drive vertically up.
*   **Variation C (Trap Focus):** Explosive trap shrug and ankle extension from the thigh position without leg drive.

**5. Relevance to this athlete's profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The "Legs Only" variation is highly relevant. It isolates leg drive while maintaining a neutral spine, reducing shear force and lumbar flexion/extension stress compared to full pulls where athletes often over-rotate the back.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation (OHS 50kg):** While this drill targets the *snatch*, the emphasis on "vertical energy transfer" and avoiding back compensation is crucial for a clean/jerk athlete who relies on leg drive rather than upper body strength to stabilize heavy loads overhead.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** The text emphasizes "not rushing" and taking time to feel the correct position before exploding. This mental cue can help mitigate fatigue-related technical breakdowns common in shift workers.
*   **Intermediate Status:** The drill isolates the "final drive" (the most critical part of the snatch for intermediate lifters) without the complexity of the full pull from the floor, allowing for higher quality repetitions to fix specific timing issues.
