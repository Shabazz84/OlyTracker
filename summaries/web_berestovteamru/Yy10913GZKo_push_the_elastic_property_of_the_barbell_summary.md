# Push! The elastic property of the barbell

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** Low volume, high intent. Perform 3–5 sets of 2–4 reps with light-to-moderate loads (just enough to bend the bar).
*   **Intensity:** Focus on speed and "feel" rather than load; the goal is to elicit bar deflection, not maximal force production.
*   **Frequency:** Can be used as a primary technical drill 1–2 times per week during the technique phase.
*   **Periodization:** Best utilized in early-to-mid phases of a cycle to ingrain the "dip-drive" connection before moving to heavier loads where bar deflection is less perceptible.

**2. Technique cues (Push Jerk focus)**
*   **The Dip:** Execute a sharp, explosive dip ("rescue movement") rather than a slow, controlled squat. The speed of the dip must be sufficient to bend the bar.
*   **Bar Contact:** Keep the bar resting on the clavicles and shoulders; do not let it roll forward or lift off during the initial dip phase.
*   **The Drive:** The bar should only leave the chest at the exact moment of maximum upward drive, driven by the extension of hips/knees and a push through the head.
*   **Transition Speed:** Immediately after the bar "flies" up due to deflection, accelerate your body downward to meet it (switch speed). Do not "brake" or slow down under the bar; the bar's rebound dictates the need for rapid footwork.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Primary Drill:** Push Jerk from the chest using a thinner barbell (15kg women's bar, 10kg, or even 5kg "child" bars) with plates loaded at the very ends of the sleeves to maximize deflection.
*   **Variations:** Can be adapted for the "Push Press" style (shvung) by using a slightly slower start followed by an explosive finish to bend the bar.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   *Note: The text does not contain specific recovery, sleep, or mobility protocols.* It focuses exclusively on mechanical feel and timing.

**5. Relevance to athlete's profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** This drill is highly relevant for the athlete with chronic back pain because it teaches the "dip-drive" connection using minimal load. Mastering the speed of the dip ensures the bar leaves the chest efficiently without requiring excessive upper-back or lower-back torque to stabilize heavy weights prematurely.
*   **Technique Transfer:** Since this athlete only performs a push jerk (no split), mastering the timing of the bar deflection is critical for generating vertical force without relying on leg drive that might compromise spinal alignment if technique is poor.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** As a night shift worker, energy levels may fluctuate. This drill allows for high-quality technical work with low systemic fatigue (light weight), making it suitable for days when the athlete needs to train but feels fatigued from shift work.
*   **Constraint Check:** The text suggests using very thin bars (15kg/10kg). For a 102.5 kg athlete, ensure the chosen bar is long enough to accommodate plates at the ends without hitting the collar; if not, standard barbells with plates on the outermost collars will suffice for the "deflection" effect.
