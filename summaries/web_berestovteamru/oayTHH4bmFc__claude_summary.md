## 2. TECHNIQUE CUES: SNATCH PULL

**Starting Position:**
- Feet shoulder-width (adjust individually as needed)
- Bar over mid-foot, close to shins
- Hips slightly lower; can vary based on mobility
- Straight, neutral spine with tension
- Full foot contact with platform
- Load begins from legs, not back

**Phase 1 – Takeoff (to mid-shin):**
- Drive with legs; hips don't rise prematurely
- Keep back angle stable
- Maintain bar close to body

**Phase 2 – Knee Pass:**
- Knees retreat sideways slightly (knees "out")
- Hips rise more
- **Critical error to avoid:** Full leg extension here (causes bar drift forward)

**Phase 3 – Knee Recovery (invisible but important):**
- Knees rebend under bar as hips rise
- Creates "spring" position (compressed angles)

**Phase 4 – Acceleration & Extension (most important):**
- **Shoulders stay over bar until final explosion**
- **Drive vertically upward** (not backward)
- Synchronized leg + back extension
- Back body angle: energy directed UP, then trails back via momentum
- Common error: Premature spinal extension backward → bar drifts forward
- **Cue:** Pull straight up; back extension follows naturally, not leads

## 3. EXERCISE RECOMMENDATIONS
- Use straps (frees wrist, improves technical execution)
- Snatch-grip width (athlete-dependent—"practically to the edge")

## 5. RELEVANCE TO THIS ATHLETE

| Factor | Relevance |
|--------|-----------|
| **OHS 50 kg limiter** | Vertical pull pattern weakness; this snatch pull cue (vertical drive, not back) directly addresses bar path/overhead positioning |
| **Back pain** | ✓ Emphasizes neutral spine in start; discourages excessive spinal extension as compensation |
| **Push jerk only** | Pull mechanics transfer to both snatch and clean; technical foundation critical |
| **Intermediate level** | Common errors listed (premature leg extension, shoulder drift, spinal extension) are typical of this stage |

**Key takeaway:** Master vertical pull mechanics to improve snatch positioning and reduce compensatory back stress.
