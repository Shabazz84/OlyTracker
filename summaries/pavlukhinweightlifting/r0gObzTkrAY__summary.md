# Хочу быть сильной и стройной. Кристина и тяжёлая атлетика

## Key Points
*   **Profile:** Intermediate lifter (102.5 kg) transitioning to Olympic weightlifting with chronic back pain; currently limited by a 50 kg Overhead Squat (OHS).
*   **Schedule:** Night shift worker (Wed–Sun off); requires training adaptation for irregular sleep patterns and recovery management.
*   **Current Status:** Push Jerk only (65 kg), no Split Jerk yet. Back Squat 118 kg, Clean 80 kg.
*   **Primary Goals:** 6-week hypertrophy block followed by technique/strength focus.
*   **Critical Constraints:** Chronic back pain requires strict spinal stability; patellar tendon sensitivity noted in similar profiles (avoid aggressive stretching/injections); nutritional deficits (low carbs) directly impair explosive performance.

## Technique Cues
*   **Bar Path & Footwork:** Keep the bar close to the body ("pull towards knees"); avoid jumping forward during the jerk or snatch—step in place ("topat' na meste") rather than "jumping sideways." Receive with heels down, not on toes.
*   **Spinal Stability (Back Pain):** Prioritize a "stable line" over depth; never round the back ("like a dog"). If form breaks, stop or reduce load to maintain spinal integrity. Use core stiffness (planks) to support the spine under load.
*   **Jerk Mechanics:** Drive vertically with feet; keep elbows high and forward to prevent hyperextension of the lower back. Do not let elbows drift back.
*   **Squat Depth:** Aim for depth but prioritize stability; do not drop too low with an empty bar if it triggers pain or rounding.
*   **Pulling Mechanics:** Slightly bend knees ("podzhat' kolena") before turnover to maintain tension without inertia.

## Programming Principles
*   **Periodization Structure:** Begin with a 6-week hypertrophy block (higher volume, moderate intensity) to build tissue resilience and muscle mass, transitioning into technique/strength work thereafter.
*   **Frequency & Split:** Target ~6 sessions/week if sleep allows; utilize an alternating Upper/Lower split or dedicated Olympic lift days paired with accessory work.
    *   *Example:* Legs/Olympic lifts on one day; Back/Upper strength on another.
*   **Intensity Management:** Avoid training to failure ("energy runs out" is a stop signal). Focus on "making heavy weights workable" (e.g., 3 sets of 1 rep at high intensity) rather than accumulating fatigue.
*   **Volume Progression:** Shift from low-rep stabilization (5s) to slightly higher volume (10s) as technique stabilizes, specifically targeting the OHS limitation.
*   **Accessory Focus:** Include paused Bench Press for upper body strength and Planks for core stiffness. Avoid rigid knee sleeves; use loose medical wraps for warmth only if needed.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Back Pain Management:** The program explicitly prioritizes spinal stability over depth or speed. Isometric holds and low-load movements within a pain-free range (1-3/10) are recommended for tendon adaptation rather than aggressive stretching or dry needling.
*   **Nutritional Strategy:** High protein intake (~2g/kg) with diverse amino acids is crucial for inflammation management. Crucially, **carbohydrate loading** before training is non-negotiable; low carb intake leads to poor explosive performance and fatigue.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** While specific sleep hygiene protocols are not detailed in the source text, the emphasis on "stopping when energy runs out" suggests a need for conservative volume management to accommodate irregular circadian rhythms.
*   **Psychological Approach:** Acknowledge frustration but return immediately to training ("go squat and press"). Avoid suppressing emotions; focus on mechanical loading as superior to passive recovery methods (injections) for tendon health.
