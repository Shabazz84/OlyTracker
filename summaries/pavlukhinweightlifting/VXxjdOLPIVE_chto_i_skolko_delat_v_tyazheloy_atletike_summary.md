# Что и сколько делать в тяжёлой атлетике (What and how much to do in weightlifting)
**Channel:** Pavlukhin Weightlifting | **Date:** 2026-05-25 | **Language:** ru

## Key Points
- **The sport itself answers "what and how much":** WHAT = snatch + clean & jerk; HOW MUCH = (1) heaviest weight for *today* lifted with genuine overcoming effort, (2) reps = **singles** (ВПД / high-threshold work), (3) sets = depends on training level. "Lift relatively heavy with overcoming effort" is in every weightlifting textbook worldwide — if it feels light, you aren't straining enough. (Not 200 kg daily — just a *today-heavy* weight.)
- **Sets scale with training level:** advanced lifter (Kirill) runs 12–20 heavy series across 3–5 sessions/day; Pavlukhin himself does 1–3 heavy singles ~6×/week. A 3×/week beginner managing ~9 heavy series/week is enough for ~a month — then add frequency or add GMV (hypertrophy) volume work. Training level also dips periodically for recovery; volume of heavy sets drops then.
- **When results stall, add training frequency — specifically squat sessions, not more classic lifts.** Example: 36-yo amateur (best 122/155 = 277) stalled on 3×/week → added a **4th session of just squats** and progress resumed. Pavlukhin would run 5×/week if schedule allowed.

## Programming Principles
- **The "reps → max" prediction rule is a myth.** "Squat X for 3 → you'll hit X+10 / a single at X×%" is not written in any source and "never worked on anyone in my memory." It's nervous-system dependent, so as a universal rule **it doesn't exist** — classic survivorship-bias dogma (the lifters it failed are forgotten). Demonstrated live: squatted 142.5 kg (95% of 150) for 2 reps and that does **not** guarantee 150. Don't draw conclusions from one session; daily state ("from the head"), fatigue, and rest between sets all move the result.
- **Squat = leg POWER, not just strength.** Specificity (Zatsiorsky): the squat must train *power* (force per unit time) — spring out of the bottom fast, because you accelerate/“подрыв” the bar. **Don't descend slowly** (you bleed off stretch-reflex energy).
- **Paused squats = NEGATIVE transfer for weightlifting.** A pause teaches *stopping* in the hole; then you receive the clean, get "nailed" in the bottom, and can't stand up fast. You can squat 200 paused and still fail to recover a clean. Pauses have *some* value but are not the main squat purpose.
- **Don't train the lifts in pieces.** Snatch from elements (snatch pull + hang snatch + overhead) doesn't reassemble into a full snatch for many lifters — the nervous system doesn't learn a complex movement in chunks. Many who pull/hang fine "lose it" pulling from the floor. The C&J is one two-part movement; don't split it.
- **Jerk from the rack (толчок со стоек) doesn't transfer** for anyone past ~first-rank level — it skips the clean and the integrated structure; only a beginner tool.
- **Belt protocol:** strengthen the core *beltless* up to ~near-heavy weights; add the belt at ~90–95%. The belt only "works" if you brace the abdomen *into* it (intra-abdominal pressure) — people who "don't feel" the belt are using it wrong. Brace the core AND use the belt's advantage.
- **Positive vs negative transfer (Zatsiorsky):** choose accessories that transfer *muscular/tendon strength at the specific working angle and stretch amplitude*, not ones that conflict with the competition movement. Overhead squat is fine for *mobility/balance*, not for "building" the snatch.

## Technique / Exercise Recommendations
- **Scissor / split-lunge (ножницы) for the jerk** — fixes the fault of driving the jerk onto a **straight rear leg** (causes pelvic skew, can't bend the rear knee, injury + back-spasm risk). Goal: bend the rear knee, knee travels **down** (not in, not forward), rear heel pressed in; this strengthens muscle/tendon in the *stretched* lunge angle so the body stops protectively blocking it. **Positive transfer** of strength at the receiving angle (note: tendon stretches only ~5% before tearing → build muscle so it absorbs the load).
  - Loading: bodyweight (film self / mirror) → empty bar (10–20 kg) → add weight gradually; **10 reps/leg, every rep in good technique (no failure reps).** Train **both** legs for motor symmetry even if you only split to one side.
- **What NOT to add (debunked viewer suggestions):** jerk-from-rack (doesn't transfer, it's a sharp/explosive move; the lunge work is a calm tendon-prep), and overcomplicated lunge variants — keep it simple, just develop the legs.

## Athlete Relevance (102.5 kg, BS 118 kg, Jerk 65 kg push-jerk only, chronic back pain, tall)
- **Back-pain resonance:** Pavlukhin himself was sidelined ~6 months by a back spasm and now carries chronic back tonus/hypertonus + a *fear* of the weight — same psychological pattern this athlete must manage. Reinforces Principle 12 and pain-gated loading.
- **Reps→max myth is directly actionable:** don't infer a snatch/clean/squat max from a rep set; the program's TM%/estimated-max logic should be treated as a *rough* guide, confirmed by feel on the day — fits the existing feel-based-loading principle.
- **Stall protocol = add a squat-only session**, not more classic lifts — concrete lever if Block 1 progress flattens (note the night-shift frequency cap, though).
- **Singles-first, today-heavy, overcoming effort** = exactly the existing Pavlukhin/Golovinsky "daily max single, no grinding miss" approach for the jerk.
- **Scissor/lunge caveat for THIS athlete:** he is push-jerk-only (no split), so the *fault-fix* framing is less direct — but it confirms the existing CLAUDE.md note ("lunge squats for jerk tendon development even without split jerk"). Use the loaded lunge as **tendon prep at the split-receiving angle** (bodyweight → bar → load, 10/leg, both sides), not as split-jerk technique work.
