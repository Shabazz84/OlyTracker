# Штангистов не учат приседать. Присед — забытая техника

## Key Points
*   **Bar Path & Back Health:** The barbell center must move strictly vertically; any forward shift significantly increases perceived weight and directly overloads the lower back (critical for an athlete with chronic pain).
*   **Force Production:** Power generation relies on "jump-like" mechanics where speed and force *increase* as legs extend past the dead point, rather than peaking early. This elastic energy transfer is essential for overhead stability.
*   **Ankle Mechanics:** Tight soleus muscles restrict knee travel, forcing a forward lean and heel lift. Correcting this via specific calf stretching is a prerequisite for depth and balance.
*   **Squat Specificity:** Hip-dominant squats (butt-back) are inferior to Olympic lifting; the athlete must adopt knee-dominant, vertical-torso squats that simulate lifting angles.

## Technique Cues
*   **"Full Foot" Contact:** Maintain flat heels throughout the entire movement. Heel lift indicates a loss of power and technique failure.
*   **Knee Drive:** Widen knees during the squat descent to maintain a vertical torso, engaging quadriceps while preventing forward lean.
*   **Explosive Intent:** Squats must be performed with an intent to jump at the top; the movement should mimic an explosive jump rather than a slow powerlift ascent.
*   **Vertical Bar Path:** Visualize the bar moving strictly up and down within the range of motion to minimize spinal shear forces.

## Programming Principles
*   **Foundational Technique First:** Prioritize high-frequency technical practice over maximal loading; modern methods focusing solely on strength are inferior without this foundational work.
*   **Explosive Loading:** Emphasize velocity and "jump-like" mechanics in training sets rather than pure static strength.
*   **Mobility Integration:** Dedicate specific time to soleus stretching and ankle mobility drills before lifting sessions to ensure the capacity for deep, vertical squats.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain Mitigation:** The strict requirement for a vertical bar path directly addresses the athlete's back pain by eliminating forward lean caused by poor ankle mobility or hip-dominant patterns.
*   **Push Jerk Limitations:** Since the athlete only performs push jerks, the "jump-like" force generation described is vital to generate enough upward momentum to stabilize heavy loads overhead without a split jerk.
*   **OHS Limiter (50 kg):** The inability to keep heels down or maintain a vertical torso prevents the power transfer needed to increase Overhead Squat strength; addressing ankle elasticity is the primary fix for this limiter.
*   **Night Shift Considerations:** While not explicitly detailed in the source, the reliance on "tendon elasticity" and motor control suggests that fatigue from night shifts (Wed–Sun) must be managed to prevent degradation of these fine motor skills.
