# Merged Summary: Weightlifting Coaching for Intermediate OL Athlete

## Key Points

- **Shoulder stability is the primary limiting factor** for jerk and snatch progression, not raw strength
- OHS at 50 kg is critically low relative to back squat (118 kg); mobility and shoulder stability must be addressed before split jerk learning
- Chest and lat training serve injury prevention (shoulder dislocation risk during jerk turnover), not direct strength gain
- Technique work yields fastest motor learning at competition/near-maximal weights, not submaximal loads
- Daily mobility work must accompany main lift training to address pec/shoulder tightness
- Chronic back pain requires spine-conservative programming; avoid heavy wide-grip pressing

## Technique Cues

**Pressing (for jerk preparation):**
- Elbows tucked (NOT flared outward) to reduce shoulder joint moment arm
- Pecs drive the movement via adduction (bring arms together), not triceps alone
- Lower to mid-chest with tight elbows; avoid "bodybuilder" depth to solar plexus
- Avoid wide-elbow mechanics—causes shoulder injury in weightlifters

**Overhead Position (OHS/Snatch):**
- Pec and lat tightness directly limits overhead mobility; daily drills mandatory
- Weak OHS (50 kg) is risk factor before learning split jerk—build shoulder stability first

## Programming Principles

- **6-week hypertrophy block priority**: Horizontal pressing (floor press, bench with tucked elbows) + daily pec/lat mobility drills
- Accessory work targets posterior chain and rotator cuff stabilizers to handle heavy technical work safely
- After hypertrophy block, transition to technique/strength work on classic lifts (snatch, clean, jerk)
- Schedule pressing earlier in training week if night shift (Wed–Sun) causes fatigue-related form breakdown
- Injury prevention > short-term maxes—robust shoulders reduce jerk/snatch injury risk during skill acquisition

## Athlete Relevance

| Limiting Factor | Intervention |
|---|---|
| **OHS 50 kg (major)** | Daily pec/shoulder mobility + tucked-elbow floor press during hypertrophy block |
| **Chronic back pain** | Avoid heavy bench; prioritize shoulder stability via lats/pecs; spine-conservative pressing only |
| **Push jerk only** | Cannot safely learn split jerk until OHS improves and shoulder stability is robust |
| **Night shift fatigue** | Front-load pressing work Wed–Thu; technical work on classic lifts when CNS fresh |

**Bottom line**: Dedicate the 6-week hypertrophy block to shoulder stability and mobility as direct prerequisites for jerk progression and OHS improvement.
