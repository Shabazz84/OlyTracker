# Merged Summary

## Key Points
- **Squat is the foundation**: Establish back squat strength before expecting proportional snatch/clean/jerk development. Expected ratios: snatch ~60% of squat, jerk ~75% of squat.
- **Your athlete's profile is underdeveloped at the pulls**: With a 118 kg back squat but only 80 kg clean and 65 kg jerk, there is significant room for strength/technique gain.
- **OHS (50 kg) is the primary limiter**: This confirms the stated constraint—overhead stability must improve before jerk progression.
- **Technique plateaus without competition/loading**: After achieving "medium-OK" technique, focus shifts from refinement to force development and heavy load exposure.
- **Velocity/tempo as readiness**: Squat ascent time (2–2.2s) indicates load capacity; use as a daily readiness marker.
- **Individualize by anthropometry**: Limb length and leverages dictate stance, depth, and positioning—do not copy others.

## Technique Cues
- Snatch/clean landing position and balance refinement are necessary but secondary to strength.
- Jerk: prioritize arm relaxation and consistent back leg timing.
- Breathing and muscular awareness prevent compensatory injury patterns.
- OHS work must address overhead stability to unblock jerk progression.

## Programming Principles
- Periodize: 6-week hypertrophy block, then transition to technique/strength work.
- High-threshold motor unit recruitment occurs at heavier loads—hypertrophy block should include heavy sessions.
- Measure effort and velocity as progression drivers, not ego-based loading.
- **Not addressed**: Chronic back pain management, night shift recovery optimization—both require individual assessment.

## Athlete Relevance
- **Immediate focus**: Squat strength development supports clean/jerk ratio correction.
- **OHS priority**: Direct overhead work (front rack mobility, OHS volume) before complex jerk variations.
- **Push jerk limitation**: Consider split jerk introduction only after OHS improves and back pain is stabilized.
- **6-week hypertrophy block**: Design with back-pain-safe exercise selection and night shift recovery windows (training mid-sleep-cycle if possible).
