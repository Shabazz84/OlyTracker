# PAVLUKHIN WEIGHTLIFTING COACHING PHILOSOPHY
## For Intermediate Strength Athlete Transitioning to Olympic Lifting

---

## 1. CORE TRAINING PHILOSOPHY (5 Principles)

1. **Technique Drives Strength**: Perfect movement patterns under light load > heavy loads with compensation. Technical proficiency directly reduces fatigue and injury risk; allow efficiency gains to unlock load increases naturally.

2. **Frequency Over Volume**: 5–6 short sessions weekly (~45min) with 1–2 heavy lifts per session outperforms single large-volume days. Accumulated quality reps (100/movement/week) and frequent CNS arousal without catastrophic fatigue build sustainable adaptation.

3. **Movement Pattern Specificity**: Train the actual competition movements (snatch, clean, jerk, front squat). Accessory work is supportive only; hypertrophy blocks isolated from Olympic lifts during technical phases.

4. **Strength = Muscle + Neural Drive**: Two independent adaptations. Neural drive plateaus ~4 weeks; muscle growth sustains longer. 6-week hypertrophy blocks leverage this: early neural gains, then muscle development as neural ceiling is hit.

5. **Conservative Intensity for Reliability**: 80% of perceived maximum is baseline. Load increases only when movement quality remains high and recovery allows. One poor session followed by reduction prevents injury spirals.

---

## 2. PROGRAMMING APPROACH

### **Macrocycle Structure**
- **Block 1 (Weeks 1–6): Hypertrophy Block** — High frequency (5–6x/week), moderate intensity (70–85%), high rep ranges (10–15), accessory-heavy, machine-based back work to rebuild pain-free capacity.
- **Block 2 (Weeks 7–10): Strength/Technique Phase** — Classical lifts emphasized, intensity increases (85–95%), volume moderate, focus on snatch/jerk refinement.

### **Weekly Microcycle (Sample)**
| Day | Focus | Structure |
|-----|-------|-----------|
| Mon | Heavy squat / press | 1–2 rep maxes; 2–3min rest |
| Tue | Light OHS / jerk drills | Accessory emphasis; 10–15 reps |
| Wed | Rest/active recovery | Mobility, conditioning |
| Thu | Heavy snatch pull / clean | Explosive intent; 2–3min rest |
| Fri | Light front squat / jerk | Technique focus; 8–10 reps |
| Sat | Back strength (rows, lats) | 12–15 reps; machine-based |
| Sun | Rest / light conditioning | Sleep priority |

### **Load Progression**
- **Fixed increments** (~5–10 kg per week) over percentage-based scaling
- **Autoregulation**: If CNS state low (night shift fatigue), use preset "light week" 10–15% below target
- **Deload principle**: Every 4 weeks, reduce volume/intensity 20–30% to prevent overuse inflammation

## 3. KEY TECHNIQUE CUES & EXERCISE PREFERENCES

### **Snatch**
- **High-start position**: Shorter pull arc, direct upward trajectory, reduces patellar tendon and lower-back stress (critical for chronic pain).
- **Weight distribution**: Full-foot contact throughout; avoid heel lift or toe-bias (forward weight = bar drift).
- **Vertical bar path**: Lats engaged ("pull bar toward body, not just up"). If lats weak, bar drifts forward, increasing lumbar shear.
- **Catch position**: Mid-foot balance; avoid forward collapse (exacerbates back pain and OHS weakness).

### **Clean & Jerk**
- **Pull mechanics**: Body drives bar; arms follow passively until lockout. Pressing mid-pull = energy loss and injury risk.
- **Jerk dip depth**: Adequate to use elastic recoil; avoid overshooting (unnecessary spinal load).
- **Rack position**: Full lat engagement; cue lat activation ("pull lats tight"). Relaxed arms in hook grip only until pull begins.
- **Barbell drop**: Controlled drop with minimal forward lean/spinal flexion (protects back during eccentric phase).

### **Squat Variations**
- **Front squat priority**: More upright positioning, safer for chronic back pain than high-bar back squat. Vertical knees-forward bias.
- **Back squat positioning**: If used, forward-lean "weightlifting style" is contraindicated for this athlete; opt for upright positioning or pause tempo (≤2 sec concentric to prevent spinal erector fatigue).
- **Explosive intent**: Rapid concentric phase; no grinding. Speed = efficiency = reduced compensation patterns.

### **Accessory Exercise Hierarchy** (Hypertrophy Block Focus)
1. **Vertical pulls** (lat pulldowns, band-assist chin-ups): Direct lat/upper-back strength for pull mechanics and OHS stability.
2. **Horizontal rows** (machine rows, dumbbell rows): Trap/rear delt development for rack position and overhead stability.
3. **Pressing variants** (bench, floor press, incline): Full pectoral recruitment protects shoulder joint during jerk/OHS.
4. **Single-leg work** (Bulgarian split squats, pistol progressions): Core/spinal stability while addressing anterior-chain dominance.
5. **Core activation** (glute bridges, dead bugs, pallof press): Essential for spinal stability; daily integration.
6. **RDLs / Hip hinges**: Posterior chain elasticity; tendon prep for pulling phase.

---

## 4. RECOVERY & MOBILITY APPROACH

### **Chronic Back Pain Management**
- **Avoid excessive lumbar extension**: Cue neutral spine during pressing/rack positions; no full-lockout hyperextension.
- **Machine-first progression**: Weeks 1–2 use machines (leg press, lat pulldowns) to build pain
