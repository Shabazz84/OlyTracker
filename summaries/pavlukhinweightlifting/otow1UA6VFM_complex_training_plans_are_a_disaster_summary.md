# Complex training plans are a disaster 🏋🏽‍♀️

## Key Points
*   **Readiness-Based Intensity:** Training load is self-regulated daily (~70% of current readiness) rather than fixed to historical maxes. High inflammation, poor sleep (common in night shifts), or joint pain mandates dropping to sub-maximal loads immediately.
*   **Speed Over Endurance:** The primary goal of sub-maximal work is neural speed ("quick off the floor," "fast drop under"). If movement slows at 70%, the load is too heavy for this specific stimulus; if it moves fast, the intensity is correct.
*   **Pull Mechanics:** Snatch and Clean Pulls are preferred over conventional deadlifts to maintain spinal continuity and speed. Pulls must be continuous from the lift position, never starting from a dead stop on the floor, to avoid excessive lumbar compression.
*   **Hypertrophy vs. Speed:** A dedicated hypertrophy block is distinct from this speed/technique phase. Pure strength/speed work avoids high-volume grinding that causes joint inflammation.

## Technique Cues
*   **The Start (Even Pull):** Execute a uniform removal of the bar ("even pull") to prevent jerky starts and forward jumping. Keep the bar close to the body throughout.
*   **Footwork:** Drop onto the full foot, not just the toes, ensuring stability during the catch.
*   **Posture & Verticality:** Maintain vertical shins during the initial pull; avoid excessive forward lean or hip dipping (bumping the bar).
*   **Core Engagement:** Engage abs from the start of the lift ("from here to the groin") to prevent thoracic collapse and shift load away from the lumbar spine. This intra-abdominal pressure is critical for protecting a chronic back injury.
*   **Hip Drive:** Focus on lifting the knee, not just the hip; utilize pelvic rotation/tuck (like a caterpillar) at the transition point.

## Programming Principles
*   **Volume & Frequency:** Low volume with high focus. Utilize single repetitions ("singles") with long rest periods (2+ minutes) to preserve technical precision and speed. Avoid low-percentage sets of 3–5 reps.
*   **Periodization Structure:** Alternate heavy weeks with light/recovery weeks (1:1 or 2:1 ratio). Light sessions serve as active recovery following heavy blocks, illness, or injury flare-ups.
*   **Exercise Selection:**
    *   **Primary:** Snatch/Clean Pulls and Front Squats.
    *   **Front Squat Constraint:** If replacing back squats due to pain, keep weight ≤10% above the current snatch max to ensure verticality without excessive spinal loading.
    *   **Exclusions:** Heavy conventional deadlifts and heavy rack pulls are discouraged as they reduce speed and exacerbate lumbar fatigue/back pain.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Back Pain Management:** The protocol explicitly avoids heavy conventional deadlifts that cause "stiff back" and favors front squats with strict verticality to protect the lumbar spine. Core engagement cues are essential for maintaining spinal integrity during lifts.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** The philosophy of training based on *daily physiological readiness* rather than a rigid calendar is critical for shift workers with disrupted sleep cycles. If the athlete feels "messed up" or inflamed, they must drop to 70% singles immediately regardless of the scheduled plan.
*   **Transition Strategy:** For an intermediate athlete moving to Olympic lifting, this approach prioritizes building neural pathways and technique speed at sub-maximal loads rather than grinding through high-volume fatigue, which is beneficial for a bodybuilder transitioning to powerlifting/Olympic styles with existing joint issues.
*   **Specific Limiters:** The focus on verticality and core pressure directly addresses the 50 kg OHS (Snatch) limiter by ensuring thoracic stability, while the exclusion of heavy deadlifts mitigates the chronic back pain risk during the transition to higher loads.
