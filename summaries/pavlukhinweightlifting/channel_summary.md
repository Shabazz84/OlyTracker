# Coaching Philosophy: Pavlukhin Weightlifting Adaptation

### 1. Core Training Philosophy
*   **The Back is a Stabilizer, Not a Mover:** The erector spinae must remain rigid and static during pulls. Dynamic lifting of the bar by the lower back causes shear force and pain. Power comes from glutes/hamstrings; the spine acts as a lever.
*   **High Start > Low Start:** Avoid the "low start" (deep hips, forward knees) which creates dead points and arcs the bar. A high start with vertical shins shortens the pull path, reduces knee valgus, and minimizes lumbar strain.
*   **Neural Drive Over Hypertrophy (Initially):** Strength is a product of muscle cross-section *and* neural recruitment. For an intermediate athlete, technical precision and speed (<2s concentric) take precedence over grinding heavy loads.
*   **Pain-Driven Periodization:** Training load is not rigid; it is reactive. If back "tonus" or spasms occur, immediately reduce weight/volume. Do not train through sharp pain or inflammation-induced stiffness ("skovannost").

### 2. Programming Approach
*   **Structure & Frequency:** Given the night shift (Wed–Sun) and chronic pain, limit sessions to **2–3 per week**. Use "partial volume" strategies: spread total weekly volume across multiple short sessions rather than one grueling block.
    *   *Hypertrophy Block (Weeks 1-6):* Focus on sub-maximal loads with higher reps (12–15) to build muscle mass and tendon resilience without excessive spinal compression.
    *   *Technique/Strength:* Low reps, high focus on speed. If a lift fails due to form, reduce load; do not force the PR.
*   **Exercise Selection for Back Safety:**
    *   *Primary:* Front Squats preferred over Back Squats (reduced spinal compression, better Olympic transfer).
    *   *Substitutes:* Leg Press or unilateral machines (abduction/adduction) to build leg mass without axial loading.
    *   *Back Work:* Chest-supported rows, high-pulley vertical rows, and machine extensions. Avoid hyperextensions/Good Mornings during active spasms.
*   **Progression:** Use a "step" approach (+5kg or +10kg) rather than rigid percentages. Alternate heavy/light weeks to manage accumulated fatigue from shift work.

### 3. Key Technique Cues & Exercise Preferences
*   **"Pull, Don't Lift":** Engage lats to pull the bar toward the body. Failure causes the bar to drift forward, forcing the back to compensate.
*   **Static Back Visualization:** Visualize the erector spinae as a rigid column that does not move dynamically during the pull.
*   **Full Foot Contact:** Stand on the "full foot" (entire sole). Lifting heels or toes compromises balance and forces the bar path forward, increasing shear force on the spine.
*   **Push Jerk Specifics:** Since split jerk is excluded, focus on a rigid "statue-like" fixation at the top of the pull. Drop the bar immediately after the lift to prevent biceps/shoulder strain from absorbing impact.
*   **Elbow Tension:** In overhead positions (OHS), actively pull the hand toward the elbow to engage lats and prevent hyperextension of the lumbar spine.

### 4. Recovery and Mobility Approach
*   **Active Movement vs. Immobilization:** For chronic back pain, avoid total rest. Use "walking" or light movement to aid circulation and reduce stiffness. Immobilization often worsens spasms.
*   **Inflammation Management:** Distinguish between muscle soreness (good) and sharp nerve pain (bad). If inflammation is present, prioritize rest and active recovery over loading.
*   **Circadian Adaptation:** Adjust intensity based on daily mental state and sleep quality. Night shift workers are prone to residual fatigue; if the "brain turns off" or form degrades due to tiredness, stop immediately. Do not force heavy sessions when circadian rhythm is disrupted.
*   **Tendon Adaptation:** Recognize that bones/tendons adapt slower than muscles. The 6-week hypertrophy block is essential to build tissue tolerance before increasing Olympic loads.

### 5. Application to the Athlete (102.5 kg BW, Intermediate)
*   **Transition Strategy:** Your high squat (118kg) indicates strong legs; do not focus on increasing squat volume. The limiter is technique and overhead stability (OHS 50kg). Shift immediately to refining the "High Start" pull mechanics to protect your back while building strength.
*   **Back Pain Protocol:** Implement a strict "no heavy spinal loading" rule during pain flare-ups. Replace Back Squats with Front Squats or Leg Press for the hypertrophy block. Use chest-supported rows to build upper back strength without lumbar shear.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** The lack of split jerk requires exceptional shoulder/core stability. Focus accessory work on triceps, biceps, and chest stability (bench press variations) to support the overhead position. Ensure the "drop" is immediate to prevent tendonitis.
*   **Night Shift Management:** With Wed–Sun shifts, prioritize recovery windows between sessions. Avoid consecutive high-intensity days. Use the "reactive programming" model: if sleep was poor or pain is high, reduce volume rather than following a rigid plan. The 6-week block should be viewed as an investment in tissue health to allow long-term progression without injury.
