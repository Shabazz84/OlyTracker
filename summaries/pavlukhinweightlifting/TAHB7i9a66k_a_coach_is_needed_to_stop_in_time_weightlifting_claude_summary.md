# A coach is needed to stop in time. Weightlifting

## Key Points

- **Back pain management**: Distinguish microtrauma (necessary adaptation) from acute injury; compensation patterns (anterior dominance) require intelligent volume distribution across snatch/clean/squat rather than separate sessions.
- **Time-constrained programming**: With limited training windows (night shift Wed–Sun), prioritize movements and distribute volume strategically—cannot afford isolated sessions for each lift.
- **Recovery optimization**: Short rest intervals between sets and aerobic conditioning improve training density and inter-session leg recovery.
- **Jerk mechanics (push jerk priority)**: Dip should be short and fast (drive-based, not deep squat); bar path must be vertical (not backward); body generates force; hands/arms only stabilize at lockout; simultaneous knee bend + foot placement prevent bar instability.
- **Common error**: Pressing the bar instead of driving/throwing it.

## Technique Cues

**Push Jerk:**
- Short, explosive dip (drive-like, not squat-deep)
- Vertical bar path (straight up; avoid backward arc)
- Drive from legs; lockout is stabilization, not pressing
- Synchronized knee bend + foot repositioning for platform stability

## Programming Principles

- Compress volume across lifts within sessions rather than isolating movements
- Use small load increments (0.5 kg plates) for competition-simulation attempts
- Incorporate aerobic work to improve inter-session recovery capacity
- Manage microtrauma through distributed loading and varied positioning

## Athlete Relevance

- **OHS (50 kg, primary snatch limiter)** and **back pain** necessitate careful load distribution; avoid excessive compensation patterns from overhead mobility deficit.
- **Push jerk only (65 kg)**: Jerk mechanics cues apply directly; consider shoulder press (70 kg observed) as reception/overhead stability accessory during hypertrophy block.
- **Night shift schedule**: Prioritize time-efficient sessions; consolidate snatch, clean, squat work within single training window rather than splitting.
- **6-week hypertrophy block**: Build capacity via distributed volume while managing back load; aerobic work aids recovery between sessions.
