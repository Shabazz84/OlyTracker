# Моё тело меняется! Кристина и тяжелая атлетика

## Key Points
*   **Foundation First:** Prioritize leg strength and posterior chain development (glutes/hamstrings) over technical perfection in the early stages; strong legs reduce the perceived weight of the barbell.
*   **Hypertrophy Block Strategy:** Utilize sub-maximal loads with higher volume to build muscle mass and condition tendons, rather than chasing 1-rep maxes immediately. This builds the "hard skeleton" necessary for bone density and joint health.
*   **Tendon vs. Muscle Adaptation:** Recognize that bones and tendons adapt significantly slower than muscles; they require specific tension stimuli over time to prevent injury (especially crucial for chronic back pain).
*   **Mental & Circadian Awareness:** Training intensity must be adjusted based on daily mental state, mood fluctuations, and sleep quality (critical for night shift workers), rather than a rigid schedule. Do not force heavy sessions if the mindset or recovery is compromised.
*   **Movement Quality:** Eliminate technical errors (e.g., jumping forward, toe-dominance) before increasing load. Focus on elastic energy utilization ("working in return") rather than pure muscular force.

## Technique Cues
*   **Bar Path & Stability:** Keep the bar moving vertically along the body; avoid "jumping" toward the bar or shifting weight onto the toes (stay flat-footed).
*   **Pull Mechanics:** Focus on turning elbows vertical and pulling straight up rather than pushing out. Drive through the hip joint for extension while keeping calves vertical during the pull phase.
*   **Spinal Rigidity:** Maintain a neutral spine acting as a rigid lever, particularly during accessory work (e.g., glute bridges). Avoid arching or rounding the lower back; support the upper back on the bench, not the neck.
*   **Grip Mechanics:** For athletes with smaller hands/fingers, adjust grip pressure to ensure stability without excessive discomfort. In the snatch, thumb wrap may be less full than in the clean; use magnesium carbonate carefully to avoid skin breakdown.

## Programming Principles
*   **Accessory Focus for Hypertrophy:** Prioritize Romanian Deadlifts (RDLs) and weighted Glute Bridges to correct quad dominance and strengthen the posterior chain. Perform these near failure with short rest intervals (20–30 seconds) to build endurance and muscle tension.
*   **Progression Logic:** If a lift fails due to technical error, reduce load slightly to rebuild confidence and technique before attempting a PR again. Avoid rushing max attempts immediately after a success.
*   **Exercise Sequencing:** While the athlete currently starts with squats for leg strength, the long-term goal is to begin sessions with Snatch/Clean once technical errors are eliminated.
*   **Deload & Recovery:** Stop or deload before starting a new cycle if fatigue is high ("nowhere to rush"). Listen to signs of "brain turning off" and stop the session if form degrades significantly due to fatigue.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Back Pain Management:** The emphasis on spinal rigidity during squats, RDLs, and glute bridges directly addresses chronic back pain by ensuring the spine acts as a stable anchor rather than a flexible lever under load.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** The principle of adjusting intensity based on daily mental state and circadian rhythm is vital for a night shift worker (Wed–Sun). Training should be flexible, acknowledging that hormonal fluctuations and sleep debt will impact performance.
*   **Transitioning Athlete Profile:** As an intermediate lifter with strong raw lifts (Squat 118kg) but limited Olympic technique, the focus on "cleaning up" errors and building tendon resilience is essential before attempting a Split Jerk or increasing Clean/Jerk loads significantly.
*   **Hypertrophy Goal Alignment:** The strategy of using high-volume sub-maximal work to thicken muscle fibers in the legs, back, and shoulder girdle supports the 6-week hypertrophy block goal, specifically targeting the posterior chain needed for Olympic stability.
