# Merged Summary: Russian Weightlifting Coaching Analysis

## Key Points

- **Mobility as movement foundation:** Achilles/calf tightness is the primary cause of heel lift, anterior pelvic tilt, and excessive forward lean in squat—directly linked to lower back stress
- **Intensity over volume:** High-frequency squat schemes (3–4× per session) are energy-inefficient; prioritize bar speed and load over rep accumulation
- **Technical proficiency precedes strength phases:** At intermediate level with developing clean technique (4–5 month progression), strength work is premature without solid movement foundation
- **Full range-of-motion requirement:** Partial-amplitude work causes tissue deconditioning and reinjury risk; maintain elasticity through consistent stimulus
- **Recovery markers matter:** Deload based on CNS fatigue and tendon freshness, not arbitrary schedules

## Technique Cues

**Squat:**
- Fix ankle mobility *before* squatting (prevents compensatory forward lean)
- Shin vertical, neutral knee (slight external rotation of feet, equal quad/glute/posterior chain loading)
- Avoid knee valgus collapse; activate external hip rotators for stability
- Vertical torso > forward lean (protects lumbar spine)

**Clean:**
- Control bar trajectory throughout pull; avoid "throwing" without command
- Full ROM standard; partial reps reinforce poor pattern

**General:**
- Explosive warm-up squats (not slow); rest 30–60 sec between warm-ups, 10–15 min for main sets
- Competition standard: ~60 sec between attempts
- Pelvic floor activation ("Kegel-type" contractions) for intra-abdominal pressure stability

## Programming Principles

- **Training frequency:** Increases with experience; heavy singles allow frequent training despite high CNS demand (short duration)
- **Intensity prescription:** 60–70% load with extended time under tension (30 sec) delivers stimulus efficiently without volume overhead
- **Recovery management:** Shift to technique work when compromised rather than forcing intensity
- **Aerobic capacity:** Low-intensity work (walking, cycling) builds mitochondrial capacity in legs—relevant for Olympic lifting endurance
- **Mobility prep:** Thoracic/shoulder mobility essential before upper-body work

## Athlete Relevance

1. **Chronic back pain (PRIORITY):** Forward lean in squat is major risk factor. Address Achilles mobility immediately—this is the mechanical root cause, not core weakness. Vertical torso emphasis protects lumbar spine.

2. **Ankle/Achilles tightness:** Likely driving heel lift and movement compensation. Pre-squat calf/Achilles mobilization non-negotiable.

3. **OHS limiter (50 kg):** Thoracic/shoulder mobility work essential; prioritize alongside ankle work.

4. **Clean technique (80 kg):** Technical foundation still developing—front-load hypertrophy block with controlled bar speed and full ROM before peak strength phase.

5. **Night shift fatigue:** High-frequency schemes inefficient for sleep-deprived athletes. Favor intensity (60–70% + speed) over volume; include low-intensity aerobic work to support recovery.

6. **Push jerk only (no split):** Continue technique refinement; strength phase premature without movement pattern mastery.
