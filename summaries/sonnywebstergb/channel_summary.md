# Coaching Philosophy: Sonny Webster GB (Synthesized)

### 1. Core Training Philosophy
*   **Technique Over Load:** The "quickest way to lift more" is improving Range of Motion (ROM), not just strength. Heavy loading without mobility is a primary cause of failure and injury. Prioritize mastering the bottom position before increasing intensity.
*   **Controlled Deceleration:** Athletes must learn to "beat the bar." Actively decelerate the bar at the peak of the pull to meet it under the hips, rather than letting gravity drop the weight onto the spine. This reduces spinal shear and protects chronic back pain.
*   **Vertical Force & Stability:** Visualize lifting "between two boxes" to ensure vertical force production. Avoid "jumping" or kicking the bar forward/backward by maintaining a consistent bar path (staying on the same part of the bar).
*   **Mobility as Prerequisite:** Do not rely on maximal deadlifts to fix Olympic lifts. If you cannot get under the bar, fix the mobility first. A "question mark" back indicates ankle or thoracic limitations that must be addressed via specific drills (Duck walks, Turtle squats) before heavy loading.

### 2. Programming Approach
*   **Structure:** Avoid using Power Snatches as a primary strength builder; they are accessory work for speed only. The Squat Snatch and Clean are the primary lifts to develop strength and technique safely.
*   **Periodization:** Implement a **6-week Hypertrophy Block** focused on volume and mobility integration, followed by a Technique/Strength block.
    *   *Hypertrophy:* Focus on controlled eccentrics (tempo squats), high-rep accessory work for overhead stability, and specific mobility drills (90 mins dedicated pre-lift).
    *   *Technique:* Low volume, high intensity of movement quality. Use "stripping down" (reducing load) to refine mechanics when fatigue sets in.
*   **Intensity Management:** Do not rush the dip in the Jerk; control the descent and maintain contact with the bar. For an athlete with chronic back pain, avoid "hitting and hoping." Stop attempts immediately if confidence or position falters to prevent injury.

### 3. Key Technique Cues & Exercise Preferences
*   **Snatch/Clean Setup:** Feet inside shoulder-width; bar over top of shoe, touching shins. Look up (sky/eyeline) to keep the chest from pointing down and maintain spinal extension.
*   **First Pull:** Lead with the chest/maintain back angle until mid-thigh. Avoid the "Stripper Snatch" error where legs straighten too early. Squeeze glutes ("bum") to lock the neutral spine.
*   **The Catch:** Tuck elbows immediately under the bar. Keep torso upright at the sticking point; do not let hips shoot back or fold. Use a "platform" of high elbows and loose lats/triceps for the front rack.
*   **Jerk (Push Jerk Only):** Do not rush the dip. Control the descent, fight against the bar's downward momentum before driving. Since no split jerk is used, overhead stability via Snatch Balance and OHS is critical to support the load without dynamic drive.
*   **Preferred Exercises:**
    *   *Mobility:* PVC Wall Slides, Duck Rocks, Weighted Hip Glides, Right Backs/Turtle Squats.
    *   *Strength/Technique:* Front Squats (no belt initially for core engagement, unless pain flares), Snatch Balance, Overhead Squats.

### 4. Recovery and Mobility Approach
*   **Pre-Lift Protocol:** Dedicate 90 minutes to mobility work before lifting sessions. Focus on ankles and thoracic extension to prevent the "bent leaf" back posture. Use specific warm-ups (PVC shapes, banded lockouts) rather than generic routines.
*   **Injury Management:** For chronic back pain, prioritize upright torso positions in squats (quad dominance) to reduce lumbar shear. If pain flares during beltless squats, revert to belt-assisted variations or substitute with safer mobility drills.
*   **Sleep & Schedule:** Acknowledge the night shift schedule (Wed–Sun). Recovery is non-negotiable; fatigue management must dictate intensity. Do not attempt max lifts when sleep debt from night shifts compromises technique.

### 5. Application to Athlete Profile (102.5 kg, Intermediate)
*   **Addressing Chronic Back Pain:** The athlete's back pain is likely driven by mobility deficits (ankles/thorax) causing a "question mark" spine during the clean and snatch. The coaching plan mandates thoracic extension drills and upright squatting cues to minimize lumbar shear. Deceleration cues are essential to prevent the bar from falling on the spine.
*   **Overhead Limiters:** With an OHS of 50kg, the athlete cannot safely handle heavy cleans/snatches without a stable platform. The plan prioritizes Snatch Balances and overhead stability work (loose lats, high elbows) over heavy pulling to build the necessary shoulder strength for the push jerk.
*   **Push Jerk Constraint:** Since the athlete lacks split jerk technique, all jerk volume must focus on the vertical drive and strict overhead lockout. The "fight" against the bar in the dip is critical to prevent double-weighting the shoulders.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** Programming will be conservative regarding intensity spikes during shift changes. The 6-week hypertrophy block serves as a bridge to build tissue resilience before introducing higher intensities, ensuring the athlete does not "hit and hope" when fatigued.
