# Merged Weightlifting Coaching Summary

## Key Points

- **Snatch reception is the primary technical limiter** for this athlete (OHS 50 kg); the session focuses entirely on automating receiving mechanics via light, high-rep work.
- **Athlete has significant squat depth reserve** (10–15 cm below competition depth); improved reception comfort could yield +10–15 kg snatch gain without additional loading.
- **Defer jerk training to a later block** to avoid mixing stimuli; prioritize snatch/pull mechanics first, then layer in jerk complexity.
- **Light loads + quick pacing** allow CNS adaptation without fatigue, enabling motor learning to proceed automatically ("record to subconscious") rather than through conscious overthinking.
- **Chronic back pain context:** Light snatch/pull work poses lower lumbar risk than heavy backend loading.

---

## Technique Cues

| Movement | Cue |
|----------|-----|
| **Snatch reception** | Narrow setup → spread legs wide on descent; plant full foot to full foot (consistent contact); receive bar on palm; relax between reps |
| **Snatch pull** | Keep bar close to body (avoid wide pathway); achieve full extension with trap/back involvement |
| **Snatch pull (paused)** | 3 stops: mid-shin, mid-thigh, power position; maintains pulling mechanics awareness |
| **Motor automation** | Reps 1 often overthought; reps 2–4 auto-correct at quick pace; minimize conscious cueing mid-set |

---

## Programming Principles

- **Exercise selection:**
  - Snatch pull with 3 pauses: 4–5 reps × light weight
  - Snatch (straps allowed): 4–5 reps × light weight, emphasis on speed
  - Trap shrugs as finisher
  - **Jerk work: defer to later block**

- **Loading logic:** "Simple to complex" — establish solid reception/pulling mechanics before adding jerk stimulus.

- **Pacing:** 1–2 sec rest between reps; quick execution minimizes thinking time and prevents fatigue accumulation.

- **Rep range:** 4–5 reps per set; light loading preserves technique quality over intensity.
