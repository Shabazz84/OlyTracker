## Technique Cues

### **Snatch Setup & Pull Sequence**
- **Start position**: Full foot contact, knees out (abduction), hips lower than shoulders, chest up, slight spine extension (back arch)
- **Bar path**: Stay just above kneecaps → maintain distance before recontacting thighs ("hit the pubic bone" vertically through midline, not scraping)
- **Power position (mid-pull checkpoint)**: Slight knee bend preserved, extended-back angle maintained—this is your weak point
- **Upper back rigidity**: Keep hands straight, upper back tense; relaxed shoulders collapse the position
- **Foot pressure**: Full foot contact maintained (not heel-dominant); knees drive outward during descent
- **Catch mechanics**: Aggressive leg drive outward, chest up, maintain back extension through receiving—do NOT abandon the movement after the pull
- **Emotional/nervous system phases**: Each checkpoint accented before transition; catch phase requires maximum tension; verbally acknowledge successful reps to reinforce nervous system memory

### **Pull Mechanics (Extended-Back Focus)**
- Drive through heels with hip extension angle as critical variable
- Energy sourced from posterior chain (hamstrings/back), then controlled with elbows
- "Like a bow and arrow"—load tension, then release
- Achieve full extension before receiving

---

## Programming Principles

- **No straps for snatch work**—develop grip/hand positioning without aids; technical errors shouldn't be masked
- **Progressive complexity**: Feet positioning → leg position → hip drive → upper body engagement → catch
- **Daily exercise**: Snatch pull from hang (knee height) in every session until movement feels effortless and automatic
- **Light touch-and-go work** (50–70 kg) post-snatch maintains nervous system arousal; prevents settling into comfortable but weak positions
- **Multi-set structure**: 2–3 sets with emotional/neurological management between attempts rather than strict passive rest
- **Intensity escalation**: Start (calm, controlled) → Pull/Drive (moderate intensity) → Catch (maximum aggression/tension)
