## 1. CORE TRAINING PHILOSOPHY

**a) Position Before Load**
Technical precision and receiving mechanics take absolute priority over weight lifted. Your 50 kg OHS ceiling despite 118 kg back squat confirms that **catching position quality, not leg strength, limits your snatch**. High, rigid receiving posture—with extended spine, active full-foot contact, and controlled descent—unlocks immediate gains (+10–15 kg snatch) before hypertrophy loading begins.

**b) Posterior Chain Dominance (Not Quad-Driven)**
You default to a "pusher" pattern (quad/glute dominance), creating short pull amplitude and weak extended-spine positioning. Snatch and clean pulls must be hip-driven (sequential leg drive → back extension), not knee-dominant. This corrects your pull mechanics AND protects your chronic back pain by distributing load across the posterior chain rather than forcing lumbar flexion.

**c) Motor Learning via Repetition, Not Maximal Attempts**
Light loads (50–75 kg) with high technical focus (4–5 reps × multiple sets) build subconscious movement patterns faster than heavy singles. This suits your night-shift fatigue; quality reps at manageable intensity preserve CNS function and allow motor automation to proceed without conscious overthinking.

**d) Vertical Bar Path & Positional Rigidity**
Minimize forward bar drift through maintaining back extension and chest position throughout the pull. Contact point matters: forehead contact (hard bone) redirects energy upward; soft-tissue absorption kills trajectory. Apply this to every snatch/clean rep, regardless of load.

**e) Recovery Constraints Dictate Frequency**
Elite athletes tolerate 16+ sessions/week; you require 4–5 sessions/week given chronic back pain, night shift disruption, and limited medical support access. Quality over volume. One focused weak point per session prevents cognitive overload on fatigue-compromised nights.

## 2. PROGRAMMING APPROACH

### **Structure: Position-First Block (Weeks 1–3 of Your 6-Week Hypertrophy Intent)**

**Immediate Priority (Before Hypertrophy Loading):**

| Phase | Focus | Exercises | Frequency |
|-------|-------|-----------|-----------|
| **Weeks 1–3** | Receiving Position Mastery | Snatch pulls (paused) + snatch squats + overhead holds + back extension work | 4x/week |
| **Weeks 4–6** | Technical Hypertrophy | Higher-volume snatch/clean variation + backend accessory (RDL, pause squats, trap bar) | 4x/week |

**Session Structure (4–5x/week, Evening Preferred):**

1. **Warm-up**: Horizontal back extension machine (mobility + spinal stability)
2. **Snatch pull from hang (paused)**: 3 stops (mid-shin, mid-thigh, power position) × 4–5 reps, light weight (50–70 kg)
3. **Snatch squat or full snatch**: 4–5 reps × light weight, emphasis on HIGH rigid catch
4. **Overhead hold or back extension with load**: Postural stability reinforcement
5. **Optional finisher**: Trap shrugs or light pull variation (1–2 sets, sub-maximal)

**Rest Between Reps:** 1–2 seconds; quick pacing minimizes thinking and prevents fatigue accumulation.

### **Periodization Model**

- **Intensity Distribution (Weekly):**
  - Mon: 90% of daily max (maximal-effort snatch/clean)
  - Tue: 70–80% (receiving position work, accessory)
  - Wed: OFF or 50–60% mobility work
  - Fri: 90–95% (test snatch/clean)
  - Sat: 70–75% (technical drill, light pulls)
  - Sun–Thu: OFF (shift work recovery)

- **Macro-Progression (6-Week Block):**
  - **Weeks 1–3:** Establish vertical pull mechanics and high-catch comfort at submaximal weights (70–75 kg). Drill snatch pauses, snatch squats, overhead static holds. OHS work with emphasis on position over load.
  - **Weeks 4–6:** Layer in volume (higher reps per set or additional sets) while maintaining technical priority. Introduce modest load increases (80–85 kg) only if position remains rigid. Add RDL variations and backend accessory for posterior chain resilience.

- **Competition Integration:** 4 competition lifts/month minimum (snatch, clean, jerk attempts at 75–90%) embedded in regular training; defer heavy jerk work to strength block (Week 7+) to avoid mixing stimuli.
