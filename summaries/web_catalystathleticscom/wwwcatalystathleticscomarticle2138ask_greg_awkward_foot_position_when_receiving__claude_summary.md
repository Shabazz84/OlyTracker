# Analysis: Awkward Foot Position in Snatch & Clean Receive

## Relevant to This Athlete

**High relevance:** Low OHS (50 kg) suggests mobility restrictions and foot/ankle issues are likely limiting snatch depth and positioning—this article directly addresses that limiter.

---

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- None explicitly stated (diagnostic/technical focus)

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Consistent stance across all lifts:** Back squat, front squat, overhead squat, snatch, clean should use identical foot placement
- **Foot placement drill:** Chalk outline your desired stance; check alignment every rep
- **Common error:** Feet landing on inner edges suggests feet are too wide → forces toe-out and knee valgus

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
**For snatch foot positioning:**
- Drop snatch, snatch balance, tall snatch, snatch from power position, dip snatch
- Pause snatches & overhead squats (bottom holds strengthen positional awareness)

**For clean foot positioning:**
- Tall clean, high-hang clean, clean from high blocks

**General:**
- Pause squats (all varieties, especially warm-up)
- Single-leg work (if mobility asymmetry present)

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- **Primary suspect for this athlete:** Tight ankles limiting dorsiflexion → feet compensate by turning out and rolling inward
- **Long-term commitment required**—mobility work isn't quick; consistency needed
- Refer to Everett's mobility articles/videos (not detailed here)

## 5. **Specific to This Athlete**
- **OHS limiter (50 kg)** likely stems from ankle mobility + foot/stance instability
- Prioritize pause OHS + footwork drills before jerk work intensifies
- Back pain + night shift = mobility work should be non-negotiable; fits hypertrophy block recovery phase well
