# Analysis: "Multidisciplinarity in Weightlifting Programming" — Greg Everett

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Efficiency focus**: Address multiple training needs within volume constraints—avoid excessive exercises that exceed recovery capacity
- **Complexes as primary tool**: Stack exercises to hit multiple qualities (e.g., Jerk Drive + Jerk; Snatch + OHS; Clean + Front Squat)
- **Exercise modification**: Alter single movements to add secondary benefits without extra time (e.g., Press in Split stance instead of standard press)
- **Volume/frequency trade-off**: Accept slightly lower individual loads if multidimensional work provides equivalent or superior training effect

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Jerk hold**: Hold split position 3–5 seconds, then hold overhead 3–5 seconds after recovery to demand stability & receiving position accuracy
- **Complex structure example**: 3 Jerk Drives + 1–3 Jerks with holds (drives fatigue the motion before actual jerks, forcing harder drive effort)

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- Jerk Drive + Jerk complex
- Snatch/Clean Pull + Snatch/Clean
- Snatch + OHS
- Clean + Front Squat
- Push Press + Jerk
- Press in Split (vs. standard press)

## 4. **Directly Relevant to This Athlete**
- **OHS weakness (50 kg)**: Snatch + OHS complex would build OHS strength without adding session volume
- **No split jerk**: Press in Split stance could build split stability as a technical transition prep
- **Chronic back pain + night shift**: Multidimensional approach minimizes total volume, reducing systemic fatigue and recovery demand

---
**Bottom line**: Use complexes and exercise variations to pack snatch/jerk-specific work into your hypertrophy block without grinding yourself down.
