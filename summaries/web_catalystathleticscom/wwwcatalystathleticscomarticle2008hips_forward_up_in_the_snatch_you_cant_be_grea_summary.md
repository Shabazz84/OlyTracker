# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2008/Hips-Forward-Up-In-The-Snatch-You-Cant-Be-Great-Without-It/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Not explicitly defined in this text; however, the author emphasizes that mastering this specific technical element requires "a long time" and "lots of practice," implying high frequency of technical drills over a long duration rather than short-term intensity spikes.
*   **Intensity:** The text warns against introducing aggressive hip extension ("pushing hips into the bar") at low intensities or early stages, as it causes the bar to fly forward (loop). It should only be emphasized when the athlete has sufficient strength and technique stability.

**2. Technique cues for snatch**
*   **Primary Cue:** "Extend your hips forward and up" simultaneously during the second pull finish. The goal is a "snappy lift" where force is generated into the bar while maintaining proximity.
*   **Mental Reminders (Author's personal):** 1) Back tight off the floor, 2) Push the hips as hard as possible, 3) Finish with the arms.
*   **Alternative Cue:** "Uppercut" the bar rather than "jabbing" it (a quick, clean, powerful punch).
*   **Critical Warning:** Do not push hips into the bar if you are an inexperienced lifter; this causes a "loop" and poor technique. The movement must be timed correctly to avoid early contact.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Snatch Pulls:** Specifically with no arm bend, focusing on toes and shrug at the top.
*   **3-Position Snatch Pulls.**
*   **Snatch High Pulls:** Focusing on bringing the bar to the top of the abdomen.
*   *Note:* The author stresses these must be performed correctly; otherwise, they are useless regardless of volume.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Pain Management:** If hip contact causes pain (specifically at the pubic bone), adjust grip slightly or correct timing/explosion. Do not force contact if it hurts.
*   **Equipment:** A pad may be used temporarily to allow training through discomfort, but technique adjustment is preferred over padding.

**5. Relevance to athlete's profile**
*   **Intermediate Status:** The text explicitly targets beginners/intermediates (under 5 years) struggling with the snatch. It cautions that this specific "hip drive" technique is a "magic secret" for elites but dangerous if attempted too early by novices.
*   **Back Pain:** The author notes that improper hip contact causes pain, and emphasizes keeping the bar close to the abdomen *after* the drive to maintain balance and prevent injury.
*   **Jerk Limitation (Push Jerk only):** This article focuses exclusively on the **Snatch**. It does not address the push jerk or split jerk mechanics relevant to this athlete's specific limitation.
*   **Night Shift/Chronic Pain:** While the text mentions pain management regarding hip contact, it offers no specific advice for shift work recovery or chronic back pain management outside of technical form correction.
