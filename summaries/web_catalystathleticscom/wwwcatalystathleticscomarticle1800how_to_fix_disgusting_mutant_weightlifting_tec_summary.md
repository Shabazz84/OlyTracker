# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1800/How-To-Fix-Disgusting-Mutant-Weightlifting-Technique/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Methodology:** "Strip and Rebuild." Reduce load to empty bar or very light weights (e.g., 70% of old max) for an extended period (4–8 weeks minimum).
*   **Progression:** Strictly linear weight increase only after technique is solidified at the current level. If "mutant" (sloppy) form returns, immediately drop intensity and reset.
*   **Frequency/Volume:** High frequency of technical practice with low mechanical stress; prioritize quality over quantity or load.
*   **Periodization:** Non-linear during the repair phase; the focus is on motor learning rather than strength accumulation until the new pattern is ingrained.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Primary Cue:** "Move correctly" before adding any significant weight.
*   **Focus:** Eliminate "mutant" habits (sloppy, high-rep style movements) by focusing on precision and efficiency.
*   **Goal:** Achieve a "snappy," clean movement pattern where the athlete can eventually lift their previous max with perfect form.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Tool:** Empty barbell drills (broomstick or light bar).
*   **Drills:** Specific technical corrections for Snatch and Clean & Jerk components to replace old motor patterns.
*   **Loading:** Light-to-moderate weights only during the re-education phase; no max attempts until technique is stable.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   *None explicitly mentioned in this text.* The article focuses entirely on technical retraining and load management, not specific mobility work or recovery protocols for chronic pain or shift work.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, 102.5kg, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain:** This protocol is highly relevant. By stripping the weight down and removing heavy spinal loading during the re-education phase, it allows the athlete to fix mechanics without aggravating chronic back pain.
*   **Technique Transition:** Ideal for an intermediate athlete transitioning from powerlifting/CrossFit styles (implied by "mutant" description) to Olympic lifting. The text explicitly warns against rushing back to max weights, which is critical for a lifter with poor technique history.
*   **Discipline Requirement:** Requires high discipline to resist the urge to "max out," which may be challenging given the athlete's intermediate strength levels (118kg squat).
*   **Night Shift:** The text does not address sleep or circadian rhythm issues, but the low-intensity nature of the re-education phase makes it physically less demanding on a fatigued system.
