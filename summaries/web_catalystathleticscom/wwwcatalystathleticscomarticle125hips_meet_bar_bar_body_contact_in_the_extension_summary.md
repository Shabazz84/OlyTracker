**2. Technique cues for snatch, clean, or jerk**
*   **Bar Proximity:** Keep the bar as close to the thighs as possible without dragging; shoulders must remain slightly in front of the bar until it reaches the hips/thighs.
*   **Hip Extension:** Execute an "extremely violent" hip snap where the hips move forward *into* the bar (not reaching for it), ensuring full contact at the top of the extension.
*   **Vertical Force:** Prevent horizontal force by maintaining a vertical line through shoulder-hip-ankle; do not let hips cross this plane forward during extension.
*   **Leg Drive:** Leg drive must continue as long as hip extension occurs to ensure force is directed upward, not forward.
*   **Torso Position:** Do not finish the lift with a vertical torso; the hips must open beyond neutral (leaning back) to bring shoulders behind the bar for the pull-under.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Dip variations of Snatch/Clean:** Recommended in comments to improve leg drive and ensure full knee extension before pulling under.

**5. Anything specifically relevant to this athlete's profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The article emphasizes that "banging" hips into the bar (if done correctly with leg drive) allows for a faster reversal and reduces the need for excessive upper body pulling, which can mitigate spinal shear if the lifter avoids "dragging" the bar. However, the athlete must ensure they do not compensate by leaning too far forward or hyperextending the lumbar spine to make contact.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** The text discusses Clean and Snatch mechanics extensively; the principle of maintaining a vertical bar path and violent hip extension applies directly to the transition from the Clean into the Push Jerk, but specific cues for the jerk are absent.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The article reinforces that there is no single "correct" style (e.g., "brush" vs. "bang"); the athlete should experiment with a slight "bump" contact if it allows for better vertical acceleration without pushing the bar forward, rather than forcing a rigid "drag."
