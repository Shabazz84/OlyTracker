**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Volume/Frequency:** For busy adults who haven't solidified technique, avoid "packing the week" with excessive assistance work. Prioritize mastering the snatch and clean & jerk before adding specialized upper body volume.
*   **Intensity/Progression:** Upper body strength is a "backup" asset (like an M-60 machine gun); it supports but does not drive the lift. Do not let high pressing strength lead to "muscling" the bar overhead, which ruins leg-drive technique in the jerk.
*   **Periodization:** Use upper body work to address specific deficiencies or build a stronger shoulder girdle, but ensure it doesn't compromise flexibility or technique acquisition.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Front Squat Posture:** Maintain a disciplined front rack (wide chest spread, upright torso). Do not allow "rounded turtleback" positions or fingers popping off the bar; the entire upper body musculature must contract to stabilize posture.
*   **Jerk Execution:** Rely on lower body power and efficient positioning rather than muscular pressing strength. Avoid "muscling" the bar overhead if you have high pressing strength, as this creates long-term technique flaws.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Assistance:** Push Press (best for training leg drive in the jerk), BTN Snatch-Grip Push Press, BTN Military Press.
*   **Secondary/Specific:** Front Squats (crucial for upper body posture and stability).
*   **Optional:** Bodybuilding isolation work (curls, tricep extensions) only if there is a basic deficiency; avoid if the athlete already has tight/muscular upper bodies that limit flexibility.
*   **Avoid:** Relying solely on "wacky" YouTube exercises without mastering basics first.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Flexibility Warning:** Excessive bodybuilding-style hypertrophy work can be a major hindrance for lifters who already have tight upper bodies, reducing necessary mobility.
*   **Mobility Focus:** Use BTN Military Presses as a tool to develop flexibility alongside strength.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, 102.5kg, Chronic Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Management:** The text emphasizes the Front Squat for upper body *posture*. For an athlete with chronic back pain, ensuring a "disciplined front rack" and upright torso is critical to prevent rounding and spinal shear. However, heavy overhead pressing (Push Press/Military) may aggravate existing shoulder/back issues if mobility is poor; proceed cautiously.
*   **Night Shift/Busy Schedule:** The article explicitly warns against overloading the week with assistance exercises for busy adults who haven't mastered technique. This supports a conservative approach: focus on the 6-week hypertrophy block using compound lifts (Front Squats, Push Press) rather than complex accessory variations.
*   **Jerk Technique:** Since this athlete uses only a push jerk and has no split jerk experience, the warning against "muscling" overhead is vital. High upper body strength can cause them to rely on arms rather than leg drive; they must prioritize technique over raw pressing numbers.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** The text suggests that if an athlete has high pressing strength but poor jerk technique, it hinders progress. Since the OHS is a limiter, the athlete should focus on *mobility* and *posture* (via BTN presses/Front Squats) rather than just raw benching or strict pressing volume, which could tighten shoulders further.
