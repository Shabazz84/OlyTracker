## Weightlifting Content: YES

---

### **1. Programming Principles**
- **Volume/intensity approach**: High-intensity work (referenced "Alyssa's Heavy Single Nightmare") forces correct positioning adaptation; commenter notes high-intensity weights resolved instability issues.
- **Exercise selection**: Overhead squats and snatch balances with 5-second bottom holds for stability work.

---

### **2. Technique Cues (Snatch Bottom Position)**
- **Bar position**: Vertically over back of neck, bar in palms over forearms (not behind them).
- **Shoulder blade positioning**: Fully retracted and upwardly rotated—"squeeze upper inside edges together forcefully."
- **Elbow position**: Completely extended to end range (not locked passively).
- **Torso**: Slight forward lean acceptable; head pushed forward through arms.
- **Mental approach**: Must be "aggressive, continuous effort"—bar won't stay overhead passively.
- **Common errors causing bar drop**:
  - Bar-body system shifting forward during lift (chasing bar forward).
  - Bar "crashing" into overhead position instead of staying connected during turnover.
  - Jumping forward.

---

### **3. Exercise Recommendations**
- Overhead squats (empty bar → loaded).
- Snatch balances with 5-second bottom holds.
- **Mobility test**: OHS with standard foot position → wider stance → heels elevated on change plates to diagnose ankle vs. hip restriction.

---

### **4. Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- **Ankle mobility** (elevated heels improve position).
- **Hip mobility** (wide stance, toes-out positioning).
- **T-spine/ankle mobility** highlighted as critical by responder who resolved issue.

---

### **5. Athlete-Specific Relevance**
- **OHS 50 kg (snatch limiter)**: This article directly addresses OHS stability. Mobility diagnostics essential given your low OHS.
- **Chronic back pain**: Aggressive trunk pressurization cue requires careful monitoring; ensure correct positioning, not compensation.
- **No split jerk**: OHS stability work transfers directly to jerk receiving position.
