# Analysis: Back Arch in Snatch & Clean (Greg Everett)

## Relevant to Athlete Profile

**TECHNIQUE CUES (High Priority Given OHS/Snatch Limiter):**
- **Back arch setup**: Complete extension from lumbar to cervical spine—exaggerate lumbar lordosis slightly, flatten thoracic kyphosis as much as possible
- **Rationale**: Creates rigid lever arm, shortens back length (reduces lever mechanical disadvantage), protects against lumbar flexion injury
- **Head position**: Keep upright (cervical extension)—increases overall extension force capacity
- **Lat engagement**: Activate naturally via upper back extension; keeps bar close during pull
- **Core**: Pressurize and create circumferential muscular tension before final starting position; take breath before compression

**MOBILITY RECOMMENDATIONS (Direct to Chronic Back Pain):**
- Pancake stretches (limited effectiveness for maintaining arch)
- Spiderman stretch (maintaining lumbar arch)
- Lying hamstring stretch with band (maintaining lumbar arch)
- **Back extensions with holds**—directly trains extension capacity
- **Snatch deadlift negatives** & **floating snatch DLs**—practice arch maintenance standing position to bottom

**Key Warning for This Athlete:**
Thoracic immobility and hip mobility limitations commonly prevent proper arch. Both must be addressed early. OHS at 50 kg suggests mobility constraint—prioritize T-spine and hip work before 6-week hypertrophy block.

**NOT addressed**: Programming, periodization, night-shift recovery specifics.
