# Analysis for Your Athlete Profile

## **Technique Cues (Snatch Pull)**
- **Core issue:** Hips rising faster than chest = inadequate leg strength, not technique awareness
- **Key cue:** "Push with legs as if squatting the bar up" rather than yanking—tight, controlled separation from floor
- **Position goals:** Shoulders directly above or slightly in front of bar initially; stay in front of bar after knee as long as possible
- **Warning:** Avoid overcorrection; some forward lean is biomechanically necessary

---

## **Exercise Recommendations (Priority-Ranked for Your Athlete)**

**Critical for OHS/snatch limiter (50 kg):**
1. **Segment snatch pulls** (pauses 1" off floor + at knee, 2–5 sec holds)
2. **Snatch pulls from low hang** (below knee to 1" off floor)
3. **Halting snatch deadlifts** (pause at mid-thigh/hip)
4. **Snatches with mid-thigh pause**

**Back pain consideration:**
- Prioritize **stiff-legged deadlifts/RDLs, good mornings, weighted back extensions** to strengthen lumbar extension capacity
- Slow eccentrics in pulls (if form stays strict)
- *Caution:* Heavy back-loaded movements may aggravate chronic pain; consider load management

**Strength foundation:**
- Emphasize **upright-posture squats** (directly supports snatch pull posture + your BS:Snatch ratio suggests squat deficiency)

---

## **Programming Relevance**
- Fits a **6-week hypertrophy block:** Segment pulls + pause work + accessory volume align well
- Consider **reduced snatch pull loading** temporarily while building leg/back strength to prevent cementing poor position

---

## **Recovery Notes**
- Night shift (Wed–Sun) + chronic back pain = prioritize sleep consistency and mobility on off-days (Mon–Tue)
- Pause/slow eccentric work is fatigue-friendly for shift workers
