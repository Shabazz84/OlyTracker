# Analysis: "The First Pull" by Greg Everett

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- No explicit sets/reps/intensity prescribed in this article
- Focus is purely technical/educational, not a training program
- Mentions "maximal effort" application to first pull on heavy lifts vs. controlled tempos on light warm-ups

## 2. **Technique Cues for Snatch & Clean**

**Key First Pull Cues:**
- Bar starts above balls of foot; shoulders directly above or slightly in front of bar
- Weight balanced over whole foot throughout
- Bar moves slightly backward toward body (natural, not forced)
- Shoulders move forward as hips move forward and knees extend
- Maintain constant speed or acceleration—never decelerate mid-pull
- Break from floor controlled (not violent) to maintain position/balance
- Apply maximal force/effort even if bar speed is lower than second pull

**Common Errors & Fixes:**
- **Tipping**: Don't force back angle change; knees naturally move out of bar path with normal leg extension
- **Bar hitting knees**: Start position must have shoulders above/slightly in front of bar; avoid premature hip opening
- **Too slow first pull**: Acceptable during learning/light warm-ups only; once learned, apply maximal effort (bar will be slower naturally due to mechanics)
- **Too fast/ripping**: Only appropriate for elite lifters; newer athletes with low max-to-strength ratio should control speed to match heavy lift timing
- **Losing rebalance**: Bar must shift back slightly; if rushed, lifter chases bar forward

**Corrective Exercises:**
- Halting/segment deadlifts & pulls (snatch/clean variations)
- Paused snatches/cleans
- Slow-pull snatches
- Complexes (deadlift + snatch; pull + snatch)
- Riser variations (snatch pull on riser, floating deadlifts on riser)
- Pause back squats

## 3. **Relevance to This Athlete**

**Highly relevant:**
- **Low OHS (50 kg)** = weak receiving position; first pull technique errors compound this problem. Correct positioning early is critical.
- **Back pain**: Excessive "tipping" or forward lean forces compensatory loading. Controlled first pull with proper position reduces spinal stress.
- **No split jerk + weak clean**: Technical foundation matters more; mastering first pull mechanics could improve clean (currently 80 kg vs. 118 kg squat = significant gap).
- **Night shift fatigue**: Controlled, technical work (halting deadlifts, paused pulls, segment work) suits recovery constraints better than maximal grinding.
