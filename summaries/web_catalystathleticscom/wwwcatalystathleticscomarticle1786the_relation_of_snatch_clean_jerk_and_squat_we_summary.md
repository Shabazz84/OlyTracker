**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Diagnostic Approach:** Do not use rigid formulas; ratios are diagnostic tools to identify if an athlete is "strong but slow" vs. "fast but weak."
*   **Target Ratios (Loose Guidelines):** Back Squat should be 125–135% of the Clean & Jerk; Snatch should be 78–83% of the Clean & Jerk.
*   **Strategy:** If an athlete is a strong squatter but has low competition lifts, prioritize technique, speed, and pulling strength over raw squat volume. If ratios are stable and total is progressing, do not force drastic changes to hit specific percentages.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Snatch Limiters:** Check for poor bottom position mobility, incomplete elbow extension, or short arms (which lower the snatch relative to the clean).
*   **Clean & Jerk Limiters:** Address fear of pulling under heavy weights into a deep receiving position; long limbs are a disadvantage here compared to the snatch.
*   **General:** Ensure technical proficiency and explosiveness before assuming strength is the limiting factor.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   *Note: The text does not list specific accessory exercises.* It suggests using "technique, speed, and pulling strength" drills for athletes with high squat-to-lift gaps.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Mobility:** Poor snatch bottom position mobility is a primary cause of low snatch ratios; flexibility can be addressed through training.
*   **Body Composition:** High bodyweight relative to height can negatively impact the snatch ratio; nutrition and weight management are relevant levers.
