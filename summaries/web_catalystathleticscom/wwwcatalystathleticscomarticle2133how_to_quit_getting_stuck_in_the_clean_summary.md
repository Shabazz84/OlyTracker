# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2133/How-to-Quit-Getting-Stuck-in-the-Clean/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Focus:** Isolate specific failure points (balance, turnover speed, trunk pressurization) rather than just increasing load.
*   **Frequency/Volume:** High frequency of technical drills (tall cleans, muscle cleans, slow-turnover variations) to ingrain motor patterns; moderate volume for heavy squats to build postural strength.
*   **Intensity:** Use sub-maximal loads for technique drills (80-90% focus on mechanics); use heavier loads only when posture and balance are stable.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Balance:** "Push straight down evenly across the whole foot" (slight heel preference) to load the bar like a coil spring; avoid pushing one edge of the floor.
*   **Turnover Speed:** "Get the bar racked at the same time your feet reconnect with the platform."
*   **Rack Position:** "Keep grip as long as possible"; do not release hook grip until shoulders touch and elbows rise; "Reach shoulders/chest up to meet the bar" rather than letting it crash.
*   **Trunk Stability:** "Get a big breath of air visible to an observer *before* starting the pull," then cinch down; maintain active tension throughout the lift (no sagging).
*   **Aggression:** "Blast out of the bottom like you have rockets strapped to your ass"; accelerate up the entire way without reduction in effort.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **For Balance/Posture:** Pause front squats, pause back squats, segment deadlifts, slow-pull cleans, clean pull + clean.
*   **For Turnover Speed:** Tall cleans, power cleans, cleans from high hang or high blocks.
*   **For Trunk Strength/Tension:** Back extensions (with hold in extension), crunches, planks.
*   **For Rack Security:** Muscle cleans, tall cleans.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Mobility:** Prioritize improving shoulder/elbow mobility for a secure rack position; address trunk compression issues via breathing drills.
*   **Mental/Recovery:** Use positive self-talk and "angry music" to build aggression/confidence before heavy attempts.

**5. Athlete-Specific Relevance**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly links "inadequate trunk pressurization" (lack of air) and "weak/inactive trunk" to spinal collapse and forward dumping, which are primary causes of back pain in lifters. Emphasizing the "big breath before pull" and active trunk tension is critical for this athlete's safety.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** The text notes that failing to rack quickly results in a "loaded eccentric movement" where the lifter sits at the bottom, making recovery harder. Since this athlete lacks split jerk technique, perfecting the clean-to-squat transition and trunk stability is even more vital to maximize their push jerk potential without losing balance.
*   **Night Shift Work:** The emphasis on "mental aggression," "confidence," and avoiding a passive approach ("shuffling over") is crucial for an athlete with irregular sleep patterns who may struggle with pre-lift focus or fatigue management.
