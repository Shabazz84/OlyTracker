# Analysis: Knee Valgus in the Squat (Greg Everett)

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- Slow eccentric and pause squats during early training phases for control development
- Unilateral/staggered variations (RDLs, split squats, lunges) for general hip stability across cycles
- Corrective work integrated into training rather than isolated

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Squat position goal**: Thigh-to-foot alignment (~knee at 2nd toe); minor variation based on anatomy
- Feet turned out 5–30° depending on structure to open hips and maintain upright posture
- **"Knees Out" cue misuse**: Only cue if valgus appears mid-recovery; intended to restore hip position and prevent forward trunk lean, not as primary recovery driver
- Follow "knees out" with "hips in" to regain upright posture
- **Jerk dip/pull valgus**: Actively correct—negatively affects lift execution

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- Single-leg/staggered RDLs
- Split squats and lunge variations
- Adductor and internal rotator prep work (corrective focus, not lateral hip strengthening)
- Pause/slow eccentric squats (early block)
- Avoid mini-band squats (ineffective per Everett's experience)

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- Knee valgus is **not** a mobility issue—if knees align during descent, mobility is adequate
- Focus on hip stability/control rather than isolated lateral hip strengthening

## 5. **Relevance to Your Athlete**
- **Back pain + weak OHS (50 kg)**: Poor squat posture from valgus collapse directly stresses lumbar spine; corrective adductor/internal rotator work supports spinal stability
- Unilateral variations (RDLs, split squats) help address asymmetrical loading from shift work fatigue
- Pause squats during hypertrophy block improve control before strength work
- If valgus appears in clean dip/snatch pull, must correct—directly impacts 80 kg clean and 65 kg jerk technique
