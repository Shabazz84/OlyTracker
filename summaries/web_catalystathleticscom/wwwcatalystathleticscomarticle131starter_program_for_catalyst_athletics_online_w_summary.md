## Key Points
*   **Program Structure:** Execute a 4-week hypertrophy/strength block followed by a "deload" week (Week 1 volume or reduced intensity) before cycling again.
*   **Schedule & Frequency:** Train 3–4 days per week (e.g., Mon, Wed, Thu, Sat). For night shift workers, rest days and heavy lifting sessions can be rotated (e.g., moving Saturday lifts to Friday afternoon) to align with sleep cycles; prioritize sleep before technical lifts.
*   **Progression Logic:** Use "weight by feel" rather than fixed percentages. Increase load weekly only if technique remains crisp. Stop sets while still having 1–2 reps in reserve (RPE <10); never train to failure on main lifts.
*   **Rest Protocols:** Minimum 2 minutes between Olympic lift sets; 3–5 minutes for squats/accessories. Reset completely between every rep (dead stop) to ensure spinal safety and technical precision.
*   **Conditioning Constraint:** Limit conditioning to <5 minutes, max 2x/week. Avoid leg-intensive modalities (e.g., rowing, heavy running) that compound fatigue from the squatting demands.

## Technique Cues
*   **Snatch Receiving:** Do not aim for "rock bottom" on every rep initially; meet the bar at its highest point and use the remainder of the squat to stabilize overhead. As strength increases, meet the bar lower. Focus on pulling *yourself* under the bar rather than just pulling the bar high (prevents power snatching).
*   **Overhead Stability:** "Ride" the bar into the squat; do not collapse under it. If arms bend or chest folds at the bottom of an OHS, focus on external rotation of the humerus and turning over high to maintain posture.
*   **Pull Mechanics:** Treat pulls as abbreviated full lifts to reinforce posture. Grip width dictates the lift (wide for snatch, narrow for clean). For back pain management, ensure strict control during descent; avoid "touch-and-go" or bouncing on the floor.
*   **Jerk Execution:** Since utilizing a Push Jerk only: Focus on vertical drive and overhead stability. While Split Jerks are generally preferred for long-term development, the program allows Power/Push Jerks as valid alternatives for athletes with limited shoulder mobility or specific technique constraints.

## Programming Principles
*   **Exercise Selection:**
    *   **Primary:** Snatch, Clean & Jerk (Power variations acceptable), Hang variants.
    *   **Squats:** Front Squat is primary; Back Squat can be added for strength but requires careful volume management given chronic back pain. Overhead Squat (OHS) is critical for addressing the specific 50kg limiter.
    *   **Accessories:** Daily ab work and back extensions are mandatory for spinal support. Upper body "beach work" (pull-ups, rows, presses) 2–3x/week. Hamstring work (SLDL/RDL) only if weakness is identified.
*   **Notation & Execution:** "2+1" denotes 2 reps of Lift A immediately followed by 1 rep of Lift B in the same set. Perform all Olympic lifts from a dead stop to reset spinal position and tempo.
*   **Pain Management:** If squatting causes pain, utilize partial squats (above 90 degrees) or blocks/hang positions to adjust bar height rather than forcing full depth immediately. Prioritize structural integrity over volume.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Transitioning Intermediate:** The program is explicitly designed for athletes with a solid strength base (e.g., Squat >100kg) moving from powerlifting or general fitness to Olympic lifting, matching the user's background.
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The mandatory daily core/back extension work and emphasis on "feeling" over heavy loading initially provide a safe entry point for pain management. The strict reset requirement between reps reduces spinal compression risks compared to rapid cycling.
*   **Night Shift Constraints:** The flexibility to swap training days (e.g., moving Saturday sessions) allows the athlete to align heavy lifting with peak alertness/sleep windows, provided adequate rest is prioritized before technical work.
*   **Specific Limiters:** The inclusion of progressive OHS volume directly targets the 50kg overhead limitation. The allowance for Push Jerk-only execution accommodates the user's current technique constraints while building shoulder strength.
