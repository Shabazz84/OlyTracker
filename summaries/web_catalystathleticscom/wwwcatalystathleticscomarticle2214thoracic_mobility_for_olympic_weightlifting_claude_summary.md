# Analysis: Thoracic Mobility for Olympic Weightlifting (Greg Everett)

## Relevant to This Athlete

**1. Recovery & Mobility Advice (HIGHLY RELEVANT)**
- **T-spine mobility is the priority limiter**: Article directly addresses OHS weakness (athlete's 50 kg OHS vs 80 kg Clean). T-spine extension is foundational for overhead positions.
- **Chronic back pain consideration**: All exercises emphasize avoiding T/L junction hinging and lumbar over-arching—critical for athlete's back pain history.
- **Night shift worker note**: "Can't overcome 12 hours of hunching"—postural work essential; mobility work must be frequent/consistent, not just warm-up.

**2. Exercise Recommendations (DIRECTLY APPLICABLE)**
- **Foam rolling T-spine** (warm-up/cool-down): Keep trunk straight, don't arch over roller
- **Leaning bar hangs** (warm-up/cool-down): Traction + shoulder/lat stretch
- **Quadruped T-spine rotations** (warm-up/cool-down): Tension release
- **Clean-grip OHS** (2–3x/week): Build T-spine extension strength; athlete should progress gradually from PVC → empty bar
- **Dumbbell back extensions** (2–3x/week, end of workout): 10–20 reps, controlled tempo; complement hypertrophy block
- **Behind-the-neck pressing**: Only after baseline mobility achieved (not ready yet)

**3. Programming Principle**
- Frequency over volume: Accumulated time/reps across week beats single sessions
- Include pauses and slower tempos in lifts for sport-specific mobility

**NOT COVERED**: Snatch/clean/jerk technique cues, or periodization for 6-week hypertrophy block.
