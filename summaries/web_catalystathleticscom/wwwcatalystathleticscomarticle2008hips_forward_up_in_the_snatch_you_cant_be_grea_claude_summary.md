# Analysis: "Hips Forward & Up In The Snatch"

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Emphasis on technique mastery over intensity** for intermediate lifters (under 5 years experience)
- **Progression timeline:** Requires 10+ years of dedicated training and "standing in line" before mastering aggressive hip drive
- **Coaching judgment needed:** Don't cue aggressive hip extension too early—premature implementation causes bar loop/forward flight
- **Assistance exercises:** Snatch pulls (no arm bend, shrug only), 3-position snatch pulls, snatch high pulls—must be executed correctly to be effective

## 2. **Technique Cues for Snatch**
- **Hip drive cue:** Push hips "forward AND up" simultaneously (not just forward)
- **Auditory feedback:** Elite lifters produce an audible "BOOM" or contact sound when hips drive into bar—indicates force generation
- **Balance:** Aggressive hip extension ONLY works when combined with correct arm usage to keep bar close to body
- **Personal cues (Foreman's example):** Back tight, push hips hard, finish with arms
- **Counterpoint (Greg Everett in comments):** Motion should happen *naturally*—don't intentionally "scoop"; emphasize knees forward, then hips up naturally

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- Snatch pulls (no arm bend)
- 3-position snatch pulls
- Snatch high pulls (bar to top of abdomen)

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- **Pain note:** Aggressive hip contact can cause pubic bone pain if grip/timing is poor or anatomy doesn't suit it—adjust grip width or focus on brushing contact instead
- No specific recovery protocols mentioned

## 5. **Relevance to This Athlete**
⚠️ **CRITICAL FOR YOUR PROFILE:**
- **OHS 50 kg limiter + chronic back pain:** Aggressive hip drive requires strong lumbar stability. Address back issues *first* before emphasizing hip extension intensity
- **Intermediate stage (clean 80 kg):** You're ready to *learn* this movement but likely not yet ready to cue it aggressively—risk of bar forward flight is high
- **Night shift (Wed–Sun):** Fatigue management critical; motor learning for this complex cue requires fresh CNS
- **6-week hypertrophy block incoming:** Use this block to build spinal stability and hip extensors; introduce the cue conservatively in technique phase afterward

**Action:** Start with snatch pulls focusing on *natural* hip extension; avoid forcing aggressive contact until back strength improves.
