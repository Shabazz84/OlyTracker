# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2261/Better-Snatch-Overhead-Stability/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Isometric Holds:** Incorporate 3–5 second forceful holds at the bottom of overhead squats (OHS), snatch balances, and after receiving snatches. Eliminate all motion during these seconds to reinforce structure.
*   **Complexes:** Add an OHS or snatch balance immediately following every set of snatches or power snatches.
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Use standalone exercises (push presses, presses in snatch) 1–2 times weekly for upper body strength without high systemic fatigue.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Scapulae:** Squeeze the "upper inside edges" of shoulder blades together to achieve retraction and upward rotation; this is the foundation of stability.
*   **Head Position:** Push head forward through the arms (not directly between them) to allow full scapular retraction.
*   **Trunk:** Maintain a slight forward inclination to orient arms vertically over the base of the neck.
*   **Elbows:** Keep arms in neutral rotation (elbow point halfway between straight down and straight back).
*   **Wrist/Hand:** Roll bar to the "back" of the hand (cradle position), slightly behind the midline of the forearm; avoid high-hand placement or straight wrist alignment.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Stability Builders:** Overhead Squat, Heaving Snatch Balance, Snatch Balance.
*   **Upper Body Strength (Low Fatigue):** Snatch Push Press (start with scapulae set tightly on shoulders), Press in Snatch (or behind-the-neck press if mobility limits).
*   **Stabilization/Conditioning:** Overhead Carries (snatch-grip push jerk up, then walk; add turns for instability).
*   **Advanced Instability:** Hang rubber bands from the bar with weights attached to increase stabilization demand with lighter loads.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Mobility Prerequisite:** If full overhead mobility is lacking, perform "Press in Snatch" variations (progressing through positions) before attempting heavy OHS.
*   **Daily Practice:** Utilize daily overhead lifts for general mobility and comfort with the position.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The article emphasizes that proper scapular retraction and trunk inclination are critical to creating a "rigid structure" that supports force, which is essential for an athlete managing back pain during overhead loading.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** The text explicitly identifies the OHS as the primary vehicle for improving overhead strength and stability; the recommended 3–5 second holds are specifically designed to break through plateaus in this lift.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** While the article focuses on snatch stability, the "Press in Snatch" and "Snatch Push Press" recommendations directly address upper body pressing strength and overhead security without requiring a split jerk technique.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The recommendation for "Snatch Push Press" is highlighted as a way to strengthen the upper body *without* incurring considerable systemic fatigue, making it suitable for an athlete with irregular sleep schedules (Wed–Sun shift) who needs to manage recovery load carefully.
