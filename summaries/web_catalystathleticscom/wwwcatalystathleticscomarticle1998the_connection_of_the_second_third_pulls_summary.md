**1. Programming principles:** Not discussed; text focuses exclusively on biomechanical theory of the second/third pull connection rather than sets, reps, or periodization structures.

**2. Technique cues:**
*   **Seamless Continuity:** The second and third pulls must be a single continuous action with no hesitation, floating, or relaxing.
*   **Simultaneous Effort:** Maximize upward bar acceleration (legs/hips) while simultaneously moving the body down; do not delay the turnover to "pull higher."
*   **Extension Timing:** Break from full extension immediately after maximal force is applied to the floor; do not hold the extended position.

**3. Exercise recommendations:** Not discussed in this specific excerpt (though related articles mention Snatch/Clean Pulls and Power Position variations).

**4. Recovery or mobility advice:** None provided in text.

**5. Athlete profile relevance:**
*   **Back Pain Warning:** The text emphasizes "maximal upward force" via leg drive and hip opening. For an athlete with chronic back pain, this requires strict attention to maintaining a neutral spine during the explosive second pull to avoid shear forces on the lumbar region.
*   **Night Shift Constraint:** The demand for a "continuous action" without hesitation is critical; fatigue from night shifts can lead to the very "hesitating or floating" behaviors Everett explicitly warns against, degrading technique.
*   **Transition Context:** As an intermediate moving to Olympic lifting, understanding that "pulling under" and "elevating the bar" are not competing goals but a unified effort is essential for overcoming the current 80kg/65kg limits without developing bad habits like excessive shrugging or early turnover.
