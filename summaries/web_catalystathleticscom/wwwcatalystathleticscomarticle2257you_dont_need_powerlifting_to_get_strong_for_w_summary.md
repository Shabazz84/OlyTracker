# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2257/You-Dont-Need-Powerlifting-to-Get-Strong-for-Weightlifting/

**1. Programming principles:** The text argues against using powerlifting (high-intensity low-rep squats/deadlifts) as a primary strength base for weightlifting. It advocates for **sport-specific training**: prioritizing the snatch and clean & jerk to develop "strength in positions" rather than absolute strength in non-specific ranges of motion. Frequency and volume should focus on Olympic lift proficiency, explosiveness, and technical application of strength.

**2. Technique cues:**
*   **Squat:** Avoid the powerlifting pattern (higher hips back, shin angle forward, trunk inclined). Instead, maintain a position suitable for snatching/cleaning (lower hips, more upright torso).
*   **Deadlift:** Do not train to "get weight up any way possible" with high hips or rounded backs. Focus on balance over the foot rather than shifting weight to heels.

**3. Exercise recommendations:** The text explicitly recommends **training like a weightlifter**. It suggests prioritizing the **Snatch and Clean & Jerk** as the primary drivers of strength development, rather than substituting them with powerlifting variations (low-bar squat, conventional deadlift).

**4. Recovery or mobility advice:** None provided in this specific text excerpt.

**5. Relevance to athlete profile:**
*   **Critical Warning for Intermediate Athlete:** The article directly addresses the "hypertrophy block" plan if that block involves heavy powerlifting movements (back squats/deadlifts). Everett argues this could be counterproductive by creating "problematic movement patterns" (hip-centric squatting, rounded back) that are hard to unlearn.
*   **Back Pain:** The text warns against deadlifts with high hips or rounded backs, which exacerbate back issues; it implies the solution is better technique in Olympic lifts rather than avoiding lifting entirely.
*   **Transition Strategy:** Since the athlete has a push-jerk only and limited OHS strength, the article suggests that increasing their Clean/Snatch performance through specific practice will build necessary strength more effectively than generic powerlifting training.
