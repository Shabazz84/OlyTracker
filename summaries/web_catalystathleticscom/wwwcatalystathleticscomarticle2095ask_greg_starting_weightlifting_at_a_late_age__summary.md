# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2095/Ask-Greg-Starting-Weightlifting-at-a-Late-Age-Plateauing/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Volume/Frequency:** Drastically reduce total volume and frequency compared to younger lifters; avoid "maximal" loads that induce excessive fatigue.
*   **Periodization:** Implement a 2–3 week deload/recovery block immediately, then gradually ramp intensity/volume back up to find a sustainable average level.
*   **Exercise Selection:** Drastically simplify the program; focus exclusively on core lifts (Snatch, Clean & Jerk, Squats, Pulls). Eliminate "fancy" variations or experimental movements except for occasional fun/play.

**2. Technique cues**
*   *None explicitly provided in this text.* The text focuses on training management rather than technical mechanics.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Core Lifts Only:** Snatch, Clean & Jerk, Back Squats, and Pulls (specific variations not named).
*   **Accessory/Recovery:** Foam rolling, stretching, contrast hydrotherapy, massage.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Lifestyle Priority:** Dedicate a "considerably larger" portion of training time to recovery than younger athletes.
*   **Specific Modalities:** Contrast hydrotherapy, foam rolling, stretching, and massage are mandatory preventative measures.
*   **Nutrition/Sleep:** Must prioritize better nutrition practices and sleep hygiene to manage joint pain and injury risk.

**5. Athlete-specific relevance (Intermediate, Night Shift, Back Pain)**
*   **Biological Reality Check:** The athlete is entering a phase where "training capacity begins to wane" (late 20s/early 30s). They must accept they cannot handle the volume/intensity of younger lifters.
*   **Injury Management:** The text explicitly warns that late starters face higher risks of joint pain, mobility issues, and injuries; this aligns with the athlete's chronic back pain.
*   **Mental Framework:** Distinguish between normal sport frustration/stalling (part of the process) and signs of doing something wrong (injury/burnout).
*   **Program Fit:** The advice to "reduce volume" directly counters the likely mistake of an intermediate lifter trying to mimic high-volume programs designed for younger, more durable athletes.

*(Note: The text does not address night shift work schedules or specific split jerk mechanics, as it focuses on general longevity and program design for late starters.)*
