# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1953/Ask-Greg-How-Do-I-Get-Faster-in-the-Olympic-Lifts/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Speed Focus:** Train speed elements at **50–70% intensity**; reserve heavier loads (80%+) for strength/power development.
*   **Execution Speed:** Perform all concentric movements (lifts, pulls, squats) with **maximal speed/aggression** unless specific technical reasons dictate otherwise.
*   **Structure:** Prioritize technique mastery to improve the "appearance" of speed; use lighter weights first in a session for speed work before moving to heavier loads.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Timing & Direction:** Focus on the speed of changing direction (transitioning from lifting bar to pulling under) rather than just upward bar velocity.
*   **Upper Body:** Keep the upper body relaxed; do not let tension interfere with lower-body acceleration.
*   **Bar Path:** Keep the bar close to the body at all times.
*   **Extension:** Do not hesitate at the top of the extension.
*   **Pull Sequence:** Execute the second and third pulls as a **single continuous action**.
*   **Aggression:** The pull/push under must be as violent/aggressive as the upward acceleration preceding it.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Speed/Technique Variations:** Lifts from high blocks, hang positions (knee or higher), power variations, tall snatches/cleans, and dip snatches/cleans.
*   **Accessory Strength for Speed:** High-pulls, muscle snatches/cleans, and jumping exercises.

*   *None explicitly mentioned in this specific text excerpt.* (Note: The text focuses on speed mechanics, not recovery protocols).

**5. Relevance to athlete profile**
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The emphasis on "technical mastery" is critical for an intermediate athlete moving from general strength to Olympic lifting; speed will naturally improve as technique refines.
*   **Back Pain/Chronic Issues:** The cue to rely on the **lower body** and keep the upper body relaxed directly addresses back pain risks by reducing spinal loading via poor posture or upper-body dominance.
*   **Night Shift Work:** While not explicitly addressed in the text, the instruction to train speed at lower intensities (50-70%) allows for high-quality neural work that may be less taxing on a fatigued system compared to heavy max-effort lifting, provided sleep is managed.
*   **Jerk Limitation:** The advice regarding "push under" in the jerk applies directly to the athlete's push-jerk limitation; they must ensure the aggressive dip and drive are executed with maximal speed to maximize height without a split.
