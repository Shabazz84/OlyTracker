# Analysis: "How to Quit Hitting Your Pubic Bone When You Snatch" by Greg Everett

## Relevant to This Athlete

**Technique Cues (Snatch):**
1. **Grip Width** — Bar should contact in soft tissue between pubic crest and anterior superior iliac spines (hip crease), NOT on bone. Test: stand with bar at arms' length, lift one knee to find hip crease, adjust grip so bar hits that crease.
2. **Timing** — Initiating second pull too early causes bar contact too low/early. **Stay over the bar longer; delay hip extension initiation.** This is critical for this athlete's snatch development.

**Exercise Recommendations (directly addresses snatch timing):**
- Halting snatch deadlifts
- Snatch segment pulls with mid-thigh pauses
- Slow-pull snatches

---

## Why Relevant to This Athlete's Profile

- **OHS 50 kg is primary snatch limiter** → Poor bar position/timing in snatch setup compounds this weakness
- **Chronic back pain** → Proper grip width + staying over bar longer = safer mechanics, reduced lumbar stress
- **6-week hypertrophy block ahead** → Pause/halting variations build positional strength
- **No split jerk** → Suggests technique gaps; ensuring proper bar contact timing applies to clean too
