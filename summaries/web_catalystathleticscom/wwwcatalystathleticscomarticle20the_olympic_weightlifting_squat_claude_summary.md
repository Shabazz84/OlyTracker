# Analysis: "The Olympic Weightlifting Squat" by Greg Everett

## Relevant to Athlete Profile

**1. Technique Cues – Squat Positioning (CRITICAL for this athlete)**
- **Full depth is mandatory**: "squatting to the lowest position possible without surgical alteration while maintaining correct posture"
- **Knee tracking**: Knees must travel forward over toes; restricting forward knee travel forces excessive hip-back shift and spinal flexion (directly problematic for chronic back pain)
- **Ankle dorsiflexion**: Lifted heels in weightlifting shoes address limited ankle mobility
- **Foot position**: Slightly outside hip-width, 20–35° toe-out; hips positioned between heels (not directly stacked)
- **Bottom position criteria**: Foot/thigh parallel when viewed overhead; foot under knee when viewed from front; knees follow foot angle throughout descent/recovery
- **Spinal integrity**: "Correctly arched back" must be maintained; flexibility limitations (hamstrings, hip flexors, glutes, ankles) restrict this

**2. Directly Applicable to Chronic Back Pain + Low OHS (50 kg)**
- Back pain likely driven by spinal flexion under load → emphasizes need for mobility work (hamstrings, hip flexors, glutes, ankles) to allow upright torso in squat bottom
- Poor OHS likely stems from ankle/hip/thoracic mobility restricting full squat depth while maintaining neutral spine
- **Prescription**: Prioritize mobility correction before intensity increases; limit squat depth to maintain back arch until flexibility improves (addresses both back pain and OHS deficiency)

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
- Universal foot position should be identical across back squat, front squat, overhead squat (OHS)
- If athlete uses different foot positions for different squat types = indicator of incomplete squat development/poor flexibility

**4. Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- Stretch hamstrings, hip flexors, glutes, ankles
- Strengthen back extensors
- Tighten quads can cause knee pain during squat
