**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** High frequency for main lifts (Squats daily or near-daily; Snatch/Clean variations multiple times weekly).
*   **Intensity/Volume:** Heavy singles at ~90% of max effort followed by drop sets, but explicitly avoiding failure ("grinding").
*   **Progression:** Loads chosen by "feel" with a goal to increase week-to-week without reaching exhaustion.
*   **Periodization:** A phased approach where the first 4 weeks are essentially weightless on technical lifts (Snatch/C&J) before ramping up, focusing initially on leg strength and bodyweight restoration.

**2. Technique Cues & Focus**
*   **Movement Quality:** Focus on "postural strength," reinforcing positions rather than heavy loading immediately.
*   **Variation:** Use different variations for each exposure (e.g., snatch pull on risers, segment pulls) to reinforce speed and movement patterns without repetitive strain.
*   **Speed:** Emphasis on building speed in pulling movements ("reinforcing the movement and speed").

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Squats:** Daily heavy singles (Front or Back/Pause Squat) with 2 drop sets at 90%.
*   **Pulling:** Snatch pulls, Clean pulls, Muscle Snatches/Cleans, High-pulls (varied daily).
*   **Accessory/Rehab:** Internal/External rotation, IYT exercises with bands; Weighted back extensions; Planks/Sit-ups; Farmer's walks.
*   **Explosive Work:** Jump work and hill sprints once weekly.

**4. Recovery & Mobility Advice**
*   **Pre-hab:** Mandatory shoulder rehab/pre-hab at the start of *every* workout (bands, rotator cuff work).
*   **Pain Management:** The goal is to "feel good after each workout" rather than being destroyed; avoid training through pain or extreme soreness.
*   **Mobility:** Acknowledges that mobility may not improve quickly during downtime but must be maintained via specific drills (IYT, overhead work).
