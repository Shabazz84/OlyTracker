# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/78/Cutting-Bodyweight-for-Weightlifting-without-Fatigue-Perfor/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Weight Loss:** Do not add conditioning work (running/rowing) for weight loss; rely on nutrition and maintain performance training intensity. Reduce calories in small "stair-step" increments every few weeks rather than dramatic cuts to avoid fatigue and metabolic adaptation.
*   **Cutting Weight (Short-term):** Use hyperhydration protocols 5–7 days out from competition: start with 5–7 liters of water daily, tapering down by ~1 liter per day until the day before weigh-in when intake is minimal. Reduce sodium intake in the days leading up to the meet.
*   **Periodization:** Plan weight loss months in advance; do not attempt drastic cuts (e.g., 12kg) within a week of competition.

**2. Technique cues**
*   *None provided in this text.* The article focuses exclusively on weight management strategies, not biomechanics or lift technique.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Conditioning for Weight Loss:** If conditioning is necessary to assist weight loss, prioritize low-impact activities like rowing or walking. Avoid high-intensity cardio that detracts from performance training.
*   **Recovery/Competition Prep:** No specific lifting exercises are recommended; the focus is on hydration and nutrition protocols (e.g., Gatorade/Pedalite for rehydration).

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Hyperhydration Rehydration:** Immediately after weigh-in, drink 1 liter of water per kilogram lost plus extra to flush fluids. Add electrolytes (salt/potassium chloride) and carbohydrates to the water to create an isotonic solution for faster absorption.
*   **Dietary Quality:** Prioritize quality animal protein, fats, and varied vegetables during weight loss phases. Avoid severe restriction of any single macronutrient.

**5. Relevance to this athlete's profile**
*   **Critical Warning:** The text discusses "Cutting Weight" (dehydration) for competition weigh-ins, which is **contraindicated** for your current goal of a 6-week hypertrophy block and technique development. Dehydration protocols will exacerbate chronic back pain, impair recovery from night shifts, and hinder strength gains.
*   **Actionable Advice:** Apply the "Losing Weight" principles (stair-step caloric reduction, high protein/fat/veg quality) if you need to drop bodyweight for a future weight class, but strictly avoid the dehydration/sweating protocols described in the second half of the text until you are actually preparing for a weigh-in.
*   **Night Shift Context:** The article warns that dramatic caloric reductions lead to feeling "terrible" and training worse; this is particularly risky for night shift workers who already face circadian rhythm challenges.
