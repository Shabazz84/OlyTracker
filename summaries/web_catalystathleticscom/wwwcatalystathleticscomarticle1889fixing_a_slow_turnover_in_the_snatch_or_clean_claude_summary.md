# Weightlifting Analysis: "Fixing a Slow Turnover in the Snatch or Clean"

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Strength-focused accessory work**: 5–6 reps, 3–5 sets for isolation movements
- **Exercise progression by load capacity**: tall variations (lightest) → hang variations (moderate) → floor variations (heaviest)
- **Movement integration**: third pull broken into three parts (initial pull, turnover, punch under), each trainable individually or together
- **Mindset/aggression**: must be practiced every rep, every session

## 2. **Technique Cues (Snatch & Clean)**
- **Elbows**: rotate to sides in pulls 1–2; keep pointing sideways into pull 3
- **Bar proximity**: keep bar "close enough to smell"; minimize forward shift
- **Turnover**: maintain continuous tension; avoid slack; pull elbows up *while* rotating arms over
- **Timing**: finish of hip/knee extension and initiation of pull-under must occur *almost simultaneously*—treat 2nd and 3rd pull as one continuous action
- **Grip**: flip hands back; punch under with relatively loose grip
- **Meeting the bar**: stay connected and meet bar at whatever height it reaches (critical for lighter loads)

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
**For initial pull strength**: Tall snatch high-pull (3–5 sets × 5–6 reps, very light); snatch high-pulls from mid/upper thigh or floor with speed

**For turnover strength**: Tall muscle snatch (strict, no legs); muscle snatch from hang/floor; snatch long pulls

**For punch-under strength**: Snatch balances; drop snatches

**For timing/technique**: Tall snatches; high-hang snatches; power snatches; snatches from high blocks (mid-thigh)

## 5. **Relevance to This Athlete**
- **Critical**: OHS 50 kg is limiting snatch. Tall muscle snatches and drop snatches should strengthen overhead position and punch-under (direct snatch weakness).
- **Night shift consideration**: Aggression/CNS demand of high-pull and snatch balance work should align with alert windows mid-shift.
- **Chronic back pain**: Tall variations (reduced load, strict pulling) and drop snatches (minimal spinal loading during descent) are safer than heavy high-pulls from floor. Avoid excessive shrugging cues.
- **Split jerk absence**: Snatch-specific accessory plan doesn't address jerk deficit; separate jerk turnover work needed.
