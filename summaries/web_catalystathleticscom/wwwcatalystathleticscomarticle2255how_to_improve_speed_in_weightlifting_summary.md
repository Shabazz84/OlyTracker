# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2255/How-to-Improve-Speed-in-Weightlifting/

1. **Programming Principles**: Prioritize technical proficiency over raw speed generation; train 70–80% intensity to allow high velocity; execute concentric phases of squats, pulls, and lifts with maximal intent (move fast even if absolute speed is limited); limit jump training volume to 1–2 days/week due to neurological demand.
2. **Technique Cues**: Focus on "intent to move as fast as possible" rather than absolute velocity; eliminate arm swing during jumps to mimic Olympic lift mechanics; ensure optimal mechanics to minimize resistance and maximize force transfer into the bar.
3. **Exercise Recommendations**: Snatch/Clean pulls (with speed intent); Squats (concentric phase must be explosive); Power/Hang/Block variations with lighter loads; Jump training (Back squat jumps at 20–25% of max back squat, box jumps, or vertical jumps) combined immediately after squats without rest.
4. **Recovery/Mobility**: Acknowledge jump training is highly neurologically demanding; avoid overdoing volume to prevent burnout; "ease into" jumping exercises. (Note: Text does not address chronic pain or specific mobility drills).
5. **Athlete Profile Relevance**: The text directly addresses the athlete's need to maximize speed despite genetic limits and lack of split jerk experience by emphasizing that *technical efficiency* is the primary driver for moving faster; however, it offers no specific guidance for managing chronic back pain, night shift fatigue (Wed–Sun), or the transition from push-jerk to split-jerk mechanics.
