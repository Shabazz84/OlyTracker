# Analysis: "Quit Letting Your Shoulders Sag Overhead in the Snatch" — Greg Everett

## Relevant to Athlete Profile

**HIGHLY RELEVANT.** This athlete's OHS is 50 kg (primary snatch limiter) and uses push jerk only. Overhead stability is critical.

---

## 1. **Technique Cues — Snatch Overhead Position**
- **Scapular positioning:** Retract forcefully + upwardly rotate (squeeze inner edges of shoulder blades together, lift outer corners up)
- **Timing:** Secure overhead *before* bar weight settles — punch up aggressively during third pull turnover
- **Bar proximity:** Keep bar extremely close during turnover to minimize horizontal momentum
- **Grip maintenance:** Maintain tight connection throughout turnover; grip stays on bar until hands flip into final position
- **Bottom holds:** 3 sec in snatch bottom + 2–3 sec overhead before dropping (habit-building)

---

## 2. **Exercise Recommendations**
- Snatch press, snatch push press, press in snatch, heaving snatch balance, snatch balance, OHS
- Upper back work: rows + scapular-focused band work (retraction emphasis)
- **Note:** Directly applicable to athlete's weak OHS; pairs well with hypertrophy block

---

## 3. **Programming Principle**
- **Daily practice** of aggressive overhead lockouts (even empty bar) to establish motor habit
- Consistent, patient work over time (motor habits don't change instantly)

---

## 4. **Caution for This Athlete**
⚠️ **Back pain consideration:** Heavy OHS holds + scapular retraction intensity may load lumbar spine. Ensure neutral spine alignment and adjust volume if aggravating. Upper-back-dominant bracing strategy recommended.
