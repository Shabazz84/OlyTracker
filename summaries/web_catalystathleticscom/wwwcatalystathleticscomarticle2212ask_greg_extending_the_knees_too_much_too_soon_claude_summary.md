# Analysis: Greg Everett on Snatch Pull Mechanics

## 1. **Technique Cues for Snatch (Highly Relevant)**

**Bar Path & Hip/Shoulder Mechanics:**
- Hips move forward significantly (starting behind feet → above feet at top of pull), but bar moves back a smaller distance
- **Focus on bringing bar back, not hips forward** (hips moving forward is automatic; conscious focus on hips = weak leg drive + excessive horizontal movement)
- Bar moves vertically; horizontal movement = "looping" (athlete's stated problem)

**Common Error—Extending Knees Too Soon:**
- Caused by excessive shoulder-over-bar positioning → knees extend without proportional hip extension → premature knee extension lockout
- Results in extreme double knee bend (re-bending, not scooping) → reduced bar speed + horizontal force → looping
- **Fix: Maintain back angle/shoulder position until bar passes knees** (position maintenance, not position change)

## 2. **Exercise Recommendations (Direct for OHS Limiter)**

- **Snatch segment deadlifts/pulls** with pauses (floor, knee, mid-thigh) — emphasize back angle maintenance, prevent knee over-extension
- **Segment snatch high-pulls** (pause at knee, then full drive) — practice vertical leg drive
- **Muscle snatches & tall snatches** — arm action + bar proximity in turnover
- **Segment snatches** (pauses off floor/knee) + **slow pull snatches** — positional setup for finish

## 3. **Relevance to This Athlete**

⚠️ **Critical match:** Athlete has 50 kg OHS (primary snatch limiter) with chronic back pain. Early knee extension + forward lean = spinal stress. These cues (maintain back angle, vertical drive, reduce horizontal bar movement) directly address both technical and injury concerns.
