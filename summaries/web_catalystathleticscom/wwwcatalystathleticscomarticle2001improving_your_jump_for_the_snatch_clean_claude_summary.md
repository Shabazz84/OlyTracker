# Analysis: "Improving Your Jump for the Snatch & Clean"

## Relevant to This Athlete

**1. Programming Principles**
- Jump work should be integrated wisely into training cycles, not randomly added
- Two distinct approaches: direct jumping exercises vs. auxiliary barbell exercises emphasizing speed/accuracy
- Jumping exercises placed end-of-session or separate GPP days
- Emphasis on *variation* to maintain progression

**2. Technique Cues**
- **Critical for this athlete**: Land in same position you started (no forward/backward drift)—directly applicable to jerk transition issues
- Power snatch/clean emphasis: pull bar high enough to rack standing—forces powerful leg extension (addresses weak clean at 80 kg)
- Snatch balance & power jerk useful for focusing leg drive without technical complexity of full lifts

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
- **Most relevant**: Power snatch/clean, snatch balance, power jerk (from back)—all bypass weak OHS (50 kg) limiter
- Snatch pull with jump, clean/snatch "out of the pit" (jump onto bumper plates)
- Jump squats with bar (full/half bottom, dynamic/static variations)
- Depth jumps between boxes

**4. Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- None provided in text

**5. Athlete-Specific Applicability**
- **Strong match**: Jump work develops leg speed/power crucial for weak clean (80 kg) relative to squat (118 kg)
- **Caution**: Night-shift schedule may limit recovery capacity—jumping volume needs monitoring
- **OHS limiter**: Jump-focused work bypasses mobility bottleneck; allows strength accumulation for 6-week hypertrophy block
- **Back pain**: Verify jumping mechanics don't aggravate; power jerk from back safer than full snatch complexity
