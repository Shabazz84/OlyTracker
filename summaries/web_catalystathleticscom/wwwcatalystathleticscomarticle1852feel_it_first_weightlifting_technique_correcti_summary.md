# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1852/Feel-it-First-Weightlifting-Technique-Corrections/

1. **Programming principles**: The text advocates for "Feel It First" methodology: use abbreviated complexes where athletes perform specific drills or hold simulated finish positions (e.g., 3 knee-to-hip deadlifts with vertical legs/hip hyperextension) to physically ingrain the movement pattern before attempting the full lift. This prioritizes kinesthetic awareness over conceptual instruction, aiming for immediate technical correction through repetition of the correct segment rather than high-volume strength sets or complex periodization structures.

2. **Technique cues**: Focus on "feeling" the position rather than thinking about it. Specific cue: In the snatch/clean pull, ensure hips are slightly hyperextended with shoulders behind the hips at the finish position; keep legs vertical and slide the bar lightly up the thighs to maintain proximity. The goal is to establish balance and orientation before the explosive phase.

3. **Exercise recommendations**:
   - Snatch/Clean Deadlifts from the knee to a simulated finish (legs vertical, slight hip hyperextension).
   - Static holds in the simulated finish position (standing) to feel body orientation/balance.
   - Follow-up with the full lift immediately after the drill ("before the athlete has time to think").

4. **Recovery or mobility advice**: The text does not explicitly address recovery, sleep schedules, or specific mobility routines; it focuses solely on neuromuscular coordination and technique acquisition through drilling.

5. **Athlete profile relevance**:
   - **Chronic back pain**: The "Feel It First" approach is highly relevant as it reduces cognitive load and potentially minimizes the risk of compensatory movements caused by fatigue or poor motor control, which often exacerbate back issues in athletes with chronic pain.
   - **Intermediate/Transitioning**: The method of isolating specific failure points (e.g., hip extension) via drills is ideal for an athlete transitioning to Olympic lifting who needs to correct foundational mechanics quickly without overloading the CNS.
   - **Night shift worker**: While not mentioned in the text, the "immediate improvement" and efficiency of this approach could be beneficial for a night-shift worker with limited recovery windows, as it maximizes technical gains per training session. However, the lack of specific recovery advice in the text is a gap for this profile.
