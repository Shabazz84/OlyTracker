# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1874/The-Clean-and-Jerk-Rack-Positions/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Not addressed:** The text focuses exclusively on technique mechanics (rack positions) and does not specify sets, reps, intensity percentages, frequency, or periodization models.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Trunk Support:** Bar must rest directly on the trunk; arms should *not* hold the weight.
*   **Scapular Position:** Shoulders slightly elevated and protracted (pushed forward) to create a secure channel between shoulders and throat. Avoid rounding the thoracic spine.
*   **Bar Placement:** Rest in the "channel" above collarbones, never directly on the bone (prevents bruising/occlusion). Keep bar as far back as possible without compromising upright posture.
*   **Clean Elbows:** Lift elbows high to clear knees and secure shoulders; prioritize shoulder position over maximal elbow height if they conflict.
*   **Jerk Elbows:** Lower and spread elbows to the sides (slightly in front of the bar) to optimize pressing mechanics. Do not drop elbows before the drive.
*   **Grip Pressure:** Maintain a "relatively loose" grip; tight grips cause elbows/shoulders to drop and slow turnover.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Mobility Drills (Jerk Rack):**
    *   *Bar on Back:* Hold bar in jerk grip, lift elbows forward/high, push shoulders forward while maintaining full grip.
    *   *Squat Rack Setup:* Partial squat under a loaded bar to set elbow/hand position, then drive up with legs to load the rack without losing hand placement.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Collarbone Health:** Elevating shoulders prevents direct pressure on collarbones, avoiding permanent bruising and tissue buildup.
*   **Circulation:** Shoulder elevation prevents carotid artery occlusion (preventing fainting).
*   **Mobility vs. Structure:** Acknowledges that some grip limitations are structural; if mobility-limited, work on stretches to improve the position over time.

**5. Relevance to athlete profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The emphasis on maintaining a "straight and erect spinal position" and using the trunk as a rigid piston is critical for minimizing shear force on the lumbar spine.
*   **Push Jerk Only (No Split):** The text explicitly details the "Jerk Rack Position" mechanics required to press under the bar, which aligns with the athlete's specific constraint of using only a push jerk.
*   **OHS Limiter:** Proper shoulder protraction and elevation cues are directly applicable to improving overhead stability for the Overhead Squat.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The distinction between Clean vs. Jerk rack positions addresses a common gap in technique for athletes transitioning from powerlifting (who often use identical grips) to Olympic lifting.
