# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2219/Programmings-Effect-on-Technique/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Interdependence:** Treat strength and technique as inseparable; loading (volume/intensity) dictates technical accuracy under fatigue.
*   **Fatigue Management:** Do not train when physically/mentally exhausted ("beat down"). If 95% of volume is performed with poor form, the program is failing.
*   **Ordering:** Reorder exercises within a session or week to ensure fresher states for priority lifts (e.g., weaker lifts earlier in the week).
*   **Specificity:** Exercise selection must target specific gaps (e.g., weak trunk = beltless squats; tipping forward = more front squats/pause variations).

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **"Practice Makes Permanent":** Never train a movement pattern that reinforces bad technique, even during strength work (e.g., if you tip over the bar in snatches, do not perform segment pulls with the same faulty posture).
*   **Positional Integrity:** Strength is specific to position; ensure accessory movements reinforce optimal balance and postures required for heavy lifts.
*   **Gradual Correction:** For intermediates with suboptimal motor patterns, use gradual improvement via closely monitored accessory work rather than resetting entirely to square one, unless the issue is severe.

**3. Exercise Recommendations (Derived from Principles)**
*   **Variations:** Use pauses, slow eccentrics, holds at the top, flat feet pulls, complexes, or risers to address weak points in pulling mechanics.
*   **Specific Fixes:** If finishing the pull is an issue or footwork is premature, utilize "no jump" snatch/clean variations or power position lifts.
*   **Strength Support:** Avoid lightweight drills that do not develop specific strength under load; ensure strength work is performed with high technical quality to avoid grinding into poor execution.

**4. Recovery & Mobility Advice**
*   **Holistic Fatigue:** Acknowledge non-lifting stressors (sleep, nutrition, life stress) as primary drivers of poor technique.
*   **Scheduling:** Adjust training schedules to allow for fatigue dissipation before high-focus technical sessions.
*   **Mobility/Strength Link:** Physical qualities like mobility and strength dictate how accurately skills are performed under load; deficiencies here cause technical breakdowns.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, 102.5kg, Chronic Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Management:** The text explicitly advises athletes with a "weak trunk" to squat without a belt to build stability, which is relevant for chronic back pain management if done cautiously.
*   **Fatigue & Sleep:** As a night shift worker (Wed–Sun), the athlete faces high fatigue risks. The text warns that poor sleep/stress leads to practicing poorly; the program must account for this by potentially reducing volume or adjusting timing to ensure "on game" performance.
*   **Intermediate Correction:** Since the athlete is intermediate with likely entrenched motor patterns, the advice suggests gradual correction via accessory work rather than a full reset, provided the specific weak points (e.g., trunk stability) are targeted.
