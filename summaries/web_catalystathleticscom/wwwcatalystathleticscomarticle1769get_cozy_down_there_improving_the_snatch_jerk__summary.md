# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1769/Get-Cozy-Down-There-Improving-The-Snatch-Jerk-Receiving-Pos/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Intensity/Frequency:** Apply to *every* snatch, power snatch, jerk, power jerk, overhead squat (OHS), and snatch balance variation.
*   **Specific Protocol:** Hold the receiving position for **2–3 seconds** before recovering. Upon recovery, hold the bar for 1 second before dropping.
*   **Purpose:** Expose hidden instability/imbalance; prevent "soft" lockouts; ensure competition-ready stability (avoiding red lights).

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Snatch/Jerk Receiving Position:** Imagine holding a coin between each shoulder and the neck to maintain active shoulders.
*   **Elbow Lockout:** Focus on keeping elbows fully locked out immediately upon receiving the weight; do not allow them to unlock during the squat phase.
*   **Mindset:** Make the bottom position feel like "home" by spending significant time there under load.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Drill:** Snatch balances (to practice locking out while squatting).
*   **Accessory Work:** Pressing and overhead strength work (specifically for those with weak lockouts or upper body imbalances).
*   **Variations to Include:** Power snatches, power jerks, overhead squats (OHS), snatch balances.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   *Note: The text does not explicitly address recovery protocols, mobility drills, or injury management.* It focuses strictly on stability and technique execution.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Critical for OHS Limiter:** The 2–3 second hold directly addresses the athlete's primary limiter (OHS) by forcing stability at the bottom of the overhead squat position.
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** Holding the position exposes instability that often causes athletes to "rush" or drop the bar, which can exacerbate chronic back pain through poor mechanics; this drill forces a stable, controlled descent and ascent.
*   **Jerk Specifics:** The advice is vital for an athlete using a push jerk (no split), as they must rely entirely on overhead stability without the dynamic adjustment of a split to secure the bar.
*   **Night Shift Consideration:** While not mentioned in text, the low time-cost ("only a few seconds") makes this drill feasible for a shift worker with limited recovery windows.
