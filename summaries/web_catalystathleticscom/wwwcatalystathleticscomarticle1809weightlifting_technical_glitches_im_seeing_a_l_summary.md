### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Intensity/Speed:** Do **not** program "slow first pulls" as a standard rule for intermediate lifters. The goal is to generate speed immediately as the plates leave the floor ("pull as fast as possible while maintaining balance").
*   **Periodization/Cues:** If an athlete exhibits technical breakdowns (rounding back, butt shooting up) due to excessive speed, do not cue "slow down." Instead, use corrective cues like "keep your back tighter" or "don't let your butt shoot straight up." Only use "slow down" as a rare exception for retooling mechanics in beginners.
*   **Rhythm:** Maintain a consistent rhythm; the second pull (mid-thigh to finish) will naturally appear more explosive than the first, but this is due to the weight's inertia, not an intentional deceleration at the start.

    *   "Don't let your butt shoot straight up at the beginning."
*   **Conceptual Cue:** Treat the first pull like a sprinter's drive phase; it is violent and explosive, not slow.
