### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Pull Intensity:** Do not prescribe pulls at a fixed percentage (80–105%) of the snatch/clean for athletes with a surplus of strength relative to technique. For this athlete (likely strong puller, weak overhead), pulls should be set based on observed movement quality and effort level, potentially exceeding 105% of their current clean/snatch max if technical execution remains sound.
*   **Progression:** Start conservatively and increase weight in small increments until the lifter is moving properly at the desired effort level. Adjust intensity based on cycle phase: light/technical early in the cycle; challenging/heavy near the end.
*   **Volume Control:** Avoid adding excessive extra reps to pulls ("make do with the volume given"). Prioritize sharp, high-quality reps over quantity to prevent sloppy technique from carrying over to full lifts.

### 2. Technique Cues
*   **Start Position:** Set tension against the bar *before* initiating the pull; ensure a smooth pull off the ground.
*   **Bar Path & Posture:** Keep the chest up and shoulders on top of the bar as much as possible during the break from the floor to prevent being pulled forward/over the bar due to lack of tension.
*   **Extension:** Push longer at the top; extend straight (avoid drifting back).
*   **Elbow Position:** Pull elbows upward to keep the bar close; do not let them drift backward as the bar rises.

### 3. Exercise Recommendations
*   **Pull Variations:** Utilize standard Snatch and Clean Pulls, but consider **High Pulls** only if weight is increased appropriately (not just a variation of light pulls).
*   **Contrast Method (Novice/Intermediate):** Set up two bars: one heavier for the pull (single/double) and one at the current lift weight. Perform the heavy pull, step over to the lighter bar, and immediately execute the full snatch/clean. This builds confidence and allows the athlete to utilize their available strength in the full movement.

### 5. Relevance to Athlete Profile
*   **Strength vs. Technique Mismatch:** The athlete's profile (strong squat/pull potential but low overhead numbers due to OHS limitations) fits the "technically challenged" category described by Everett. Prescribing pulls at standard percentages will be ineffective; weights must be pushed higher to challenge strength development without compromising form.
*   **Cycle Planning:** Since the goal is a 6-week hypertrophy block followed by technique/strength, the pull programming should reflect this: keep pulls technical and manageable during the initial hypertrophy phase (early cycle) but progress toward heavier, more challenging loads as the transition to technique/strength begins (late cycle).
*   **Back Pain Consideration:** While not explicitly mentioned in the text, the cue "keep chest up" and "smooth pull off ground" is critical for athletes with chronic back pain to minimize shear forces during the initial pull phase.
