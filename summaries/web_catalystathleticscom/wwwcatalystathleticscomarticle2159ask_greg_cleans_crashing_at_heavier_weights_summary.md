# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2159/Ask-Greg-Cleans-Crashing-at-Heavier-Weights/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Intensity/Frequency:** Do not alter technique mechanics based on load; the movement pattern must remain identical regardless of weight, only the force output changes.
*   **Periodization:** Use heavy loads to reinforce correct mechanics rather than relying solely on light weights for technical drills (countering the "bad habits decrease with weight" counter-argument found in comments).
*   **Structure:** Implement complexes where the receiving height is kept constant across different pull variations (e.g., Power Clean + Hang Clean) to force consistency.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Turnover Concept:** "Meet the bar with your shoulders at whatever height you've pulled it to." Do not separate from the bar; stay connected throughout the turnover.
*   **Receiving Position:** If pulling high, keep the body relatively high; if pulling low, drop under quickly. The mechanics dictate the position, not an arbitrary decision to pull higher for heavier weights.
*   **Chest Drive:** "Reach your chest up into the bar" during the turnover phase.
*   **Pull Finish:** Maintain an abrupt, explosive upward finish with a quick change of direction; do not lean back further or continue pulling longer just because the weight is heavy.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Drill:** Complexes pairing a Power Clean (or Hang Clean) immediately followed by a Clean/Hang Clean. The goal is to receive the bar at the *same height* for both reps, forcing the lifter to adjust their drop speed rather than pulling higher.
*   **Secondary Drills:** Cleans from high hang positions or cleans performed off high blocks to isolate turnover mechanics under load.
*   **Note on Tall Cleans:** While useful for turnover mechanics, they are less effective than weighted variations for this specific athlete profile (crashing at heavy weights).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   *None provided in the text.* The article focuses exclusively on technical execution and force application during the clean.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Transitioning Intermediate:** The advice directly addresses the common pitfall of "crashing" when increasing weight, which is critical for an athlete moving from 80kg cleans toward heavier loads.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** The text emphasizes that heavy weights do not require a higher pull; this reinforces that the athlete should not compensate for their lack of split jerk mechanics by altering clean turnover speed or height.
*   **Chronic Back Pain/Strength Gap:** The instruction to "not lean back farther" and maintain an explosive finish is crucial for spinal safety, as leaning back under heavy loads exacerbates lower back stress.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** The cue to "meet the bar with shoulders at whatever height you've pulled it to" applies directly to the Snatch; if the pull height is limited by mobility/strength, the turnover must be adjusted instantly rather than trying to pull higher and crashing.
