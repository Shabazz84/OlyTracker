# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1903/The-Jerk-Technique-Nobody-Talks-About/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Focus:** Prioritize standard "dip-drive" fundamentals over advanced adaptations (like the "flex jerk") until the athlete is more advanced.
*   **Intensity/Load:** The "elastic/flex" technique relying on bar whip requires heavy loads (approx. >120kg for men's bars) to be effective; it does not work with light weights.
*   **Progression:** Teach standard basics first; allow personal adaptations only if the athlete is naturally gifted and making progress, but never as a starting point.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Dip-Drive Depth:** Aim for a controlled dip of at least "a few inches" (approx. 10% of body height) rather than an extremely short, abrupt flex.
*   **Timing:** Avoid pausing in the bottom position; utilize the stretch reflex continuously without dissipating elastic energy.
*   **Bar Contact:** Maintain continuous contact between the bar and shoulders throughout the dip and drive; avoid "violent contact" or bouncing where the bar loses connection.
*   **Force Application:** Focus on aggressive upward leg drive to get the bar over the head with accuracy, rather than relying solely on a "bounce."

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   *Note: The text does not recommend specific accessory exercises.* It focuses exclusively on jerk mechanics (dip-drive vs. flex jerk).

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Jerk Style:** As an intermediate athlete, you should strictly avoid attempting a "flex jerk" or relying on bar whip now; your weights are likely too light for this technique to work effectively and it is considered an advanced adaptation. Focus entirely on mastering the standard dip-drive.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** The text emphasizes that foot positioning (split vs. power) is secondary to a perfect dip-drive. Since you only use a push jerk, your success depends entirely on the quality of your leg drive and bar path accuracy, not footwork.
*   **Back Pain/Strength:** While the text doesn't address back pain directly, it warns against "violent contact" with the shoulders (often caused by poor dip mechanics) which can indicate a lack of connection and potential strain. A controlled, deep dip is safer than an abrupt bounce.
