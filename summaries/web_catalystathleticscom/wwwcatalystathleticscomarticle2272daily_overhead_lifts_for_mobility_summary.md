# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2272/Daily-Overhead-Lifts-for-Mobility/

1. **Programming Principles**: The text advocates for **daily frequency** of overhead activity (not necessarily heavy lifting) to build stability as a skill. It emphasizes that consistency is more critical than volume or intensity; even 3–4 days/week requires daily overhead exposure on off-days to prevent the body from "settling" back into restricted ranges.

2. **Technique Cues**:
   - **Stability over Flexibility**: Treat mobility as an active skill requiring stability, not just passive stretching.
   - **Symmetry & Independence**: Use double-arm exercises (barbell/dumbbells) to prevent compensation; single-arm work is included specifically to force independent arm stabilization and improve symmetry.
   - **Active Range of Motion**: The effort to move and hold arms overhead *is* the mobility training; focus on setting a strong, stable position dynamically rather than static stretching.

3. **Exercise Recommendations** (Categorized):
   - **Jerk Variations**: Split jerk, power/push jerk, pause jerk, jerk behind the neck.
   - **Overhead Squat Variations**: Snatch, overhead squat, snatch balance, press in snatch, push jerk in snatch.
   - **Pressing/Pushing**: Push press, snatch push press, push press behind the neck, Press (barbell/DB/KB), presses within snatch/clean/split contexts.
   - **Overhead Carries**: Snatch/clean grip barbell, barbell with hanging weights, DB/KB 1- and 2-arm carries + OHS.
   - **Vertical Pulls**: Pull-ups, chin-ups, pulldowns (including behind the neck), hangs from a bar.

4. **Recovery or Mobility Advice**:
   - **Daily Exposure**: Perform overhead activity daily, even on non-gym days.
   - **Low-Intensity Off-Day Work**: Use hanging from a pull-up bar or door jambs to simulate hangs; perform dynamic range-of-motion warm-ups daily.
   - **Concept**: "Mobility requires stability." The body only allows access to ranges it deems safe through regular, frequent use.

5. **Relevance to Athlete Profile**:
   - **Chronic Back Pain & OHS Limiter**: This approach directly addresses the 50 kg OHS limitation by treating overhead stability as a trainable skill rather than a fixed anatomical limit, potentially reducing pain through improved load distribution and active engagement.
   - **Night Shift Worker (Wed–Sun)**: The "daily exposure" rule is critical here; on off-days (Mon/Tue), the athlete should perform low-intensity overhead hangs or dynamic movements to maintain mobility gains despite irregular sleep/work schedules.
   - **Push Jerk Only**: The text explicitly lists "power/push jerk" and "jerk behind the neck," validating the athlete's current constraint while suggesting variations to build stability for future transition if desired.
   - **Intermediate Transition**: Emphasizes that the "effort to move and hold" is dynamic training, which builds the foundational strength required before heavy hypertrophy or technical refinement blocks.
