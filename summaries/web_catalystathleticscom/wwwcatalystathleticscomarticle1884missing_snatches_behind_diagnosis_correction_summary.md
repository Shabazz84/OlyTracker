# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1884/Missing-Snatches-Behind-Diagnosis-Correction/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** High frequency for overhead stability work (holding bottom positions 2–5 seconds) to build structural integrity.
*   **Intensity/Volume:** Moderate intensity focused on technique correction rather than maximal load; prioritize quality over quantity during the "diagnosis" phase.
*   **Periodization:** Immediate focus on error correction (grip width, timing, structure) before moving into a hypertrophy block. The text implies a technical reset is required *before* loading.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Grip Width:** If missing behind frequently, narrow the grip incrementally to prevent backward momentum during turnover; do not make dramatic changes.
*   **Overhead Structure:** Maintain a "slight forward inclination" of the trunk (bar over back of neck); avoid excessive lean which forces arms backward and destabilizes the bar.
*   **Turnover Timing:** Do not dive under early ("ducking head/chest"); wait until the bar is high on the thigh before initiating the pull-under to ensure adequate space/time for receiving.
*   **Third Pull Mechanics:** Elbows must move up and out (internally rotated start); avoid "stiff arms" which cause forward swinging of the bar leading to backward swing overhead.
*   **Extension Geometry:** Hips should not travel forward past a vertical line through the ankle/hip/shoulder; drive against the floor until hips fully extend, then pull under.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Snatch Push Presses & Push Jerks in Snatch:** To strengthen overhead position and bar control.
*   **Overhead Squats (OHS):** Critical for diagnosing ankle mobility limitations that force trunk lean.
*   **Presses in Snatch:** For overhead stability.
*   **Snatch Balances:** To practice receiving the bar with proper posture.
*   **Static Holds:** Hold the bottom position of snatches and overhead exercises for 2–5 seconds before recovering to build structural strength.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Ankle Mobility:** Address limited ankle ROM immediately; lack of dorsiflexion forces knees back, hips behind feet, and trunk forward (causing the miss).
*   **Thoracic Spine:** Correct excessive kyphosis (rounding) which shifts shoulders forward and compromises shoulder blade retraction.
*   **Shoulder Mobility:** Tight shoulders force an excessively wide grip; address via dislocates or mobility work to allow a narrower, more stable grip.

**5. Athlete-Specific Relevance**
*   **Chronic Back Pain & OHS Limiter:** The article identifies "limited ankle mobility" and "tight thoracic spine" as primary causes of poor overhead structure (forward lean). Addressing these specific mobility restrictions is likely the key to resolving the athlete's 50kg OHS limiter without exacerbating back pain.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** The text explicitly recommends "push jerks in snatch" and "presses in snatch," which aligns with an athlete who cannot perform split jerks, reinforcing the need for strict overhead stability drills.
*   **Night Shift Work:** While not mentioned in the text, the emphasis on "holding positions for 2–5 seconds" provides a low-movement, high-stability stimulus that may be less taxing on a fatigued nervous system than high-velocity explosive work during irregular sleep cycles.
*   **Transitioning Athlete:** The advice to "narrow grip incrementally" and avoid "dramatic changes" is crucial for an intermediate athlete who may have developed bad habits (like wide grips) that need systematic correction rather than a complete overhaul.
