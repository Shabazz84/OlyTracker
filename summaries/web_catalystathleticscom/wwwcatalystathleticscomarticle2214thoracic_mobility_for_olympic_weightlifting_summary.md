# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2214/Thoracic-Mobility-for-Olympic-Weightlifting/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** Perform mobility work "as many times per day and week as you can manage." Consistency is prioritized over volume intensity.
*   **Sets/Reps:**
    *   Clean-Grip Overhead Squat: 5–10 reps (hold bottom for a few seconds).
    *   Dumbbell Back Extension: 10–20 reps (controlled, pause at top).
    *   Foam Rolling/Bench Mobilization: No specific rep count; focus on independent vertebrae movement.
*   **Intensity:** Low intensity for mobility drills; avoid pain in partner mobilization. Use pauses and slow movements to increase time under tension.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **T-Spine vs. Lumbar:** In all exercises, strictly "avoid hinging at the T/L junction or excessively arching the lumbar spine." Ensure movement originates from the thoracic vertebrae, not the lower back.
*   **Overhead Position:** Maintain tension in abs to prevent lower back compensation; imagine stretching the trunk upward rather than arching.
*   **Overhead Squat:** Push straight up against the bar and reach head/shoulders toward the ceiling while sitting slowly into the squat.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Foam Rolling:** Trunk in a straight line from pelvis to shoulders; arms start across chest, progress to overhead. Do not arch over the roller.
*   **Leaning Bar Hang:** Toes on floor/box behind bar; hang straight then lean chest through arms while relaxing and maintaining abdominal tension.
*   **Quadruped T-Spine Rotation:** One hand on upper back, other arm locked straight; rotate elbow toward opposite hand then to ceiling. Turn head with shoulders.
*   **Partner T-Spine Mobilization:** Kneeling position leaning elbows on a box/bench; partner applies downward pressure on sides of spine (not painful).
*   **Clean-Grip Overhead Squat:** Start with PVC pipe and wide grip if immobile; progress to empty bar. Wear flat shoes if lower body is flexible but upper back is not.
*   **Dumbbell Back Extension:** Prone position, dumbbell behind neck, glutes/abs tight, pause at top extension.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Frequency:** Foam rolling and partner mobilization can be daily; Dumbbell extensions limited to 2–3 days/week (end of workout).
*   **Posture Correction:** Actively maintain upright posture during sitting and standing ("stretch it upward") throughout the day to counteract "hunching."
*   **Time Factor:** Acknowledge that a few minutes daily cannot overcome years of poor posture; accumulated time is key.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text explicitly warns against compensating with lumbar arching, which is critical for an athlete with chronic back pain. It emphasizes "ab tension" and "glutes tight" to protect the lower spine during overhead work.
*   **OHS Limiter (50 kg):** The Clean-Grip Overhead Squat section directly addresses this limitation by suggesting starting with a PVC pipe/wide grip if mobility prevents depth, ensuring T-spine extension is used rather than lumbar compensation.
*   **Night Shift/Hunching:** The article notes that "hunching around like a slob 12 hours a day" (relevant to night shift sedentary periods) requires constant posture correction ("Stand and sit up straight!") beyond just workout mobility work.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** While the text discusses overhead positions generally, the emphasis on T-spine extension for stability is directly applicable to maintaining a rigid platform for the push jerk without relying on lumbar hyperextension.
