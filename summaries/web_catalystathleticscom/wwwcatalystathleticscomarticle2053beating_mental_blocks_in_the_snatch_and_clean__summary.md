# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2053/Beating-Mental-Blocks-in-the-Snatch-and-Clean-Jerk/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Progressive Waves:** Use 3-wave structures (e.g., 90-93-96, 92-95-98, 94-97-100) to approach a "freakout threshold" weight gradually rather than linearly. If a miss occurs, drop down slightly (e.g., from 100kg to 97kg), make it, and attempt the target again immediately.
*   **Threshold Training:** Perform 3–5 singles at the heaviest weight currently confident for that session, increasing by 1–2kg weekly until the mental block is overcome.
*   **On The Minute (OTM):** Use a Joe Mills structure: 5 singles each of 3 progressive weights on the minute (70–80% intensity), followed by heavy singles with short rest (~2 mins). Increase OTM weights by 2–3% if all sets are made.
*   **Deload/Reframe:** If stuck, spend 3–6 weeks avoiding max singles entirely; focus on complexes, variations (hangs/blocks), or doubles/triples to reduce pressure and rebuild confidence.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **General Focus:** Shift attention from the weight on the bar to the movement itself ("snatching," not "100kg").
*   **Overhead Confidence:** Hold overhead positions (OHS, snatch balances) for 3–5 seconds; use jerk supports/recoveries and jerks behind the neck.
*   **Aggressive Pulling Under:** Use tall snatches/cleans or power position lifts to train aggressive body movement under the bar.
*   **Front Squat Support:** Use pause front squats and clean rack supports if fear of "getting crushed" exists.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Variations for Weak Points:** Hang/blocks, 2- or 3-position snatches, pull + snatch (for extension issues); snatch + OHS/snatch balance (for overhead stability).
*   **Pull Strength:** Heavy pulls, deadlifts on risers, overloaded pulls from blocks at the knee.
*   **Overhead Stability:** Snatch balances, jerk supports, jerks behind the neck.

**5. Athlete-Specific Relevance**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The recommendation for **Complexes & Reps** is highly relevant; shifting focus from max singles to controlled variations (hangs, blocks) allows strength work without the spinal compression of heavy 1RMs during a mental block phase.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning Status:** The **OTM (On The Minute)** structure is ideal for building consistency and confidence in technique before increasing intensity, addressing the "freakout threshold" common when transitioning to Olympic lifts.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** The article's emphasis on **"Unit Confusion"** (lifting in pounds instead of kg or vice versa) can serve as a low-stakes mental reset if fatigue from shift work is contributing to performance anxiety.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** While the text mentions "jerks behind the neck" and general jerk confidence, it does not specifically address the mechanics of push jerks vs. split jerks; however, the principle of using **jerk supports** applies directly to stabilizing the overhead position required for a push jerk.
