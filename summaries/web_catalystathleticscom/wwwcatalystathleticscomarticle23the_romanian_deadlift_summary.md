# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/23/The-Romanian-Deadlift/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** Moderate volume (e.g., 3–5 sets of 3–6 reps) for strength; higher reps (8–12) only if flexibility is the primary goal.
*   **Intensity:** Sub-maximal to heavy (70–90% 1RM); avoid maximal attempts frequently as it induces high neural fatigue.
*   **Frequency:** Low frequency (1x/week max) due to spinal loading and recovery needs for a chronic back pain sufferer.
*   **Periodization:** Use during the hypertrophy block for posterior chain development; reduce or replace with Snatch/Clean pulls during technique/strength phases to avoid "strength-speed" mismatch with Olympic lifts.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Start Position:** Begin standing, then bend knees ~15° and retract scapulae before initiating the descent.
*   **Spinal Alignment:** Maintain a fixed, naturally arched spine (anterior pelvic tilt/concave lower back); do not round or over-extend at the top.
*   **Hip Hinge:** Push hips back while keeping knee flexion constant; keep bar close to body (shoulders anterior to bar).
*   **Tempo:** Steady and controlled, not explosive.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary:** Romanian Deadlift (RDL) with weightlifting shoes on.
*   **Alternative/Supplement:** Snatch pulls or Clean pulls (preferred for Olympic transferability over RDL).
*   **Advanced Variation:** Standing on blocks to increase range of motion (only if flexibility allows without rounding).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Flexibility Focus:** Use the bottom position of the RDL as a mobility drill; stop descent immediately before low back rounding or excessive knee bending.
*   **Grip Management:** Use lifting straps to preserve grip strength for main lifts (acceptable for this exercise).
*   **Footwear:** Wear competition weightlifting shoes during training for maximum transferability.

**5. Athlete-Specific Relevance**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The RDL is a "pure strengthening" tool but carries risk if form breaks; the text warns that excessive RDL volume can reduce Olympic lift performance and increase injury risk if flexibility limits are ignored. Given the athlete's pain, prioritize strict spinal alignment over depth.
*   **Strength-Speed Mismatch:** The article notes RDLs optimize strength-speed (high force, low velocity), whereas Olympic lifts require speed-strength. For an intermediate lifter with a 6-week hypertrophy goal, this is acceptable for building posterior chain base, but the athlete must transition to explosive pulls later.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** Due to high neural fatigue potential and chronic pain, limit RDLs to once weekly during the shift-heavy Wed–Sun schedule to prevent overtraining.
