# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1948/Ask-Greg-Cant-Receive-the-Snatch-Low-with-Heavier-Weights/

### 1. Programming Principles
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Increase time spent in the bottom receiving position; perform overhead squats (OHS) and snatch balances frequently as warm-ups or main lifts.
*   **Intensity/Load:** Use loads for OHS and snatch balances that are slightly heavier than your current max snatch to build confidence under the bar.
*   **Tempo/Execution:** Perform slow, smooth dips with minimal leg drive during snatch balances; focus on speed of the "punch" (arms/elbows) rather than leg drive.
*   **Isolation:** Hold the bottom position for 2–3 seconds on every rep to build mental comfort and physical stability.
*   **Periodization Strategy:** Eliminate intentional power snatching; only perform full snatch variations or squat immediately after receiving a heavy pull.

### 2. Technique Cues
*   **Mental Cue:** "Allow yourself to pull under the bar"; overcome the lack of confidence that causes you to stay high (power snatch).
*   **Arm Action:** Aggressive pull with arms; elevate elbows as much as possible before turning the bar over.
*   **Dip Mechanics:** Use a slow, smooth dip with minimal leg push—just enough time to get under the bar.
*   **Footwork/Balance:** Focus on balancing over the foot when adjusting to new shoes (higher heels); keep the pull to mid-thigh relatively slow to feel and correct imbalances.

### 3. Exercise Recommendations
*   **Primary Drills:** Overhead Squats (OHS), Snatch Balances, High-Hang Snatches, Tall Snatches, Snatches from high blocks (mid-thigh).
*   **Variations for Confidence:** Snatch pulls and deadlifts performed slowly to mid-thigh; Deadlift/Pull + Snatch complexes.
*   **Correction Drill:** If receiving heavy weights causes heels/knees issues, perform OHS with the target weight first to isolate mobility/stability before snatching.

### 4. Recovery or Mobility Advice
*   **Mobility Focus:** Work on flexibility and stability in the bottom position if the issue persists during overhead squats (e.g., heels rising, knees valgus).
*   **Equipment Adjustment:** If switching to shoes with higher heels causes the bar to drift forward, spend time on snatch pulls/deadlifts to recalibrate balance over the foot.

### 5. Relevance to Athlete Profile
*   **Transitioning from Powerlifting:** The text explicitly warns against "power snatching/clean day" reverting technique away from full squat movements for athletes transitioning from strength training (like your Back Squat background).
*   **OHS Limiter:** The advice directly addresses the 50 kg OHS limitation by suggesting you train OHS and snatch balances at weights *heavier* than your current max to build confidence.
*   **Chronic Pain/Mobility:** While not addressing back pain directly, the emphasis on "slow, smooth dips" and holding bottom positions can help identify if pain stems from poor positioning or lack of stability in the receiving phase.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The recommendation to hold the bottom position for 2–3 seconds serves as a mental reset, which is crucial when fatigue (from night shifts) causes technique breakdown.
