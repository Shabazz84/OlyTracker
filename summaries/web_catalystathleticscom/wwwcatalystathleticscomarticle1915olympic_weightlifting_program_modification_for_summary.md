# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1915/Olympic-Weightlifting-Program-Modification-for-Injury/

**Note:** The provided text is a specific case study on **modifying Olympic weightlifting programs for injuries** (specifically an iliacus tear preventing split jerks and hip flexion). It does not contain general programming principles for hypertrophy blocks, sleep hygiene for night shift workers, or specific mobility advice for chronic back pain unrelated to the injury context.

However, extracting relevant **principles** from this text that apply to your profile:

1.  **Programming Principles**
    *   **Substitution over Removal:** Do not remove an exercise entirely; replace it with a variation that avoids aggravating the specific movement pattern while maintaining training volume/intensity (e.g., replacing Split Jerk with Power Jerk).
    *   **Priority Re-ranking:** Identify what you can do without pain, prioritize those movements, and fill the "gaps" left by removed exercises with new work for secondary priorities.
    *   **Intensity Adjustment:** Maintain relative intensity percentages but adjust absolute loads based on the modified movement's capacity (e.g., if a power jerk is used instead of a split jerk, ensure the load is within that specific variation's limit).

2.  **Technique Cues**
    *   **Power Jerk Focus:** If forced to use Power Jerk (due to injury), focus on driving the bar high enough and strengthening the "lower receiving position" via rack pulls or supports to compensate for lack of split mechanics.
    *   **Single-Leg Stability:** Use single-leg squats on a box with the non-working leg hanging straight down as a substitute for lunges to maintain unilateral strength without hip flexion stress.

3.  **Exercise Recommendations (Injury Context Only)**
    *   **Jerk Modification:** Swap all Split Jerk variations for Power Jerks or Power Jerk Recoveries from pins/racks.
    *   **Core Work:** If hip flexion is painful, limit core work to crunches and planks only; avoid weighted back extensions if they aggravate the lower back/hip.
    *   **Lunge Substitute:** Single-leg squats on a plyometric box (non-squatting leg hanging) instead of traditional lunges.

4.  **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
    *   **Psychological Maintenance:** The text emphasizes that "not training" is a psychological problem; maintaining *some* form of training keeps focus and momentum, preventing the need to rebuild from zero upon return.
    *   **Long-term Perspective:** Accept short setbacks to avoid long-term injury protraction.

5.  **Relevance to Athlete Profile**
    *   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text advises that if an exercise (like weighted back extensions) aggravates the injury, it must be removed or modified immediately; do not "push through" pain as this is counter-productive.
    *   **Night Shift Worker:** *Not addressed in text.* However, the principle of maintaining training consistency applies: even with fatigue from night shifts, finding a way to train (even at reduced intensity) prevents total loss of momentum.
    *   **Push Jerk Only:** The text supports your current limitation; Greg Everett notes that being forced into Power/Push Jerks can actually improve strength in the receiving position and upper body stability if done with heavy loads from pins.

**Critical Gap:** This text does not address **hypertrophy programming**, **night shift recovery protocols**, or specific **chronic back pain mobility drills**. It is strictly a guide on *injury modification*, not general periodization for an intermediate athlete.
