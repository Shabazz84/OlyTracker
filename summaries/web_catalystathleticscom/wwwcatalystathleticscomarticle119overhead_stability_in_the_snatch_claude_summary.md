# Analysis: "Overhead Stability in the Snatch" by Greg Everett

## Technique Cues (Snatch Overhead Position)

**Scapular/Shoulder Foundation:**
- Retract shoulder blades tightly; pinch top inside edges together (not a shrug)
- Allow upward rotation to open space for humerus
- This creates rigid foundation—critical for this athlete (OHS 50 kg is primary limiter)

**Elbow Position:**
- Lock elbows into full extension/slight hyperextension (bone lock principle)
- Squeeze into extension directly; avoid indirect cues like "pull bar apart"
- Tight grip inhibits elbow extension

**Hand/Wrist Position:**
- Bar in palm slightly behind forearm centerline
- Allow wrist to extend (not neutral)—requires mobility work
- Head pushed forward through arms; bar over back of neck/top of traps
- Avoid head tucked back (prevents proper scapular position)

**Grip Width:**
- Balance between hip crease contact and elbow extension ability
- Wider grip increases over-rotation risk

## Exercise Recommendations

- **Overhead mobility work** (wrist extension, thoracic spine, ankle/hip flexibility) — essential for this athlete's chronic back pain and low OHS
- Diagnose lower-body inflexibility (ankles/hips/T-spine) as overhead instability source

## Specific to This Athlete

⚠️ **Critical:** OHS at 50 kg suggests structural/mobility issues rather than pure strength deficit. This article's diagnostic framework is directly applicable. Prioritize:
1. Scapular positioning drills
2. Wrist/ankle/hip mobility (back pain + low OHS = forward trunk lean likely)
3. Elbow lockout strength if structure is sound

**Relevant given chronic back pain:** Address lower-body flexibility and squat mechanics to prevent excessive forward trunk lean overhead.
