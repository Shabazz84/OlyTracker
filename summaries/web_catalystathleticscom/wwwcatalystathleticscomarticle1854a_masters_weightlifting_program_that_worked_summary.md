# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1854/A-Masters-Weightlifting-Program-that-Worked/

**This text is a retrospective case study of a Masters lifter (age 36) and does not contain specific programming for an intermediate athlete with chronic back pain or night shift work.**

However, based on the principles in the text applied to your profile:

1.  **Programming Principles:**
    *   **Frequency:** Limit to **2 sessions/week** (e.g., Tue/Sat). High volume is counter-productive for recovery at this age/stress level.
    *   **Intensity:** Avoid training to failure or maximums in the gym. Stick to **80–90% of 1RM**. Save max attempts for competition days only.
    *   **Periodization:** Use a "light/heavy" split (e.g., Light Snatch/Heavy Clean & Jerk on one day; Heavy Snatch/Light C&J on the other). Include planned deload weeks every 4–6 weeks.

2.  **Technique Cues:**
    *   The text emphasizes **"technical solidity"** over weight. If a top set is sloppy, repeat it to fix the movement rather than adding weight.
    *   Focus on "getting back in shape" gradually; do not "rip into big lifts right away."

3.  **Exercise Recommendations:**
    *   **Core Lifts Only:** Snatch, Clean & Jerk, Back Squat.
    *   **Exclusions:** The author explicitly removed Front Squats due to "wear and tear," relying on Back Squats for leg strength.
    *   **Assistance:** RDLs/Core work are secondary; do not program heavy assistance loads that compete with main lifts.

4.  **Recovery/Mobility Advice:**
    *   **Crucial for Night Shifters:** The text highlights that high job stress (60–70 hours/week) necessitates low training volume to avoid overtraining. Your night shift schedule requires similar prioritization of sleep and recovery over extra sets.
    *   **Deloads:** Deliberately plan deload weeks to prevent burnout.

5.  **Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Chronic Back Pain, Night Shift):**
    *   **Back Pain:** The author's decision to drop Front Squats and limit heavy squatting frequency (keeping it 2–3 weeks away from meets) is highly relevant for managing chronic back pain.
    *   **Night Shifts:** The "Less is More" philosophy is critical here; your recovery capacity is likely lower than a day-shift athlete, making the 2-day/week high-intensity model superior to a 4-6 day split.
    *   **Transitioning:** Since you are intermediate with a strong squat (118kg) but weak OHS (50kg), the text advises against "maxing" in training. Focus on consistent, sub-maximal technical work rather than chasing PRs in the gym.

*Note: The author's specific program was designed for an elite Masters lifter with 20 years of experience; as a transitioning intermediate, you may need slightly more volume on technique drills initially, but the recovery constraints remain identical.*
