# Analysis: Patellar Tendonitis Recovery Protocol

**Relevance to athlete:** HIGHLY RELEVANT. This athlete has chronic back pain and will squat heavily during a 6-week hypertrophy block. Patellar tendonitis prevention/management is critical given the OHS limitation (50 kg) and upcoming volume increases.

---

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Return-to-squat structure:** Light, high-rep work first (50–60% for 8–10 reps, 3–5 sets) for 2 weeks to prepare connective tissue
- **Progression timeline:** 2 weeks @ 8s → 2 weeks @ 6s → lighter/lower-volume week → heavy 5s
- **Olympic lifts during rehab:** Max 3 reps/set, light loads (avoid power variations—they cause excessive quad tension at bottom)
- **Tempo prescription:** Controlled eccentric (3–8 sec down), 1 sec pause, 1–3 sec concentric; avoid bounce
- **Volume management:** Lower total squat reps if pain recurs; prioritize quality over intensity

---

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- Controlled descent with sustained tension through bottom position
- No bouncing at the bottom—change direction smoothly
- Maintain foot alignment: thigh parallel to foot when viewed from above (prevents knee valgus/varus tracking issues)
- For OHS specifically: athlete prone to riding inside of feet and knee valgus—emphasize external foot/hip positioning

---

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
**Prehab/Mobility:**
- Foam roll quads (especially rectus femoris and lateral quad/ITB junction) before training
- Stretch quads and hip flexors post-warm-up; repeat between sets
- Lacrosse ball work: above kneecap (quad tendon convergence) and ITB
- Ankle/calf mobility; anterior tibialis activation; glute strengthening/activation
- Consider eccentric/isometric quad work (8 sec down, 1 sec pause, 8 sec up tempos)

**Supplemental:**
- Unweighted lunges (8–12 reps, high reps, controlled)—start unilateral work conservatively
- Bilateral movements prioritized initially
- Leg press machine (light, high volume)
- Stationary bike (low knee stress, high volume blood flow)

**Avoid:**
- Ballistic/plyometric work
- Running, cutting, rapid direction changes until heavy squat phase resumes

---

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- **Warmup:** Extended knee warming (don't rush); consider training in pants vs. shorts to retain heat
- **Post-session:** Lacrosse ball work to quad tendon area → ice 15–20 min (2× daily if possible)
- **Investigation:** Address ankle/calf mobility, hip mobility, glute function—pain often originates above/below knee
- **Sleep consideration:** Night shift schedule (Wed–Sun) may impair recovery; prioritize sleep quality around training

---

## 5. **Athlete-Specific Application**
| Issue | Recommendation |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Chronic back pain + heavy hypertrophy block** | Manage squat loading carefully; high-rep work (8–10 reps) may distribute load better than heavy 5s early on |
| **Low OHS (50 kg)** | Tendonitis/quad tightness likely limiting depth/stability—address mobility + quad strength in light-load phase first |
| **Night shift worker** | Front-load recovery practices (ice, mobility) post-training; consider warm-up duration critical given circadian fatigue |
| **Push jerk only (no split jerk)** | Less knee demand than split jerk; can prioritize lighter OHS/squat work without compromising jerk development |

---

**Bottom line:** Delay heavy squatting 2–3 weeks into hypertrophy block using 50–60% for 8–10 reps with strict tempo and extensive prehab/mobility work to bulletproof the knees before ramping volume.
