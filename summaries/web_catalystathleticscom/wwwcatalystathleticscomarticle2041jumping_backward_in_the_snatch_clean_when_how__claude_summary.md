# Analysis: "Jumping Backward in Snatch & Clean" by Greg Everett

## Relevant to This Athlete

**Technique Cues (Snatch/Clean):**
- **Backward jump assessment**: Acceptable only if body, barbell, feet move equally backward AND lifter receives in solid balanced position. This athlete's weak OHS (50kg) suggests balance/positioning issues—backward jumping may mask technical deficits.
- **Foot placement problem** (likely relevant): If feet slide backward while bar/body stay centered, it's an error. Cause: backward balance or excessive knee flexion without hip flexion.
  - *Fix*: Think "lift knees up, keep soles parallel to floor" (active hip flexion)
- **Common causes of backward jump errors**:
  1. **Over-extending** → hips too far back
  2. **Weight on heels** → retrain neutral foot balance
  3. **Pushing bar too far back** into lap (incomplete hip extension)
  4. **Opening hips too soon** → shoulders pull back at knee instead of staying over bar

**Exercise Recommendations (Progressional):**
- Drop snatches / snatch balances (foot/leg focus)
- Tall snatches / tall cleans
- Snatches from power position / cleans from power position
- Slow-pull snatch/clean
- Halting deadlifts / segment pulls
- Pull + lift complexes
- Rubber mat cue (behind heels, don't touch it)

**Recovery Note:**
- Back pain context: maintaining "pressure against platform until finished extending" requires posterior chain stability—relevant for chronic pain management during hypertrophy block.

---

**Bottom line**: This article directly addresses technical debugging for an intermediate lifter. The backward foot-sliding error is likely contributing to this athlete's weak OHS and positional instability.
