# Analysis for Intermediate OLY Weightlifter (102.5kg, Back Squat 118kg, Clean 80kg, Jerk 65kg, OHS 50kg)

## 1. Programming Principles
- **Posterior chain strengthening** via RDL develops strength-speed (high force, low velocity) — useful but less specific than snatch/clean pulls
- **Lower frequency recommendation**: RDL not mandatory; clean/snatch pulls may be superior for OLY specificity
- Suggests varying intensity throughout training cycles to avoid overtraining

## 2. Technique Cues (RDL)
- Start: ~15° knee flexion, scapular retraction, anterior pelvic tilt, braced spine
- Descent: hip-hinge (push hips back), maintain spinal arch, bar close to body, shoulders anterior to bar
- Common fault: excessive knee flexion + lumbar rounding (common in tight athletes)
- Cue: steady/controlled tempo, not explosive

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
- **RDL limitations for OLY lifters**: less specific than clean/snatch pulls; may reduce competitive lift performance if overdone
- **Better alternatives**: prioritize snatch pulls, clean pulls for posterior chain work
- Useful for: general posterior chain strength, mobility (flexibility tool at range limit)

## 4. Recovery/Mobility Advice
- RDL as **mobility tool** in tight athletes (use bottom position as stretch point)
- **Functional** for hip extension strength (running, jumping, injury prevention)

## 5. Athlete-Specific Relevance
- **High priority**: chronic back pain + weak OHS (50kg) → posterior chain & spinal stability work indicated; RDL's emphasis on anterior pelvic tilt & spinal bracing is **relevant**
- **Caution**: night shift fatigue → prioritize high-specificity lifts (pulls/competitive lifts); RDL secondary
- Consider RDL as **supplemental mobility + stability tool** rather than primary strength builder; snatch pulls more efficient
