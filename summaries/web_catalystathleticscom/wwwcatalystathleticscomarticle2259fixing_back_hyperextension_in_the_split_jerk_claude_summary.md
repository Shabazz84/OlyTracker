# Analysis: Fixing Back Hyperextension in the Split Jerk

## Relevant to Athlete Profile

**HIGHLY RELEVANT** — This athlete has chronic back pain and uses push jerk only (no split jerk). This article directly addresses lumbar hyperextension mechanics that likely contribute to back issues.

---

## Key Extracts by Category

### 1. **Technique Cues – Split Jerk (Back Hyperextension)**
- **Spine neutral, forcefully braced trunk** in receiving position
- **Back knee slightly bent** (not locked straight)
- Avoid forward drive/push of arms into split
- **Drive the bar high enough** to receive it cleanly (don't wedge under)
- Poor positioning causes compensatory lower back extension

### 2. **Root Causes (Diagnostic)**
1. **Limited overhead/thoracic mobility** → lower back extends to compensate
2. **Weak abs/poor bracing** → trunk becomes loose link in chain
3. **Back leg too straight** → anterior pelvic rotation extends lumbar spine
4. **Forward drive** → imbalance forces backward compensation
5. **Insufficient bar drive** → forces body to shorten via lumbar extension

### 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- **Daily trunk stability work:** flexion, lateral flexion, rotation, isometric, anti-rotation, back extension
- Improve shoulder/thoracic mobility (referenced videos)
- Hip flexor flexibility work
- Proper bracing on all lifts, even warm-ups

### 4. **Programming Principle**
- Trunk bracing as **daily priority** across all loads
- Gradual adaptation: "the more you do it, the better you get"

---

## Critical Note for This Athlete
Push jerk eliminates split position risk but **OHS weakness (50 kg) + chronic back pain suggests mobility and trunk control deficits.** Prioritize thoracic mobility and anti-rotation/isometric core work *before* hypertrophy block to protect spine during increased volume.
