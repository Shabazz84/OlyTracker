**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Prioritization:** Identify the single biggest limitation (e.g., leg strength) and prioritize that lift in the program structure; drop exercises that do not serve this priority.
*   **Frequency Modification:** When reducing frequency (e.g., from 5–6 days to 3), compress the weekly volume into fewer sessions rather than simply deleting random days.
*   **Order of Operations:** Perform technical/competition lifts (Snatch, Clean & Jerk) at the start of the session while fresh; perform strength work (squats) immediately after or as the primary focus depending on the specific goal (e.g., if leg strength is priority, squat first).
*   **Volume Management:** Start with a "minimal" end of the spectrum to test recovery capacity. If recovery fails, strip volume further rather than starting high and risking overtraining.
*   **Flexibility:** Use a template approach; be willing to swap exercises (e.g., snatch balances for overhead squats) or drop accessory work based on daily fatigue levels.

*   **General Principle:** Perform speed and precision work (competition lifts) when "freshest" to ensure quality execution.
*   **Warm-up Logic:** Use lighter variations of the main lift (e.g., Overhead Squat before Back Squat) as a warm-up for the heavier movement rather than doing them after heavy fatigue sets.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Lifts:** Snatch, Clean & Jerk (CJ), Back Squat, Front Squat.
*   **Pulling Variations:** Snatch Pull, Snatch Deadlift, Clean Pull, Clean Deadlift.
*   **Accessory/Strength:** Overhead Squat (OHS), Stiff-Legged Deadlift (SLDL) – *Note: SLDL can be dropped if squatting volume is high; replace with back extensions for direct back work.*
*   **Dropped Exercises:** Conditioning workouts, Muscle Snatch, Push Press (if OHS/Snatch Balance covers the goal).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Recovery Strategy:** If recovery is a limiting factor, start on the minimal end of the training spectrum and add volume only after confirming adaptation.
*   **Non-Lifting Activities:** Temporarily drop non-essential training (e.g., cardiorespiratory conditioning) to free up recovery resources; these qualities are regained quickly once lifting resumes.
*   **Fatigue Management:** On "beat down" weeks, strip away more accessory work; on high-energy days ("saucy"), add minor accessories like muscle snatches if time permits.
