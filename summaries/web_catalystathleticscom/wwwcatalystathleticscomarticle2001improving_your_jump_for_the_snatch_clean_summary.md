# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2001/Improving-Your-Jump-for-the-Snatch-Clean/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Placement:** Introduce jump work at the end of a training session or on a separate General Physical Preparedness (GPP) day to avoid interfering with main lift quality.
*   **Progression:** Do not introduce all variations simultaneously; introduce gradually into the training cycle.
*   **Focus:** Distinguish between "Jumping Exercises" (maximizing height/length for raw power) and "Auxiliary Exercises" (focusing on timing, coordination, and landing position).

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Landing Position:** When performing jumps with the barbell (e.g., Clean/Snatch Pulls), land in the exact same spot where you started; do not jump forward or backward.
*   **Execution:** The jump must be a logical, powerful finish to the pull, emphasizing speed and accuracy over pure height in auxiliary work.
*   **Depth Jumps:** Execute as a single continuous movement (jump down into a pit, immediately rebound up) without delay.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Bodyweight/Barbell Jumps:** Box jumps, obstacle jumps, long/broad jumps, depth jumps.
*   **Squat-to-Jump Variations:** Jumping out of the full bottom position with a barbell on the back (can be dynamic or paused).
*   **Auxiliary Barbell Drills:**
    *   Clean/Snatch "Out of the Pit": Finish the lift by jumping onto two bumper plates placed outside the feet.
    *   Clean/Snatch Pull with Jump: A pull finished with a vertical jump.
    *   Snatch Balance & Power Jerk (from back): Focuses on leg drive and drop speed; easier to balance than full lifts.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Positive:** The "Jumping out of full bottom with barbell" exercise directly targets leg speed and explosive power, which is crucial for an intermediate lifter transitioning to Olympic lifts who currently relies on a push jerk (lacking split mechanics).
*   **Cautionary:** The athlete has chronic back pain. Exercises involving jumping *out of the full bottom position* or depth jumps with a barbell place significant compressive and shear forces on the spine. These high-impact movements must be introduced very cautiously, potentially starting without weight or using lighter loads to monitor spinal tolerance before progressing to "Out of the Pit" drills.
