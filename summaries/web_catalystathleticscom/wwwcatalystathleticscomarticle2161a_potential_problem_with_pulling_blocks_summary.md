# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2161/A-Potential-Problem-With-Pulling-Blocks/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Caution over exclusion:** Blocks are useful for building "snap and pop" but must be used with strict monitoring of technique.
*   **Intensity management:** The primary risk factor is using too much weight on blocks, which forces the lifter to compensate with poor mechanics.
*   **Frequency/Usage:** Can be incorporated regularly by advanced lifters without issue, but requires a "keen eye" for new and intermediate athletes.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Primary Cue:** Initiate the pull by moving hips **UP**, not forward. Avoid shoving hips forward too early or dramatically.
*   **Visual Check:** Ensure shoulders do not stretch backward simultaneously with hip movement; maintain vertical bar path.
*   **Error Correction:** Prevent "jumping forward" during turnover, which occurs when the lifter tries to "weasel under" the bar due to insufficient upward drive.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Pulling Blocks:** Specifically recommended for developing explosive power ("snap and pop") but only if the lifter can maintain vertical hip movement.
*   *Note:* The text does not recommend specific accessory exercises, focusing solely on the mechanics of block pulls.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   None provided in this specific text excerpt.

**5. Relevance to athlete profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **High Risk for Technique Flaw:** As an intermediate lifter, this athlete is explicitly identified as a demographic prone to developing the "jumping forward" habit when using blocks due to leg/hip strength limitations.
*   **Back Pain Warning:** The text notes that lifting heavy from blocks often causes lifters to "ram their hips forward and stretch shoulders backward." This specific compensation pattern (excessive anterior pelvic tilt/forward lean) is a known aggravator for chronic back pain.
*   **Actionable Advice:** If this athlete uses pulling blocks, they must strictly avoid high intensities that force the hip-forward compensation. Given the chronic back pain and intermediate status, floor pulls or hang pulls with lighter loads may be safer than block pulls until upward drive mechanics are solidified.
