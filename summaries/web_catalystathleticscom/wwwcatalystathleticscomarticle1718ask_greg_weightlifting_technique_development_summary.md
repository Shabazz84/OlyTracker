# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1718/Ask-Greg-Weightlifting-Technique-Development/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Progression Strategy:** Graduate loading conservatively (1–2 kg jumps) to prevent movement breakdown and psychological doubt.
*   **Volume Distribution:** Accumulate high volume of accurate practice at lighter weights (e.g., 70kg) while keeping heavier attempts (e.g., 75kg+) infrequent (mostly singles). Heavier loads should represent a small percentage of total volume until consistency improves.
*   **Skill Acquisition Rule:** "The only technique that will come is the one that's practiced." Avoid training to failure or maxing out early, as this entrenches poor mechanics.
*   **Foundation First:** Start with very light weights/empty bar to master basic positions before adding load; do not attempt lifts entirely properly with an empty bar eventually, but use it for initial consistency.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Snatch Specifics:** Stop power snatching; squat everything on every rep. Hold the bottom receiving position for 3 seconds on every single rep to build confidence in the low position. Use snatch balances as a reference point rather than Overhead Squat (OHS) maxes, as there is no strong correlation between OHS strength and snatch weight.
*   **General:** Maintain consistency with one approach initially; do not "spin wheels" by constantly switching methods or reading conflicting advice.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Snatch Balance:** Essential for building confidence in the receiving position and linking overhead stability to the pull.
*   **Overhead Squat (OHS):** Keep training this, but prioritize time-under-tension/holding at the bottom over increasing load immediately if it is a limiter.
*   **Demo/Imitation:** Use coach demonstrations or video analysis for initial learning phases before adding load.
