# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/37/Breathing-and-Breath-Control-for-Olympic-Weightlifting/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Intensity/Frequency:** Not explicitly defined in this text; the focus is strictly on intra-lift mechanics rather than periodization or volume schemes.
*   **Breath Strategy:** Inhale deeply (75% max) into the belly *before* the lift to expand the thoracic cavity and push the diaphragm down. Hold breath while tightening abs/back to create internal pressure (Valsalva). Exhale only at the sticking point if dizzy, or after the rep is completed.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **"Do Not Hollow":** Explicitly avoid drawing abdominals in; this reduces the base of support and structural integrity.
*   **Cylinder vs. Hourglass:** Visualize the torso as a rigid cylinder (broad base) rather than an hourglass shape to maximize force transfer from legs/hips to bar.
*   **Push Abs Down:** To activate stabilization without hollowing, cue "pushing the abs down" to increase circumferential tightness while maintaining abdominal expansion.
*   **Glottis Closure:** Close the glottis (naturally) during the effort phase to trap air and pressurize the trunk.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Unweighted Squats:** Use as a diagnostic tool to practice torso pressurization without load before adding weight.
*   **Submaximal Loads:** For lighter weights, normal breathing patterns are acceptable; for heavy loads, strict breath-holding is required.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Dizziness Management:** If light-headedness occurs (due to vagus nerve stimulation from high pressure), release a small amount of air with noise during the sticking point to drop pressure without losing total core rigidity.
*   **Safety Protocol:** If dizziness is considerable, immediately drop the bar and sit down safely; do not attempt to finish the rep while unconscious or near-fainting.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The text directly addresses this by explaining that pressurization reduces pressure on intervertebral discs and preserves spinal extension, which is critical for an athlete with chronic back pain.
*   **Transitioning to Olympic Lifting:** The emphasis on "structural integrity" and avoiding the "hollow" cue is vital for a beginner transitioning from powerlifting (where hollowing is common) to Olympic lifting, where rigidity under dynamic load is required.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery Context:** While not explicitly linked to circadian rhythms in this text, the warning about dizziness and blood pressure drops during breath-holding is crucial for a night-shift worker who may have altered physiological responses or fatigue levels; they must be vigilant about releasing air if lightheaded.
*   **Jerk Limitation (Push Jerk only):** The technique of explosive second pulls with slight air expulsion ("making noise") to increase aggressiveness applies directly to the drive phase of a push jerk.
