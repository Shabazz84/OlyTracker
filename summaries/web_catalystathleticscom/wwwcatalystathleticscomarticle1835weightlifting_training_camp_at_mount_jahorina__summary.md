# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1835/Weightlifting-Training-Camp-at-Mount-Jahorina-Bosnia-and-Her/

**Note:** This text is a case study of an elite-level Bulgarian-style training camp for youth/national team athletes in Bosnia, not a specific coaching plan for the intermediate athlete described. However, relevant principles can be extracted with significant caveats regarding intensity and recovery.

1.  **Programming Principles**
    *   **Structure:** High-frequency "Bulgarian" model (2 sessions/day on heavy days: AM/PM; 1 session/day on lighter days).
    *   **Frequency:** 6 days/week of training. Heavy days involve maximal attempts at Squats and Snatch/C&J, followed by back-off sets (80-85%).
    *   **Intensity:** High reliance on "max" attempts daily. The author notes this caused psychological burnout after ~14 days for the athletes involved.
    *   **Periodization:** Short-duration block (15–21 days max). The text explicitly warns against extending this intensity beyond 3 weeks without steroids or elite recovery resources.

2.  **Technique Cues**
    *   *None provided in the text.* The article focuses on volume, frequency, and load management rather than technical cues for the lifts.

3.  **Exercise Recommendations**
    *   **Primary Lifts:** Front Squat, Back Squat, Snatch (Power & Full), Clean & Jerk (Power & Full).
    *   **Accessory/Supplemental:** Hyperextensions, Abs, Push Press, Snatch/Clean Pulls.
    *   **Specific Note:** The author explicitly avoided "max squats" for his athletes to prevent injury, opting instead for back-off sets at 80-85% of max.

4.  **Recovery or Mobility Advice**
    *   **Contrast Therapy:** Alternating hot and cold showers immediately post-workout.
    *   **Active Recovery:** 5–10 minutes of easy swimming in a pool after every workout.
    *   **Supplements:** Creatine monohydrate, L-Arginine, Beta Alanine, ZMA, "Horse Power" (proprietary blend).

5.  **Relevance to Athlete Profile**
    *   **Critical Warning on Intensity:** The text explicitly states that even elite youth athletes struggled psychologically and physically with daily max attempts after 2 weeks. For an intermediate athlete with **chronic back pain**, attempting a daily "max" squat/snatch regimen is contraindicated and likely to cause injury or flare-ups.
    *   **Recovery Mismatch:** The recommended recovery (daily hot/cold showers, pool access) assumes resources the user may not have, but the *concept* of contrast therapy is applicable.
    *   **Frequency vs. Capacity:** The "2 sessions/day" model is unsustainable for a night-shift worker with limited sleep and an intermediate strength base; it risks overtraining rather than hypertrophy.
    *   **Conclusion:** Do **not** adopt the daily max squat/snatch frequency. Use the *structure* of alternating heavy/light days (e.g., Heavy AM/Light PM) but strictly limit intensity to sub-maximal back-off sets (70-80%) to accommodate the chronic back pain and night shift schedule. The 21-day cap mentioned is a hard ceiling for high-intensity blocks, not a recommendation for a 6-week hypertrophy block.
