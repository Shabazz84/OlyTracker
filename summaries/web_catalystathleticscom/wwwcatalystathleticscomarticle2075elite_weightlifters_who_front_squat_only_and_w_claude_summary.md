# Analysis: "Elite Weightlifters Who Front Squat Only"

## **Relevance to This Athlete**
Highly relevant. This article directly addresses squat programming for intermediate OWL athletes—your profile.

---

## **Key Extractions**

### 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Both back squat AND front squat required** for intermediate/development years
- Back squat develops greater overall leg strength (handles heavier loads) → glutes, spinal erectors, core
- Front squat strengthens posture, clean positions, upper body stability (torso, shoulder girdle, arms)
- Elite lifters who "front squat only" did so *after* years of back squat accumulation in early-intermediate phases
- Individual exceptions exist but are rare; follow methods that work for the majority

### 2. **Exercise Recommendations**
- **Use both back and front squats** throughout your training cycle
- Elite examples (Naim, Gough) eliminated back squats only *at advanced years*, not early career
- CJ Cummings includes back squats for "several weeks at the beginning of training cycles" despite being a phenom

### 3. **Directly Applicable to Your Profile**
- Your OHS is your snatch limiter (50 kg) → front squat emphasis improves torso stability, shoulder girdle strength
- Your back squat (118 kg) > clean (80 kg) suggests quad/leg strength baseline exists
- **Chronic back pain context**: Article notes back squats build spinal erector strength; should be programmed carefully (not eliminated)
- Night shift worker status → consistent frequency matters more than exotic programming

---

## **Recommendation for Your 6-Week Hypertrophy Block**
Include both; prioritize front squats for clean posture/OHS carryover, but maintain back squats to build spinal stabilizer resilience (addresses chronic pain) and overall leg mass.
