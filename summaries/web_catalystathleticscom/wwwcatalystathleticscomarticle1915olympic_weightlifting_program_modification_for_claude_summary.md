# Analysis: Olympic Weightlifting Program Modification for Injury (Greg Everett)

## 1. PROGRAMMING PRINCIPLES
- **Injury-first approach**: Avoid aggravating the injury; prioritize long-term recovery over short-term gains
- **Strategic substitution**: Replace removed exercises with work addressing other priorities—don't just cut volume
- **Maintain intensity**: Adjust weights proportionally when swapping exercises (e.g., if original was 85%, adjust modified version to 75%, then follow same weekly percentage increases)
- **Periodization flexibility**: Modify within existing meso structure; reassess after each block
- **Maintain psychological momentum**: Continue training in *some* capacity to preserve motivation and mental focus

## 2. TECHNIQUE CUES / EXERCISE SUBSTITUTIONS
- **Power Jerk Recovery** (pins): Strengthens lower receiving position when split jerk unavailable
- **Jerk Supports** (high pins): Heavy isometric holds to build lockout strength without full ROM demands
- **Single-Leg Box Squats**: Alternative for single-leg work when lunges contraindicated (preserves weakness focus without hip flexor load)

## 3. EXERCISES RELEVANT TO THIS ATHLETE
**Highly relevant—chronic back pain + night shift stress:**
- ✅ Back extensions, planks, crunches (avoid hip flexion ab work)
- ✅ Pause front squats (controlled, reduced spinal load vs. dynamic)
- ✅ Power jerk emphasis (forces better drive mechanics; this athlete's stated limiter is snatch + split jerk weakness)
- ⚠️ **OHS work**: Already limited (50 kg). Consider regression to unloaded OHS drills or rack positions before progressing
- ⚠️ Avoid: High hip flexor demand (lunges, certain ab work) given back history

## 4. RECOVERY / MOBILITY
- Warm-up pattern given: high-knee walking lunges + back extension holds
- No split-position work during injury phase
- Controlled ROM progression (passive → unloaded → light loaded → full)

## 5. SPECIFIC TO THIS ATHLETE'S PROFILE
- **Back pain + night shift (fatigue)**: Injury risk is elevated. Use hypertrophy block (high volume, lower intensity) wisely—consider lighter technical work early in night shifts
- **Split jerk deficit**: This text's power jerk emphasis could actually benefit this athlete's jerk weak point, but OHS (50 kg) is true snatch limiter—needs targeted mobility/receiving position work separate from injury management
- **6-week hypertrophy block ahead**: Ideal window to build robustness *before* heavy jerk work; integrate back extension/stability work throughout

---
**Bottom line**: Everett's framework—find what *you can* train, avoid re-injury, maintain stimulus—is directly applicable to your chronic back situation during the planned hypertrophy block.
