# Analysis: "Ask Greg: Slow Learning & Slow Lifting"

## 1. Programming Principles
- **Motor learning emphasis over speed**: Prioritize technique consistency before explosiveness; speed develops naturally with proper mechanics
- **Volume of practice**: Invest substantial repetitions (thousands) to reprogram movement habits—20 reps insufficient
- **Progressive complexity**: Break lifts into smaller components, master each in isolation, then string together gradually
- **Genetic reality**: Natural speed/athleticism limits rate of improvement; all athletes can improve but within genetic constraints

## 2. Technique Cues (Snatch & Clean)
- Three elements make lifts quick: (1) explosive knee/hip extension at top of pull, (2) timing of direction change at top, (3) aggression pulling under bar
- **Abbreviated positions**: High-hang variations, knee-bend-only with vertical torso creates "snappy, abrupt" movement
- Address immobility/form breakdown: May indicate poor warm-up or inconsistent foot placement, not always a mobility issue

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
- Box jumps (30") and single-leg work (mentioned as current tools)
- **High-hang pulls/snatch** for speed development
- **Knee-bend-only vertical pulls** to isolate explosive extension

## 4. Recovery/Mobility Advice
- Inadequate warm-up may cause intermittent form breakdown (heels turning in)
- Consistency in setup (foot placement) essential before addressing mobility

## 5. Relevance to This Athlete's Profile
- **High priority**: OHS weakness (50 kg) likely reflects mobility + motor learning deficit—apply "boring repetition" model to OHS progressions before loading
- **Back pain consideration**: Emphasize overhead position consistency and stability via abbreviated drills before heavy loading
- **Night shift worker**: Consistency in warm-up/readiness critical; form breakdown on fatigued days expected
- **Push jerk only**: Split jerk timing/coordination adds complexity—master current pattern before advancing
