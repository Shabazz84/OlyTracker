# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2215/Fix-the-Hips-Shooting-Up-in-the-Snatch-Clean/

1. **Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency/Volume:** Implement high-frequency practice of the first pull (snatch/clean) using pauses and slow eccentrics to reinforce position before adding intensity.
*   **Intensity:** Reduce load on pulls/deadlifts if necessary to maintain perfect posture; prioritize accuracy over weight.
*   **Periodization:** Use "position-specific strength" blocks where exercises are only valid if the lifter can hold the correct starting angle (small knee angle) without hip rising.

2. **Technique Cues**
*   **The "Squat" Cue:** Imagine pushing legs through the floor to drive the chest up, exactly as in a back squat, rather than just pulling with arms.
*   **Tension Check:** Ensure full-body tension before bar separation; eliminate slack by pushing against the floor immediately.
*   **Speed vs. Position:** Do not confuse hip speed with bar speed; if hips rise significantly faster than the bar, the pull is "yanking" (bad). Bar must move commensurately with hips.
*   **Back Angle:** Maintain a fairly constant back angle from the moment the bar leaves the floor to the knee.

3. **Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Pause Pulls/Deadlifts:** Pause immediately after bar separation and at the knee to enforce correct posture.
*   **Floating Pulls/Deadlifts:** Lift the bar, reset position, and pull again without lowering fully (or with minimal drop) to practice starting mechanics.
*   **Pulls from Riser:** Elevate the bar on a riser to force smaller knee angles at the start, building strength in that specific range of motion.
*   **Pause Back Squats:** Perform squats with pauses at the bottom to develop strength from small knee angles and prevent early hip rise.

5. **Specific Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The article's emphasis on maintaining a "constant back angle" and avoiding the "hips shooting up" is critical for this athlete, as hip rising often exacerbates lumbar shear forces in lifters with back issues.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The advice to reduce weight to fix position ("Everyone hates doing this...") directly addresses the common intermediate trap of lifting too heavy before mastering the first pull mechanics.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** While not explicitly about OHS, strengthening the legs at "smaller knee angles" via riser pulls and pause squats will improve the initial drive, potentially aiding overall bar path control needed for overhead stability.
