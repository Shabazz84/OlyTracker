# Analysis: Top 5 Assistance Exercises for the Snatch

## Relevant Extracts for This Athlete

### 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Snatch pulls**: 90–105% of snatch max (can vary by development level); can overload, train speed, or focus on mechanics
- **Muscle snatch**: 3 reps as technique primer before snatches; 1–2 reps + snatch as warm-up couplet; sets of 3–5 as standalone strength work
- **Snatch balance**: Progress from overhead squats → snatch balances as lifter advances; use drop snatch for speed/aggression, snatch balance for strength/confidence
- **Tall snatch**: 25–30% of snatch max; 2–3 rep sets; prioritize form over load

### 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Muscle snatch**: Proper "pull under" mechanics (third pull) — must match snatch pull-under, not cheat with partial range
- **Tall snatch**: Pull elbows up/out hard and high; move feet and plant flat under bar; minimize initial bar lift, focus on moving down fast; can do flat-footed (preferred for balance) or on toes
- **Dip snatch**: Aggressive leg drive finish; better bar proximity; correct hip overreach; practice foot transitions
- **General**: "Properly controlled aggression—maximal effort channeled into precise movement with perfect timing"

### 3. **Exercise Recommendations for This Athlete**
**Highly Relevant (OHS 50 kg = primary limiter):**
- **Overhead squat** → **snatch balance progression** (addresses receiving position strength, lockout timing, precision, footwork, confidence)
- **Tall snatch** (can use ~20–25 kg; teaches pull-under aggression and foot mechanics without load)
- **Muscle snatch** (primes turnover; light weights OK)
- **Drop snatch** (emphasizes footwork, confidence in receiving)

**Secondary (strength builders):**
- **Snatch pulls** (90–105% of 80 kg = 72–84 kg; overloads posterior chain; reduce back load via blocks if chronic back pain flares)

### 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
None explicitly provided in text, but snatch pulls off blocks noted as option to "reduce load on legs for recovery purposes."

### 5. **Profile-Specific Notes**
- **OHS limiter (50 kg)**: Text emphasizes snatch balance and overhead position work—critical for this athlete
- **Back pain**: Snatch pulls off blocks (reduced ROM) may be safer than full pulls; high-volume snatches/pulls risk aggravation during 6-week hypertrophy block
- **No split jerk context**: Text doesn't address; purely snatch-focused
- **Night shift (recovery)**: Not addressed; prioritize technique primers and lighter assistance over heavy volume

---

**Bottom line**: Use muscle snatch, tall snatch, and snatch balance progressions to fix OHS/receiving position deficiency. Manage volume on pulls/snatches due to chronic back pain.
