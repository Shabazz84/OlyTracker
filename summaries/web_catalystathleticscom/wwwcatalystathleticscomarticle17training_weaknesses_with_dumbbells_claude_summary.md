# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/17/Training-Weaknesses-with-Dumbbells/

**This text is about accessory training methodology using dumbbells, not directly about Olympic weightlifting programming or technique.**

**Relevant to this athlete:**

1. **Exercise recommendation (posterior chain emphasis)**
   - Hammer lunge series addresses quad dominance → critical for this athlete with chronic back pain and weak overhead position (50 kg OHS)
   - Unilateral work identifies side-to-side imbalances that destabilize lifts
   - Start light (2–5 lb dumbbells); focus on form, not load

2. **Specific cues applicable here**
   - "Sink hips—don't move knee ahead of ankle" (ankle mobility/positioning)
   - "Pull toe up to fully engage glutes" (posterior chain activation)
   - Perform extra sets on weaker side (addresses imbalances)
   - Don't train to failure during accessory work

3. **Recovery/mobility relevance**
   - Posterior chain weakness (tight hamstrings/glutes) can cascade to lumbar spine issues—directly relevant to chronic back pain
   - Unilateral training may help redistribute load asymmetries contributing to back pain

**Application for this athlete:** Add hammer lunge variations (front, side, overhead) 2–3x/week during 6-week hypertrophy block to shore up posterior chain and lateral stability before addressing jerk mechanics and OHS limitation.
