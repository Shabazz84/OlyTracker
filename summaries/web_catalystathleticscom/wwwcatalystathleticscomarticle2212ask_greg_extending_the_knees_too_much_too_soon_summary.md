# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2212/Ask-Greg-Extending-the-Knees-Too-Much-Too-Soon-in-the-Pull-a/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** High frequency of technical variations (snatch segment deadlifts, pulls with pauses, slow pull snatches).
*   **Sets/Reps:** Low volume, high precision. Focus on "pauses" at specific points (floor, knee, mid-thigh) to ingrain position rather than heavy loading.
*   **Intensity:** Sub-maximal; used for technical drilling and feeling the bar path, not maximal strength development.
*   **Periodization:** Not explicitly defined in this text, but implies a focus on correcting fundamental mechanics before progressing intensity.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Hip vs. Bar Movement:** "Hips move forward significantly," but cues should focus on **"bringing the bar back"** rather than pushing hips forward to avoid weak leg drive.
*   **Shoulder Position:** Maintain a fixed back angle and shoulder position relative to the bar until it passes the knees ("staying over the bar, not moving over the bar").
*   **Knee Extension:** Avoid extending knees too early; this causes "tipping over" and excessive horizontal force (the "rainbow loop"). Knees should not re-bend significantly but rather scoop forward with minimal flexion change.
*   **Bar Path:** Focus on driving the bar **up**, not out. The goal is continuous vertical drive from the legs.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Snatch Segment Deadlifts/Pulls:** With pauses at the floor, knee, and mid-thigh to maintain back angle.
*   **Segment Snatch High-Pulls:** Pause at the knee, then focus on leg drive to move bar up.
*   **Slow Pull Snatches:** To practice finishing from the correct position.
*   **Muscle Snatches & Tall Snatches:** For practicing active arm motion and keeping the body close to the bar during turnover.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The cue to **"maintain a back angle"** and avoid "tipping over" by extending knees prematurely is critical for reducing shear force on the lumbar spine, which is vital for an athlete with chronic pain.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The emphasis on fixing the "rainbow loop" (horizontal bar path) and eliminating the "double knee bend" addresses common technical flaws that limit strength transfer in athletes moving from powerlifting to Olympic lifts.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** While not mentioned, the mental aspect of the text ("endless loop of mental snatch reps") highlights the importance of sleep quality for motor learning; this athlete's schedule requires strict attention to recovery windows to prevent technical regression due to fatigue.
