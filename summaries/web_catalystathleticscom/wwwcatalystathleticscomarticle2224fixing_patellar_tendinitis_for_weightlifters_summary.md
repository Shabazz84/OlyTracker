# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2224/Fixing-Patellar-Tendinitis-for-Weightlifters/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Volume/Intensity:** Temporarily reduce total volume and intensity of squatting/jumping to eliminate the cause of pain; reintroduce gradually once healed.
*   **Rehab Integration:** Perform leg extension negatives (3 sets x 10 reps, 5–6 sec eccentric) and unilateral hamstring curls (3 sets x 10–15 reps) 2–3 days/week at the end of workouts.
*   **Frequency:** Rehab exercises performed daily for massage; rehab strengthening done 3x/week.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   *None provided in text.* The article focuses on injury management rather than lifting mechanics.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Warm-up:** Stationary bike (low resistance), foam rolling quads (10–20 passes), dynamic leg swings, knee circles, unloaded squats.
*   **Rehab/Prevention:** Leg extension negatives (5–6 sec eccentric), unilateral hamstring curls (machine or band).
*   **Massage:** Cross-friction massage on the patellar tendon daily (3–5 mins) perpendicular to fiber direction.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Stretching:** Prioritize rectus femoris and hip flexors; stretch before, after, and *between sets*.
*   **Contrast Therapy:** Use hot/cold contrast baths to stimulate blood flow/healing (do not use ice alone).
*   **Medication:** Discontinue NSAIDs (ibuprofen) as they slow tissue healing.

**5. Athlete-Specific Relevance**
*   **Critical Warning:** The text explicitly advises against using NSAIDs for pain management, which is vital for an athlete with chronic back pain who may be self-medicating.
*   **Injury Context:** While the article addresses *patellar tendinitis*, not the athlete's chronic back pain, the protocol for "fixing early tendinitis" (immediate action, identifying triggers like sudden volume spikes) applies directly to managing their existing condition before it worsens during a hypertrophy block.
*   **Shift Work:** The emphasis on daily mobility and contrast therapy is highly relevant for a night-shift worker with limited recovery windows; these can be done independently of sleep cycles.
