# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2149/Ask-Greg-Hips-Shooting-Back-in-Squats-Cleans/

1. **Programming Principles**: The text does not provide specific sets/reps/intensity/frequency/periodization structures; it focuses on corrective technique drills (pause squats, slow eccentrics) to address movement faults rather than general hypertrophy or strength programming blocks.

2. **Technique Cues**:
   - **Primary Cue**: "Actively fight that tendency" by leading with the chest and driving head/shoulders up first before following with the rest of the body.
   - **Recovery Cue**: Stand straight up from the bottom; do not let hips rise faster than shoulders (tipping over).
   - **Ankle Mobility Focus**: Ensure shins/knees move forward adequately to prevent rebounding backward due to tension at the ankle.

3. **Exercise Recommendations**:
   - **Pause Squats**: Specifically for back squat, front squat, and clean transitions out of the bottom; used to retrain the pattern without the "backward rebound."
   - **Slow Eccentrics**: Controlled lowering phase in squats to strengthen the proper position under load.

4. **Recovery or Mobility Advice**:
   - **Ankle Mobility**: Identified as a root cause for hips shooting back; requires slow, dedicated improvement.
   - **Pattern Retraining**: Emphasizes active mental focus ("fighting" the tendency) during lifts to override bad motor patterns.

5. **Relevance to Athlete Profile**:
   - **Chronic Back Pain**: The advice to "lead with your chest" and avoid tipping over (hips rising faster than shoulders) is critical for maintaining spinal neutrality and reducing shear force on the lumbar spine, which benefits an athlete with chronic back pain.
   - **Transitioning Athlete**: The focus on fixing fundamental squat mechanics (ankle mobility, hip positioning) addresses common issues for intermediate athletes moving to Olympic lifts who may have developed compensatory patterns from powerlifting or general strength training.
   - **No Split Jerk/Push Jerk Only**: While the text focuses on squats/cleans, the principle of "driving head and shoulders up first" is universally applicable to maintaining an upright torso for a push jerk, preventing the hips from shooting back during the dip and drive.
