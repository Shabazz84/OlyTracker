# Analysis: "Quit Relaxing at the Top of Your Pull" — Greg Everett

## Relevance to Athlete Profile
**High relevance.** This article directly addresses snatch and clean technique—critical for this athlete's primary limiter (50 kg OHS) and overall Olympic lifting development.

---

## 1. **Technique Cues: Snatch & Clean**

- **Top-of-pull tension maintenance:** Athletes often collapse/relax at peak extension, losing bar elevation and transition speed. Must stay "rigid" through completion of extension.
- **Pre-lift setup:** Create full breath + forceful back arch + trunk tension *before* initiating lift. Trunk tension never changes until lift completion.
- **Continuous tension rule:** Entire lift is active/aggressive start-to-finish. Pull against bar continuously; only legs change when transitioning under the bar.
- **Top-of-pull cue:** Feel "like someone hit you with an electric cattle prod—extended in a rigid position."
- **Vocalization (grunt/yell):** Creates additional trunk rigidity via pressurization. Should be *result* of aggressive effort, not forced noise-making.

---

## 2. **Direct Problems Addressed (Relevant to This Athlete)**

- Loss of barbell elevation → limits snatch height (acute issue given 50 kg OHS weakness)
- Slow transition under bar → incomplete positioning
- Loose receiving position → pressing out snatches/jerks, chronic pain (THIS ATHLETE HAS BACK PAIN—loose positioning exacerbates it)
