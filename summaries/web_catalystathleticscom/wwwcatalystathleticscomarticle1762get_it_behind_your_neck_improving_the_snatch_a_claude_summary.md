# Analysis: "Get it Behind Your Neck" by Greg Everett

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Sets/Reps:** 5–8 rep range for behind-the-neck pressing work (NOT heavy singles)
- **Frequency:** Beginners with mobility issues = daily; advanced lifters = reduced frequency, increased loading early in training cycle
- **Intensity:** Light loading; all reps must be smooth—no grinding at bottom/middle
- **Structure:** Used as warm-up, accessory, or cool-down work (not a primary strength exercise)

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Overhead position:** Bar must travel straight path from behind neck to overhead position
- **Upper back:** Must extend; shoulder girdle must open to press
- **Setup:** Retract and elevate shoulder blades to create solid shelf for bar
- **Bar path between reps:** Careful, consistent guidance to correct position

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
**For Snatch (OHS limiter):**
- Snatch push press (behind neck) — *primary choice*
- Snatch press (seated/standing)
- Sots press variation (snatch-grip press in squat position)

**For Jerk:**
- Press & push press behind neck
- Split push press behind neck
- Jerk/power jerk behind neck

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**
- **Prerequisite:** Athletes must be mobile enough to perform safely; extremely inflexible may only tolerate light push presses
- **Warning:** Avoid grinding reps; do not force immobile athletes into position (shoulder risk)
- **Progression:** As mobility improves, increase loading for upper-back strengthening

## 5. **Relevance to This Athlete**
✅ **HIGHLY RELEVANT** — OHS 50 kg is primary snatch limiter; chronic back pain requires careful loading.
- **Action:** Begin light behind-the-neck snatch press work (warm-up/cool-down frequency) as foundation for OHS strength/mobility
- **Caution:** Monitor back; prioritize smooth reps over load; avoid grinding
- **Fit:** Can integrate into 6-week hypertrophy block as mobility/technique accessory (3–5×/week, light)
