# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2220/Hierarchy-for-Learning-the-Snatch-and-Clean-Jerk/

1. **Programming principles**: Prioritize a strict hierarchy of **Position → Movement → Speed → Load**. Do not increase load until the movement pattern is mastered at slower speeds; use reduced speed drills (e.g., slow pulls to hold) within sessions to ingrain mechanics before introducing high velocity or heavy loads.
2. **Technique cues**: Focus on establishing and holding correct **starting and receiving positions** for several seconds before moving. Use "abbreviated movements" (muscle snatch, press balance, jerk balance) to isolate specific segments at controlled speeds rather than rushing the full lift.
3. **Exercise recommendations**: Incorporate drills that allow slow execution: **slow pull to hold**, muscle snatch/clean, pressing snatch balance, muscle clean/press, push press, and jerk balance. Avoid relying solely on empty barbell or PVC work for speed/balance feedback as they lack the load required to reveal technical flaws.
4. **Recovery or mobility advice**: The text does not explicitly address recovery protocols or specific mobility exercises; it emphasizes "patience" and taking time to feel tension and balance during slow movements.
5. **Athlete-specific relevance**: This article is highly relevant for your transition phase: it warns against emulating elite training volumes/intensities prematurely (which risks reinforcing poor patterns due to chronic back pain) and supports a "backing up" approach to fix technique before adding the hypertrophy load needed for your 6-week block.
