# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2050/The-Effects-of-Knee-Position-in-the-Split-Jerk/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency:** Practice the correct split position in *every* jerk rep; do not skip this focus.
*   **Volume/Intensity:** Hold all jerks in the receiving (split) position for 2–5 seconds before recovering to strengthen stability and identify balance errors.
*   **Correction Protocol:** If a rep lands in a bad position, stop immediately, adjust feet to the optimal alignment, hold, then recover. Do not "bounce" out of poor positions.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Front Knee:** Position directly above the ankle or slightly *behind* it (not forward). This creates a stronger, more stable base to resist forward momentum and secure the bar.
*   **Back Knee:** Keep unlocked but not dramatically bent; aim for a slight bend. The knee should be positioned behind the hips when viewed from the side. Avoid locking the leg straight or bending excessively (90 degrees), which strains hip flexors and risks lower back hyperextension.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Drills:** Drop to split, Jump to split, Jerk from split, Push jerk in split, Jerk balance, Step to split, Walk to split, Jerk recovery, Tall jerk.
*   **Specific Focus:** Prioritize "Jerk from split" and "Push jerk in split" given the athlete's current push-jerk-only limitation.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Stability Training:** Spend time holding the correct split position to build structural integrity rather than relying on momentum.
*   **Injury Prevention:** Avoid excessive back knee bending and locked legs to prevent hip flexor strains and lower back hyperextension.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Push Jerk Only, Back Pain)**
*   **Push Jerk Transition:** The text explicitly supports the athlete's current limitation by emphasizing stability over explosive depth; the "slightly behind ankle" cue is crucial for a push jerk where forward drive must be controlled rather than absorbed via a deep split.
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** The instruction to keep the back knee slightly bent (not locked) and positioned behind the hips directly addresses the athlete's chronic back pain by reducing lumbar shear force and preventing hyperextension during the receiving phase.
*   **Technique Correction:** For an intermediate lifter, the emphasis on "holding" the position for 2–5 seconds is vital to break the habit of masking poor balance with speed, which is common when transitioning from powerlifting-style strength to Olympic technique.
