# Analysis: "Tipping in The Squat: Legs Not Hips" by Greg Everett

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Slow eccentrics**: 3–6 second lowering to emphasize leg strength through mid-to-lower squat range
- **Paused squats**: 2–3 second pause at parallel or bottom to rebuild postural strength
- **Volume distribution**: Keep 1 heavy squat session/week (top sets, low reps, suboptimal form); prioritize 2 sessions of quality reps with proper posture
- **Temporary load reduction**: Expect to drop squat weight initially to rebuild positional strength

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Root cause of forward lean**: Relative leg weakness, NOT hip weakness (counterintuitive)
- **Key cue**: Legs must open the knee joint from a small angle against full load resistance without shifting hips
- **Diagnostic timing**: If hips rise before back rounds = leg strength issue; if back rounds first = trunk stability issue
- **Secondary issues**: Knee valgus (inward collapse) → work adductors; poor bottom bounce control → minimize bouncing

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- Slow eccentric squats (3–6 sec)
- Pause squats (2–3 sec at parallel or bottom)
- Parallel/segment squats
- **Adductor-specific work** if knees cave inward
- Clean/snatch pull posture correction + pulls from riser + floating pulls

## 5. **Athlete-Specific Relevance**
🎯 **HIGHLY RELEVANT**: This athlete has **OHS 50 kg (primary snatch limiter)** and chronic back pain. Forward lean in squats directly limits overhead squat strength. Implementing slow eccentrics and paused squats with reduced loads could:
- Rebuild leg strength → improve OHS stability
- Reduce spinal stress (matches chronic back pain management)
- Prevent reinforcing poor posture under night shift fatigue

**Immediate action**: Add 1–2 paused squat sessions/week; temporarily reduce squat loads to focus on perfect positioning.
