# Analysis: Weightlifting Program Design: The Week Structure

## 1. Programming Principles

**Frequency & Scheduling:**
- 5 days/week is standard for intermediate lifters
- **Athlete's constraint:** Works night shift Wed–Sun → should prioritize Mon–Tue–Thu–Sat or similar early-week heavy days when freshest
- Alternate "big" and "little" days to manage fatigue

**Big vs. Little Days:**
- **Big days:** Heavy snatches, C&J, pulls, squats (systemically taxing)
- **Little days:** Speed work, technique, overhead lifts, power variants (lower intensity/volume)
- Can have 10% volume difference or fewer numerical differences if exercise selection qualifies

**Recommended 5-day structure:** Mon (Big) - Tue (Little) - Wed (Big) - Fri (Big) - Sat (Little)
- Or: Mon–Tue–Wed–Fri–Sat (packed early week, heavy Saturday total)

**Intensity & Volume Distribution:**
- Don't program heavy lower back work before heavy squat/clean days
- Accessory: Tough posterior chain work → end of big days; bodybuilding/stability work → little days

---

## 2. Relevant to This Athlete

**Critical consideration:** Night shift worker (Wed–Sun) = unavailable Wed–Sun mornings
- **Feasible schedule:** Mon–Tue–Thu–Sat (matches Everett's 4-day secondary option; leaves Wed off, then training Thu–Sat possible if evening sessions)
- Prioritize **Mon–Tue as fresh, heavy days** before fatigue from shift work

**Back pain + OHS limiter (50 kg):**
- Schedule aggressive OHS/mobility on little days (lower systemic fatigue)
- Avoid cumulative spinal loading (heavy pulls → heavy squats back-to-back without recovery day)

**6-week hypertrophy block→technique/strength transition:**
- Apply big/little day structure to manage volume in hypertrophy phase
- Shift to heavier intensity (lower reps) in strength phase while maintaining recovery days

---

## 3. Key Takeaway
Structure around **night shift availability** + **alternate big/little days**. Use early-week slots (Mon–Tue) for heaviest work; leverage Saturday as secondary heavy day post-rest.
