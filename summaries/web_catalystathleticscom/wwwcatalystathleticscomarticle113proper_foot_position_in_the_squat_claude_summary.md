# Merged Summary: Squat Foot Positioning for Olympic Weightlifting

## Key Points
- Foot position directly impacts spinal loading and knee tracking—critical for this athlete's chronic back pain and low OHS (50 kg).
- Olympic weightlifting squat stance differs from powerlifting: feet under hips/thighs, not wide.
- Proper mechanics must precede the 6-week hypertrophy block to prevent compensatory patterns under fatigue.
- Knee valgus during recovery indicates lateral hip weakness, not foot positioning error.

## Technique Cues
**Foot Position:**
- Place feet under thighs with toes turned out ~25° to match thigh angle.
- Individual variation is acceptable if it maintains knee alignment and reduces back pain.

**Knee Tracking:**
- Knees track over toes (externally rotated feet with knees out).
- Maintain knee-over-toe alignment throughout the concentric phase.

**What to Avoid:**
- Forcing toes-forward to mimic sport-specific jumping mechanics—strength training and sport-specific work are separate.
- Assuming knee valgus is a foot position problem when it reflects glute med/maximus weakness.

## Programming Principles
- **Movement quality before load:** Establish proper squat mechanics before initiating hypertrophy block.
- Address mobility limitations (ankle dorsiflexion, hip internal/external rotation, hip adductor flexibility) to prevent compensation.
- Prioritize lateral hip strength work to stabilize knees through the recovery phase.
- Given night shift fatigue, emphasize technique precision over volume to avoid overtraining compromised joints.

## Athlete Relevance
- **Chronic back pain:** Knee valgus → lateral hip weakness → pelvic instability → lumbar compensation. Proper foot positioning and lateral hip engagement are essential.
- **Low OHS (50 kg):** Limited hip ROM in squat restricts overhead mobility. Assess whether current stance allows full depth with neutral spine.
- **Back squat 118 kg foundation:** Adequate loading base; refine mechanics now before hypertrophy block to prevent injury under volume.
- **Pre-hypertrophy assessment:** Ankle/hip mobility work is prerequisite; excessive toe-out compensation may mask spinal position issues.
