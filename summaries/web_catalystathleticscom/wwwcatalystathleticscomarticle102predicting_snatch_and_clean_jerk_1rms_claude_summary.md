# Analysis: Predicting 1RMs by Greg Everett

## Relevance to Athlete
**Moderate relevance.** Article addresses max lift testing frequency and prediction—applicable to this intermediate lifter, but doesn't address their specific constraints (chronic back pain, night shift, low OHS as limiting factor).

---

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Max lift frequency:** Attempt heavy singles weekly or near-max snatches/C&Js every 8-12 weeks minimum
- **Why frequent testing:** Provides accurate ability assessment; newer lifters improve faster with frequent max attempts
- **Performance improvement expectations:** Properly peaked athletes see 1-5% improvement over previous PRs
- **Caution on submaximal testing:** Multiple reps accumulate back fatigue more than single attempts—relevant given this athlete's chronic back pain

---

## 2. **Competition Strategy** (relevant for future meets)
Two approaches:
- **Conservative (6 for 6):** Opens at ~90%, smaller jumps, all lifts <100%
- **Aggressive (PRs):** Opens at 93-95%, second attempt targets current PR, third attempt attempts new PR
- Athletes can exceed gym PRs by 3-5% in competition if properly peaked

---

## 3. **Specific to This Athlete**
⚠️ **Back pain concern:** Article warns lower back becomes "more and more vulnerable as fatigue accumulates from multiple reps"—suggests **avoiding high-rep back squat work** in hypertrophy block; prioritize technique/volume in lighter ranges or singles.

**OHS limiter:** No specific guidance. Article focuses on snatch/C&J prediction, not addressing weak overhead position as bottleneck.

**Night shift worker:** No accommodation discussed.

---

## 4. **Key Quote**
"A lifter whose technique is still developing needs to make that a focus, but being focused on technique and lifting heavy...are not mutually exclusive."
