# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2097/Ask-Greg-Driving-Forward-in-the-Split-Jerk/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency:** High frequency of specific jerk variations (push press + split jerk complexes; power jerk + split jerk complexes) to ingrain the vertical dip and drive pattern.
*   **Sets/Reps:** Not explicitly defined in text, but implies low-to-moderate reps for technical drills (complexes) to maintain high quality and avoid fatigue-induced forward driving.
*   **Intensity:** Focus on "learning" and "feeling" mechanics rather than maximal loading initially; intensity increases as the vertical drive becomes automatic.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Dip/Drive:** Must be identical to a power jerk: straight down, straight up, complete. No forward lean or dive of the chest during the split initiation.
*   **Balance:** Weight balanced back toward heels but maintaining full foot contact (do not rock so far back that pressure is lost on the balls of the feet).
*   **Knee/Tension:** Unlock knees from passive lockout; create quad tension to prevent "freefall" and forward shift. Dip must be controlled enough to keep bar connected to shoulders (no bounce).
*   **Breathing/Bracing:** Big breath expanding abdomen, locked in tightly; activate glutes to turn trunk into a rigid piston moving only vertically.
*   **Split Action:** Focus on lifting/reaching the front heel and driving hips forward under the bar. Do *not* think about pushing the back foot back (causes hip pull-back).
*   **Overhead Position:** Push bar back behind the head during the split to achieve ideal position; do not push head/chest forward prematurely until the bar is secured overhead.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Drills/Complexes:**
    *   Push press + Split jerk (replicate vertical dip/drive and "push bar back" cue).
    *   Power jerk + Split jerk (maintain balance/vertical drive in power before splitting).
    *   Push jerks in a split position.
    *   Presses in the split position.
    *   Jerk balances.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Flexibility:** The text notes the split jerk requires the "least flexibility" of the three main jerks compared to squat/power, making it accessible for those with limited range of motion.
*   **Stability:** Emphasis on rigid trunk bracing (glutes/abs) rather than mobility work in this specific excerpt.

**5. Relevance to athlete's profile**
*   **Jerk Style Transition:** The text strongly advises against abandoning the split jerk for a power/push jerk solely due to current inability, noting that "driving forward" is a common novice error fixable with time and technique, not a reason to switch styles permanently.
*   **Back Pain Consideration:** While the text advocates for the split jerk (which allows deep receiving positions), it emphasizes a rigid trunk ("piston") and vertical movement. This aligns with managing back pain by avoiding excessive spinal flexion or shear forces during the drive, provided the athlete maintains the "rigid piston" bracing described.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The text does not address circadian rhythms or sleep; however, the recommendation to "give yourself time" and avoid rushing mastery is crucial for a night-shift worker where recovery windows may be irregular.
*   **OHS Limiter:** The focus on "pushing bar back behind head" helps stabilize the overhead position, potentially aiding an athlete with a 50kg OHS who struggles with stability under load.
