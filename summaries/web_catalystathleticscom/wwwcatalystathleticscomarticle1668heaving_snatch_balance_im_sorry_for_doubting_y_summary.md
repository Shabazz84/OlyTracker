# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1668/Heaving-Snatch-Balance-Im-Sorry-for-Doubting-You/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** 2–5 reps per set.
*   **Intensity:** Do not aim for maximal loads; prioritize smooth movement over heavy weight.
*   **Frequency/Timing:** Use as an auxiliary exercise near the end of a workout, or with lighter weights at the beginning to loosen up and reinforce positioning before main snatch work.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Footwork:** Keep feet flat on the floor throughout; do not lift them during the transition (prevents "mule kicking" or wide landing).
*   **Action:** Dip smoothly at the knees, heave the bar slightly upward, and punch down under it into an overhead squat.
*   **Posture:** Focus on quick elbows, perfect balance, and posture; resist the bar rather than letting it fall on you.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Primary:** Heaving Snatch Balance (HSB).
*   **Supplementary for shoulder stability:** Snatch push press, Press in snatch, Overhead squats (including clean/jerk grip variations).

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Focus Areas:** Hip and shoulder flexibility; ankle and hip mobility to prevent the "elbow/shoulder giving way" under load.
*   **Benefit:** Provides active stretching and overhead strength without the wrist strain associated with high-rep overhead squats.

**5. Relevance to athlete profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The HSB is a strong candidate because it avoids the "moment of slack" found in standard snatch balances, forcing a connected position that can reduce spinal shear compared to dropping under heavy loads. It also builds stability without the high-wrist strain of overhead squats.
*   **OHS Limiter (50 kg):** The text explicitly states HSB allows for active stretching and overhead strength development specifically to address weaknesses in supporting weight at the bottom of the overhead squat position, directly targeting your primary limiter.
*   **Technique Transition:** Ideal for an intermediate athlete transitioning to Olympic lifting as it reinforces proper foot placement and prevents the common error of "picking up feet" too early during the snatch turnover.
