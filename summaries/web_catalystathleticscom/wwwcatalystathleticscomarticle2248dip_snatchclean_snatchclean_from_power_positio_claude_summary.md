# Analysis: Dip Snatch/Clean & Snatch/Clean from Power Position

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Exercise selection logic**: Use these lifts when weak/poorly timed leg drive exists (common problem)
- **Not universal**: Only prescribe if addressing specific weaknesses; don't use if athlete already excels at leg drive timing and pull-under mechanics
- **Progression**: Start with **from power position** (static hold) → advance to **dip variation** (countermovement)

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Power Position**: trunk vertical, knees bent (identical to jerk dip position)
- **Purpose**: Improve vertical leg drive at end of pull; prevent early cutoff or excessive backward lean
- **Pull-under cue**: Keep tension on bar, punch aggressively overhead, lock shoulder blades
- **Key benefit**: Teaches aggression/speed in pull-under; builds confidence with heavy weights

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- **Dip Snatch/Clean from Power Position**: High-position starts to force more force generation in less time (improves rate of force development/explosiveness)
- **When to use**: Timing of direction change and pull-under mechanics need work more than absolute leg drive power

## 4. **Relevance to This Athlete**
⚠️ **Highly relevant**: OHS 50 kg (major snatch limiter) suggests weak leg drive/vertical positioning. Dip snatches from power position could address this by reinforcing proper scoop position and vertical trunk during leg drive phase. Static power position variation recommended first (easier learning).

**Caution**: Chronic back pain—monitor trunk vertical cueing; avoid excessive backward lean compensation.
