# Analysis: "Does it Matter When You Jump in the Snatch or Clean?"

## Relevant to This Athlete

**Technique Cues (Snatch/Clean):**
- Stay over the bar with shoulders until intentional jump initiation—avoid early shoulder retraction
- Maintain full foot contact during pull; weight shift to toes premature causes forward bar displacement and power loss
- Complete full hip and torso extension; cutting hip extension cuts power
- Landing should be same plane or <6 inches behind starting position
- Forward jump increasing with heavier weight = technical problem to address

**Exercise Recommendations (for early jumping):**
- **Muscle snatches** → diagnose pull mechanics without jump assistance
- **Hip/hang snatches, snatches from blocks** → feel weight distribution on full foot before jump
- **Flat-footed pulls** → extend torso/hips/knees without ankle plantarflexion; rewire early-jump timing
- **Complexes** → pull to jump position + full lift (reveals early jumping)

## Athlete-Specific Application

**Critical for this athlete:** OHS (50 kg) is primary snatch limiter. Early jumping likely contributes—these drills address pull mechanics before receiving position becomes the constraint. Muscle snatches particularly valuable given snatch weakness.

**Back pain consideration:** Flat-footed pulls may reduce spinal loading vs. standard pulls if implemented with vertical torso alignment.
