## Analysis: Shoulder Dislocation Rehabilitation for Weightlifting

**Relevance to Athlete Profile:**
This article documents a catastrophic shoulder dislocation during competition and subsequent rehabilitation. **Highly relevant** to your athlete due to:
- Chronic back pain (compounded by injury mechanics)
- Night shift worker context (sleep affects recovery)
- OHS weakness (overhead stability concern)
- Push jerk only (shoulder stability limitations already present)

---

### **1. Programming Principles**

**Injury-Phase Training (Weeks 1-2 post-injury):**
- Complete rest days initially (3 days), then gradual reintroduction
- Lower body emphasis (squats, lunges, jumps)
- Core/stability work: decline sit-ups, back extensions, Russian twists, GHRs
- Accessory grip work (grippers)
- Safety squat bar preferred over barbell for shoulder safety
- Contrast therapy: 10 min heat + 10 min ice cycles (progressing to heat/ice)

**For your athlete:** Safety squat bar work fits chronic back pain profile; avoid overhead until cleared.

---

### **2. Technique Cues**
None directly provided; article is injury narrative, not technique instruction.

---

### **3. Exercise Recommendations**

**Lower body (shoulder-safe):**
- SSB parallel squats, pause squats, good mornings
- Bulgarian split squats, lunges
- Squat jumps (arms held against chest, no swing)
- GHRs

**Core/stability:**
- Decline sit-ups, crunches, back extensions, Russian twists
- Neck extensions

**Avoid:** Barbell overhead work, unilateral arm stability movements during early rehab.

---

### **4. Recovery & Mobility**

- **Ice protocol:** 10 min bouts, 2–3x daily early phase; progressed to contrast (heat/ice alternation)
- **Sleep positioning:** Critical issue; pain worst mornings (poor positioning during sleep)
- **Immobilization:** Everett opted for kinesio tape + self-bracing over slings for functionality
- **Timeframe:** 4–5 days to minimal daily pain; ROM progressed slowly (5° → 30° passive abduction over first week)

**Relevant to night shift worker:** Sleep quality severely impacted recovery; schedule fatigue + poor sleep positioning = compounded healing.

---

### **5. Athlete-Specific Red Flags**

⚠️ **Your athlete's vulnerabilities:**
- **OHS @ 50kg** = shoulder stability already compromised; dislocation risk elevated
- **Push jerk only** = never developed split jerk stability; shoulder absorbs force
- **Chronic back pain** = core/posterior chain already weakened; injury cascade risk
- **Night shift (Wed–Sun)** = inconsistent sleep, delayed recovery, postural fatigue

**Recommendation:** Before 6-week hypertrophy block, assess shoulder stability with band work, prone Y-T-W raises, and strict form on low-load OHS progressions. Consider delaying split jerk introduction until shoulder integrity confirmed.
