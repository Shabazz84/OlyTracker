1. **Programming Principles**: Use these as high-intensity technical drills (not max effort) to improve rate of force development (RFD). Prioritize the "from power position" variation first for learning proper trunk/knee alignment and eliminating elastic contribution, then progress to the "dip" variation once timing is solidified. Frequency: 1–2 times per week during the hypertrophy block as a primary accessory or technique focus.

2. **Technique Cues**:
   - **Position**: Maintain a vertical trunk with knees forward (identical to jerk dip) before initiating the pull.
   - **Aggression**: Generate maximum force in minimal time/distance; eliminate hesitation at the top of the scoop.
   - **Under the Bar**: Keep tension on the bar while pulling under; punch shoulders aggressively overhead and lock scapulae immediately upon turnover (do not "drop" under).

3. **Exercise Recommendations**: Dip Snatch/Clean and Snatch/Clean from Power Position. Use these to address weak or poorly timed leg drive, specifically targeting the transition from the scoop to full extension.

5. **Athlete Profile Relevance**:
   - **Chronic Back Pain**: The "power position" variation (holding static vertical trunk) is highly relevant as it reinforces proper spinal alignment and reduces the risk of excessive backward lean, which often exacerbates back issues.
   - **Intermediate/Transitioning**: These drills are ideal for an athlete with a strong squat but limited clean/jerk efficiency to isolate and fix "leg drive" timing without the complexity of full lifts.
   - **Push Jerk Only**: The text explicitly links the "power position" to the mechanics of a jerk dip, reinforcing the specific movement pattern this athlete already utilizes.
