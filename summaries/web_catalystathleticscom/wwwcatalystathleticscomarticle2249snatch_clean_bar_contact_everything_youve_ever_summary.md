# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2249/Snatch-Clean-Bar-Contact-Everything-Youve-Ever-Wanted-to-Kn/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Sets/Reps:** High frequency of low-repetition pull variations (snatch/clean from power position, no-jump pulls, high-pulls) to ingrain bar path mechanics without excessive fatigue.
*   **Intensity:** Focus on speed and proximity rather than heavy loading during the pull phase; use light-to-moderate weights for technical drills.
*   **Frequency:** Daily or near-daily practice of "bar contact" awareness through deadlift variations and partial pulls.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **"Wait until thighs":** Do not initiate the second pull (hip extension) until the bar reaches the mid-thighs to avoid premature contact on the knees/hips.
*   **Pendulum Control:** Keep shoulders slightly behind the bar during the first pull; use back and shoulder tension to prevent the bar from swinging forward, but do not push it backward artificially.
*   **Brush vs. Bang:** Aim for a "brush" (minimal disruption) rather than a collision; contact should occur only as hips fully extend and knees reach their furthest forward position.
*   **Vertical Drive:** Maintain vertical leg drive through hip extension while keeping the bar close to the body.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   Snatch/Clean from Power Position.
*   Snatch/Clean with No Jump (emphasizing full extension without leaving ground).
*   Pull to Hold + Snatch/Clean.
*   High-Pull + Snatch/Clean.
*   Deadlift variations focusing on maintaining bar proximity and tension.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   *None explicitly mentioned in this text.* (The text focuses entirely on bar path mechanics).

**5. Relevance to athlete profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The emphasis on "waiting until the thighs" and avoiding "artificially forcing contact early" is critical for reducing shear force on the lumbar spine during the transition phase, which often exacerbates back pain in lifters who rush the second pull.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The distinction between a "brush" and a "bang" addresses common errors where new Olympic lifters (often coming from powerlifting) push the bar aggressively into the hips, disrupting balance and speed.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** While not mentioned in the text, the recommendation for high-frequency, low-fatigue technical drills (pulls/powers) is ideal for managing energy levels around irregular sleep schedules compared to heavy, taxing max-effort sessions.
