## Key Points
*   **Stance Biomechanics:** Adopt a "toes-out" stance (approx. 25°) to align feet with thigh direction, facilitating femoral rotation within the hip socket and allowing full depth while maintaining lumbar extension.
*   **Root Cause Analysis:** Differentiate between *structural* necessity for external rotation (valid for depth/spine safety) and *compensatory* rotation caused by ankle dorsiflexion limitations or tight adductors/internal rotators. The latter requires mobility work, not just stance adjustment.
*   **Unilateral vs. Bilateral Distinction:** While bilateral squats benefit from a wide, toes-out stance for force absorption, unilateral movements (like the split jerk) rely on lateral hip stability and control rather than fixed foot angles; do not blindly replicate squat stance in single-leg drills.
*   **Knee Tracking:** Knees must hinge in the intended plane of motion. Avoid "twisting" by ensuring feet are positioned slightly wider than shoulder-width, ideally just outside the direct line of the legs to allow hips to sit between thighs.

## Technique Cues
*   **Foot Alignment:** Rotate feet outward to match thigh direction (approx. 25°); do not use excessive rotation as a crutch for poor mobility.
*   **Knee Path:** Ensure knees track in the intended plane; avoid valgus collapse by strengthening lateral hip musculature rather than altering stance width if instability occurs during the ascent.
*   **Hip Depth:** Utilize slight external rotation to unlock femoral range of motion, enabling full depth without lumbar rounding.
*   **Stance Width:** Position feet slightly wider than shoulder-width ("very slightly outside directly under the legs") to optimize hip placement between thighs for force absorption.

## Programming Principles
*   *Note: The source text is a biomechanical discussion and contains no specific programming data (sets, reps, intensity, or periodization).*
*   **Mobility Integration:** Prioritize ankle dorsiflexion and adductor/internal rotator mobility drills if the athlete requires excessive toe-out to achieve depth.
*   **Targeted Strength:** If valgus knee movement occurs during the recovery phase of squats, program lateral hip strengthening exercises rather than modifying foot stance.
*   **Motor Pattern Separation:** Do not rely on squatting mechanics to teach jumping/landing or split-jerk stability; practice sport-specific motor patterns separately from heavy bilateral loading.
