# Analysis: "Strength Lifts and Classic Lifts for Weightlifting" by Greg Everett

## 1. Programming Principles
- **Specificity over generality**: Strength development must be sport-specific (snatch/C&J positions), not generic (e.g., deadlift alone won't drive competition lifts)
- **Squat frequency**: Top lifters train squats 4–6 days/week, sometimes twice daily, with substantial volume
- **Classic lifts as strength builders**: Snatches and C&Js themselves build weightlifting-specific strength when performed at sufficient intensity (60%+ of max deadlift for snatches, not just 30%)
- **Accessory hierarchy**: Not all accessories are equal; press is least helpful for jerk improvement despite common assumption

## 2. Technique Cues
- **Jerk**: Emphasis on *putting* weight overhead (dynamic drive), not just supporting it—pressing strength is secondary
- **Snatch/Clean**: Avoid "triple extension" cue (artificial plantar flexion/shrug); allow extension to occur naturally; focus on pulling body under bar
- **Cueing flexibility**: Hip pop vs. quad pop—coaches should adapt cues per athlete to prevent forward chase

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
- Halting deadlifts (to hip crease)—valuable for technique reinforcement
- Pulling variations (pulls are standard across most US programs)
- **NOT recommended as primary drivers**: Generic press work for jerk, excessive deadlift focus

## 5. Relevance to This Athlete
- **OHS limiter (50 kg)**: Emphasis on jerk-specific work over press; suggests jerk technique/positioning drills over heavy pressing
- **Chronic back pain**: Frequent squatting (4–6×/week) requires careful programming; consider lighter, higher-frequency approach vs. heavy, low-frequency
- **Push jerk only**: Text argues jerk is a *drive* skill, not press strength—split jerk may solve positioning/stability issues better than pressing gains
- **6-week hypertrophy block**: Text supports dedicated strength/squat phases but doesn't detail hypertrophy-specific programming

---

**Key takeaway**: Specificity over generality. Build strength in snatch/C&J positions and squat; don't chase generic strength markers.
