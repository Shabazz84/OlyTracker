# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2109/The-High-Pull-Pros-Cons/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Frequency/Usage:** Do not use high-pulls as a primary staple for novices or intermediates lacking solid fundamentals; they are a "trap" exercise if used prematurely.
*   **Prerequisites:** Only introduce the high-pull after mastering four specific priorities: simultaneous hip/knee extension, correct muscular effort (legs/back driving), keeping the bar close to the body, and mastering a fast drop.
*   **Intensity/Volume:** Not specified in text; implies low frequency or exclusion for those not meeting prerequisites due to risk of reinforcing bad patterns (early arm bend, delayed groundless phase).

**2. Technique cues**
*   **The "Active Drop" Warning:** Avoid focusing on using arms to push the body down ("active drop") until the leg-drop mechanism is automatic; otherwise, you cannot contract arms and relax legs simultaneously.
*   **Arm Path:** Do not bend arms at the start of the second pull; keep them straight (like a rope) to transmit speed from hips to bar without absorbing energy.
*   **Ground Connection:** Ensure feet remain on the ground during the high-pull to maintain power transmission; do not allow the "groundless phase" (jump) to occur prematurely as it does in the actual lift.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Primary Recommendation:** The text advises against using the Snatch/Clean High-Pull for this athlete profile until they are confident in their angles, muscle effort, bar path, and drop speed.
*   **Alternative Focus:** Prioritize drills that teach dropping the body "like a rock" (fast leg bend) rather than pulling with arms.
