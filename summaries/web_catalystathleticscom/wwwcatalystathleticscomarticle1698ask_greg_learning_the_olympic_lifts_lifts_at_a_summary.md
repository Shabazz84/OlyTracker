# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1698/Ask-Greg-Learning-the-Olympic-Lifts-Lifts-at-Age-40/

**1. Programming principles**
*   **Progression:** Start with power variations (power snatch/clean) to learn mechanics at a shallower receiving height; transition to full squat variations once flexibility and confidence improve.
*   **Frequency:** Perform flexibility work daily or twice daily; practice power lifts immediately, introducing squat variations later as comfort grows.
*   **Intensity/Volume:** Not explicitly defined in text, but implies starting with lighter weights for the power versions while focusing on technique acquisition.

**2. Technique cues**
*   **Core Concept:** Power and full lifts are identical movements; the only difference is bar height at reception.
*   **Learning Method:** Learn the power variations using the exact same technical framework as the classic lifts.
*   **Overcoming Barriers:** Address lack of confidence in lower receiving positions through regular squatting and smart training progression.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Primary Skills:** Power snatch, power clean, full snatch, full clean (introduced later).
*   **Assistance/Prep:** Overhead squats, behind-the-neck presses, push presses, front squats.
*   **Mobility Prep:** Foam rolling (daily), stretching hip extensors/adductors, ankles, thoracic spine, and shoulder girdle.

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Flexibility Strategy:** The primary limiting factor for older athletes; requires persistence, consistency, and frequency ("do it frequently, regularly").
*   **Tissue Quality:** Daily (or twice daily) foam rolling to release fascial adhesions; "the more it hurts, the more you need it."
*   **Timing:** Stretch ideally when warm and immediately after foam rolling.
*   **Injury Management:** Consult a manual therapist for past injuries or specific range-of-motion limitations.

**5. Relevance to athlete's profile (Intermediate, 102.5kg, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Age/Transition Context:** The text strongly validates the user's transition from powerlifting/strength training to Olympic lifts at an intermediate level, emphasizing that age is not a barrier if flexibility and technique are prioritized.
*   **Back Pain/Mobility:** The author explicitly links back pain/range of motion limitations to the need for manual therapy and specific mobility work (thoracic spine/shoulders). The recommendation to "see a good manual therapist" is critical given the athlete's chronic back pain.
*   **Night Shift/Sleep:** While not addressed directly in this text, the author emphasizes that "adequate quality sleep" is the most significant factor for recovery and progress, which is vital for a night shift worker.
*   **Push Jerk vs. Split:** The text supports starting with power variations (similar to push jerk mechanics) before attempting full squat catches, aligning with the athlete's current limitation of only using a push jerk.
