# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2134/Ask-Greg-Improving-Overhead-Strength-Stability/

1. **Programming Principles**: Implement two dedicated weekly sessions for significant overhead strength (push presses/snatch push presses). Incorporate static holds of 3–5 seconds in the bottom and/or locked-out positions during all overhead lifts to build stability without altering volume significantly.

2. **Technique Cues**:
   - **Shoulders**: Forcefully squeeze upper inside edges together (retracted/upwardly rotated) to create a solid base.
   - **Elbows**: Point halfway between straight down and back (snatch) or slightly out (jerk); maintain neutral humeral rotation.
   - **Hands**: Bar in palm slightly behind center, heel of palm up; grip only tight enough for control.
   - **Trunk**: Slight forward inclination to place bar over the back of the neck while maintaining balance.

3. **Exercise Recommendations**: Jerk supports, jerk recoveries, push presses, and snatch push presses. Strict pressing is secondary but has value in preparation phases.

4. **Recovery/Mobility Advice**: The text prioritizes "diagnosing the source" (mobility vs. strength) before prescribing solutions. It suggests static holds to improve stability without requiring extra training time or specific mobility drills, though it implies mobility checks are necessary first.

5. **Profile Relevance**:
   - **Overhead Limiter**: Directly addresses the athlete's 50kg OHS limitation by focusing on "overhead support strength" and "position/stability."
   - **Chronic Back Pain**: The cue to incline the trunk slightly forward to place the bar over the back of the neck is critical for spinal alignment, though the text does not explicitly address pain management.
   - **Intermediate/Transitioning**: Recommends a gradual progression in pressing strength similar to squat development and emphasizes regular exposure (frequency) as key.
