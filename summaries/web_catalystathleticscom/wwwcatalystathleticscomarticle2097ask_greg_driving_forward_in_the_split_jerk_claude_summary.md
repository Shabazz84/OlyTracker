# Analysis: Split Jerk Technique Article by Greg Everett

## Technique Cues for Jerk (Highly Relevant)

**Core Problem & Solution:**
- Forward shift during split jerk is common; caused by initiating split too early + chest diving forward
- Dip and drive must be **identical to power/push jerk**: straight down, straight up, complete
- Balance toward heels but maintain full foot contact (don't rock back excessively)

**Key Cues:**
1. **Unlock knees actively** → create quad tension before dip initiation (prevents slack/freefall that shifts you forward)
2. **Controlled dip acceleration** → bar stays connected to shoulders (abrupt dip = bar bounce = forward shift)
3. **Big breath + core lock** → rigid trunk acting as vertical piston; recheck balance after breathing
4. **Split mechanics**: Lift/reach front heel, drive hips forward under bar; ignore back foot (thinking of it pulls hips back)
5. **Push bar back behind head** (not head/chest forward) → achieves overhead position while staying under bar

**Relevant to This Athlete:**
- Currently **push jerk only** → split jerk cues are essential for progression
- Chronic back pain + night shift fatigue = need controlled, efficient technique (forward shifting increases spinal load)

## Exercise Recommendations

- Push jerk + split jerk complex (replicate vertical dip/drive from push jerk)
- Power jerk + split jerk complex (if maintaining balance)
- Split-position presses, jerk balances, split jerks behind neck

## Key Message

Split jerk is superior for most lifters (better margin for error, least flexibility demand). Give it time—don't abandon for power jerk prematurely.
