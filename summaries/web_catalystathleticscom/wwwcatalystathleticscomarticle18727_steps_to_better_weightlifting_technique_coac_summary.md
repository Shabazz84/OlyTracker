1.  **Programming Principles:**
    *   **Dosage Strategy:** Do not practice technique endlessly (Takano warns against this). Remediation work must be prescribed in "optimal dosages" based on neural fatigue. For an intermediate transitioning athlete, limit technical drills to high-quality sets (e.g., 3–5 reps) early in the session when the CNS is fresh.
    *   **Periodization:** Integrate remediation work immediately after the main movement or as a standalone block only if the error is complex; otherwise, attach it at the end of training.

2.  **Technique Cues (Derived from Takano's Logic):**
    *   **Diagnosis First:** Do not cue "stick your head through" for jerk issues unless you have identified the root cause (e.g., locked knees causing forward hip flexion).
    *   **Feel-Based Communication:** You must explain how a correction *feels*, not just what to do. If an athlete dumps the bar forward, the cue must address the sensation of leading with the chest, not just "stand up straight."

3.  **Exercise Recommendations (Based on Takano's Examples):**

    *   **Remedial Selection:** Select exercises that aid in "conceptualizing" the movement pattern rather than just strengthening it.

4.  **Recovery/Mobility Advice:**
    *   The text does not provide specific mobility drills, but emphasizes that if an athlete cannot perform a portion of a lift correctly due to lack of conceptualization, the remedy is a specific drill, not general stretching.

5.  **Relevance to Your Profile (102.5 kg, Chronic Back Pain, Night Shift):**

    *   **Night Shift Work:** Adhere strictly to the principle of performing remediation work when the nervous system is fresh (early in the session) to avoid compounding fatigue on a disrupted sleep schedule.
    *   **OHS Limiter:** The specific recommendation for "pausing at the bottom" in OHS is highly relevant to your 50 kg ceiling, as it forces chest engagement and prevents forward dumping.
