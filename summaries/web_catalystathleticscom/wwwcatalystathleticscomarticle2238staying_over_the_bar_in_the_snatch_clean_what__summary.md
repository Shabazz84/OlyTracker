# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2238/Staying-Over-the-Bar-in-the-Snatch-Clean-What-Why-How/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Focus:** Skill acquisition tied to strength; "staying over the bar" (shoulders above/in front of bar) until mid-thigh.
*   **Intensity/Volume:** Not explicitly defined in text, but emphasizes using pauses and slow eccentrics to build posture-specific strength for heavy weights.
*   **Periodization:** Skill is inextricably tied to strength; exercises must be performed with correct positioning to strengthen that specific balance.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **First Pull:** Shoulders directly above bar at start (arms vertical). Keep shoulders over or slightly in front of the bar until mid-thigh.
*   **Transition:** Do not open hips early; maintain back angle from start to second pull entry. Push against ground with legs while actively pushing bar back toward shins.
*   **Second Pull Entry:** Initiate only when bar approaches mid-thigh.
*   **Finish (Top of Pull):** Shoulders *must* move behind the bar at full extension for maximal force, but this is distinct from the "staying over" phase.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Halting Deadlifts:** Pause at mid-thigh to enforce position.
*   **Segment Deadlifts/Pulls:** Pauses at knee and mid-thigh.
*   **Paused Snatches/Cleans:** Segment lifts with a pause at mid-thigh.
*   **Complexes:** Combine paused pulls/snatches/cleans.
*   **Slow Eccentrics:** On any pull or deadlift variation to control the descent and reinforce posture.

**5. Athlete-Specific Relevance**
*   **Back Pain & "Staying Over":** The cue to keep shoulders over the bar and maintain a neutral back angle during the first pull is critical for your chronic back pain, as it prevents early hip opening which can shear the spine.
*   **Leg Dominance:** As an intermediate lifter with a 102.5 kg bodyweight, you likely have "shorter/stronger legs" relative to your height compared to elite lifters; this text notes such athletes move behind the bar sooner. You must consciously delay this transition until mid-thigh to maximize hip extension force rather than relying on premature back leverage.
*   **Jerk Limitation:** While the text focuses on pulls, the principle of "pushing the bar back toward the legs" applies to your Jerk setup; keeping shoulders over the bar ensures vertical leg drive, which is essential for a stable Push Jerk (your current style) before transitioning to Split.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** The text emphasizes that skill requires strength to execute under load; given your night shift schedule, prioritize these paused exercises when you are most alert to ensure the "posture-specific strength" is built correctly without fatigue compromising form.
