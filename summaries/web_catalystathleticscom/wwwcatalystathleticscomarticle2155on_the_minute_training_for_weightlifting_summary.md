# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2155/On-The-Minute-Training-for-Weightlifting/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Structure:** "On the Minute" (OTM) format: Start a set at the beginning of every minute; rest is whatever time remains before the next minute starts.
*   **Volume/Intensity Variations:**
    *   *20/20 Style:* 5 singles x 3 increasing weights (15 total sets). Progress by increasing all weights once 20 lifts are hit cleanly. Target ~90% on the final set initially.
    *   *Progressive Load:* Increase OTM weight every session regardless of previous heavy single performance; focus shifts to hitting high intensity consistently rather than using it as a warm-up.
    *   *Volume Reduction:* Reduce sets per weight (e.g., 5-4-3 pattern) approaching competition while maintaining structure.
    *   *Single Weight:* 10–15 sets at one moderate weight, or start at ~70% with small increments (2% or 2–3kg) per set for 8–12 reps.
*   **Frequency:** Can be used as a primary workout structure or as a warm-up (5–6 sets @ 70%) before heavy singles.
*   **Conditioning:** Designed to simulate meet conditions where quick, repeated heavy lifts are required with minimal rest.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Primary Goal:** Technical consistency and circumventing mental blocks by removing time for over-thinking or self-doubt between sets.
*   **Clean & Jerk Specifics:** For lifters with disparities between clean and jerk, separate the two lifts during OTM training (clean one minute, jerk the next). Closer to competition, treat Clean & Jerk as a single unit but allow 1-minute rest (rather than strict on-the-minute) to prevent fatigue-induced technical breakdown.
*   **Doubles/Triples:** Only recommended at the start of a cycle for volume/conditioning; not suitable for heavy intensity work.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Snatch:** Ideal for standard OTM (singles).
*   **Clean & Jerk:** Can be done as a combined unit or separated based on lift disparity.
*   **Heavy Singles:** Perform *after* an OTM warm-up or use "drop sets" immediately following a heavy single to build consistency without pre-fatigue.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Rest Management:** For Clean & Jerks, explicitly allow ~1 minute rest (vs. strict on-the-minute) to ensure technical quality and prevent "dying" from fatigue.
*   **Warm-up Protocol:** Use 5–6 OTM sets at ~70% intensity as a warm-up before heavy singles to induce consistency without excessive fatigue.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shifts)**
*   **Caution on Intensity:** The text warns that OTM is "not the best approach" for lifters who are not already technically consistent and confident; this athlete should master technique before attempting high-intensity OTM blocks.
*   **Back Pain Consideration:** While the text does not explicitly address back pain, the recommendation to separate Clean & Jerk components or allow extra rest (1 min) is crucial for an athlete with chronic back issues to manage spinal loading fatigue during rapid-fire sets.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** The high efficiency of OTM allows a large volume of quality work in a short window, which may benefit a night-shift worker with limited training time availability.
*   **Mental Health:** The "head out of your ass" aspect is highly relevant for an athlete transitioning to Olympic lifting who may struggle with the complexity and mental blocks associated with new movement patterns.
