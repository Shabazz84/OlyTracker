# Analysis: Heel Position in Push Press/Jerk

## Technique Cues (Primary Relevance)
- **Heels naturally rise during drive phase** — natural ankle extension (plantarflex) occurs with forceful leg extension; forcing heels flat indicates insufficient leg drive
- **Dip and drive must be vertical, knee-extension dominant** — NOT hip-centric; flexion/extension at knees only
- **Heel timing**: Stay down during dip, rise naturally as drive is completed
- **Cue: Treat push press/jerk as a leg exercise**, not upper body — maximizes force generation into the bar

## Relevance to This Athlete
**DIRECTLY APPLICABLE**: Athlete uses **push jerk only (no split jerk)**. This article emphasizes proper dip-drive mechanics are foundational for both movements. Since split jerk isn't in the athlete's arsenal yet, mastering aggressive vertical drive with proper heel mechanics is essential preparation.

**Back pain consideration**: Emphasis on vertical knee drive (vs. hip-back dipping or forward lean) may reduce spinal stress compared to poor mechanics.

- Example shown: 7×3 at fixed weight (general accessory work structure)
