# Analysis: Slow Snatch Turnover

## **Technique Cues (Snatch)**
- **Problem diagnosis**: Slow turnover often *misdiagnosed*—may actually be:
  - Late turnover initiation
  - Insufficient bar acceleration/elevation in 2nd pull
  - Poor transition timing into 3rd pull
  - Incomplete hip extension (partial snap vs. full drive/opening)

- **Cues/fixes**:
  - Tall snatches (gold standard for turnover speed)
  - **Snatch from power position** (forces full vertical trunk extension; easier to achieve complete hip opening than hang variations)
  - **3-position snatch** (floor → knee → mid-thigh): escalating fatigue + reduced time = forced aggression
  - Start **higher hang positions** (mid-thigh+) to enforce aggressive extension

## **Relevant to This Athlete**
- **OHS 50 kg limiter**: Slow turnover + weak overhead position directly correlate. Prioritize 3-position snatches and power position work pre-hypertrophy block.
- **Back pain**: Ensure tall snatches/power position drills use *light* weights initially to prevent compensation patterns.
- **Programming note**: These drills fit *technique block* phase well (post-hypertrophy, week 7+).

## **Key Principle**
Don't chase speed in isolation—address *acceleration/elevation* in pull first, then turnover follows.
