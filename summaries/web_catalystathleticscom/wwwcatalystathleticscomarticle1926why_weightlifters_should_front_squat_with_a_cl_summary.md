**2. Technique Cues**
*   **"Closed Fist" Rule:** Keep as much of the hand on the bar as possible; avoid "hooking two fingers."
*   **No Fingers Allowed to Pop Off:** A strict rule of thumb: if a finger pops off, the grip is too loose.
*   **Wide Shoulder Spread:** The closed grip forces elbows up and shoulders wide, preventing the "turtleback" (rounded upper back).
*   **Stabilizer Activation:** Focus on contracting muscles from waist up to hold the bar without arm movement; this builds static stabilizer strength for the rack position.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary:** Front Squat (mandatory grip modification: closed fist/whole hand).
*   **Secondary:** Back Squat (to maintain leg strength since front squat loads may drop).
*   **Mobility Work:** Lat and wrist stretching (essential to achieve the closed grip without pain).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Wrist Safety Warning:** A commenter notes that forcing a full grip with poor mobility can cause sprains; if mobility is lacking, use 3 fingers temporarily while actively stretching lats/wrists before progressing to a full fist.
*   **Bail Strategy:** The text mentions a user asking about bailing but offers no specific advice; standard front squat bail (drop forward) applies.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain & "Turtleback":** This is the most critical intervention for this athlete. The article explicitly links poor grip ("two fingers") to a rounded upper back, which exacerbates back pain and ruins jerk mechanics. Switching to a closed fist is a direct remedy for the athlete's chronic back issues caused by poor rack posture.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** Since the athlete only uses a push jerk, they rely heavily on an upright torso and wide shoulder shelf. The "turtleback" position described in the text directly prevents effective jerking; correcting this via front squat grip is essential for their specific goal.
*   **OHS Limiter (50 kg):** The article notes that upper back strength developed during front squats transfers to holding heavy cleans. Strengthening the stabilizers here may indirectly help stabilize the OHS position, though the primary benefit is the rack posture for the Clean & Jerk.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** While not mentioned in text, the recommendation to lower weight temporarily (to fix form) aligns well with a fatigue-managed approach for a night shift worker; prioritize technique over heavy loading during low-recovery weeks.
