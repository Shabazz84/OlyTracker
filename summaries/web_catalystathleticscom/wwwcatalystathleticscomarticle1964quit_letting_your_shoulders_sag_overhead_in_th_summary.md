# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1964/Quit-Letting-Your-Shoulders-Sag-Overhead-in-the-Snatch/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Frequency/Reps:** Practice daily overhead movements with a specific focus on holding the bar overhead for 1–3 seconds before lowering.
*   **Intensity:** Apply "aggressiveness" to lockouts even during empty bar or maximal lifts; treat every rep as an opportunity to build habit.
*   **Periodization:** Focus on motor learning and stability first ("be patient," habits don't change instantly) before increasing load intensity for PRs.

**2. Technique Cues (Snatch Overhead)**
*   **Scapular Action:** "Squeeze the upper inside edges of the shoulder blades together" to achieve forceful retraction and upward rotation. This ties arms tightly to the trunk.
*   **Timing:** Prepare to receive the bar *before* it settles; do not wait until feeling the weight. The final third pull is a vertical punch up against the bar while squeezing scapulae tight.
*   **Connection:** Keep grip on the bar throughout the turnover ("smell the bar") and maintain connection until hands flip over to prevent dropping out from under the bar.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Overhead Stability/Strength:** Snatch press, snatch push press, press in snatch, heaving snatch balance, snatch balance, overhead squats (OHS).
*   **Accessory Work:** Rows and band pre-hab exercises focused on scapular retraction and depression to build the "foundation" of the upper back.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Daily Practice:** Hold everything overhead for at least a second during *every* overhead movement (presses, squats) to ingrain stability habits.
*   **Static Holds:** Hold snatches in the bottom position for 3 seconds before standing; hold overhead for 2–3 seconds after standing before dropping.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The emphasis on "tying arms tightly and securely into the trunk" via scapular retraction reduces reliance on arm/shoulder isolation, potentially lowering shear forces on the spine during overhead locks.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** The article explicitly targets overhead stability as a prerequisite for handling heavier loads; strengthening the "foundation" of the upper back is critical to progressing past 50kg OHS.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** While focused on Snatch, the principle of "forceful retraction and upward rotation" applies directly to securing the Push Jerk overhead position, preventing shoulder sag which wastes energy and risks injury.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The advice to "hold the bar overhead for 3 seconds" is an excellent hypertrophy/stability bridge for a lifter moving from powerlifting (squat focus) to Olympic lifting, forcing the nervous system to adapt to new motor patterns before heavy loading.
