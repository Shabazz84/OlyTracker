# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2055/Get-Out-of-the-Clean-Faster/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Intensity/Frequency:** Train cleans and front squats with "maximal concentric acceleration" on every rep; do not grind through the top half of the lift.
*   **Sets/Reps:** Not explicitly defined, but implies high-intensity technical work (low reps) focused on speed rather than volume fatigue.
*   **Periodization:** Emphasizes mastering technique and timing before increasing load; "chasing max" without proficiency leads to failure.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **"Get Out Faster":** The primary goal is minimizing time spent in the bottom of the squat.
*   **The Bounce:** Utilize the elastic recoil (stretch-shortening cycle) of the legs and barbell; treat the bottom position as a spring, not a dead stop.
*   **Aggressive Rack Position:** Secure the bar in the rack with the same aggression used elsewhere; prevent sliding or forward drift.
*   **Full Acceleration:** Do not "let off the gas" partway up; accelerate maximally from the bottom until standing.
*   **Trunk Pressurization:** Stabilize and rigidify the trunk *before* initiating the pull to ensure force transfer rather than absorption by a soft core.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Primary Lifts:** Cleans, Front Squats (must train with bounce), Back Squats.
*   **Speed/Power Drills:** Box jumps, tuck jumps, or broad jumps from the bottom of a squat (with and without pauses).
*   **Specific Variations:** Back squat jumps (with/without pause), Pause squats (maximal concentric acceleration), Clean-jerks.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Mobility Prerequisite:** Must be able to sit into a full-depth squat with an upright posture; inability to close the knee joint forces reliance on muscular effort, slowing recovery.
*   **Strength Work:** Explicitly prescribe dedicated back and abdominal work to ensure trunk stability.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** The text emphasizes "trunk pressurization" and "rigid core" as the mechanism to prevent force absorption by a soft trunk, which is critical for an athlete with chronic back pain.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery Context:** While not explicitly mentioned in this specific excerpt, the emphasis on "neurological patterns" and "timing" suggests that training should be performed when alert (potentially requiring strategic scheduling around night shifts) to ensure proper intent.
*   **Transition Strategy:** The advice to "get your squat stronger" aligns with the athlete's goal of moving from a 102.5kg bodyweight base; increasing back squat strength is identified as a direct driver for clean speed.
*   **Limitation Check:** The text warns that poor mobility (specifically knee flexion) leads to trunk collapse and slower transitions, directly addressing the "OHS limiter" issue by linking ankle/knee mobility to upper body stability.
