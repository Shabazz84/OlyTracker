# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/1937/Pulling-Matters-5-Situations-In-Which-You-Need-Pulls/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Intensity Zones:** Use 90–100% of max for speed/acceleration (competition phase); use 100–150% of max for technique stability and strength reserve (base period).
*   **Volume/Frequency:** High volume in base periods (e.g., 5 reps at heavy weights), tapering to low volume/high intensity near meets (2–3 reps).
*   **Periodization:** Pulls are essential in the base phase to build a "strength reserve" for the pull; reduce frequency as meet date approaches.
*   **Progression:** Start with partial ROM pulls (mid-thigh/knee) to isolate mechanics, progressing to full platform pulls.

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **Speed:** Focus on maximum acceleration of the bar in the second pull ("Getting Your Pull Faster").
*   **Angles:** Maintain correct joint angles throughout the motion; heavy pulling ensures muscles can hold these angles under load.
*   **Bar Path:** Use pulls to fix specific errors like "not keeping the bar close" or "lack of a solid shrug."
*   **Sequence:** In combination sets, use the first 1–2 reps to memorize the power position before attempting the full lift.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **Variations:** Hang pulls (mid-thigh/knee), Block pulls, Platform pulls, Deficit pulls.
*   **Specific Drills:**
    *   *Hang Clean Pulls:* Mid-thigh → Knee level → Full hang cleans (combination set).
    *   *Heavy Static Pulls:* 100–150% of max for stability.
    *   *Explosive Pulls:* Platform pulls at 90–100% for speed.

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   **Injury Management:** For minor knee injuries, substitute squats/cleans with heavy pulls to maintain upper body strength and pulling groove without joint impact.
*   **Risk Warning:** Avoid all pulling if the injury is significant; consult a doctor first.
*   **Energy Conservation:** Isolate specific mistakes via pulls rather than repeating full lifts to save energy for technical focus.

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile (Intermediate, Back Pain, Night Shift)**
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** The text explicitly notes that heavy pulling work can maintain strength levels while avoiding the spinal loading of squats and cleans during recovery phases or if pain flares up (provided it is a minor injury). *Note: Given chronic back pain, this athlete must ensure pulls do not aggravate the spine; deficit pulls may be risky.*
*   **Technique Correction:** As an intermediate transitioning to Olympic lifting with a "push jerk only" limitation and OHS weakness, using hang pulls from mid-thigh/knee is highly relevant to isolate the second pull mechanics without the fatigue of full lifts.
*   **Strength Reserve:** The article emphasizes that neglecting pulls leads to form breakdown at high weights; this athlete needs heavy pulling (100-150%) in the upcoming 6-week hypertrophy block to build the necessary strength reserve for their clean and jerk.
*   **Night Shift Consideration:** While not explicitly mentioned, the advice to "minimize risks" is critical for a night shift worker with chronic pain; heavy pulling should be programmed carefully to avoid fatigue-induced injury during off-hours recovery.
