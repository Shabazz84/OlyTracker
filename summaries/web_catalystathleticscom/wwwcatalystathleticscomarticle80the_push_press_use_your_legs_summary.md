# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/80/The-Push-Press-Use-Your-Legs/

1. **Programming Principles**: The text advocates for the push press as a staple exercise to bridge the gap between the strict press and the jerk. It emphasizes using aggressive leg drive (treating it primarily as a leg exercise) rather than an upper-body movement, with appropriate weights selected to strengthen arm lockout while maintaining smooth bar acceleration from legs to arms.

2. **Technique Cues**:
   - **Leg Drive**: Execute an "extremely aggressive drive" with the legs; do not remain flat-footed (allow ankle extension).
   - **Timing/Smoothness**: The transition from leg drive to arm press must be smooth; the bar should not abruptly decelerate as effort shifts.
   - **Positioning**: Maintain the exact rack position, hand placement, and arm structure used in the jerk.

3. **Exercise Recommendations**: Push Press (specifically for developing leg drive transfer to the jerk).

4. **Recovery or Mobility Advice**: The text recommends weightlifting shoes (e.g., Asics) over trainers to provide a stable platform and artificial ankle flexibility, which aids in maintaining an upright torso during squats and presses.

5. **Athlete Profile Relevance**:
   - **Jerk Technique**: Directly supports the athlete's current "push jerk only" limitation by teaching the critical leg-drive mechanics required for a future split jerk or improved push jerk efficiency.
   - **Back Pain/Strength Transfer**: The emphasis on aggressive leg drive reduces reliance on the lower back and upper body to initiate the lift, potentially mitigating strain while strengthening the lockout.
   - **Footwear**: Recommends weightlifting shoes to improve stability and ankle mobility, which is crucial for an athlete with a 102.5 kg bodyweight performing overhead work.
