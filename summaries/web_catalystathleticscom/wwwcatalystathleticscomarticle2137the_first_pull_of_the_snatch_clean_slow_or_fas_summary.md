# https://www.catalystathletics.com/article/2137/The-First-Pull-of-the-Snatch-Clean-Slow-or-Fast/

**1. Programming Principles**
*   **Intensity/Speed:** For heavy loads (cleaning >150% bodyweight), pull the first phase "as fast as possible" without sacrificing position; speed is naturally limited by biomechanics in this range.
*   **Light Loads:** Intentionally limit first-pull speed when lifting well below capacity to maintain proper mechanics and timing.
*   **Continuity:** The bar must never slow down once it leaves the floor (constant speed or acceleration only).

**2. Technique Cues**
*   **"Pull, Don't Yank":** Separate the bar from the floor with a quick but controlled action; avoid "ripping" which launches the bar past body tension and forces a deceleration to reconnect.
*   **Position First:** Move as fast as possible *only if* proper position and balance are maintained.
*   **Acceleration Focus:** The goal is noticeable explosive acceleration in the second pull, not just raw speed off the floor.

**3. Exercise Recommendations**
*   **First Pull Variations:** Practice the initial separation (floor to knee) with light weights to ingrain controlled speed without rushing.
*   **Heavy First Pulls:** Use heavy loads to train pulling "as fast as possible" while respecting biomechanical limits (speed will naturally be lower here).

**4. Recovery or Mobility Advice**
*   *None explicitly mentioned in this text.* (Note: The text focuses strictly on bar speed mechanics, not recovery protocols.)

**5. Relevance to Athlete Profile**
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The emphasis on "controlled" separation and avoiding "yanking" is critical; rushing the break often compromises spinal alignment and exacerbates back issues.
*   **Intermediate/Transitioning:** The distinction between light-load speed control (for technique) vs. heavy-load maximal intent (for power) provides a clear framework for programming as they transition to Olympic lifts.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** While the text discusses Clean & Snatch generally, the principle of "never letting the bar slow down" applies equally to the jerk's first pull phase if incorporating it into training.
