## Key Points
*   **Intensity Definition:** Heavy training days for competition movements are defined as 60–75% of 1RM; auxiliary exercises should match or stay below this range (50–65%).
*   **Volume & Reps:** Power phases utilize 4–6 reps per set across 2–6 sets. High-intensity single attempts (>90%) are strictly discouraged due to excessive ligament/muscle loading and CNS burnout risks.
*   **Periodization Logic:** Intensity and total tonnage should ideally move in counter-phase (inverse relationship). Light/medium days can be dosed by weekly volume targets rather than fixed percentages.
*   **Recovery & Deloading:** A full week of rest results in only a 3–5% strength loss; immediate return to training is safe, starting at ~65% intensity if confidence is low.

## Programming Principles
*   **Avoid Max Testing:** Eliminate "pass-throughs" or max attempts during training blocks to preserve the CNS and prevent psychological barriers.
*   **Load Management:** Structure heavy days (60–75%) with sufficient rest intervals before subsequent high-volume sessions; alternate intensity levels rather than stacking heavy loads consecutively.
*   **Hypertrophy Adaptation:** While not prescribing specific hypertrophy rep ranges, the framework supports "light/medium" regimes to accumulate volume without inducing CNS fatigue, suitable for a transition phase.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The strict prohibition against >90% single attempts and max testing directly mitigates the risk of acute injury for an athlete with chronic back pain.
*   **Transition Phase:** The 60–75% intensity cap is ideal for an intermediate lifter transitioning to Olympic lifting, prioritizing technique stability over maximal strength testing.
*   **Hypertrophy Block:** The ability to manage volume via tonnage rather than fixed percentages allows for the necessary accumulation work required in a hypertrophy block without compromising recovery.
*   **Night Shift/Recovery:** While the text does not address circadian rhythms, its emphasis on avoiding CNS burnout and allowing rapid return after deloads supports an athlete with irregular sleep schedules (Wed–Sun shift) by reducing cumulative fatigue load.
