## Key Points
*   **Specialization vs. General Prep:** Avoid "plane training" (equal agonist/antagonist volume). Strength athletes typically require a 4.3:1 extension-to-flexion ratio. For an intermediate transitioning to Olympic lifting, prioritize general muscle mass (hypertrophy) over specific movement patterns for the upcoming 6-week block, provided it does not create "stiffness."
*   **Hypertrophy Risks:** Excessive growth in antagonists or specific muscles (biceps, posterior delts) can mechanically restrict range of motion and lockout mechanics. This is critical given your push-jerk-only constraint; bulk must not compromise overhead stability.
*   **Recovery Protocol:** Stretching is mandatory daily. Split mobility work into blocks (e.g., Hamstrings/Splits on Day I; Back/Spine on Day II) rather than one long session. Use a "cool-down" (Zaminka) with very light loads (10–25% 1RM) to increase blood flow without fatigue accumulation.

## Technique Cues
*   **Overhead Stability:** Monitor shoulder flexibility strictly during hypertrophy work. Excessive posterior delt or bicep bulk will mechanically hinder the lockout phase in pressing movements, directly impacting your push jerk ceiling.
*   **Extension Mechanics:** Ensure general muscle development does not create stiffness that inhibits hip extension. The athlete must maintain a high ratio of extensor strength relative to flexor mass.

## Programming Principles
*   **6-Week Hypertrophy Block:** Prioritize general muscle mass accumulation over specific Olympic technique drills, but strictly monitor for "stiffness" in the posterior chain and shoulders.
*   **OFP Selection:** Focus General Physical Preparation (GPP) on muscles directly involved in the lift (posterior chain for squat/deadlift mechanics). Avoid excessive hypertrophy of antagonists that restrict range of motion.
*   **Cool-Down Strategy:** Implement a mandatory light cool-down routine (hyperextensions, light pulls at 10–25% 1RM) to maintain blood flow. This is non-negotiable for chronic back pain management and fatigue mitigation for night shift workers, even if the athlete feels exhausted.
*   **Mobility Scheduling:** Distribute stretching sessions daily in short blocks rather than one long session to prevent stiffness accumulation.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The "light cool-down" protocol (hyperextensions/light pulls) is critical for maintaining spinal blood flow without heavy loading, directly addressing your pain constraints while adhering to the need for movement quality.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** Since you lack a split jerk, overhead stability relies entirely on strict pressing mechanics. The warning against posterior delt/bicep hypertrophy is vital; your 6-week block must avoid bulking muscles that limit shoulder flexion/extension, which would cap your OHS and push jerk potential.
*   **Night Shift Work (Wed–Sun):** While the source suggests reducing cool-downs for exhausted athletes, it warns against making this a habit. For shift workers, maintaining the light cool-down routine is superior to skipping it entirely, as it mitigates fatigue accumulation better than total rest in your specific context.
*   **Transition Phase:** As an intermediate moving to Olympic lifting, you are at the stage where "plane training" myths should be discarded; focus on extension dominance and mass that supports, rather than restricts, the lift.
