## Key Points
*   **Source Limitation:** The provided source texts are exclusively focused on Powerlifting (Squat/Bench/Deadlift) or contain irrelevant spam; they offer no direct programming for Olympic lifts (Snatch/Clean & Jerk).
*   **Critical Safety Warning:** High-rep endurance blocks (e.g., 20-rep squats) and powerlifting-style "deep squat" variations that increase lumbar shear are **contraindicated** due to the athlete's chronic back pain.
*   **Transferable Principles:** The only applicable concepts are **volume cycling** (accumulation before intensification), structured **unloading weeks** to reset fatigue, and reducing accessory volume during peak intensity phases.
*   **Recovery Constraints:** As a night shift worker (Wed–Sun), the athlete must prioritize sleep hygiene over "hormonal recovery" cycles; sudden weight loss indicates lifestyle error or overreaching, not adaptation.

## Technique Cues
*   **Squat Depth:** Avoid transitioning to "powerlifting style" (thigh parallel) which increases lower back engagement. Maintain a depth that preserves spinal neutrality given the chronic pain history.
*   **Overhead Stability:** The athlete's primary limiter is the Overhead Squat (OHS) at 50 kg; focus on thoracic mobility and core bracing rather than pushing through shoulder weakness with heavy loads.
*   **Jerk Mechanics:** Strictly utilize the **Push Jerk** only. Do not attempt to learn or implement a Split Jerk, as the athlete currently lacks this skill and it introduces unnecessary complexity during a hypertrophy/technique block.

## Programming Principles
*   **Block Structure:** Execute a 6-week Hypertrophy Block first (focusing on technique and work capacity) before transitioning to strength/intensity phases.
*   **Load Management:** Avoid "smearing" intensity with high-volume failure training. Use linear progression only after establishing technical mastery at low intensities (<60% 1RM).
*   **Accessory Reduction:** During weeks where main lift intensity peaks, reduce accessory volume by ~25% to prevent CNS burnout and spinal fatigue.
*   **Unloading Strategy:** Implement a dedicated unloading week (lower intensity, maintained or slightly reduced volume) every 4–6 weeks to manage cumulative fatigue without compromising recovery for night shifts.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** Replace high-rep barbell squats with machine variations or RDLs at reduced loads if spinal loading becomes problematic; avoid conventional deadlifts as a primary driver during the transition phase.
*   **Shift Work Adaptation:** Align "light days" and recovery windows with Wed–Sun off-days to ensure sleep quality is not compromised by training stress, acknowledging that circadian disruption lowers tolerance for high-volume endurance work.
*   **Goal Alignment:** The program must prioritize technique refinement (specifically OHS) over maximal strength gains in the first 6 weeks, as the athlete's current strength levels (Clean 80kg/Jerk 65kg) indicate a need for technical stability before loading heavier weights.
