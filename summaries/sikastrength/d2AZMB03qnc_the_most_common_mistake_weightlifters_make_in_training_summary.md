# The Most Common Mistake Weightlifters Make in Training!

# Merged Summary: Programming for Olympic Weightlifting

## Key Points

**Direction Over Perfection**
- Most progress stalls due to lack of structured progression, not genetics or training intensity
- Define 8-week blocks (subdivided into 4-week phases) with weekly changes to load, volume, or exercise variation
- Something must change every session; repeating weights/reps = zero progress

**Accumulation → Intensification Progression**
- Start significantly lighter than feels necessary (undershoot > overshoot)
- Accumulation phase: higher volume, sub-max work (70–80% loads) for technical precision
- Example: 5×3@70kg → 4×3@75kg → 5×2@80kg → 3×1@85kg → max attempt
- Most lifters waste time near maxes out of ego; technical work is impossible at high intensity

**Critical Programming Errors**
- Jumping loads too aggressively (program failure inevitable)
- Staying near maxes too long (ego + fear of detraining)
- Refusing to reset after failed maxes (>1 week spinning wheels = negligent)
- Missing 1 rep doesn't require retry—reset and reprogram forward

**Back Squat–Snatch Ratio**
- Target 160–180 kg BS for 100 kg snatch
- At 102.5 kg BW with BS 118 kg, need parallel squat development (especially front squat for clean)
- Progress both lifts simultaneously; don't neglect squat work

## Technique Cues

**When to Practice Technique**
- Best developed during accumulation phase (lighter loads, lower fatigue, technical focus possible)
- Pre-performance cues require automaticity → ingrain at 70–80% loads, NOT maxes
- Example cues: knee positioning, hip extension timing, arm relaxation

**Clean vs. Jerk Emphasis Structure**
- If clean needs work: 3×clean + 1 jerk (preserve technical focus on cleans)
- If jerk needs work: 5×(1+2) or 1+3 structure (bias toward jerk reps)

**Identify Weaknesses & Build Blocks**
- Assess what fails at heavy weights: "What do you miss?"
- Build 3–4 week blocks addressing that specific deficit before progressing

## Programming Principles

**Minimum Effective Program**
- Snatch session: snatch + snatch pulls/deadlifts + back squat
- C&J session: clean + jerk + clean pulls + front squat
- Frequency: ≥1 snatch session + 1 C&J session weekly

**Volume Foundation**
- Don't skip adequate volume in lower rep ranges (tens/eights/sixes) before heavy phases—avoid "running out of runway"
- Missing 1 rep in a set (e.g., 4×3 becomes 3×3 + 1×2) is acceptable; don't waste time retrying

**Squat Emphasis Blocks**
- When BS/FS ratios are close (e.g., FS 87.5 vs Clean 82.5), use 2-week emphasis blocks on squatting while maintaining snatch/clean at lower intensity
- In final 2–3 weeks before heavy snatch sessions: maintain or peak BS, then reduce squat load while pushing snatch

**Mobility & Recovery Work**
- Consistency > perfect programming (5 min daily > sporadic intensive work)
- Assess effectiveness after 1–2 sessions; improvement = keep it; no change = wrong approach
- Commit 4+ weeks consistently before judging effectiveness
- Diligence and frequency trump variation; avoid expecting results from 5-minute sessions

**Before Blaming Programming**
- Assess sleep, illness, injury, stress, nutrition first
- Progressive failures are rarely mysterious—athlete usually knows the cause
- Don't chase failed sessions; if conditions were normal, reset and reprogram

## Athlete Relevance

**OHS 50 kg Limiter (Primary Snatch Constraint)**
- OHS deficit is the bottleneck, not snatch technique
- Back squat 118 kg is sufficient; **overhead mobility/stability is the constraint**
- Mobility work directly addresses this via soft tissue + positional consistency
- Spend accumulation phases at 60–80 kg snatches, emphasizing OHS quality and overhead position
- Target 6-week snatch progression: begin 55–60 kg, realistic endpoint 85–90 kg (accounts for OHS lag and 6-week window)

**Chronic Back Pain**
- Mobility/soft tissue work is non-negotiable for pain management
- Accumulation phase structure (lighter loads) ideal for pain-free technical practice
- Undershooting essential; avoid aggressive loading early
- Volume management and exercise selection critical; monitor intensity/frequency tolerance

**Night Shift (Wed–Sun) & Recovery**
- Sleep quality directly impacts recovery—monitor as a key variable
- 6-week hypertrophy block requires consistent recovery; may need longer accumulation phases
- Schedule heavy sessions on better recovery days (Tue or early Wed if possible)
- Mobility work becomes even more critical (fatigue + circadian disruption compounded)

**Push Jerk Only (No Split Jerk)**
- Split jerk requires ankle stability + mobility (likely exacerbated by chronic back pain)
- Undershooting load progression even more important initially
- Prioritize mobility before introducing split jerk mechanics
- Realistic 6-week jerk target: 70–75 kg (push jerk progression, mobility dependent)

**Front Squat Development**
- At BS 118 kg and Clean 80 kg, front squat likely 87.5–100 kg range
- Front squat progress critical for clean development; consider emphasis block within hypertrophy phase
