# Fix Your Snatch: 3 Mistakes Every Lifter Makes!

# Analysis: Snatch Technique Errors

## Technique Cues (Snatch)

**1. Looping Around Knee**
- Barbell must travel in curved S-path toward your center of mass, not away
- Insufficient knee clearance forces barbell separation → loss of power position
- Common cause: knees stay too forward during knee passage (first-to-second pull transition)
- **Cues:** Push legs off floor; adjust start position (hips often too low); think "knees back" not "knees out"
- **Caution:** Avoid overcompensation (hips rising, chest dropping)
- **Variations:** Pause snatch @ knee, low hang/block snatch, snatch pull + snatch
- **Timeline:** 2–3 weeks to refine

**2. Hips Into Barbell Too Soon**
- Brings you toward barbell (imbalance) instead of barbell toward hips
- Results: forward jump, barbell swing, slow turnover at heavy weights
- Root cause often: legs disproportionately stronger than back → quad-dominant pull
- **Cue:** Keep hips back as long as possible; extend second pull duration; shoulders stay in front
- **Strength fix needed:** Heavy snatch pulls, snatch deadlifts, block pulls, weighted good mornings, back extensions → build hip/lower/upper back strength
- **Variations:** Paw snatch, block snatch (limited help; patience + repetition essential)

**3. Bending Arms in Snatch**
- Reduces upper body musculature available for turnover (traps, arm pull)
- Shifts load to weaker arms → slows barbell; reduces speed at hip
- **Fix:** Perfect repetition only; no effective variations
- **Secondary checks:** Ensure shoulder mobility, arm relaxation; hypertonic arms slow barbell even without pulling
- **Cues:** Snatch pull + snatch, slow pull snatch (focus only)
