# Why Your Jerk Sucks & How To Fix It!

# Analysis: Split Jerk Coaching Transcript

## 1. Programming Principles
- **Push press**: Load by barbell speed & technical position, NOT max weight. Treat as technical exercise first, power tool second.
- **Front/back squat strength**: Minimum 8-week cycle needed for meaningful gains; correlates directly with dip drive force production.
- **Plyometrics** (depth jumps, weighted back squat jumps ~20% BW): Only effective if front squatting 2x+ bodyweight; otherwise prioritize squat strength first.

## 2. Technique Cues: Split Jerk
- **Dip position**: Hips move in straight line (not forward). Keep barbell within "safe window" (arch of foot width). Vertical leg drive = vertical bar path.
- **Lower back**: Maintain arch/extension through dip; butt wink indicates anterior pelvic tilt—a major error costing 10–15 kg.
- **Extension**: Maximize "up" phase before getting under bar; don't cut extension short prematurely.
- **Dip speed**: Fast rebound is critical (stretch-shortening cycle). If knees visibly pause at bottom = too slow.
- **Cue**: "Send it" when heavy; prioritize effort over form fixes at max loads.

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
- **Slow eccentric/pause dip split jerks**: Fix forward hip drift & lower back control.
- **Barbell good mornings**: 10–45 sec isometric holds at bottom (exaggerated arch) to improve hamstring/glute pliability.
- **Push press**: Load conservatively; prioritize fast lockouts & perfect positions over max weight.

## 4. Recovery/Mobility
- **Posterior chain mobility** (hamstrings, glutes) essential for maintaining lower back extension; directly limits dip mechanics.

## 5. Athlete-Specific Relevance
⚠️ **CRITICAL FOR THIS ATHLETE:**
- **Push jerk → split jerk transition**: This athlete uses push jerk only. The lower back extension & hip control issues described here will become limiting once transitioning to split jerk—prioritize mobility work now (good mornings).
- **Chronic back pain + lower back arch demands**: Dip mechanics here place significant stress on lumbar spine. Butt wink diagnosis & hamstring/glute pliability work are non-negotiable before heavy split jerk volumes.
- **Weak OHS (50 kg)**: Related to mobility deficits; improved posterior chain pliability (good mornings) aids overhead positions too.
- **Night shift fatigue**: Slower dip speeds may worsen under fatigue; emphasize intent/cueing ("send it") over load in later training sessions.
