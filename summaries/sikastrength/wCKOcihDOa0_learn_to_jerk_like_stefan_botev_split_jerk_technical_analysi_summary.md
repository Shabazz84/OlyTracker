## Relevant to This Athlete's Profile

---

## 1. Programming Principles
- **Jerk Practice Frequency:** Daily practice of split jerk positions (bodyweight, broomstick, isometric holds) — not just loaded attempts
- **Strength Foundation:** Front squat is most tangible to split jerk development; back squat supports snatch/clean. Athlete's back squat (118 kg) and presumed low front squat require addressing
- **Periodization Note:** Practice smooth dip-drive tempo across ALL jerk attempts, from warm-ups to heavy singles

---

## 2. Technique Cues: Split Jerk

**Dip & Drive (Tempo):**
- Fast AND smooth — compress like a spring rapidly but smoothly
- **NO pause at bottom** — transition direction immediately (common error)
- Speed limited by: ability to maintain positions + ability to stay smooth
- Leg strength enables fast, controlled dip/drive; weaker lifters often have poor tempo practice

**Split Position (Lower Body):**
- **Front shin angle:** >90° to perpendicular (back behind laces, not forward)
- **Back heel:** High off floor, NOT touching ground (critical stability)
- **Back knee:** Bent (not straight-legged)
- Simple mental cue: "back knee bend" → rest follows automatically

---

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
- **Front squat:** Increase volume/frequency relative to back squat (most directly transfers to jerk)
- **Split jerk holds/pauses:** Daily isometric practice in correct position
- **Broomstick drills:** Non-loaded split jerk reps to lock in pattern before loaded attempts
- Push press can supplement but won't fix technical errors — practice > accessory work

---

## 4. Recovery/Mobility
- Adequate dorsiflexion mobility helps front shin angle management
- Back heel elevation requires ankle/calf mobility and quad strength coordination

---

## 5. Key Takeaway for This Athlete
**Split jerk is hard, technical, and fatiguing—especially post-clean.** With chronic back pain, prioritize:
- Correct split position learning (bodyweight/broomstick daily)
- Front squat strength development
- Smooth, controlled dip-drive (NO pausing)
- Frequent low-load position practice rather than heavy attempts with poor form
