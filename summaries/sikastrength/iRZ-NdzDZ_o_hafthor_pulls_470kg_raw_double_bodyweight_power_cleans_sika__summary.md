# Merged Summary: Weightlifting Programming & Technique

## Key Points

- **Volume & fatigue management**: Heavy deadlifts (280–300 kg) are highly fatiguing for weightlifters; frequency and intensity must be managed to preserve Clean/Jerk quality. Heavy deadlifts impact the next 2–3 sessions of Olympic lift training.
- **Data tracking**: Monitor bar speed, time under tension, and rep/set schemes to optimize training—useful for hypertrophy block planning, but avoid analysis paralysis.
- **Upper back development**: Building trap/upper back shelf improves squat stability, rack position tightness, and overhead positioning—directly addresses your OHS limitation.
- **Lower back extension mechanics**: Actively extend lower back during squat descent (not neutral spine) to stabilize upper back position for heavy loads—may help manage chronic back pain.

## Technique Cues

**High-Bar Back Squat** (directly relevant to OHS):
- Lower back actively extends as you squat deeper; drive shoulders back into bar as weight pushes forward
- Maintain upright torso; neutral/extended wrist position increases stability and upper back tightness

**Clean**:
- Maintain vertical bar path at blocks; avoid early hip push or shoulder dump
- Aggressive, decisive foot placement into receiving position

**General mobility**: Shoulder, pecs, and forearm mobility essential for close-grip squat rack position and overhead work

## Programming Principles

- Consistent, structured loading drives progression (youth athletes show 20+ kg/year gains with structured programs)
- Every second session should include upper/lower back work (back extensions, abs)
- Clean deadlifts have an optimal ceiling around 280–300 kg for weightlifters; minimal gains beyond this
- Program recovery windows around heavy pulling to preserve technique quality in Olympic lifts
