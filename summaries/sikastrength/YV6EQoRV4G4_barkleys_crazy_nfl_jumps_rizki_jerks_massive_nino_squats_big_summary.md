# Merged Summary: Weightlifting Analysis

## Key Points
- Block snatches (above-knee) develop speed and positions; useful for technique transfer but must be monitored for floor lift carryover
- Resetting between heavy reps reinforces start position and competition-ready consistency
- Power clean emphasis: good catch position, rack position, and extension are critical; full cleans more robust than power variations
- Single-leg work (Bulgarian split squats, Romanian split squats) reduces lumbar loading—relevant for chronic back pain management
- High-rep squat work demands consistent setup and breathing discipline

## Technique Cues
**Snatch:** Avoid artificial block techniques that don't transfer to floor lifts; maintain consistent positions throughout
**Clean:** Posterior chain dominant sweep (knees back) effective for lifters with developing leg strength; focus on good catch position, clean rack position, and full extension
**Back Squat:** 
- Keep elbows rigid at consistent angle throughout; avoid pulling elbows high (causes forward bar drift)
- Place bar on wide part of shoulders, not high/close to neck
- Maintain upper back tension for spinal stability

## Programming Principles
- Block work early in training block for speed development and heavy weight practice
- Reset between reps on heavy attempts to build grip strength and technical consistency
- Single-leg variations (Bulgarian/Romanian split squats, lunges) suitable for chronic back pain while building work capacity

## Athlete Relevance
**OHS limiter (50 kg):** Block snatch work could target speed/positions; monitor transfer to floor snatches
**Chronic back pain:** Upper back tension cues for squat safety; prioritize single-leg work to reduce lumbar loading
**Not addressed:** Split jerk development, night-shift recovery strategies, hypertrophy block periodization
