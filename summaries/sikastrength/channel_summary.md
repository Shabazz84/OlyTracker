# **SIKA STRENGTH COACHING PHILOSOPHY: SYNTHESIS DOCUMENT**

## **1. CORE TRAINING PHILOSOPHY (5 Principles)**

### **Principle 1: Position Over Loading**
Train positions first, load second. Perfect reps at 70% are preferable to flawed reps at 90%. Emphasize consistency before intensity advancement. This is critical for your athlete's OHS limitation (50 kg)—excessive load masks positional deficiencies and creates compensation patterns that aggravate chronic back pain.

### **Principle 2: Efficiency Through Periodization**
Structure training in 6–16 week blocks, not sessions. Expect technical mastery to take years, not months. For your athlete: this 6-week hypertrophy block establishes muscular adaptation; don't expect snatch jumps. Long-term thinking reduces injury risk, especially with chronic back pain.

### **Principle 3: Mobility = Performance Ceiling**
Full range of motion under load is the primary mobility strategy (not static stretching isolation). Thoracic extension, lat/posterior chain lengthening, and ankle mobility directly limit snatch depth, OHS position, and jerk lockout. Address mobility systematically; it's not optional.

### **Principle 4: Pain ≠ Progression**
Training through significant pain (>6/10) is counterproductive. Modify technique or exercise selection to remain pain-free. For your athlete: chronic back pain requires conservative loading and form precision—avoid grip adjustments or postural deviations that bypass pain rather than resolving it.

### **Principle 5: Recovery > Volume**
Night shift work + chronic pain demands intelligent frequency management. High volume without adequate sleep/nutrition triggers overtraining, immune suppression, and injury. Prioritize sleep, caloric surplus, and soft tissue work over additional sessions.

## **2. PROGRAMMING APPROACH**

### **Periodization Structure**
**6-Week Hypertrophy Block (Your Athlete's Current Phase)**
- **Frequency:** 3 days/week Olympic lift focus (snatch, clean & jerk variations)
- **Rep ranges:** 3–5 reps snatch/clean; 6–8 reps jerks; 8–12 reps squats/pulls/accessory
- **Intensity:** 70–80% of 1RM (lighter than strength phases but higher volume)
- **Purpose:** Build work capacity, muscular adaptation, and postural resilience before subsequent strength/technique block

**Weekly Structure (Adapted for Night Shift Wed–Sun)**
- **Monday**: Snatch focus (6×2 at 75%, then 3×5 front squat)
- **Tuesday**: Clean & Jerk focus (clean deadlifts, 5×2 power clean, 4×3 front squat, 3×5 push jerk)
- **Thursday**: Combined volume day (3×2 snatch variation, 3×2 clean, 3×3 back squat, accessory)
- **Wed–Sun night shifts**: Recovery priority—light conditioning or mobility only if energy permits; no heavy lifting

### **Post-Hypertrophy Block (Weeks 7+)**
Transition to 4–6 week strength/technique block with lower frequency (2–3x/week), heavier singles/doubles, and variable practice (pause snatches, no-foot snatches, low-hang variations).

### **Exercise Sequencing (Per Session)**
1. **Snatch work** (Olympic lift complex: main movement first)
2. **Clean & Jerk work** (second lift)
3. **Pulls** (snatch-grip or clean-grip deadlifts)
4. **Squats** (front squat frequency 4–5x/week; back squat 1x/week max to manage back stress)
5. **Accessory** (back extension, thoracic mobility, core work, 10–15 min cap)

## **3. KEY TECHNIQUE CUES & EXERCISE PREFERENCES**

### **Snatch (Your Primary Weakness: OHS 50 kg)**
- **First pull:** Maintain flat back; extend maximally; keep barbell close (2–3 cm from quads)
- **Transition (first-to-second):** Aggressively get knees out of the way while pulling barbell back toward body (not allowing barbell to loop forward)
- **Second pull:** Accelerate barbell *past* knee through hip contact—extension speed is non-negotiable; don't decelerate early
- **Turnover:** Reduce time from bar's peak height to squat catch; move faster under the bar
- **Overhead position:** Tight core; shoulders back over heels; full elbow extension (no shrugging/rounding)
- **Common error for weak OHS:** Insufficient thoracic extension + weak back → forward lean and missed lockouts. Address mobility + back extension work aggressively.

**Recommended Snatch Variations:**
- **Pause snatch at knee** (2+1 or 1+2): Reinforces atomicity and transition position
- **Low-hang snatch** (2–3 reps): Reduces variability in start position; high transfer to full snatch
- **Muscle snatch + OHS combo** (low fatigue, high frequency): Builds overhead stability; directly addresses 50 kg OHS limit
- **Avoid:** High-hang snatches (crutch for weak second pull) unless specifically diagnosing an issue

### **Clean (80 kg; Intermediate Limiter)**
- **Start position:** Vertical shin angle; knees out of barbell path; chest up; flat back; shoulders slightly in front of bar
- **First pull:** Maintain position; don't allow hips to rise early
- **Transition:** As bar passes knee, aggressively extend hips; initiate pull-under (drop) *immediately
