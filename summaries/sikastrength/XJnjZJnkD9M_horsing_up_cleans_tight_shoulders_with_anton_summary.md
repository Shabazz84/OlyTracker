# Weightlifting Analysis

## 1. Programming Principles
- **12-week block structure**: 4 sessions/week, peaks for snatch/C&J/squat at end (start 13 weeks pre-competition)
- **Coach frequency**: Athlete programs independently but sees coach (Anton) every 2 weeks for technique feedback/updates
- **Volume approach**: Building volume with power cleans; progressing from block cleans to competition lifts
- **Intensity progression**: Working up to heavy singles (130 kg clean, 120 kg jerk in session shown)

## 2. Technique Cues (Highly Relevant to This Athlete)

**CLEAN:**
- **Bar path**: "Bring bar to you, don't bring yourself to barbell" — prevent premature forward lean/center-of-mass collapse
- **Shoulder position**: Keep shoulders OVER bar longer; avoid pulling shoulders back too early (identified issue)
- **Hip positioning**: Hips slightly BACK in bottom position (not sitting fully in with upright torso) — allows vertical hip travel, better bounce, faster stand
- **Arm tension**: Relax arms during pull; looser arms = smoother turnover and better force transfer
- **Block cleans**: Low blocks (just below knee) simplify movement, isolate key positions

**JERK (Behind-Neck Split Jerk):**
- **Overhead position**: Push barbell BACK behind head (not vertical); athlete feedback: feels "shark squat level" but video shows it's actually normal
- **Load distribution**: Triceps must take primary load; relax shoulders to allow triceps engagement
- **Mobility work**: Heavy jerk holds at position (not stretching) best reinforce overhead mobility — light stretching insufficient
- **Current limitation**: Right shoulder tension/instability because overhead mobility regressed; needs reinforcement

## 3. Exercise Recommendations
- **Block cleans** (low, below knee) for technical focus
- **Power cleans** (progressing well, continue)
- **Jerk holds** at heavy weights for overhead position/mobility reinforcement
- **Lightweight jerk practice** acceptable but heavy weights necessary for position adaptation

## 4. Recovery/Mobility Advice
- Jerk holds with heavier weight = primary mobility tool (not door frame stretches/prayer stretch alone)
- Coach recommendation: Practice position under load; light stretching insufficient for regaining lost overhead mobility
- Athlete notes right shoulder delts inflamed after returning to heavy overhead position work (expected muscular response)

## 5. SPECIFIC TO THIS ATHLETE'S PROFILE

**🎯 CRITICAL RELEVANCE:**
- **OHS 50 kg limiter**: Overhead mobility work (jerk holds, behind-neck jerks) directly addresses snatch limiting factor
- **Chronic back pain**: Hip positioning cue (hips slightly back, not full forward fold) may reduce lumbar stress vs. traditional full hip extension
- **Push jerk only**: Behind-neck work = direct overhead position specificity; identifies shoulder tension/asymmetry (right arm weaker/more tense)
- **Night shift worker**: 2-week coach feedback cycle sustainable; doesn't require daily coaching
- **Strength level** (80 kg clean): Block cleans + technique focus appropriate; mobility/technique precedes heavy C&J
- **6-week hypertrophy block**: This session shows technical refinement phase — coach clearly emphasizing position over load (working 130 kg clean while capable of more)

**⚠️ Flag**: Right shoulder asymmetry + overhead mobility loss = potential factor in jerk plateau (65 kg). Behind-neck jerk work + jerk holds should accelerate jerk recovery.
