# Analysis: Snatch Session Training Vlog

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Frequency balance**: 3x/week squats; 2 quality squat sessions prioritized weekly
- **Session structure**: Snatch pulls BEFORE squats on high-quality snatch days (preserves squat freshness on other days)
- **Snatch pull volume**: ~6 sets of 3 at meaningful weights; stop when back position degrades or bar slows
- **Intensity management**: Alternates heavy snatch/pull days with moderate squat days across the week
- **Recovery windows**: 3-day gaps between priority squat sessions allow resource distribution

## 2. **Technique Cues**
- **Upper back/chest**: Keep chest proud, maintain slight arch (not flat back); deliberate setup focus
- **Second/third pull**: Long but fast extension; think "warp speed" after knee—semi-automatic timing cue
- **Back squat**: Prevent elbow flare and back rounding in later reps (fatigue-induced technical decay)

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- Heavy snatch pulls (150 kg from ~140 kg max) as primary positional/strength tool
- Snatch pulls balance squat fatigue management
- Back squat 220 kg for 3-4 sets (quality sessions)

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility**
- Weekly therapy (Kevin Pry, KP Therapy) for elbow extension mobility—noted significant improvement
- Coach (Anton) feedback on technique priorities
- Nutritionist/lifestyle coaching (Alex)

## 5. **Relevance to Your Profile**
⚠️ **Limited direct applicability**: This athlete is advanced (snatching 120s), injury-free currently, and works standard hours. Key difference for you:
- Your OHS (50 kg) is primary snatch limiter—prioritize OHS development over pure snatch pulls initially
- Night shift fatigue requires energy management; don't replicate 3x/week squat + heavy snatch pulls without recovery monitoring
- Chronic back pain demands stricter positional work (his arch focus is critical for you)
- Consider reducing snatch pull frequency; emphasize quality OHS/snatch balance work in hypertrophy block
