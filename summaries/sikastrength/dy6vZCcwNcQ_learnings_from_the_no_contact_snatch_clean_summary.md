# Learnings From The No Contact Snatch & Clean

**Not a structured coaching plan.** This is a conversational video transcript about no-contact lift technique and philosophy, not a periodized program with sets/reps/intensity.

---

## Extracted Relevant Points:

**Programming:**
- No-contact lifts: 1–2x/week, work to moderate singles
- Suggested for lifters >6 months experience
- Described as "novel training effect," not a game-changer

**Technique Cues:**

*Snatch (no-contact emphasis):*
- Full extension + maximum bar height (1.65–2.05 m/s end speed)
- Pull vertically, get high bar position (~75% body height)
- Acceleration phase: 0.15–0.25 seconds
- Timing: coordinate lower body extension with upper body pull

*Clean (no-contact emphasis):*
- Get under *fast* (not maximum bar height)
- Bar speed lower than snatch (1.2–1.6 m/s)
- Bring bar back to center of mass/midfoot (longer shoulder position)
- Acceleration phase shorter: 0.1–0.2 seconds
- Pull yourself *down* with upper body, not up

**Both lifts:**
- Correct arm timing/use (snatch: follow through; clean: pull yourself under)
- Detect excess hip contact (slows bar, misdirects it)

---

## Relevance to Your Athlete:
- **OHS limiter (50 kg)**: No-contact snatches could reinforce full extension/bar height—critical for snatch pull
- **Push jerk only**: Clean mechanics cues (getting under fast) applicable to jerk footwork transition
- **Back pain**: Lower-intensity no-contact work may serve as learning tool without heavy load
- **Night shift**: 1–2x/week frequency is manageable recovery-wise
