# Fixing The Split Jerk!

# Analysis: Jerk Technique Coaching Session

**Not directly applicable to athlete profile:** This is a coaching session transcript with a different athlete (CrossFit background, transitioning to weightlifting). However, several coaching cues are transferable.

---

## Relevant Extractions for Your Athlete:

### **Technique Cues – Jerk (Most Relevant)**
- **Grip transition:** Move from fingertip to full palm grip before unracking to reduce timing variables and enable heavier loads
- **Dip mechanics:** Load quads (not heels), avoid excessive forward hip shift; think "jumping down a box" (vertical drive, not deep squat)
- **Head position:** Tuck chin to keep spine neutral; prevents torso tilt
- **Speed emphasis:** Prioritize fast, explosive dip-drive over depth alone
- **Split stance:** Avoid chasing the bar backward; load quads to reduce backward displacement; use small, controlled steps (45° angle, not straight forward)
- **Stance width:** Too narrow = balance loss; adjust from lunge-like pattern to 45° pressing angle

### **Programming Note**
- Heavy push press 2–3x/week recommended to build quad-loading mechanics and jerk drive (relevant for push jerk progression)

---

## **Specific to Your Profile:**
- **Push jerk limitation:** These quad-loading and dip-speed cues directly address your push jerk (no split yet). Master these before attempting split jerk.
- **Back pain:** Forward hip shift and excess depth in dip may aggravate lower back. Quad-loading approach (staying more upright) is safer.
- **Night shift:** Push press accessory is time-efficient for jerk development.
