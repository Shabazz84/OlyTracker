# 5 Ways To Improve Your Turnover in The Snatch

# Weightlifting Coaching Analysis

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **No-contact muscle snatches**: 15–20 total reps in warm-ups, very light (30–40 kg for 100 kg snatcher). Emphasize frequency/skill over load.
- **High pull + full snatch complex**: ~1×/week at 60–70%. Start 1+2 or 2+1, reduce volume progressively over weeks until singles.
- **Hang snatches (above-knee)**: 4–5 sets of 2–3 reps initially, progress to heavy singles. Cap at ~90% of best snatch from floor.
- **Trappy (high pull to hip)**: 4–5 sets of 5 reps, can pair with full snatch (4+1). Good for eccentric loading.
- **Upper body hypertrophy**: 2–3 sessions/week, 50 min of quality work. Critical for late-transitioning lifters.

## 2. **Technique Cues**
**No-contact muscle snatch**: Sequence = traps → high elbows → smooth bar turnover. Avoid early hip smash/swing.

**High pull**: Full extension (chest proud, arched back), NO rebend. Aggressive upper-body pull toward sternum afterward.

**Hang snatch (above-knee)**: Emphasize lower/upper back extension, leg extension, aggressive upper-body pull. Pull the bar, don't swing.

**Trappy**: Full hip extension first, keep arms straight initially, let hips pull bar to chest, then arms bend. Do NOT bend arms early.

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
Upper back: barbell rows (strict, heavy), single-arm rows, rear delts, weighted pull-ups, weighted push-ups.  
Lower back: back extensions (high volume), core work.  
*Rationale*: Muscle mass initiates pulls; strong back maintains second/third pull position.

## 4. **Recovery/Mobility**
None explicitly stated.

## 5. **Relevance to This Athlete**
- **Critical**: OHS 50 kg is primary snatch limiter → upper body hypertrophy (2–3×/week) directly addresses bottleneck.
- **Back pain consideration**: High pull + trappy + back extensions build posterior chain, but chronic back pain warrants load management on heavy pull variations.
- **Push jerk only**: Upper body emphasis aligns with jerk weakness; supports split jerk transition later.
- **Shift work**: Frequent, lighter skill work (muscle snatches) fits fatigue management better than heavy daily snatches.
