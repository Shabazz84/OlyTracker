# Analysis: Jerk Variations & Clean Contact

## Programming Principles
- **Jerk from blocks vs. rack**: Blocks allow greater volume/safety; rack requires larger deficit for equal carryover but builds positional strength
- **Hybrid approach**: High-volume work from blocks (triples) → heavy singles from rack
- **Clean plateau strategy**: Increasing reps (4-5s) in jerk can break through plateaus; volume matters significantly

## Technique Cues - CLEAN (Primary Relevance)
**Critical insight for this athlete:**
- **Overemphasis on contact is inhibitory** — most common amateur mistake
- Contact contributes <20% to lift; pulling speed & getting under faster = 80%
- Cue: "Don't make contact" even though heavy cleans look powerful
- Problem: Excessive contact = extended pulling phase → delayed turnover → slower under

**For 80 kg clean → progression:** Focus on faster turnover/getting under, not harder bar contact.

## Exercise Recommendations
- **Jerk from rack with walkout** — builds positional stability & command; walk-out mechanics critical to prevent platform crushing sensation
- **Caution re-racking heavy weights** — injury risk high; requires skill; don't do heavy rack jerk reps carelessly
- Push jerk work safer from blocks

## Recovery/Mobility Advice
- Re-racking = injury risk; knees/back vulnerable during unload

## Athlete-Specific Relevance
- **OHS limiter (50 kg):** Clean contact obsession likely limiting rack position confidence; shift focus to speed/turnover
- **Back pain concern:** Emphasize block jerks over rack for volume safety
- **Push jerk only:** No split jerk complicates rack walkout; blocks may be safer progression

---

**Bottom line:** Reduce contact fixation in cleans; prioritize turnover speed and positioning before loading contact harder.
