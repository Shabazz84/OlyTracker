# Weightlifting: Cause or Prevention of Hemorrhoids?

## Key Points
*   **Avoid Max Attempts:** Frequent attempts at 100% intensity ("passing through to maximum") cause dynamic stops that damage ligaments and the spine, posing a high risk for an athlete with chronic back pain.
*   **Speed Over Brute Force:** Transitioning from powerlifting requires shifting focus from raw strength to explosive speed; heavy pulls do not guarantee a good bar path without acceleration.
*   **Leg Strength Correlation:** Clean & Jerk pull strength is directly dependent on squat and leg strength, but only if technique remains stable.
*   **Recovery Management:** High-frequency training (e.g., twice daily) or insufficient rest leads to systemic fatigue. For night shift workers, prioritizing sleep quality over volume is critical; the body cannot recover without adequate downtime.

## Technique Cues
*   **"Run" the Bar:** Focus on accelerating the bar ("running") rather than pulling with brute force to achieve a high bar path.
*   **Pause Variations:** Utilize deadlifts or pulls paused near knee height (second pull transition) to isolate the timing of the jump and distinguish between speed-driven movement and raw strength.
*   **Stability First:** If technique is unstable, do not increase load; focus on holding correct angles at sub-maximal weights sufficient for progression rather than current maxes.

## Programming Principles
*   **Sub-Maximal Volume:** Prioritize high-volume work at 80–90% intensity over frequent max attempts to build strength without excessive spinal loading or joint inflammation.
*   **Rest on Demand:** Do not strictly time rest periods; allow recovery until the athlete feels "fresh" and ready, simulating competition pressure where rest is forced but training should be self-regulated.
*   **Hypertrophy Block Strategy:** Begin with controlled volume to build work capacity (aligning with the 6-week goal) before introducing high-intensity technical drills, ensuring the nervous system adapts to speed rather than just load.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Back Pain Mitigation:** The avoidance of "dynamic stops" and max attempts is critical for managing chronic back pain; the program must emphasize smooth acceleration over heavy grinding.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** Given the Wed–Sun night shift schedule, training frequency should be adjusted to ensure sleep quality is not compromised by high-volume fatigue, as recovery time is a limiting factor.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** The focus on "speed" and "explosiveness" applies directly to the push jerk; since the split jerk is excluded, the ability to accelerate the bar quickly under the body becomes the primary driver for height and stability.
*   **Transition Phase:** As an intermediate lifter moving from powerlifting, the immediate goal is retraining the nervous system for Olympic speed rather than relying on existing raw strength (Squat 118kg), which currently limits the OHS and Clean efficiency.
