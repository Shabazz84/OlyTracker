# Рывок, толчок и приседания на максимуме

## Key Points
*   **Hypertrophy-First Approach:** Prioritize "volume weeks" (high repetitions at 80–90% intensity) over constant maximal loading to build muscle mass and work capacity without excessive CNS fatigue. This aligns with the planned 6-week block.
*   **Back Safety Protocol:** Strictly avoid heavy Romanian Deadlifts (RDLs), Good Mornings, or conventional deadlifts due to high lumbar shear forces. These are identified as primary causes of chronic back pain in similar profiles.
*   **Shift Work Adaptation:** Acknowledge that night shift work (Wed–Sun) increases mental fatigue and injury risk. Success relies on "enjoying the process" and maintaining a calm, analytical mindset rather than reacting with frustration ("hysterics") to performance dips caused by circadian rhythm disruption.
*   **Progressive Overload:** Transition from heavy maximal efforts to technical precision. Even at high intensities (90%), the focus must remain on movement quality to prevent injury in an athlete with a 118 kg squat and chronic back issues.

## Technique Cues
*   **Hang Pulls (Primary Posterior Chain Driver):** Perform Snatch/Clean pulls from a hang position below the knee (mid-shin or just below). This distributes load across hips and knees, minimizing lumbar compression compared to RDLs.
    *   *Cue:* Initiate by leaning back slightly to engage hamstrings/glutes before extending; do not rush. Feel ground reaction force ("support") before exploding upward.
*   **Spinal Integrity:** Maintain a neutral spine at all times. Do not round the back during pulls or squats. If flexibility limits depth, use pauses in the hang position to find correct angles rather than forcing range of motion under load.
*   **Pull Mechanics:** Drive with legs and hips; avoid "snatching" with arms. Use straps for low-hang pulls to ensure grip failure does not interrupt hip extension or wrist protection.
*   **Push Jerk Specifics:** Focus on a vertical bar path generated by the dip and drive from the hang position. Since split jerk is excluded, emphasize explosive shoulder/leg coordination without relying on arm strength alone.

## Programming Principles
*   **Structure:** Implement a 6-week hypertrophy block characterized by high volume (moderate weights) followed by technique/strength phases. Avoid constant maximal loading cycles.
*   **Exercise Selection:**
    *   **Primary:** Snatch/Clean pulls from hang (below knee), Hang Clean + Push Jerk complexes.
    *   **Accessory:** Back Squats at moderate intensity with high reps (finishers) to build leg strength without excessive spinal shear.
    *   **Excluded:** Heavy RDLs, Good Mornings, and heavy conventional deadlifts due to back pain contraindications.
*   **Intensity Management:** Train at 90% intensity only when technical precision is maintained. If fatigue sets in (common with night shifts), reduce load to maintain form rather than pushing for PRs.
*   **Recovery Strategy:** Prioritize mental recovery and stress management. Use "analyzing errors" as a tool for adaptation rather than emotional reaction, acknowledging that endurance training can negatively impact ligaments if not managed carefully.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The methodology explicitly replaces high-shear exercises (RDLs/Good Mornings) with hang pulls and squat variations that distribute load to the hips/knees, directly addressing the athlete's injury history while building posterior chain strength.
*   **Night Shift Schedule:** The emphasis on mental discipline, avoiding "hysterics," and prioritizing technical consistency over maximal output is tailored for an athlete training during circadian low-points (Wed–Sun), reducing burnout risk.
*   **Intermediate Status & OHS Limitation:** The focus on hang pulls allows the athlete to develop pulling strength without being limited by their 50 kg Overhead Squat (OHS) ceiling immediately, while the push jerk focus accommodates their current technical specialization.
*   **Hypertrophy Goal:** The "volume week" philosophy supports the specific goal of a 6-week hypertrophy block before transitioning to heavy technique work, ensuring muscle mass is built safely without compromising spinal health.
