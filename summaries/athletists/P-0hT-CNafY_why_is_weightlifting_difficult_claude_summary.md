# Weightlifting Technical Summary

## Key Points

- **Jerk ceiling limited by leg drive, not arm strength:** Push/power jerk relies heavily on upper body; split jerk activates larger muscle groups (legs, glutes, back) and would likely increase jerk capacity significantly beyond current 65 kg.
- **OHS limiter (50 kg) stems from arm/shoulder dominance:** Low overhead strength relative to back squat (118 kg) suggests premature arm engagement rather than core strength deficit. Prioritize leg-driven mechanics.
- **Kinetic chain sequencing:** Large muscle groups (legs/back) transfer force first; arms remain passive until catch phase. Premature arm involvement creates weak links.
- **Tension-relaxation cycling:** Olympic lifting demands rapid tension/relaxation transitions (especially in pulls and catches)—distinct from powerlifting's sustained tension model.
- **Programming order:** Build physical preparedness (hypertrophy block) before refining technique under load.

## Technique Cues

- **Pull (подрыв):** Explosive knee/hip/ankle extension at maximal muscular tension. Generate bar height through legs/back, not arms.
- **Receiving position:** Relax during transition; catch bar mid-air with rapid tension/relaxation cycling (especially relevant for back pain—excessive tension aggravates chronic issues).
- **Head positioning:** Move head back to avoid bar contact; supports tension management.
- **Jerk mechanics:** Initiate with powerful leg drive before arm contribution. Split jerk recruits larger muscle groups than push/power jerk.
- **Arm role:** Passive in clean/snatch pulls and until catch phase; arms are small relative to legs/back and cannot be primary force generators.

## Programming Principles

- **Match progression to physical preparedness:** Technical skill development must align with current strength/mobility capacity.
- **Hypertrophy-first approach:** 6-week hypertrophy block builds muscular capacity before technique refinement, enabling higher-quality movement patterns under load.
- **Movement-pattern learning:** Intermediate athletes from strength backgrounds require deliberate coaching of Olympic lifting movement quality, not just load.

## Athlete Relevance

**Critical issues:**
- **Split jerk urgently needed:** Current 65 kg power/push jerk leaves significant capacity untapped. Split jerk training would activate glutes/legs and likely add 10–15+ kg to jerk total.
- **OHS → overhead stability path:** 50 kg OHS vs. 118 kg back squat gap suggests arm-dominance pattern. Reframe as leg-drive and core-stability issue, not arm weakness.
- **Back pain + tension patterns:** Chronic back pain may be exacerbated by excessive upper-body tension during pulls and catches. Cue relaxation during receiving positions and avoid forced tension in static holds.
- **Night shift scheduling:** Prioritize lower-volume, technically-focused sessions aligned with recovery windows.
- **6-week hypertrophy block appropriately timed:** Builds OHS, split jerk positioning strength, and pulling capacity before technique refinement.
