# Merged Weightlifting Coaching Summary

## Key Points

- **Snatch Day Structure:** 5-exercise session, max 2 hours. Avoid excessive volume (5-7 exercises at 70-80% intensity undermines weightlifting specificity).
- **Warm-up Complex (6 sets, light weight):** Hip snatch pulls (30-50 kg, no leg drive) → 3 snatch receives (legs-driven descent) → 3 behind-neck presses in low squat → focus on mobility assessment and shoulder mechanics.
- **Main Snatch Work:** Progressive loading—50% (1 floor + 2 above-knee, 2 sets) → 70% (1 floor + 2 above-knee, 2 sets) → 80% (1 floor + 1 above-knee, 2 sets) → 85% (1 floor + 1 above-knee, 1-2 sets).
- **Deadlift Mechanics:** Chest on bar, shoulders over bar through mid-thigh pull, finish at hip crease. Contact point (shin/knee) is technique key, not explosive violence.
- **Jerk Weakness Addressing:** Push press 3×/week (currently 1×/week) to build lockout strength for power jerk transition.
- **Lower Body Structure:** Front squats mid-week to allow recovery between heavy back squats and snatches; front squats also spare lower back.
- **No percentage prescriptions for warm-ups**—use athlete feel and readiness.

## Technique Cues

**Snatch Receives ("Ухода"):**
- **Common Error:** Athletes "push" bar away (mimics push press).
- **Correct:** Drive legs DOWN with straight legs from receive position. Go under the bar from straight legs, prioritizing speed/timing over power.
- **Cue:** "Make noise with your feet"—encourage audible foot contact to develop speed under bar.
- **Progression:** Master with empty bar → 30-40 kg before adding load.

**Hip Snatch Pulls:**
- Performed WITHOUT leg drive or back extension.
- Hold bar at height with arms while body descends (shoulder mobility/positioning work).
- Prepare overhead mechanics safely.

**Above-Knee Snatches:**
- Emphasize final acceleration and velocity application.
- Educational variation for understanding power mechanics.

**Deadlift/Pull Sequencing:**
- Chest on bar, shoulders over bar → pull to mid-thigh → drive hips → finish at hip crease.
- Contact point (shin) is technique key before heavy loading.

**Push Press:**
- Primary tool for building jerk lockout strength and overhead stability.

## Programming Principles

- **Exercise Density:** 5 exercises per session maximizes technical learning without excessive joint stress; 5-7 exercises at 70-80% fatigue CNS and reduce technique quality.
- **Loading Progression:** Pairs floor and above-knee work at same intensity to reinforce pull mechanics and address weak positions.
- **Session Duration:** 2 hours max respects recovery, especially for night-shift workers with accumulated fatigue.
- **Strap Usage:** Use for all "from hang" work (grip recovery, focus on pulling mechanics); no straps for floor pulls (grip/technique integrity).
- **Readiness Assessment:** Use empty bar contact to gauge daily fatigue, soreness, and joint feel—experienced lifters immediately sense training capacity.
- **Hypertrophy Block Transition:** Current session emphasizes technique/strength; lighter loading supports shift to 6-week hypertrophy block.

## Athlete Relevance

| Factor | Application |
|--------|-------------|
| **Chronic back pain** | Hip snatch pulls (minimal spinal load) preferred over full pulls; front squats mid-week spare lower back; avoid excessive volume. |
| **Low OHS (50 kg primary limiter)** | Heavy emphasis on behind-neck presses, hip snatch pulls, and snatch receives for mobility/overhead stability assessment. Identify specific constraint (hips vs. ankles vs. shoulders). |
| **102.5 kg BW / Intermediate** | Estimated percentages: 50% = 40 kg, 70% = 56 kg, 80% = 64 kg, 85% = 68 kg snatch. Programming weights are realistic and progressive. |
| **Night shift worker (Wed–Sun)** | Recovery is paramount; 2-hour max session respects accumulated fatigue; assess readiness daily from bar feel. |
| **Power jerk (no split jerk trained)** | Above-knee snatch work and push press 3×/week transfer directly to second pull mechanics and lockout strength. |
| **Back Squat 118 kg / Clean 80 kg / Jerk 65 kg** | Push press frequency increase addresses jerk lag (likely lockout/overhead stability constraint). Front squats build leg strength while reducing spinal load. |
