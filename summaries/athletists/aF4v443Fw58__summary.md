# Тренировка с тяжелыми приседаниями

## Key Points
*   **Self-Regulation Over Rigid Percentages:** Written percentages are drafts, not rules. Intensity must be adjusted daily based on fatigue and pain levels; if symptoms arise, reduce load to 90% or skip explosive variations rather than pushing through.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** Irregular recovery windows require strict listening to the body ("feel your body"). Do not blindly follow a plan (e.g., 105 kg) after poor sleep; prioritize self-coaching over external targets.
*   **Back Pain Management:** High-velocity pulls and heavy loading must be modified if chronic back pain or fatigue is present. Avoid overusing high-weight snatch pulls that stress the lower back or knees.
*   **Heavy Squat Safety:** For heavy squatting, utilize a 2-person spotter system (one on each side, one behind) to ensure safety during failed attempts and prevent injury.

## Technique Cues
*   **Snatch Pulls & Explosiveness:** Initiate movement with maximum speed immediately; do not start slow. Focus on generating height and trap engagement while maintaining a vertical bar path.
*   **Bar Proximity:** Keep the bar close to the body during the pull-up phase to prevent it from drifting away ("dropping" the bar).
*   **Torso Stability Cue:** Use a "tie a knot" or shirt-fold cue to ensure the torso remains tight and rigid, preventing the belt/shirt from interfering with the vertical bar path.
*   **Push Jerk Drive:** When performing push jerks (from behind the neck), focus on feeling the "drive" (*posyl*) while keeping elbows down for shoulder safety and stability.

## Programming Principles
*   **Dynamic Complexity:** Utilize complexes like "Snatch Pulls into Squat" to train speed and height, but strictly manage weight to protect joints; prioritize explosive intent over maximal load in these variations.
*   **Accessory Integration:** Use movements like "Snatch to Overhead Press" as secondary or warm-up variations to build shoulder stability without the full technical demand of a snatch.
*   **Squat Volume & Progression:** Heavy squats should be performed with high volume, potentially utilizing ladder progressions, prioritizing speed and explosiveness before heavy loading phases.
*   **Frequency:** Maintain a high-frequency approach for specific lifts (e.g., dedicated heavy squat days) rather than spreading load too thinly across the week.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain & Night Shifts:** The core philosophy of "dropping intensity" and avoiding pain is critical for this profile. The emphasis on daily self-assessment directly addresses the recovery challenges of a night shift worker (Wed–Sun), ensuring training adapts to irregular sleep cycles rather than rigid schedules.
*   **Transition Phase:** Cues regarding torso tightness and bar proximity are essential for an intermediate athlete transitioning to Olympic lifting, as poor posture under load exacerbates back pain.
*   **Current Limitations:** While the source does not address the specific 50 kg OHS limitation or split jerk mechanics (as this athlete uses push jerks), the principles of stability and "drive" apply directly to overcoming overhead weaknesses safely.
