# Consolidated Weightlifting Analysis

## Key Points

- **Snatch programming for this athlete**: Use high-hang/above-knee positions and pulls from blocks to reduce ROM and spinal load while leveraging strong legs. Partial-range work manages chronic back pain.
- **OHS is the primary limiter**: Mobility and stability, not strength, constrain progress. Requires dedicated flexibility work before heavy jerk progression.
- **Jerk progression**: Small incremental jumps (80→85→90 kg) with stabilization at comfortable weights before advancing. Current 65 kg push jerk indicates weak drive phase despite strong squat.
- **Leg drive emphasis**: Strong squat (118 kg) isn't translating to C&J. Coach cues emphasize explosive leg extension in jerk; must avoid early arm tension.
- **Recovery principle**: Vary intensity daily (some heavy, some lighter/higher reps); avoid constant unrecovered state. Back pain requires modified range—standing/semi-standing variations preferred during recovery phases.

## Technique Cues

- **Full foot pressure**: Maintain complete foot contact; avoid early heel lift (reduces control and bar acceleration).
- **Leg-driven acceleration**: Drive bar upward with legs rather than relying on back strength—critical given chronic back pain and leverage profile (tall, strong-legged athlete).
- **Jerk drive phase** ("Качнул, ушел"): Sharp, explosive leg extension followed by rapid exit. Mimic boxer's jab—loose, explosive, not tense lockdown.
- **Catch positioning**: Use leg drive to meet bar high; don't drop under early. Limited mobility (OHS 50 kg) requires driving bar higher before catching.
- **Stance stability**: Scissor stance—front leg is primary support and must be firmed/turned; back leg shouldn't collapse under load.

## Programming Principles

- **Hypertrophy block (6 weeks)**: Employ 3×5 to 5×5 rep ranges at 70–95% intensity; vary daily intensity; avoid constant max attempts.
- **Exercise selection for injury management**: Elevated positions (high hang, blocks, standing variations) reduce spinal load; reserve full-depth work for later strength phase.
- **Snatch strategy**: High-hang partials + snatch pulls from blocks maximize leg involvement and acceleration distance without excessive ROM demand.
- **Mobility integration**: Daily stretching/flexibility work non-negotiable (especially shoulder/thoracic for clean position, ankle/hip for catch depth).
- **Progression pace**: Use small jumps when struggling; stabilize at comfortable weights before advancing (avoid volatile progress pattern).

## Athlete Relevance

✓ **Chronic back pain + night shift (Wed–Sun recovery challenge)**: Prioritize standing/elevated variations, avoid deep spinal flexion, manage fatigue-driven form breakdown.

✓ **OHS 50 kg limiter**: Mobility > strength. Dedicate hypertrophy block to leg-drive mechanics + flexibility work before heavy jerk progression.

✓ **Strong squat (118 kg) / weak jerk (65 kg) ratio**: Indicates drive phase inefficiency and arm tension under load. Focus jerk work on explosive leg extension + relaxation cues; small incremental jumps (80→85→90 kg).

✓ **Snatch gap (80 kg clean vs. ~60 kg estimated snatch)**: High-hang partials + block pulls align with leverage profile and back-pain constraints; builds leg drive before full-ROM work.

✓ **6-week hypertrophy block timing**: Ideal for addressing OHS mobility deficit and jerk drive mechanics before strength phase; schedule lighter/moderate weeks around night shift fatigue.
