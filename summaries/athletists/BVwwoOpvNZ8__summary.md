*   **Core Philosophy:** Speed is the primary driver; "heavy" pulls often result in slower bar paths and reduced power compared to lighter, faster execution. Gravity accelerates all weights equally, so the lifter must actively pull themselves *under* the bar rather than waiting for it to fall.

## Technique Cues
*   **The "Shvung" (Power Snatch/Clean):** Perform the pull without splitting feet ("soldier stance"). Squat down on one line and push up to ingrain speed and vertical force while keeping the bar close.
*   **Bar Path & Height:** Avoid high pulls that lead to loss of control. Start from a lower position (below knees) to engage hips correctly before extension. The bar should not rest heavily on the knees; keep it suspended just above the knee caps to maintain tension and prevent shoulder collapse.
*   **Head Position:** Do not lift the head during the pull. Push the head down/forward slightly as you extend, then snap the body under.
*   **Receiving Depth (OHS Focus):** "Meet" the bar on the toes, not flat-footed. Drop deep into a squat to catch the weight; do not stand up with it. This directly addresses the OHS limiter by forcing ankle mobility and depth.
*   **Bracing & Stability:** Maintain rigid torso tension throughout the lift ("do not spread the belly"). Do not release tension at the top or "lock out" prematurely, which can collapse the lower back.
*   **Jerk Mechanics (Push Jerk):** Push up, hold briefly to stabilize, then drop under quickly ("drop like a stone") to ensure core stability and prevent collapsing into the catch position.

## Programming Principles
*   **Speed Over Load:** Prioritize light-to-moderate weights for speed drills over heavy loading that degrades bar path velocity.
*   **Isometric & Paused Drills:**
    *   **Static Holds at Knees:** Pull to knees, hold for 5 seconds with isometric contraction to strengthen grip and back connection before the explosive pull.
    *   **Paused Squats/Front Squats:** Perform squats with a 2–3 second pause at the bottom to ensure depth and stability before standing up.
*   **Slow-Motion Mechanics:** Practice dropping under the bar slowly (unweighted or light weight) to ingrain timing between the drop and the bar's descent.
*   **Grip Conditioning:** Utilize dead hangs after training sessions to strengthen wrists and forearms, addressing grip fatigue common in transitional athletes.
