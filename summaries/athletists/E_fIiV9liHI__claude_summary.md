# Merged Summary

## Key Points
- **Split jerk development is priority**: Athlete currently uses push jerk only but should develop split jerk competency first, as it offers greater leverage variability and is more forgiving for athletes with mobility/stability constraints
- **Split jerk advantages for this athlete**: Better for chronic back pain (allows load distribution adjustment via foot placement, less overextension demand); more scalable than push jerk for weak leg strength
- **Receiving position is limiting factor**: OHS at 50 kg directly links to split jerk receiving mechanics—this is the primary technical bottleneck before hypertrophy block
- **Technique-first approach**: Master positioning and transitions before adding intensity; rushing movement patterns with heavy loads compounds injury risk (especially relevant given chronic back pain)

## Technique Cues

### Split Jerk
- **Scissor length**: Moderate split; avoid excessively long positions (harder to exit under load)
- **Front leg**: Maintain proper knee angle; prevent inward collapse
- **Exit pattern**: Drive hips backward; shift weight cleanly to rear leg; **avoid excessive forward movement** when exiting (energy waste, spinal shear risk with load)
- **Posture**: Chest up, maintain internal pressure; slight posterior pelvic tilt (don't over-squeeze glutes—creates tension blocks)
- **Shoulder stability**: Don't drop shoulders in split; keep upper body stable
- **Head/chin**: Tuck chin slightly; maintain neutral head position

### Common Errors
- Elbow bend during pull/transition (creates tension block)
- Forward knee drift in split
- Exiting with unnecessary horizontal movement
- Rushing transitions

## Programming Principles
- **Pause jerk squats**: 4 reps with pauses at each dip station (develops stability, positioning, leg coordination)
- **Paired pulls**: Snatch pulls with straight-arm finish; trap-dominant cue (shoulders + legs, not arms); avoid premature elbow bend
- **Exercise sequencing**: Skill work before intensity; alternate push/split jerk to identify weaknesses and build confidence in split mechanics
- **Night-shift consideration**: Prioritize quality CNS-focused technique sessions; pair with hypertrophy block to avoid fatigue accumulation

## Athlete Relevance
1. **Chronic back pain**: Split jerk safer than push jerk (less overextension; foot placement allows postural adjustment)
2. **OHS at 50 kg** (primary snatch limiter): Directly improves through split jerk receiving-position work; focus on rib cage stability and core bracing (internal pressure, not glute tension)
3. **No split jerk competency yet**: Dedicate early sessions (before 6-week hypertrophy block) to ingrain split mechanics—this unlocks better jerk/OHS stability and reduces spinal load
4. **Weak leg strength (relative to bodyweight)**: Split jerk more learnable than push jerk at this strength level; improved leg mechanics will transfer to clean receiving position

**Actionable priority**: Develop confident split jerk mechanics in weeks 1–2; then enter hypertrophy block with solid technical foundation.
