# Do you do a lot of squats? That's not enough for weightlifting!

## Key Points
*   **Success Rate vs. Confidence:** Avoid artificially lowering weights to guarantee 100% success, as this creates "unstable" confidence. Conversely, limit failed attempts to fewer than three per session; excessive failures build nervous system instability and doubt. Aim for near-maximal loads (90–95%) with immediate cessation upon technique breakdown or fatigue.
*   **CNS Management:** The Central Nervous System is the primary limiter for high-intensity lifting. Signs of "instability" or "doubt" indicate CNS fatigue, not just muscle soreness. Training must be frequent enough to maintain neural connections but flexible enough to reduce intensity when internal signals suggest fatigue (crucial for night shift workers).
*   **Progression Model:** Progress is a "tandem" adjustment between coach and athlete based on daily feel rather than rigid adherence to a plan.

## Technique Cues
*   **The Pause (Snatch Pulls):** Hold the bottom position of snatch pulls (knee or hang) for ~5 seconds before exploding. This prevents "jerky" hip movement, forces spinal stabilization under load, and ensures muscles are fully tensioned ("filled") before the explosive phase. *Critical for chronic back pain management.*
*   **Dip & Drive:** Execute a controlled dip; do not rush. The bar falls at gravity's speed; the lifter must "overtake" it by dropping under quickly after the pull, rather than attempting to lift higher with arms alone. This is essential for progressing beyond 65kg in the push jerk.
*   **Grip & Lockout:** In wide-grip snatch pulls, focus on leg drive and stabilizer engagement rather than arm strength. "Lock" the position before initiating the explosive extension.

## Programming Principles
*   **Frequency:** Aim for two sessions per week to maintain CNS connections without burnout, adjusting volume based on daily recovery status.
*   **Exercise Selection:** Prioritize Snatch Pulls from Hang/Knees and Snatches with Pauses for intermediates to build posterior chain stability and eliminate momentum errors.
*   **Adaptability:** Utilize a "listen to your body" approach. If night shift fatigue or back pain flares, lower intensity or stop before failure rather than forcing a PR attempt.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The mandatory 5-second pause in snatch pulls is the primary intervention here; it forces spinal and hip stabilization under load, reducing the risk of aggravating movements common in "jerky" lifts.
*   **Night Shift (Wed–Sun):** The philosophy of prioritizing internal CNS signals over rigid programming allows for necessary intensity adjustments on days following night shifts, preventing injury and burnout.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** While the push jerk is a valid preference, the text warns that without mastering the speed of dropping under the bar (overtaking gravity), progress will stall at 65kg. The focus must shift from "lifting with arms" to "dropping under."
*   **Hypertrophy Block Context:** The emphasis on stability and CNS management supports a transition into a hypertrophy block, as building tissue quality without neural fatigue is safer for an athlete with back issues than high-intensity maxing.
