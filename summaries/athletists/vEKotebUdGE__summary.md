# Подсобные упражнения в тяжелой атлетике

## Key Points
*   **Training Focus:** The current phase prioritizes speed, technique, and motor pattern ingrainment over heavy loading ("not lifting big kilos"). High-repetition sets (e.g., 3x5) at moderate weights are used to reinforce movement quality.
*   **Periodization Strategy:** Utilizes a wave-like model with specific "light unloading" phases prior to testing or competition peaks. This is critical for managing fatigue, particularly given the athlete's night shift schedule and chronic back pain.
*   **Endurance vs. Power:** Explicitly states that long-distance running is incompatible with significant Olympic lifting progress; the athlete must prioritize explosive power development over aerobic capacity during this transition.
*   **Nutrition & Recovery:** Emphasizes a balanced diet (protein + moderate carbs/fats) to support hypertrophy without excessive fat gain. Consistent warm-ups are non-negotiable, even when fatigued or sleep-deprived, to prevent injury and ensure joint stability.

## Technique Cues
*   **The Pull (No Arm Pulling):** Do not "pull up" with the arms after the first pull phase. The bar must fly freely on its own momentum ("free fall"). Power is generated by leg drive and trapezius engagement; arm pulling to the chest/neck kills speed and increases spinal shear force.
*   **The Catch (Proprioception):** Do not visually track the bar during the descent. Rely on proprioception ("feeling") to drop into the squat position. This trains consistent depth without visual crutches, essential for stability under fatigue.
*   **Dead Stop Execution:** Practice starts from a dead stop (full foot contact) and pauses at the bottom ("the hole"). This eliminates the stretch-shortening cycle, forcing power generation from zero speed and ensuring full-foot stability upon landing.
*   **The Drive (Explosive Speed):** Emphasize an explosive "shove" in the second pull. Any deceleration during the catch indicates a failure to maintain bar momentum; the athlete must drive through the floor to keep the bar flying.

## Programming Principles
*   **Exercise Selection:**
    *   **Snatch/Clean Pulls & High Pulls:** Focus on allowing the bar to fly freely without arm assistance.
    *   **Paused Squats:** Incorporate pauses at the bottom or in the hole to isolate and strengthen weak points (specifically addressing the OHS weakness).
    *   **Plyometrics:** Jumping exercises from a squat position mimicking the final drive, performed on full feet for stability.
    *   **Wall/Partner Drills:** Use static resistance to practice the "shove" mechanics without bar displacement.
*   **Volume/Intensity Management:** Structure sessions around speed and technique (moderate load, high reps) rather than max effort. Implement planned unloading weeks to mitigate cumulative fatigue from night shift work.
*   **Specific Adaptation:** For a Push Jerk specialist, the "let the bar fly" cue is vital: ensure the legs generate all necessary vertical velocity before the arms engage in the jerk phase.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The emphasis on "dead stop" starts and strict bar path mechanics (no arm pulling) reduces shear forces on the spine compared to sloppy, momentum-dependent lifts. Joint stability cues are prioritized over raw load.
*   **Night Shift & Recovery:** The protocol's inclusion of mandatory unloading phases is essential for managing fatigue from irregular sleep cycles. The focus on proprioception over visual cues helps maintain technique consistency despite potential cognitive fatigue.
*   **Transition to Olympic Lifting:** Directly addresses the "running vs. lifting" conflict, validating the need to drop endurance training to maximize power output.
*   **Hypertrophy Block Alignment:** The moderate-weight, high-repetition approach combined with specific nutrition advice supports the 6-week hypertrophy goal while building the technical foundation required for the subsequent technique/strength phase.
