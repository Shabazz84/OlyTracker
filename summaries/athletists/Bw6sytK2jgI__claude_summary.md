## Key Points
- Intensity ≥90% required for heavy singles; mental readiness = physical readiness
- Don't follow rigid programming templates—adapt individually based on athlete response
- Volume (tonnage) becomes meaningless with complex exercises; prioritize effort/intensity instead
- Cumulative fatigue across sessions requires active management; heavy snatch work depletes energy reserves
- Psychological fatigue matters as much as physical; recovery timelines vary (1–2+ days depending on readiness)
- Session duration: ~90min optimal; excessive warm-ups (40+ min) counterproductive

## Technique Cues
- **Snatch pull (Yarkin method)**: Partial pulls 5cm above/below knee with pauses, explosive hip extension only at final knee angle—don't initiate hip drive early, stay over bar longer
- **Clean rack position**: Drive elbows up early; pause at chest in front squat catch position to build depth/stability
- **Front squat**: 1–2 second pauses at bottom to address dead-point weakness (critical for heavy clean catches)
- **Pulling mechanics**: Stay over bar longer before explosive drive phase

## Programming Principles
- Experiment with exercise variety; rigid formulas undermine individual adaptation
- Track effort/intensity for complex movements rather than raw tonnage counts
- Manage cumulative fatigue across mesocycle—heavy pulling sessions deplete resources for subsequent lifts
- Flexible recovery protocols aligned with athlete schedule (suits night shift work)

## Athlete Relevance
- **OHS limiter (50kg)**: Front squat pauses build rack stability → transfers to snatch overhead position
- **Chronic back pain**: Front squat + controlled pull work safer than heavy back squat emphasis
- **Night shift (Wed–Sun)**: Flexible intensity/recovery windows better than fixed prescriptions
- **Hypertrophy block**: Pause front squats + pull variations are metabolically demanding
