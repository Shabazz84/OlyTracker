# Coaching Philosophy: The "Stable Foundation" Protocol

### 1. Core Training Philosophy
*   **Speed Over Load:** For an intermediate athlete transitioning from powerlifting, speed is the primary driver of Olympic success. Heavy, slow pulls degrade bar path and increase spinal shear force. Prioritize explosive intent with sub-maximal loads (70–85%) to ingrain motor patterns before increasing intensity.
*   **The "Muscular Corset" Approach:** Chronic back pain must be managed by strengthening the stabilizing musculature through controlled, high-volume work rather than avoidance or total rest. Strength training acts as rehabilitation; however, spinal compression is minimized by prioritizing technique and avoiding "crashing" the bar.
*   **Self-Regulation over Rigid Percentages:** Given the night shift schedule (Wed–Sun) and irregular recovery windows, daily intensity must be dictated by CNS status ("feel your body") rather than a fixed calendar. If fatigue or pain flares, reduce load immediately; do not force PRs on compromised days.
*   **Push Jerk Specificity:** Since split jerks are excluded, the athlete must maximize leg drive and upper-body stability. The lift is driven by the legs; arms only stabilize. Failure to drop under the bar quickly ("overtake gravity") will stall progress at 65kg.

### 2. Programming Approach
*   **Structure:** A 6-week hypertrophy block followed by a technique/strength transition.
    *   **Weeks 1–3 (Hypertrophy/Technique):** High volume, low intensity (<70% 1RM). Focus on "drag" mechanics, bar proximity, and overhead stability. Use pauses to build spinal tension.
    *   **Weeks 4–6 (Transition):** Introduce moderate loads (75–85%) with increased speed emphasis. Begin eccentric loading for leg strength without high systemic fatigue.
*   **Frequency:** High-frequency technical practice (2–3 sessions/week) mixed with heavy squat days, adjusted based on daily recovery.
*   **Intensity Management:** Never train to total exhaustion ("washed out"). Stop sets while feeling strong to preserve the CNS and prevent injury. Use "rep reserves" to maintain quality.

### 3. Key Technique Cues & Exercise Preferences
*   **Pull Mechanics (The Drag):** Keep the bar in contact with the body throughout the pull ("drag" sensation). Avoid locking elbows early or pulling them back prematurely, which creates distance between the bar and the center of gravity.
*   **Spinal Alignment:** Maintain a neutral spine ("covered back") during all pulls. Do not "throw" the back backward or lean excessively early in the movement. Use a "tie a knot" cue to ensure torso rigidity.
*   **The Dip & Drop (Push Jerk):** Execute a controlled dip with hips back; do not let the torso fall forward. The athlete must drop under the bar immediately as it begins its descent, rather than waiting for peak height. This reduces impact shock on the spine.
*   **Overhead Stability:** For the OHS limiter (50kg), focus on active shoulder engagement and thoracic extension. "Meet" the bar on the toes to force ankle mobility without straining the back.
*   **Exercise Preferences:**
    *   **Block Pulls:** Use elevated platforms to isolate hip extension and prevent rounding, but revert to floor pulls if pain flares.
    *   **Paused Squats/Overhead Holds:** 2–3 second pauses at the bottom of squats or overhead positions to build stability and depth tolerance.
    *   **Eccentric Loading:** Heavy eccentric squats (slow descent with assistance) to build leg strength without high CNS fatigue.

### 4. Recovery and Mobility Approach
*   **Thermal Regulation:** Night shift workers are prone to cold drafts affecting recovery. Wear layers, cover joints (knees/elbows), and use heating pads/liniments *before* warming up to ensure internal readiness. Cold environments increase injury risk.
*   **Preventative Mobility:** Address mobility limitations (shoulders/ankles) *before* symptoms appear. Do not wait for pain to stretch; consistent, light mobility work is required to maintain range of motion under load.
*   **Sleep & Stress Management:** Acknowledge that night shift disruption impacts recovery. Prioritize sleep quality and psychological relaxation over rigid training targets on low-energy days. Use rest days for socializing or passive recovery (sauna/bath) if physical fatigue is high.

### 5. Application to the Athlete Profile
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The "paused pull" and "drag" cues are critical to prevent spinal shear. Heavy front squats should be substituted with rack pulls or chest-supported variations to reduce joint stress while maintaining posterior chain strength. If pain flares, switch to light pulls from a stool or box squats rather than stopping entirely.
*   **Push Jerk Only:** The athlete must focus on the "drop under" mechanic and leg drive to compensate for the lack of split mechanics. Heavy overhead holds (static) will build lockout strength without requiring split flexibility.
*   **Night Shift Work (Wed–Sun):** Training intensity must be self-regulated daily. Avoid high-intensity work immediately following shifts when recovery is compromised. Use video analysis for feedback instead of relying on live coaching, as night hours limit real-time supervision.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** The focus on "meeting the bar on toes" and active thoracic extension addresses the mobility bottleneck directly. Do not force weight overhead with a compromised posture; build tissue tolerance first via high-volume, low-intensity drills.
*   **Hypertrophy Transition:** The 6-week block prioritizes building the "muscular corset" through controlled volume rather than heavy maxing. This builds the necessary structural integrity to handle Olympic lifts safely before transitioning to higher intensities.
