# Тяжелая атлетика - это не только сила и взрыв

## Key Points
*   **Technique Over Raw Strength:** High squat numbers (118 kg) do not guarantee successful lifts if shoulder mobility is poor; the 50 kg OHS limit indicates a need for overhead mobility work over pure strength accumulation.
*   **Fatigue Management:** Do not train at maximum intensity when fixing technical errors or when fatigued. If form breaks, drop weight to a "borderline" level where technique can be maintained without masking errors with momentum.
*   **Injury & Pain Protocol:** Stop immediately if stiffness or pain occurs (especially from jumping drills). Micro-tears and chronic back pain require 1–2 weeks of reduced volume or rest rather than pushing through; high-volume pulling can exacerbate lumbar strain.
*   **Nutrition Timing:** Avoid training with a full stomach (heavy carbs like pasta); consume light carbs (chocolate/coffee) ~1 hour pre-lift to avoid digestive heaviness while maintaining energy.

## Technique Cues
*   **The Pull (Clean/Snatch):** Drive the bar using legs and hips; strictly avoid "throwing the back backward" or over-rotating the lumbar spine during the transition. The key is a continuous pull: after the explosive dip, maintain arm tension without letting go of the bar.
*   **Timing:** Catch timing depends on the transition from dip to pull. Pulling too early or late prevents catching the bar high enough.
*   **Jerk (Push Jerk Focus):** For lower-level athletes, focus on coordination between the "pull to chest" and the "clean with dip." Ensure shoulders are mobile enough to move at speed; static stretching is insufficient—use dynamic mobility drills before loading.
*   **Elbow Position:** Monitor for lagging elbows (e.g., one arm trailing), which restricts amplitude under load due to muscle tightening.

## Programming Principles
*   **Drill Selection:** Prioritize dynamic combinations over heavy pulling. Use specific coordination drills like "Pulls to Chest with Dip" (протяжка с подрывом) and sequences of *stretch → jerk → stretch → jerk*.
*   **Volume Management:** High-volume coordination drills (e.g., 500 pulls) are useful for motor learning but must be avoided when fatigued or injured. Reduce volume immediately upon feeling "stiff."
*   **Periodization Logic:** Balance strength development with mobility work. A hypertrophy block should include significant overhead mobility to address the OHS limiter before attempting heavy technical loads.
*   **Intensity Regulation:** Work up to 85–90% for technical work, but prioritize completing sets cleanly over forcing heavy singles.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The cue "do not throw the back backward" is critical for preventing lumbar strain. Avoid jumping drills (double-unders) which cause micro-tears and stiffness that exacerbate back pain; rest 1–2 weeks if symptoms appear.
*   **Night Shift Work:** Fatigue management is paramount. Do not attempt high-intensity technical work or heavy singles when sleep-deprived. If fatigue sets in, drop to "borderline" weights to maintain quality over quantity.
*   **Transitioning Athlete (Powerlifting to Olympic):** The profile of a strong lifter with poor overhead mobility (118 kg squat vs. 50 kg OHS) validates the need for a technique-focused block. Pure strength will not fix the snatch/jerk mechanics; focus on coordination drills and dynamic mobility rather than just pulling heavy weights.
*   **Push Jerk Specifics:** Since the athlete uses only push jerks, the coaching emphasis on "pull to chest" vs. "clean with dip" coordination is directly applicable to refining the dip-to-jerk transition without relying on split mechanics.
