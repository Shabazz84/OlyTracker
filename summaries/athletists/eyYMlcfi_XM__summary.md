# Китайская тяга и рывковая тренировка по Яркину. Тяжелая атлетика

## Key Points
*   **Drill Focus:** The "Chinese Pull" (Китайская тяга) is a high-priority technical drill designed to eliminate the "double movement" (pulling up, pausing, then dropping). It forces an immediate transition from extension to the drop under.
*   **Mechanism:** Success relies on stopping active arm lifting after the first pull/extension. The arms act as hooks; the hips must aggressively drop while the body falls rapidly under the bar.
*   **Programming Structure:** High-intensity technical drills (100–120% of snatch weight) should be integrated into main workouts to correct specific flaws, rather than isolated in separate sessions.
*   **Critical Constraints:** The source material describes a specialized camp environment (2x/day, 6 days/week). This volume is incompatible with an intermediate athlete's recovery capacity, night shift schedule, and chronic back pain. The *principle* of focused drilling must be applied at a significantly reduced frequency (1 session/day or 3–4 days/week) to prevent injury and burnout.

## Technique Cues
*   **"Drop Under" Priority:** Do not pull the bar up with your hands after extension. Focus entirely on how fast your body can fall under the bar.
*   **Arm Action:** Arms must remain passive "hooks." If you actively try to lift the bar high with your hands, you will fail the transition.
*   **Eliminating the Pause:** The drill specifically targets the habit of pulling up and pausing before dropping. The movement from extension to catch position must be continuous and explosive.
*   **Progression Integration:** To transfer the sensation, pair one rep of the Chinese Pull immediately with a full Snatch or Clean & Jerk in the same set.

## Programming Principles
*   **Intensity vs. Volume:** Use high intensity (near-maximal load) for very low volume (technical reps only). This is not a hypertrophy exercise; it is a neural reprogramming tool.
*   **Individualized Integration:** Introduce this drill only when a specific technical flaw (e.g., double movement, slow drop) is identified. Do not use as a general warm-up.
*   **Load Management for Back Pain:** Due to chronic back pain and the high spinal loading required in the transition phase of the Chinese Pull, modify the load significantly. Prioritize body mechanics over heavy weight until pain stabilizes.
*   **Schedule Adaptation:** Replace the source's "camp" model with a sustainable routine: 3–4 days/week, single session per day, prioritizing sleep recovery for night shift workers.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Transition to Split Jerk:** The athlete currently uses only a push jerk due to a lack of split technique or confidence. Mastering the "drop under" speed is a prerequisite for developing the rapid turnover required for a split jerk.
*   **Back Pain Management:** The high-intensity nature of the Chinese Pull poses a risk for an athlete with chronic back pain. It must be introduced cautiously, potentially starting with lighter loads or variations from the knee to isolate mechanics before loading the spine heavily.
*   **OHS Limitation:** While this drill targets the pull-to-catch transition, the athlete's primary limiter is the Overhead Squat (50 kg). The speed gained here will help stabilize the bar overhead faster, but dedicated OHS volume remains necessary for the 6-week hypertrophy block.
*   **Recovery Reality:** The source's advice on high-frequency training must be discarded in favor of a schedule that accommodates night shift sleep cycles and allows for adequate recovery to manage back pain.
