# Рывковая с утра, толчковая - вечером. Это сборы, детка

## Key Points
*   **Balance of Strength and Technique:** Do not eliminate Olympic lifts (Snatch/Clean) even when prioritizing leg strength; a "golden mean" is required to maintain explosive speed and muscle memory. Dropping technical work leads to stagnation despite leg growth.
*   **Intensity Management for Leg Focus:** When prioritizing heavy squats, reduce the intensity of Olympic lifts (e.g., pull at 80%) to preserve CNS energy and explosive speed without exhaustion.
*   **Eccentric Loading Strategy:** For plateaus in leg strength, incorporate heavy eccentric loading (holding max weight and lowering slowly with assistance) rather than standard high-rep sets. This builds bottom-position strength while minimizing systemic fatigue.
*   **Thermal Regulation & Recovery:** Cold environments (-10°C to -20°C) are detrimental; cold drafts during rest cause illness and injury risk. Athletes must wear multiple layers, cover joints (knees/elbows), and use heating pads/liniments *before* warming up to ensure internal readiness.
*   **Push Jerk Specificity:** Athletes often excel at one lift but struggle with the other; specific focus is required on the transition from Clean to Jerk if the push jerk is the primary limiter, as the mechanics differ significantly from a split jerk.

## Technique Cues
*   **"Covered Back" Alignment:** Maintain the spine directly over the bar ("spine covers") during pulls. This creates the most stable position for power generation and mitigates lower back strain by preventing rounding or hyperextension.
*   **Continuous Bar Path (High Pulls):** In the final phase above the knees, do not stop or pause. Keep the bar moving continuously to maintain momentum; stopping loses explosive potential.
*   **Grip Width Adjustment:** Use a slightly wider grip during high pulls to naturally encourage elbows to drop and the back to cover the bar, preventing the "throwing back" error (torso leaning too far back) that exacerbates chronic pain.
*   **Active Descent Under Bar:** Do not passively "drop" under the bar; actively pull yourself under while staying tight. Waiting too long causes the bar to fall on the athlete ("plunk"), increasing injury risk and reducing efficiency.
*   **Leg-Driven Pulls:** Keep high pulls short and explosive; engage legs immediately rather than pulling with arms alone.

## Programming Principles
*   **Session Structure:** Follow a progression of light warm-up -> technical sessions (80% intensity) -> strength session, or test placing squats at the *beginning* only if technique is stable but physical resources are low.
*   **Exercise Order Flexibility:** Traditionally, lifts precede squats to ensure fresh legs for technique; however, if leg strength is the primary limiter and technique is solid, heavy squats can be moved earlier in the session.
*   **Periodization Logic:** When entering a hypertrophy/strength block (6 weeks), reduce Olympic lift volume/intensity slightly to accommodate heavy leg work, but never eliminate technical practice entirely.
*   **Recovery Protocol for Night Shifts:** Prioritize thermal regulation strictly due to compromised recovery schedules; ensure the body is fully heated before heavy work to avoid injury from drafts common in night shift environments (e.g., cold gyms or post-shift exposure).

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain Mitigation:** The "covered back" cue and emphasis on spinal alignment directly address chronic pain by shifting load from the lumbar spine to a stable, neutral position during pulls. Avoiding the "throwing back" error is critical for this athlete's profile.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation (OHS 50kg):** Since the athlete uses only a push jerk and has a low Overhead Squat, programming must focus on the specific transition mechanics from Clean to Push Jerk rather than split-jerk variations. The "active descent" cue is vital for stabilizing under the bar without relying on leg drive that exceeds current OHS capacity.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** The strict thermal regulation advice (layers, pre-warm liniments) is essential for a night shift worker where immune function and body temperature regulation may be compromised, reducing injury risk during heavy eccentric squats or high-intensity pulls.
*   **Strength vs. Hypertrophy Transition:** The recommendation to use eccentric loading allows the athlete to build leg strength (118kg squat -> higher) without the high systemic fatigue of standard heavy sets, which is crucial for an intermediate athlete managing a 6-week block while working nights.
