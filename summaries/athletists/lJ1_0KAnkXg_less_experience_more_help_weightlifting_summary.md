# Less experience - more help. Weightlifting

## Key Points
*   **Hypertrophy & Strength Integration:** The 6-week block should prioritize accumulating stress through heavy loading, slow eccentric tempos, and isometric holds at specific "dead points" (weak ranges) to build muscle mass while fixing technical flaws.
*   **Back Pain Management:** Chronic pain requires strict adherence to pre-tension (engaging the core/back before movement initiation) and avoiding "relaxed" starts which cause sudden spinal loading. The catch phase must use knees as shock absorbers, not the lumbar spine.
*   **Training Frequency & Adaptation:** High-frequency practice is needed for technique, but variety ("shakes") in exercise selection prevents adaptation plateaus. For an intermediate transitioning to Olympic lifting, focus shifts from pure strength (powerlifting) to coordination and "feeling" the movement.
*   **Recovery Constraints:** Ligaments recover twice as slowly as muscles; avoid high-impact trauma (dropping heavy weights from blocks). Active recovery (walking) is beneficial, but excessive manual labor or fatigue must be managed around night shift schedules.

## Technique Cues
*   **The Pull:** Execute a simultaneous extension of the spine and legs ("straighten back and legs together"). Do not let one lead; focus on "pulling with the back" while driving with legs to maintain a vertical bar path.
*   **The Catch (Snatch/Clean):** Use the knee joint as a "shock absorber" (like car suspension) rather than taking impact on the full foot immediately. Crucially, avoid leaning the spine backward during the catch; keep the torso upright to protect the lumbar region.
*   **Grip & Upper Back:** Do not grip excessively wide if it forces upper back tension or compromises lat engagement. The goal is an engaged lats/spine independent of arm tension. Use straps for snatch drills to eliminate grip fatigue and focus on speed/fixation.
*   **Squat Mechanics:** Perform squats with full amplitude (deep) rather than partial reps. Utilize the "dead point" drill: pause statically at the specific depth where you stall to build stability before driving up dynamically. Maintain full foot contact during the initial pull; do not rise onto toes prematurely.

## Programming Principles
*   **Exercise Selection:** Prioritize variations that target weak points without excessive spinal shear. Recommended drills include "Snatch from Blocks" (start in partial squat, do not fully stand first) and slow squats with pauses at the bottom.
*   **Intensity & Volume:** Use sub-maximal weights for technical drills (e.g., 105kg when max is lower) to focus on speed and fixation rather than strength grinding. For hypertrophy, combine heavy loads with low-volume explosive work (3 sets of 6 reps) at moderate loads (30–40kg) to fatigue legs without spinal overload.
*   **Progression:** Move from basic pulls to complex variations (e.g., high blocks) only after mastering the vertical bar path and "pull with back" sensation. As technique stabilizes, decrease accessory volume to conserve energy for main lifts.
*   **Scheduling:** Leverage online coaching flexibility to train whenever access is available, accommodating night shift irregularities. Ensure warm-ups include pre-tensioning muscles before any heavy set to prevent injury from sudden recruitment.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The cues to use knees as shock absorbers and avoid spinal leaning during the catch directly reduce shear force on the lumbar spine. Pre-tension protocols are essential for night shift workers who may have reduced neuromuscular readiness, preventing injury from relaxed starts.
*   **Push Jerk Only (No Split):** The technique of "pulling with back" and maintaining a vertical bar path applies to the push jerk transition. The advice on grip width prevents the bar from drifting forward, which is a common cause of lower back strain in push jerks.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** To address the overhead squat weakness, practice "sitting with it" (holding the bar overhead in a squat/standing position) to build stability and confidence before adding load, rather than forcing heavy weights immediately.
*   **Night Shift Worker:** The emphasis on active recovery (walking) and avoiding high-impact trauma is critical given irregular sleep windows. The flexibility of training timing allows for consistent practice without the stress of rigid gym hours.
*   **Intermediate Transition:** The psychological aspect of overcoming "fear of dropping" or "fear of weight" is addressed by using straps during drills to focus on mechanics rather than grip failure, facilitating a smoother transition from powerlifting strength to Olympic coordination.
