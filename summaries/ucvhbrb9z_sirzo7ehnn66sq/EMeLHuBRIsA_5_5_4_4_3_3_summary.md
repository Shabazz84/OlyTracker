# "5  5", "4  4", "3  3",

## Key Points
*   **Warm-up Protocol (Low CNS):** For night shift training, never start with heavy singles/doubles. Extend warm-up duration significantly and include light cardio (treadmill/orbitrek) to raise heart rate and blood pressure before barbell contact.
*   **Rep Scheme Selection:** 
    *   **3x3:** Prioritizes strength recruitment; less hypertrophy.
    *   **4x5 (or 4x4):** Superior for natural athletes seeking a balance of muscle growth and strength; creates necessary metabolic stress without excessive CNS burnout compared to pure heavy lifting.
*   **Progression Model:** Avoid rigid linear progression ("schemes for apes"). Utilize a **wave loading model** with built-in reserves (RPE). Cycle between heavier sessions (e.g., 85% intensity) and lighter volume sessions (75% intensity, higher reps) to manage fatigue.
*   **Rest Periods:** Do not use strict timers. Rest until heart rate normalizes and subjective readiness is achieved ("until I am ready").
*   **Injury Etiology:** Joint failure stems primarily from poor mobility and bad technique rather than heavy loads alone. Strengthening stabilizers (rotator cuff, core) via isolation work is critical for injury prevention.

## Programming Principles
*   **CNS Management for Night Shifts:** Acknowledge fluctuating sleep cycles. Do not force training when fatigued; adjust intensity based on subjective readiness rather than a fixed schedule.
*   **Wave Loading Structure:** 
    *   Start sessions at 75–80% of working weight (3x3 or 4x4).
    *   Progress to 85% later in the cycle.
    *   Avoid hitting max effort on every set; maintain a reserve to prevent CNS burnout and back aggravation.
*   **Hypertrophy Block Strategy:** Utilize the 4x5 scheme or higher rep ranges (e.g., 10 reps at lower intensity) to maximize muscle growth while minimizing excessive systemic fatigue, aligning with your goal of a 6-week hypertrophy phase before technique/strength work.
*   **Accessory Integration:** Perform isolation work (e.g., weighted pull-ups, French presses, bicep curls) *after* main lifts to manage volume without overtaxing the CNS.
