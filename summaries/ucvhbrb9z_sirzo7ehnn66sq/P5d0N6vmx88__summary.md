## Key Points
*   **Flexible Periodization:** Utilize a skeletal "base plan" (e.g., 5x3) but modulate intensity daily based on subjective feel ("training by sensation"). Progress to higher percentages only if the body responds positively; reduce volume immediately upon fatigue or pain.
*   **Data-Driven Logging:** Mandatory recording of exact weights, sets, and reps (including on-the-fly adjustments) is required to track progression and identify stagnation causes.
*   **Volume for Neural Adaptation:** High repetition at moderate loads (e.g., 500 total lifts) is emphasized over heavy singles to build neural pathways and technical proficiency.
*   **Injury Prevention:** Avoid high-risk isolation movements (specifically Leg Extensions) that compromise joint integrity; prioritize training efficiency by limiting main exercises to ~4 per session.

## Technique Cues
*   **Neural Familiarity:** "The more you repeat the target movement, the better you feel it." Prioritize frequent technical practice at moderate weights over maximal loading to refine motor patterns.
*   **Pain Monitoring:** Do not train through pain or excessive fatigue; if a specific lift feels "bad" or causes discomfort (critical for chronic back pain), reduce intensity or stop immediately rather than forcing the movement.

## Programming Principles
*   **Daily Modulation:** Adjust daily load based on recovery status (e.g., shift from 140kg singles to 135kg triples if fatigued) rather than rigidly adhering to a pre-set percentage.
*   **Session Structure:** Limit main compound movements to approximately four per session to ensure efficiency for athletes with busy schedules (night shifts).
*   **Accessory Flexibility:** Accessory work is secondary; skip non-essential accessories (e.g., shoulder swings) if the primary session becomes too draining or time-constrained.
*   **Consistency over Intensity:** Acknowledge that serious progress requires managing work/life balance, but "dabbling" yields no results; consistent, efficient training is key for night shift workers.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Transition Strategy:** The "training by feel" approach suits an intermediate powerlifter transitioning to Olympic lifting, as it helps the athlete recalibrate their perception of daily capacity and recovery needs without relying solely on rigid percentages.
*   **Night Shift Adaptation:** The protocol supports a Wed–Sun shift worker by emphasizing efficiency (max 4 main lifts) and flexibility, allowing training adjustments based on sleep quality and circadian rhythm fluctuations.
*   **Pain Management:** While the text lacks specific back pain protocols, its core principle of "stopping if something hurts" is vital for an athlete with chronic back issues to prevent exacerbation during a hypertrophy block.
*   **Critical Gaps:** This framework does not address the specific technical deficits (OHS weakness, push-jerk limitation) or provide modifications for the 102.5 kg bodyweight class; it serves as a management layer rather than a technical solution for these specific biomechanical issues.
