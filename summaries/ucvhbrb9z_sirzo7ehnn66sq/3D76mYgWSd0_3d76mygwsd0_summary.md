## Key Points
*   **Specificity of Strength:** Neural adaptations are movement-specific; high back squat or bench press numbers do not directly translate to a higher Clean & Jerk without specific Olympic volume. The athlete must prioritize the actual movements (Snatch, Clean, Jerk) to improve them.
*   **Hypertrophy vs. Neural Adaptation:** Muscle growth requires working near technical failure with metabolic stress (e.g., 4 sets of 6 reps), while neural recruitment requires high-intensity sub-maximal efforts (85–90%+). These should be prioritized in separate sessions or distinct blocks rather than mixed in a single heavy set.
*   **Injury Management:** The strict rule is to stop immediately if spinal integrity (form) degrades, regardless of strength potential. Training through pain or "rounding the spine" is contraindicated, especially for an athlete with chronic back pain.
*   **Fatigue & Recovery:** High training frequency ("many repetitions") can be counterproductive without load management. For athletes >100kg or those under high stress (night shifts), a 10-day cycle may be preferable to weekly heavy loading to manage CNS fatigue and allow for recovery of the spine.

## Technique Cues
*   **Primary Cue:** "Constant repetition of the target movement" is the foundation; refine technique through high-repetition "washing out" rather than just lifting max loads.
*   **Failure Definition:** Train to **technical failure** (where form begins to degrade), not absolute muscular failure. If spinal rounding occurs, the load is too heavy for that session—stop immediately.
*   **Pressing Mechanics:** For the Push Jerk, retract scapulae, create a "bridge" with the torso, and drive legs into the floor before extending arms.
*   **Movement Order:** Perform squats or presses first (light/medium intensity) to warm up joints, then perform heavy pulling or specific Olympic lifts later in the session to preserve spinal integrity during high-load attempts.

## Programming Principles
*   **Block Structure:** Execute a 6-week hypertrophy block followed by technique/strength work. During the hypertrophy phase, prioritize volume (e.g., 3–4 sets of 4–8 reps at 70–85% intensity) to build cross-sectional area without excessive neural fatigue.
*   **Session Design:** Separate high-threshold neural work from metabolic stress. Either schedule a "Heavy/Neural" day and a "Hypertrophy/Metabolic" day separately, or structure sessions as: Heavy main lift (neural focus) followed by lighter accessory volume (metabolic focus).
*   **Frequency & Load Management:** Given the athlete's 102.5 kg bodyweight and night shift schedule, avoid weekly max attempts on heavy compound lifts. Utilize a "Max Single" followed immediately by a "Repetition Set near failure" (e.g., 130kg x 1, then 125kg x 4–6 reps) to balance drive and tissue adaptation.
*   **Accessory Integration:** Use pulling exercises (Lat Pulldowns) after pressing volume to allow prime movers to recover while maintaining training density.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Transition Strategy:** The athlete's intermediate strength in Squat/Bench will not boost their 80kg Clean without specific Olympic lifting volume. The program must shift focus from general strength to specific neural patterns for the Clean and Jerk.
*   **Back Pain & Night Shifts:** The "stop if form breaks" rule is critical for managing chronic back pain. The night shift schedule (Wed–Sun) necessitates strict load management; the athlete should use a single max attempt as a "control point" to gauge daily readiness before heavy lifting, rather than assuming baseline strength is constant.
*   **Jerk Constraint:** Programming must accommodate the **Push Jerk only** constraint. Focus pressing volume on scapular retraction and leg drive mechanics specific to the push jerk, avoiding split-jerk cues entirely.
*   **OHS Limitation:** The 50kg Overhead Squat is a primary limiter; the hypertrophy block should prioritize shoulder stability and overhead mobility (via Dips/Pressing) to support this lift without compromising spinal safety.
