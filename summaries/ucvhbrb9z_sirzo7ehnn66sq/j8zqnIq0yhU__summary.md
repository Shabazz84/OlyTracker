## Key Points
*   **Periodization vs. Cycling:** Distinct from simple linear progression; requires distinct "blocks" with specific goals (Hypertrophy vs. Strength) rather than random weight fluctuation. Weekly training must utilize "cycling" where load parameters change drastically (e.g., shifting from 6x20 to 5x4) based on the **Funtikov-Bondarenko Intensity Formula** ($F + B$), not just raw tonnage.
*   **Block Structure:** Adopt an 8-week model (adaptable to your 6-week goal):
    *   **Phase A (Hypertrophy/Resilience):** Focus on tissue adaptation for chronic back pain. Use moderate loads with higher intensity scores rather than pure metabolic conditioning (avoiding "warm-up" style high-rep sets like 8x20).
    *   **Phase B (Strength/Intensity):** Reduce reps to 5–6, increase load, and focus on heavy compound lifts.
*   **Load Management:** Do not repeat the same weight/reps consecutively ("passing one level in a game"). Adjust volume or intensity weekly based on recovery status. For night shift workers with back pain, prioritize reducing set counts over pushing through fatigue to manage spinal compression stress.

## Technique Cues
*   **General Intent:** Treat every set with specific intent (volume vs. intensity); avoid "dilettant" random training where the goal is undefined.
*   **Progression Safety:** Do not start basic exercises with singles; begin with 2x2 or 3x3 to establish motor patterns before heavy loading.
*   **Jerk Specifics:** Since the athlete utilizes a **Push Jerk only**, periodization must target shoulder endurance and stability without introducing split-jerk mechanics that could exacerbate lower back issues.

## Programming Principles
*   **Fundamental vs. Supportive:** Identify one "fundamental" exercise (e.g., Back Squat or Clean) to cycle primarily using the intensity formula, while auxiliary movements support this weekly total.
*   **Coefficient Application:** Apply specific stress coefficients for different lifts; a Push Jerk has a distinct neurological and spinal demand compared to a Split Jerk or Squat, requiring tailored load management within the same block.
*   **Recovery Adaptation:** If high-volume "Smaev-style" training (high reps, low intensity) compromises recovery due to night shifts or back pain, reduce the number of sets rather than increasing frequency or pushing through fatigue.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain & Night Shifts:** The athlete must avoid blindly following high-volume bodybuilding styles if recovery is compromised. Use the Funtikov formula to maintain neurological drive (high intensity) while manipulating volume/reps to minimize spinal compression during the hypertrophy block.
*   **Transition Strategy:** The 6-week plan validates distinct periods: Weeks 1–3 focus on building tissue resilience for the back; Weeks 4–6 shift to strength/intensity. This prevents stagnation and manages fatigue for a Wed–Sun night shift schedule.
*   **Limiters:** Address the OHS (50 kg) as a primary limiter by ensuring the hypertrophy block builds shoulder endurance without overloading the spine, specifically tailored for the Push Jerk limitation.
