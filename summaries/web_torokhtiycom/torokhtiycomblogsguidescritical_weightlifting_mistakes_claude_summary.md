# Analysis: Common Weightlifting Mistakes

## Relevance to Athlete Profile
**HIGH** – This article targets intermediate lifters, matching this athlete's level. Several points directly apply.

---

## 1. PROGRAMMING PRINCIPLES
- **Progressive Overload**: Increase weight, reps, or sets gradually—avoid plateaus
- **Volume vs. Quality**: Don't sacrifice form for higher volume (critical for this athlete's back pain)
- **Exercise Variation**: Periodically rotate exercises to avoid plateaus and imbalances
- **Recovery**: Adequate rest between sessions essential; overtraining increases injury risk

**Relevance**: 6-week hypertrophy block should prioritize quality reps with controlled progression to manage chronic back pain.

---

## 2. TECHNIQUE CUES
- None sport-specific provided (article is general, not snatch/clean/jerk focused)

---

## 3. EXERCISE RECOMMENDATIONS
- **Avoid machine-only training**; prioritize free weights and functional movements
- **Include mobility/flexibility work** regularly (stretching, foam rolling)
- **Warm-up** (5–10 min) before lifting; cool-down stretching after
- **Balanced training**: Don't overemphasize favorite lifts; address muscle imbalances

**Relevance to Athlete**: 
- OHS (50 kg) is limiting snatch—needs targeted mobility + accessory work
- Back pain requires balanced programming (avoid overloading spine)
- Night shift (Wed–Sun) requires strategic recovery prioritization

---

## 4. RECOVERY & MOBILITY ADVICE
- Regular mobility exercises, stretching, foam rolling maintain joint health
- Adequate sleep and rest critical for neurological recovery
- **Protein intake**: 1–1.5g per lb bodyweight daily (athlete: ~102.5 kg = ~227 lbs = 227–340g protein/day)
- Proper nutrition timing around workouts

**Relevance**: Night shift worker—prioritize sleep quality and pre-workout meal timing; consider mobility work during work breaks.

---

## 5. ATHLETE-SPECIFIC RED FLAGS
- ⚠️ **Back pain + heavy loading**: Avoid ego lifting; quality > load
- ⚠️ **Low OHS (50 kg)**: Indicates mobility/stability deficit—needs dedicated work before heavy jerks
- ⚠️ **Push jerk only**: Split jerk develops glute/leg drive; address before strength block

---

## KEY TAKEAWAY
Avoid overtraining during hypertrophy block. Prioritize form, mobility work, and balanced programming to manage back pain and address weak OHS before transition to technique/strength phase.
