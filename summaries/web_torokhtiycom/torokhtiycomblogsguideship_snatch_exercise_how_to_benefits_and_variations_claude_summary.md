# Hip Snatch Analysis for Your Athlete Profile

## **Relevance to Athlete: HIGH**
This exercise directly addresses your athlete's primary snatch limiter (OHS at 50 kg) and chronic back pain.

---

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Intensity**: 60–75% of snatch 1RM (athlete: ~48–60 kg)
- **Volume**: Intermediate = 4–5×3; Advanced = 5×2–3
- **Frequency**: Suitable for hypertrophy block (can be done 2–3×/week)
- **Advantage**: Removes floor pull complexity—ideal for technique refinement before strength phase

---

## 2. **Technique Cues (Snatch-Specific)**
- **Hip contact**: Precise, controlled contact at full extension (not early/soft)
- **Arm mechanics**: Arms *guide*, don't pull; direct elbows up; let legs create power first
- **Bar path**: Keep bar close to body; engage lats/t-spine to prevent drift
- **Turnover**: Aggressive, immediate "pull and drop under"—don't hesitate
- **Overhead lockout**: Lock elbows, engage traps, "grow tall" into catch; reach shoulders to ears

---

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
**Direct application:**
- **Hip Snatch**: 4–5×3 @ 50–55 kg (60–70% of current 80 kg clean snatch)
- **Hip Power Snatch**: Catches above parallel—reduces deep squat demand (benefits OHS mobility deficit)
- **Hip Muscle Snatch**: Removes squat; forces pure extension + shoulder drive (overhead strength builder)
- **Hip Snatch Pull**: Reinforces hip contact without overhead demand

**Why valuable for this athlete:**
- OHS bottleneck (50 kg) = catching/overhead weakness, not hip extension
- Hip variations isolate turnover speed + bar control without deep squat mobility demand
- Low spine load vs. full snatch (relevant: chronic back pain)

---

## 4. **Recovery & Mobility Advice**
- Text emphasizes hip snatch has **lower mobility requirement**—beneficial given chronic back
- Reduces spinal load vs. full snatch from floor
- Supports night-shift recovery (lower CNS demand; can be programmed mid-week)

---

## 5. **Athlete-Specific Notes**
| Consideration | Relevance |
|---|---|
| **OHS limiter (50 kg)** | Hip muscle snatch + hip power snatch target overhead lockout/confidence without squat depth demand |
| **Chronic back pain** | Shortened pull reduces lumbar stress; no floor pull = safer positioning |
| **Push jerk only** | Hip snatch turnover drills transfer to jerk confidence; no split needed here |
| **Hypertrophy block fit** | 4–5×3 @ moderate load maintains technical precision while building posterior chain (glutes, erectors, traps) |
| **Night shift recovery** | Lower intensity work fits mid-week; can repeat 2–3×/week without fatigue accumulation |

---

## **Programming Suggestion for 6-Week Block**
Weeks 1–3: **4×3 hip snatch @ 50–55 kg** + hip muscle snatch 3×2–3  
Weeks 4–6: Transition to **hip power snatch 5×2 @ 55–60 kg** to build catch confidence before full snatch re-entry
