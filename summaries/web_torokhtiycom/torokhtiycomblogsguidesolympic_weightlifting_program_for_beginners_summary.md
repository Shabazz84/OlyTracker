# https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/olympic-weightlifting-program-for-beginners

## Key Points
*   **Program Mismatch:** The source material is designed for novices (0–12 months experience) and lacks specific protocols for an intermediate athlete requiring a 6-week hypertrophy block or chronic injury management.
*   **Training Frequency:** Recommends 3 non-consecutive sessions per week, offering flexibility suitable for night shift workers (Wed–Sun) to manage circadian rhythm disruption.
*   **Intensity Distribution:** Suggests ~70–85% of volume at moderate intensity with minimal high-intensity work (>90%), prioritizing technique over max loading in early phases.
*   **Injury Management:** Emphasizes "spine neutrality" and paused squats to strengthen the core and control the bottom position, directly addressing chronic back pain risks.
*   **Current Lift Constraints:** The source validates the use of Push Jerk as a foundational movement but does not address the transition mechanics required for Split Jerk or the specific hypertrophy volume needed for an athlete with a 102.5 kg bodyweight and 118 kg squat.

## Technique Cues
*   **Back Squat:** Maintain strict spine neutrality; utilize **3-second pauses** at the bottom to eliminate the stretch-contraction reflex, forcing core engagement and eliminating momentum (critical for back pain).
*   **Snatch:** Focus on the "power position," rapid turnover speed, and securing the bar in the tallest possible position (Power Snatch emphasis); use deficit variations to improve flexibility.
*   **Clean:** Accelerate the bar quickly from blocks to maximize explosiveness; utilize complexes (e.g., Clean + Pull) for endurance.
*   **Jerk (Push Focus):** Drive firmly with legs and push with arms; feet move slightly sideways rather than splitting. This aligns with current limitations but requires specific cues for future Split Jerk transition.
*   **Squat Stance:** For narrow stances, keep knees tracking in the same direction as toes to preserve ankle mobility and depth without compromising comfort.

## Programming Principles
*   **Cycle Structure:** Adopt a 4–6 week cycle before changing loads; however, the source lacks specific hypertrophy set/rep schemes for intermediate athletes (e.g., 3–5 sets of 8–12 reps).
*   **Volume Ratios:** Propose a split where Snatch/Clean & Jerk drills comprise ~25% each, Pulls ~10–12%, and Squats ~25%.
*   **Session Duration:** Keep sessions between 45–80 minutes to prevent "perpetual exhaustion."
*   **Recovery Protocols:** Mandate light/medium days to prevent overuse; schedule active recovery (massages, sauna) on non-training days. Deload weeks are recommended for injury prevention.
*   **Exercise Selection:** Prioritize Power Snatch, Hang Snatch, Deficit Snatch, Power Clean, and Push Jerk as primary lifts; include Back Squat (paused), Front Squat, and Split Squats for strength.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Strength Profile:** The athlete's 118 kg squat indicates an intermediate level where the source's "novice" focus on basic technique is insufficient without adaptation to higher intensity or hypertrophy volume.
*   **Injury & Schedule:** The recommendation for non-consecutive training days and paused squats is highly relevant for a night shift worker managing chronic back pain, though specific load management for the 6-week block must be derived from external sources.
*   **Technical Limitations:** While the source supports the current Push Jerk-only approach, it fails to provide cues for the transition to Split Jerk or address the OHS (50 kg) as a primary limiter requiring specific overhead hypertrophy work.
*   **Actionable Gap:** The athlete must adapt this "Beginner's 2.0" framework by increasing volume/intensity for the first 6 weeks (hypertrophy block) and integrating specific back-strengthening accessory work not detailed in the source text.
