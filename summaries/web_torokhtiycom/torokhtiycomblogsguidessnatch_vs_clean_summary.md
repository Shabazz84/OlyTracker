# https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/snatch-vs-clean

## Key Points
*   **Movement Separation:** Snatch and Clean technique drills must be trained in separate sessions to prevent technical confusion; do not mix them in the same workout.
*   **Lifter Profile Constraints:** The general advice assumes full mobility and standard competition styles (Split/Squat Jerk). It does not account for chronic back pain, night shift fatigue, or a strict Push Jerk-only constraint.
*   **Primary Limiter:** Overhead Squat (OHS) stability is identified as the critical bottleneck for Snatch progression; current 50kg OHS limits full Snatch development.
*   **Exercise Selection:** Power variations (Power Snatch/Power Clean) are recommended over full lifts to build confidence and reduce mobility demands, which may aid an athlete with back limitations.

## Technique Cues
*   **Snatch Mechanics:** Maintain long, relaxed arms until full extension; avoid "Early Arm Bend." The bar must track vertically close to the body ("shaves the shirt"). Speed and precision are prioritized over raw pulling strength.
*   **Clean Mechanics:** Hips should initiate higher (10–15 cm above snatch height) than in the Snatch. Rise knees and hips simultaneously to prevent "pulling with the back." Focus on bar "elasticity" and aggressive elbow turnover to secure the rack position.
*   **Jerk Execution:** For Push Jerk, spread feet shoulder-width apart and stabilize the bar overhead from a semi-squat position rather than dropping into a full squat or split stance.

## Programming Principles
*   **Session Structure:** Adhere to strict separation of Snatch and Clean days (e.g., Day A: Snatch focus; Day B: Clean focus) to maintain technical clarity.
*   **Volume Management:** While generic 5-day/week programs exist, they are likely excessive for an intermediate athlete with chronic back pain transitioning from powerlifting. Volume must be managed to accommodate recovery needs.
*   **Progression Path:** Utilize Power Snatch and Power Clean as primary variations during the hypertrophy block to reduce spinal loading compared to full-depth lifts while maintaining technical patterns.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Back Pain Management:** The cue to keep the bar close and avoid pulling with the back is critical for reducing shear force on a compromised spine, though specific pain-modifying modifications are not provided in source material.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** Directly addresses the athlete's primary weakness; prioritizing OHS stability drills is essential to unlock Snatch potential.
*   **Night Shift & Recovery:** The generic advice lacks protocols for circadian rhythm disruption or sleep management required for a Wed–Sun night shift worker.
*   **Push Jerk Constraint:** The source treats Split/Squat Jerks as standard; the athlete must adapt general jerk cues specifically to the Push Jerk mechanics (no drop under) without relying on split/jerk-specific stability drills that are irrelevant to their style.
