**2. Technique cues**
*   **Push Jerk:** "Dip into a quarter squat," "explode up by extending hips and knees," "quickly drop under it," "catch with arms completely extended," "body should form a straight line." Focus on timing/coordination to catch in a partial squat before standing.
*   **Push Press:** "Bend hips/knees to quarter squat," "explosive drive from legs," "press bar overhead" (no drop under), "elbows locked out," "lower in controlled manner." Focus on using leg momentum to assist upper body pressing.
*   **General:** Keep torso upright during dip; ensure full extension of arms at the top.

**3. Exercise recommendations**
*   **Push Jerk:** Recommended for handling heavier loads, building explosive power, and improving setup/dip & drive form. Best for athletes with good leg-strength reserves.
*   **Push Press:** Recommended for building pure pressing strength (shoulders/triceps) and overhead stability. Simpler mechanics than the jerk.
*   **Split Jerk vs. Push/Power Jerk:** Split jerk preferred for agile/coordinated lifters; Push/Power Jerk better for those with strong legs or taller athletes (though text notes power jerk is not ideal for tall athletes).

**4. Recovery or mobility advice**
*   **Mobility Requirements:** Push Jerk demands high mobility in shoulders, hips, ankles, and thoracic spine due to the overhead squat catch position. Push Press requires good shoulder flexibility but less lower body/ankle mobility.
*   **Advice:** If tight in these areas, struggle will occur; recommends dedicated mobility drills and stretching to improve performance in both lifts.
