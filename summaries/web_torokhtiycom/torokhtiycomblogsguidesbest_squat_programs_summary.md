# https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/best-squat-programs

## Key Points
*   **Program Selection:** Adopt a hybrid approach prioritizing the **Torokhtiy Squat Program** (for injury prevention and accessory volume) or **Juggernaut Method** (specifically its "10s Phase" for hypertrophy). These align with the 6-week hypertrophy block goal.
*   **Contraindications:** Strictly avoid high-frequency, high-intensity models like **Squat Nemesis**, **Bulgarian Squat Method**, or **Tom Platz-style failure training**. These pose excessive injury risks given the athlete's chronic back pain and night-shift recovery constraints.
*   **Training Frequency:** Utilize a **3-day/week model** (e.g., Texas Method) to accommodate the Wed–Sun shift schedule, ensuring adequate rest days between sessions while managing circadian rhythm fatigue.
*   **Load Management:** Focus on moderate loads (50–80% 1RM) with higher rep ranges (6–12) for hypertrophy. Avoid training to absolute failure or "pushing through pain," which is explicitly warned against in the source text for intermediate lifters with back issues.
*   **Recovery Strategy:** Implement built-in deload weeks (per Juggernaut/5/3/1 principles) and utilize autoregulation—adjusting intensity based on daily sleep quality and fatigue levels rather than rigid targets.

## Technique Cues
*   **Pause Squats:** Incorporate pauses at the bottom of the squat to build isometric strength and stability in the most vulnerable position, reducing spinal shear forces for the athlete with chronic back pain.
*   **Front Squat Emphasis:** Prioritize Front Squats over heavy Back Squats where possible. This enforces an upright torso position (critical for improving the 50kg OHS limit) and reduces lumbar loading compared to traditional back squats.
*   **Mobility Integration:** Treat mobility in hips, ankles, and spine as a daily prerequisite, not an afterthought. Poor range of motion directly compromises squat depth and overhead stability.
*   **Dynamic Effort Caution:** While speed work (Westside style) is noted for power, apply it cautiously with submaximal loads (50–70%) only if form remains strict; high-speed movements can aggravate back pain if technical breakdown occurs.

## Programming Principles
*   **Phase Structure:** Execute a dedicated 6-week hypertrophy block focusing on volume and muscle growth before transitioning to technique/strength work.
*   **Accessory Focus:** Integrate "banded work," core stability drills, and joint health exercises (as highlighted in Torokhtiy) to support the primary lifts and address the OHS limitation.
*   **Exercise Selection:** Utilize a mix of Back Squats and Front Squats. The inclusion of Overhead Pressing is recommended as it directly transfers strength to the Push Jerk without requiring split mechanics.
*   **Periodization:** Follow a linear progression model initially, shifting to specialized accessory work once plateaus are reached. Avoid extreme intensity spikes; manage fatigue through structured deloads rather than pushing through chronic pain.

## Athlete Relevance
*   **Chronic Back Pain:** The recommended protocols prioritize injury prevention and stability over maximal loading. The "pause squat" variation and Front Squat emphasis specifically mitigate lumbar shear forces, making them safer alternatives to the athlete's current heavy back squat focus.
*   **Night Shift (Wed–Sun):** A 3-day/week schedule aligns best with the irregular wake cycle, preventing burnout associated with daily maxing models. Autoregulation is essential here to account for sleep debt and circadian disruption.
*   **Push Jerk Limitation:** Since the athlete lacks a Split Jerk, programming that emphasizes general overhead strength (Overhead Press) and core stability provides the necessary foundation for Push Jerk performance without relying on split mechanics.
*   **OHS Limiter (50kg):** The primary driver for improving the Overhead Squat is not direct loading but rather addressing mobility deficits in the hips/ankles and strengthening the anterior chain via Front Squats and core stability work, as identified by the source text's correlation between squat depth/stability and overhead performance.
