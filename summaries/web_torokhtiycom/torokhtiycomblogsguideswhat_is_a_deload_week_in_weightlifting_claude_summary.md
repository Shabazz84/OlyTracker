# Analysis: Deload Week in Weightlifting

## 1. **Programming Principles**
- **Frequency:** Deload every 4–8 weeks of intense training
- **Intensity reduction:** 40–60% of working weight (50% 1RM recommended)
- **Volume reduction:** 2–3 sets × 3–8 reps (vs. typical 5×5)
- **Rest intervals:** Increase to 2–3 min between sets
- **Purpose:** Prevent overtraining, hormonal rebalancing, glycogen replenishment, CNS recovery

## 2. **Technique Cues**
None provided.

## 3. **Exercise Recommendations**
- Vary movements (e.g., back squat → front squat, bench → DB bench)
- Include light aerobic work (20–30 min cardio, HR <65%)
- Incorporate mobility/stretching (30 min total body)
- Romanian deadlifts, bent-over rows, overhead press variations
- Light pull-ups (with assistance if needed)

## 4. **Recovery & Mobility Advice**
- Active recovery prioritized (light cardio, mobility drills, stretching)
- Improves circulation, flexibility, and overall well-being
- Monitor overtraining signs: plateau, strength drop, persistent soreness >72h, motivation loss, sleep/appetite disruption, elevated resting HR, frequent illness

## 5. **Athlete-Specific Relevance**
⚠️ **HIGHLY RELEVANT:**
- **Chronic back pain:** Deload + exercise variation (front squats over back squats) reduces spinal load
- **Night shift work:** Recovery/sleep disruption makes deload crucial for hormonal rebalancing (cortisol/testosterone)
- **OHS limiter (50 kg):** Mobility work directly addresses weakness
- **6-week hypertrophy block planned:** Insert deload at week 4–5 before strength phase
- **Intermediate status:** Likely needs deload every 4–6 weeks vs. beginners every 8+ weeks
